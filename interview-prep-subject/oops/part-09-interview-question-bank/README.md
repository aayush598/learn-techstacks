# Part: OOPs Interview Question Bank

## What this part covers
Everything in Parts 01-08 distilled into **practice**: a top-100 question bank (the classic questions with crisp answers), a set of coding problems with model solutions, a Low-Level Design (LLD) guide with the full design process and solved system examples (Parking Lot, ATM, Snake & Ladder), company-specific question heatmaps, and a 30-minute crash-course revision. This is the part you open the day before (and the week before) an interview — every question in it maps back to a specific section in Parts 01-08, so you can jump to the theory whenever an answer feels thin.

## Chapter map (table: chapter → sections → key skills)

| Chapter | Sections | Key skills you gain |
|---|---|---|
| ch-01 OOPs Interview Question Bank | Top-100 Q&A, coding problems, LLD guide, company-specific questions, crash course | Answer classic/theory questions crisply, solve OOP coding prompts, design low-level systems, know which companies drill what, and run a full revision pass |

## Study order
1. **Section 01 (Top-100)** first — it is the "warm-up" bank covering all the theory from Parts 01-08; use it to find your weak spots.
2. **Section 02 (Coding Problems)** — apply the theory on the whiteboard/editor; these are the 30-45 minute prompts that follow the theory Q&A.
3. **Section 03 (LLD Guide)** — the biggest differentiator at mid-to-senior interviews; learn the process and one solved design end-to-end.
4. **Section 04 (Company-specific)** — filter by your target company and drill the flagged questions.
5. **Section 05 (Crash Course)** — the night-before/morning-of rapid revision (30 minutes, all parts condensed).
For an 8-week plan: Week 1-4 → theory (Parts 01-08), Week 5 → Section 01, Week 6 → Section 02, Week 7 → Section 03, Week 8 → Sections 04-05 + mocks.

## Interview importance (0-5 stars) + which companies emphasize it
- **Importance: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5)** — this is the *payoff* of every other part; you will be asked exactly these questions.
- **Emphasized by**: all companies, but the split matters: **theory + basic coding** at fresher/backend screens (Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Uber, Flipkart); **LLD** at Amazon (LLD screens are standard at SDE-1/2), Flipkart, Walmart, Google (LLD at L4/L5), Uber, and most Indian-product-company loops; **deep Java/C++ internals** from Part 08 at JVM/C++ shops (see Section 04's heatmaps).
- Typical asked: "Explain polymorphism with an example", "Design a Parking Lot (LLD)", "How would you model a game of Snake and Ladder?", "Differences between abstract class and interface".

## How the parts connect (roadmap)
- **Sections 01, 02, and 04** pull questions from every part (01-08); each answer references its source section so you can drill the underlying theory.
- **Section 03 (LLD)** is the capstone application of SOLID (Part 06) + Design Patterns (Part 07) + object modeling (Parts 01-05) — it assumes you've done Parts 06 and 07 before attempting full designs.
- **Section 05 (Crash Course)** condenses all of Parts 01-08 into one revision pass — it's the "last mile" before interviews.
- Recommended full sequence: Part 01 → 02 → 03 → 04 → 05 → 06 → 07 → 08 → **09**. If you're short on time and target only LLD: Part 01 → 06 → 07 → Part 09 Section 03.
