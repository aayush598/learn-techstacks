## One-paragraph narrative connecting all sections
Section 01 is the core: 100 curated Q&As spanning every part — processes/threads, scheduling, synchronization, deadlocks, memory management, virtual memory, file systems, IPC, and Linux internals — each with the "interview answer" and a pointer to the deep section. Section 02 pushes into open-ended territory: the "design a scheduler / filesystem / IPC" prompts and the behavioral "tell me about a debugging war story" questions, with response frameworks (requirements → components → trade-offs → example). Section 03 compresses the whole series into a rapid-revision table so you can re-anchor everything the night before. Section 04 shapes practice by loop flavor (FAANG systems-generalist, Linux/cloud platform, distributed-systems-leaning), telling you which sub-topics deserve extra reps.

## Common interview trap in this chapter
**Trap:** Memorizing answers without structure — interviewers reward the *framework* ("the trade-off is X vs Y; here's how I'd decide"), not recall. Also: giving a one-liner where a trace is expected ("what happens when you type a command" wants the full path), and ignoring that "design a…" questions are graded on your requirements-gathering and trade-off discussion, not the final design.
