# 03 - DIGITAL CONTROL OPTIONS

**Folder:** `03-ROTATION-DUTY/05-Key-and-Control/`  
**Cross-reference:** `04-No-Single-Point-of-Failure.md`, `01-Duty-Roster/04-Duty-Log-Register.md`, `06-FINANCE/02-Fund-Management/01-Common-Fund.md`, `02-Duty-Training/03-Operation-Handbook.md`

## 1. WHY DIGITAL CONTROL

A timer or a mobile-switchable starter removes the physical key from the centre
of the system entirely. Digital control is **optional** — the physical-key
system already works — but where the assembly chooses it, it strengthens P3 by
adding a third, independent way to operate the motor.

## 2. THE OPTIONS

| Option | How it works | Strengths | Costs |
|--------|--------------|-----------|-------|
| Timer-based panel | The motor runs automatically at preset schedule times | Removes the need to be physically present | No remote control; schedule changes require panel access |
| GSM switch / smart starter | A starter with a SIM card or controller; any authorised phone switches it via call or app | Remote on/off from anywhere; logs timestamps | Monthly SIM/data cost; depends on network |
| IoT switch with mobile app | Wi-Fi/cloud-connected starter | Full log on the phone, remote control, alerts | Needs internet; more technical setup |
| Logged smart panel | Starter with built-in run logging | Tamper-evident run record | Higher purchase cost |

## 3. THE CONSTITUTIONAL REQUIREMENTS FOR ANY OPTION

Whatever technology is chosen must satisfy these rules:

1. **Everyone's phone can switch it.** Authorisation is granted to all trained
   members. If only one phone can control the motor, that phone is a single
   point of failure.
2. **Logs are tamper-evident.** Every on/off event is timestamped and the log
   cannot be edited by a member — only by a documented maintenance process.
   The log feeds the duty log register (`01-Duty-Roster/04-Duty-Log-Register.md`).
3. **The physical keys still exist as a fallback.** If the network fails or
   the app crashes, the duty box key still opens the panel. Digital control
   adds redundancy; it never removes it.
4. **No hidden access.** A member who holds a "special" control privilege that
   others lack is a new ruler; privileges are equal for all trained members.

## 4. DECISION AND COSTS

1. Installation of any digital control is decided **by the General Assembly**
   by resolution — never by one member's enthusiasm.
2. Costs are borne by the **common fund**, budgeted and approved like any other
   capital expense (`06-FINANCE/`).
3. Recurring costs (SIM, data, electricity for the controller) are regular
   budget lines and are reviewed quarterly.
4. Vendor selection follows the procurement rules; the installers' details and
   warranty documents are filed in `05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/`.

## 5. TRAINING ON THE DIGITAL CONTROLS

1. Digital operation is added to the training curriculum
   (`02-Duty-Training/02-Training-Schedule.md`) so "I don't know how to use
   the app" can never become an excuse that concentrates control again.
2. The Operation Handbook (`02-Duty-Training/03-Operation-Handbook.md`) gains a
   section on the installed option.
3. A printed instruction card is kept in the duty box.

## 6. SCENARIO

The assembly approves a GSM starter because the night duty is unpopular. Now a
member can switch the motor on or off from the family shop, and the panel logs
every event with a timestamp. When a member complains that "the motor ran an
extra 20 minutes last Tuesday", the log shows exactly who switched it, when,
and from which number. The duty log register and the digital log are
reconciled monthly.
