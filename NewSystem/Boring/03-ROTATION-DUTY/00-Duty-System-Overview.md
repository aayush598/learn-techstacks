# 00 - DUTY SYSTEM OVERVIEW

**Folder:** `03-ROTATION-DUTY/`  
**Cross-reference:** `00-CONSTITUTION/03-Fundamental-Duties/02-Duty-to-Learn-and-Participate.md`, `00-CONSTITUTION/01-Preamble/03-Definitions.md`

---

## 1. THE PROBLEM THIS SYSTEM KILLS

In the old system, one person or one family held the keys to the motor room and
decided everything: when the motor ran, who got water, when repairs happened,
and what was said to the government. That person could:

- Switch the motor on at 2 a.m. for their own use and switch it off before
  others woke.
- Hide the schedule from other members so nobody could check them.
- Deny or delay water to members "not in their good list".
- Delay repairs until a favour was done for them.
- Never train anyone else, so the moment they left, the colony was helpless.

Every one of those abuses was possible because **one person was the single
point of control**. The duty system in this folder removes that single point
entirely.

## 2. WHAT DUTY MEANS

Duty is not "ownership". Duty is a rotating, compensated service performed by
every member for the collective. One duty period consists of three tasks:

1. **Operation** — switch the motor on and off at the scheduled times.
2. **Logging** — record the run in the motor run log (time on, time off, gauge
   reading, anomalies) per `04-MOTOR-OPERATIONS/04-Monitoring-and-Logs/01-Motor-Run-Log.md`.
3. **Asset check** — verify the motor room is secure, the panel shows no fault
   indicator, and there is no leakage or tampering; report anything unusual.

## 3. THE DESIGN PRINCIPLES OF THIS SYSTEM

| Principle | How the duty system implements it |
|-----------|-----------------------------------|
| P2 Equal power | No permanent operator. The roster is drawn by lot; every member takes duty in turn. |
| P3 No single point of failure | Every adult member is trained; keys are duplicated; a backup list of at least 3 trained members covers every duty. |
| P4 Contribution mandatory and fair | Duty is compulsory for every able member; no one may silently shift their share onto others. |
| P8 Work rewarded | Every duty hour earns a time-credit or stipend; no one works free. |
| P9 Self-sufficiency | If any member leaves, refuses, or falls sick, the next trained member and the backups keep the system running. |
| P10 Dignity | Exempt members are not ridiculed; they contribute by paying the buy-out instead, which is honourable, not shameful. |

## 4. EVERY ADULT MEMBER MUST BE ABLE TO OPERATE

A member who cannot operate the motor can never take duty, so the same few
people would end up doing everything — which recreates the old hierarchy in a
new form. Therefore:

1. **Training is compulsory** before first duty
   (`02-Duty-Training/01-Compulsory-Operation-Training.md`).
2. Training is free and held at least twice a year.
3. Only members with a **documented medical hardship** are exempt from
   operating; they contribute through the buy-out (`01-Duty-Roster/01-Roster-Design.md`).
4. Training is not a favour done to the member. It is the transfer of **power
   to the member**, so no member ever depends on another person's goodwill for
   water.

## 5. WHY COMPENSATION MAKES DUTY ATTRACTIVE

Duty consumes a member's time and responsibility. Without compensation:

- Members dodge duty, and work quietly piles onto the cooperative few.
- The cooperative few become resentful, then indispensable, then in charge.

With compensation:

- Duty is seen as a fair **earning opportunity**, not a burden.
- 1 duty hour = 1 time-credit; the credit value is published quarterly
  (`03-Duty-Compensation/01-Time-Credits.md`), or a cash stipend is paid where
  the assembly chooses (`03-Duty-Compensation/02-Monetary-Stipend.md`).
- Members who cannot operate pay the buy-out, which funds the compensation of
  those who do the duty. The system is balanced by money, not by goodwill.

## 6. HOW THE SYSTEM SELF-HEALS

| Event | What happens automatically |
|-------|---------------------------|
| The only person who knew the panel leaves the colony | It does not matter. Every member is trained, the Operation Handbook is on the wall, and the backup list fills every duty. |
| The assigned duty member falls sick at 5 a.m. | The backup list (`04-Duty-Failure/02-Immediate-Replacement-System.md`) covers the duty; the sick member files a documented exemption. |
| A member refuses to train | They are placed on buy-out: they pay, others get compensated to cover their share. Water is never cut without due process. |
| The key holder moves away | Keys rotate with duty and are duplicated (`05-Key-and-Control/`); the colony is never locked out. |
| A member stops showing up for duty | Missed-duty penalty applies, then chronic-avoidance measures (`04-Duty-Failure/`) — never silent tolerance. |
