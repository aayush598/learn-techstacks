# 03 - SUBSIDY FUND HANDLING

**Folder:** `08-GOVERNMENT-INTERACTION/02-Grants-and-Subsidies/`  
**Cross-reference:** `02-Application-Process.md`, `06-FINANCE/02-Fund-Management/01-Common-Fund.md`, `00-CONSTITUTION/02-Fundamental-Rights/02-Right-to-Information-and-Audit.md`

## 1. THE GOLDEN RULE OF PUBLIC MONEY

**A subsidy received is not a gift to anyone. It is public money held in trust
for the colony and must be spent only for the purpose for which it was
sanctioned.** Every rupee of it is traceable from sanction letter to bank
statement to receipt.

## 2. WHERE THE MONEY GOES

1. Subsidies and grants received by the colony are credited to the **joint
   account** of the colony (the same account used for the common fund) or to
   the specific account the scheme requires.
2. **No subsidy money is ever credited to a personal account.**
3. The sanction letter's conditions govern the spending: if the sanction says
   "motor purchase", the money buys a motor — not a celebration, not a
   personal loan.

## 3. RECORDING

1. The sanction letter, the credit advice, and every utilisation receipt are
   filed in the government file and entered in the grants register.
2. The common fund ledger carries a **sub-schedule** for the subsidy: funds in,
   purchases out, balances — reconciled to the bank statement monthly.
3. At every assembly, the subsidy balance and its purpose are reported in the
   financial report, like every other line of the fund.

## 4. WHEN THE MOTOR BELONGS TO THE GOVERNMENT

1. If the motor itself is government property, the colony's contribution is
   **only what the colony and the department have agreed** (e.g., electricity,
   minor consumables, or the agreed repair share).
2. The colony never silently takes on the department's repair bills, and never
   quietly spends its own subsidy on a government asset without a written
   agreement — otherwise the money vanishes into a black hole.
3. The agreement (letter, minutes of meeting) is filed like any other
   government document.

## 5. WHAT SUBSIDY MONEY MAY AND MAY NOT PAY

| Allowed (per sanction) | Not allowed |
|------------------------|-------------|
| Motor, pump, panel, pipeline, tank, installation | Any purpose outside the sanction letter |
| Sanctioned maintenance or operating cost | Payments to members as "commissions" |
| Required scheme contributions | Gifts, hospitality, travel of private persons |
| Documented, receipted expenses of the scheme | Reimbursement of a single member's "efforts" without vouchers |

## 6. MISUSE IS A SERIOUS VIOLATION

1. Spending subsidy money off-purpose is treated as theft from the community
   and from the state; it is reported to the assembly and, if the assembly so
   decides, to the funding authority — the colony does not protect a member who
   abuses public money.
2. A member who collects "charges" from others while pocketing a subsidy is
   handled under `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/`.
3. The auditors (any member, any time) may trace every rupee; no book may be
   "off-limits".

## 7. SCENARIO

A motor subsidy is sanctioned. One member proposes that the colony "keep some
aside for future repairs" while buying a cheaper motor. The proposal is
rejected: the sanction is for the motor, and the balance stays recorded for
the sanctioned purpose. Two months later an auditor finds every rupee of the
subsidy traceable to the purchased motor and its receipts. The grant is
clean, and the colony's standing with the department is intact.
