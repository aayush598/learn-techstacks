# 04 - TRANSPARENCY WITH AUTHORITIES

**Folder:** `08-GOVERNMENT-INTERACTION/02-Grants-and-Subsidies/`  
**Cross-reference:** `03-Subsidy-Fund-Handling.md`, `00-Government-Scope.md`, `01-Government-Motor/01-Document-Retention.md`

---

## 1. THE PRINCIPLE

The colony is truthful with the government, and the government's dealings with
the colony are truthfully recorded. Transparency with authorities is not a
concession; it is what keeps the colony's own claim to fairness alive. A colony
that hides facts from officials cannot honestly demand officials be honest with
it.

## 2. WHAT THE COLONY GIVES AUTHORITIES

1. **True documents:** the real sanction, the real bills, the real number of
   connections. The colony never prepares two sets of papers — one for the
   department and one for the members.
2. **True facts:** actual member count, actual consumption pattern, actual
   cost of operations, actual damage.
3. **True records on demand:** duty logs, accounts, and asset registers when an
   inspection legally requires them.

## 3. WHAT THE COLONY RECORDS FROM AUTHORITIES

1. Every official decision is recorded: letters, orders, inspection findings,
   promises made at visits (`01-Government-Motor/04-Government-Visit-Coordination.md`).
2. A written assurance that the department later forgets is not lost — it is in
   the file, and it can be raised.
3. Government replies are treated as public records within the colony: any
   member may read them.

## 4. NO HIDING MEMBER DUES FROM AUDITORS

1. If an authority audits the colony (subsidy utilisation, connection count,
   electricity rebate), the colony shows **the full accounts, including the
   names and due status of every member**.
2. A member who is behind on contributions is not "protected" by the colony
   hiding their name; the colony reports facts and collects what it is owed
   through its own processes.
3. Hiding dues from an auditor is fraud-adjacent and is treated as a serious
   violation — the colony never becomes a shield for defaulters.

## 5. THE HONESTY BOUNDARY

| The colony does | The colony never does |
|-----------------|-----------------------|
| Disclose real facts, even embarrassing ones | Pad member counts to claim more subsidy |
| Report actual consumption and damage | Submit false bills or inflated invoices |
| Show the real account books | Keep two sets of books |
| Disclose a defaulting member to auditors | Coach a member to lie to an official |
| Correct its own records when errors are found | Ignore a factual error that favours the colony |

## 6. THE COST OF TRANSPARENCY — AND WHY IT IS PAID

Honesty can be costly in the short term: a subsidy might shrink, a bill might
be genuine, a defaulting member might be embarrassed. The constitution accepts
this cost, because the alternative — a lying colony — eventually collapses into
exactly the old system: secrets, favourites, and control by whoever holds the
documents. Truth (P5) is the foundation; hiding is the first step back to
tyranny.

## 7. SCENARIO

An auditor asks for the connection register during a subsidy utilisation check.
One member suggests "reporting a few extra households so the utilisation looks
fuller". The suggestion is refused. The true register is shown; the actual
shortfall is stated. The auditor notes the honest reporting, and the colony's
file stays clean — and when the next scheme opens, the colony's history of
truthful books is its best recommendation.
