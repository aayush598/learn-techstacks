# 01 - HYBRID ARRANGEMENTS

## 1. WHAT A HYBRID IS

A hybrid is any arrangement where the colony's system is **part private and
part government**: the boring was drilled with colony money but the motor was a
government subsidy; the government granted the land but the colony bought the
pump; the electricity is a scheme connection but the maintenance is colony-run.
Hybrids are common, and they are only confusing if the two rulebooks are
mixed by whoever feels like deciding that day.

## 2. THE SPLIT OF RULES

The guiding rule: **each asset is governed by the rules that apply to its
ownership.**

| Part of the system | Rules that apply |
|--------------------|------------------|
| Government-owned asset (e.g., sanctioned motor, scheme pipeline) | `01-Government-Motor/` — document retention, complaint channel, RTI, visit rules |
| Colony-owned asset (e.g., colony-funded boring, tank, colony-maintained wiring) | The constitution's own operation and maintenance folders (`04-MOTOR-OPERATIONS/`, `05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/`) |
| Money from government (subsidy, grant) | `02-Grants-and-Subsidies/` — public money, sanction-bound |
| Money from members | `06-FINANCE/` — common fund rules |
| Disputes over the government part | `04-Disputes-with-Government/` |
| Disputes over the colony part | `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/` |

## 3. MERGED FUND ACCOUNTING

1. A hybrid still uses **one bank account** and **one ledger** — never a
   "government pot" and a "private pot" that no one can reconcile.
2. The ledger carries the subsidy/grant as a marked sub-schedule
   (`02-Grants-and-Subsidies/03-Subsidy-Fund-Handling.md`) and the member
   contributions as their own lines, all in the same book.
3. Every rupee — whether it entered as subsidy or as contribution — is spent
   with a receipt and reported at the assembly. There is no second book.

## 4. ONE REGISTER FOR ALL

1. The government file holds the government documents; the colony's asset
   register holds the colony's assets; and a **single index** in the record
   keeper's register maps which document governs which asset.
2. If a dispute arises about the motor, the index answers instantly: whose
   motor is it, what rules govern it, and where is the proof.
3. The index is public and updated at every quarterly assembly.

## 5. CONTRIBUTION OBLIGATIONS IN A HYBRID

1. If the government owns the motor, the colony contributes only what was
   agreed (electricity, agreed repair share) — and that agreement is written
   and filed.
2. If the colony owns the boring and the government the motor, both parts are
   maintained per their own rules; the colony does not silently absorb the
   department's repair burden, and the department does not silently take over
   the colony's asset.

## 6. SCENARIO

The colony drilled its boring with pooled money, and later a scheme provided a
subsidy for a new submersible motor. The old "owner" claims the whole system
is now government property; the department claims it can operate the motor
whenever it likes. The assembly pulls the index: the boring is colony-owned
(contributions ledger proves it), the motor is scheme-sanctioned (sanction
letter proves it), and the electricity is a scheme connection. Each part is
handled by its own rules, and the "claims" collapse into a documented answer.
