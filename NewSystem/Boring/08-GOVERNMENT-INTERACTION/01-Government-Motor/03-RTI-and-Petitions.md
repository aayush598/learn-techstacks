## 1. THE RIGHT TO INFORMATION APPLIES TO THE COLONY TOO

The Right to Information (RTI) Act gives any citizen the right to ask for
government records. For a colony whose motor belongs to the government, RTI is a
practical tool: it can force the department to show the sanction, the repair
history, the allocation rules, and the reasons for delay. The colony's own
constitution guarantees the same idea internally (P6); RTI extends it to the
state.

## 2. WHAT THE COLONY MAY ASK FOR

Under RTI, the colony may request, from the responsible public authority:

1. Documents about the boring/motor sanction and allocation.
2. Repair and maintenance records for the equipment.
3. The scheme rules under which the colony's water supply operates.
4. Reasons for a reduction in allocation or a rejected claim.
5. The names and designations of officers responsible for a function.

## 3. THE RTI PROCEDURE

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Draft the application in simple language: one question per application (fast, cheap, reliable). |
| 2 | Address it to the Public Information Officer (PIO) of the concerned office, with the RTI fee paid per current rules. |
| 3 | Send by registered post or the online RTI portal and keep the acknowledgement. |
| 4 | Record it in the colony's RTI/register with the date and reference. |
| 5 | If the answer is late, wrong, or refused: file a First Appeal to the First Appellate Authority within the statutory window. |
| 6 | If needed, a Second Appeal to the State Information Commission. |

## 4. SIGNATURES AND WHO MAY FILE

1. An RTI application may be filed by **any member**, on the colony's behalf,
   or by the member in their own name.
2. The colony's RTI applications are recorded in the complaint register so
   that no member files a private RTI that secretly attacks another member; the
   register keeps it public and honest.
3. A member filing RTI does not need anyone's permission. Silence from the
   committee is not an obstacle — the law is the permission.

## 5. CITIZEN'S CHARTER AND PETITIONS

1. Many departments publish a **citizen's charter**: promised service timelines
   (e.g., "repair within X days of complaint"). The colony keeps a copy in the
   government file and cites it in complaints and appeals.
2. For larger issues (e.g., a new boring, a higher allocation), the colony may
   submit a **signed petition**: collected signatures of affected households,
   the demand in writing, and copies to the department and the elected
   representative (MLA/MP/corporator).
3. Petitions are recorded like complaints: register, copies, follow-up dates.

## 6. WHERE TO SEEK HELP

1. A member who finds the forms difficult may be helped by any trained member;
   training covers RTI basics in the government-interaction skill set.
2. Free RTI help lines and civil-society organisations exist in most states;
   the colony keeps a short list of verified contacts in the government file.
3. If the authority is evasive and the matter is important, the assembly may
   authorise the legal representation fund
   (`04-Disputes-with-Government/02-Legal-Representation-Fund.md`).

## 7. SCENARIO

The department says the colony's borewell "does not exist in their records" and
refuses all repairs. A member files an RTI asking for the sanction records of
the borewell by village and location. The department's own reply produces the
sanction — the borewell exists, and the refusal was baseless. The reply is
filed, cited in the next complaint, and the repair is ordered. RTI turned a
"he-said-she-said" into a documented fact.
