# STEP 1 — OPEN DIALOGUE

**Folder:** `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/02-Resolution-Process/`  
**Cross-reference:** `02-GOVERNANCE/02-Committees/05-Conflict-Review-Board.md`, `12-TEMPLATES/09-Grievance-Form.md`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

Step 1 is the **first and cheapest** step of the conflict model. Two members (or
a member and the committee concerned) sit down with a neutral facilitator and
talk. The goal is not to find who is "right" in an abstract sense — it is to
resolve the practical problem before it hardens into a case.

Most conflicts in this colony should end at Step 1. Escalation is not a failure,
but it is slower and costlier for everyone, so **good-faith dialogue is
expected before escalating**.

## 2. WHO FACILITATES

- A **neutral facilitator**: any member with no stake in the dispute, chosen
  by agreement of both sides, or a trained mediator, or a CRB-designated
  facilitator.
- The facilitator **decides nothing**. They manage the conversation: who
  speaks, when, and in what tone.
- If the parties cannot agree on a facilitator, the CRB appoints one from a
  standing list (lot-drawn if needed).

## 3. THE GROUND RULES OF THE DIALOGUE

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| No shouting, threats, or insults | The system resolves by evidence, not volume. A dialogue that turns into abuse is halted on the spot. |
| Both sides speak fully | Each side gets an uninterrupted chance to state their facts and what they want. |
| No decisions forced | Nothing is settled by whoever talks loudest or longest. Agreement must be genuine. |
| Facts, not character | Members describe actions and records, not personalities. |
| No water threats | Neither side may hint at "cutting water" or "getting back at" the other as leverage. That is targeting. |
| Private | What is said in the dialogue is confidential to the participants. |

## 4. THE STRUCTURE OF A STEP 1 MEETING

1. **Opening (5 min):** facilitator states the rules and the one question —
   "what is the problem, and what is a fair workable fix?"
2. **Each side speaks (10 min each):** facts, evidence they hold, and the
   remedy they want.
3. **Clarifying questions (10 min):** each side may ask questions of the other,
   through the facilitator.
4. **Options (15 min):** the facilitator lists the workable options — including
   "agree to disagree" on the past but agree on future conduct.
5. **Close:** either a written agreement (preferred) or a clear statement of
   what remains unresolved.

## 5. OUTCOMES

| Outcome | What happens next |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Settlement** | Written, dated, signed by both. It is binding on the parties, and it may be enforced through `05-Enforcement/` if breached. |
| **Partial agreement** | The agreed part is written down; the rest may go to Step 2. |
| **No agreement** | The facilitator records the unresolved points. Either side may file a written grievance (Step 2). |
| **Refusal to attend** | The member refusing must explain why in writing. Unreasonable refusal is noted and may accelerate the case — against the refuser, not the willing party. |

## 6. GOOD-FAITH IS EXPECTED — BUT NOT FORCED

- A member who attends, listens, and proposes options is acting in good faith
  even if they refuse an agreement.
- A member who attends only to shout, or who uses the dialogue to gather
  information for retaliation, has acted in bad faith; the facilitator records
  it and the CRB may treat it as obstruction.
- **No penalty for losing or refusing to settle.** Settlement is voluntary;
  facts are not bought by the threat of escalation.

## 7. TIMELINE AND COST

- The dialogue is held **within 3 days** of the request, normally on the same
  day where both are available.
- Cost: zero, or a nominal facilitator time-credit.
- If a member wants to escalate directly because the dispute involves
  targeting, violence, or tampering, they may skip to Step 2 or a Cease and
  Desist order (`05-Enforcement/02-Cease-and-Desist.md`) — Step 1 is never
  forced in cases of ongoing harm.

## 8. RECORD

The facilitator writes a short note: date, who attended, points agreed, points
unresolved. The note is kept in the CRB file. If the matter never escalates, the
note is the whole record. If it does escalate, the note becomes the starting
point for the written grievance — no one starts from zero twice.
