# STEP 2 — WRITTEN GRIEVANCE

**Folder:** `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/02-Resolution-Process/`  
**Cross-reference:** `12-TEMPLATES/09-Grievance-Form.md`, `02-GOVERNANCE/02-Committees/05-Conflict-Review-Board.md`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

If Step 1 dialogue fails (or is not appropriate), either side may file a
**written grievance**. The written grievance is the formal point of entry into
the conflict system: from this moment, the matter has a case number, a file, and
a right to a hearing.

The written grievance exists so that a dispute does not depend on who shouts
loudest or who has the best memory. Once written, the grievance is a fixed
object that facts and rules can be applied to.

## 2. HOW TO FILE

1. Complete the grievance form (`12-TEMPLATES/09-Grievance-Form.md`).
2. Submit it to the Conflict Review Board (CRB) inbox, in person, or through
   the anonymous channel where the member fears retaliation
   (`03-Protection-Mechanisms/03-Whistleblower-Protection.md`).
3. The CRB registers it within **1 working day** and issues a case number.
4. The CRB records the filing date. All timelines run from that date.

## 3. WHAT A VALID GRIEVANCE MUST CONTAIN

| Field | Requirement |
|-------|-------------|
| Parties | Who is complaining and against whom (or "unknown person" with evidence) |
| Facts | What happened, in order: dates, times, places |
| Evidence | Logs, receipts, photos, names of witnesses — attached or listed |
| Rule or right relied on | The specific rule cited, or the fundamental right engaged |
| Remedy sought | What the member wants: restore water, refund, correction, apology, fine |
| Good-faith declaration | The member states the facts are true to their knowledge |

A grievance missing the remedy is sent back for completion within **3 days**.
A grievance missing only evidence may proceed — evidence is gathered at the
hearing, not demanded at the gate. Filing must be possible for any member,
including one who cannot write well; the CRB intake officer writes it down for
them.

## 4. TIMELINE ONCE FILED

| Event | Time |
|-------|------|
| Registration and case number | Within 1 working day |
| Notice to the other side | Within 2 working days of registration |
| Response by the other side | Within 5 working days |
| Hearing scheduled | Within 10 working days of registration |
| Decision (Step 3) | Within 7 days of the hearing |

## 5. WHAT FILING DOES — AND DOES NOT — DO

**Does:**

- Opens the right to a hearing before the CRB.
- Triggers the status-quo rule: no step may worsen the situation while pending.
- Protects the filer from retaliation (`03-Protection-Mechanisms/04-No-Retaliation-Rule.md`).
- Where targeting is alleged, triggers the immediate water-restoration rule of
  `00-CONSTITUTION/02-Fundamental-Rights/04-Right-to-Protection-from-Targeting.md`.

**Does not:**

- Assume the filer is right. The other side is heard too.
- Freeze a legitimate pending repair. Operational work continues under the
  published rules unless the CRB orders otherwise.
- Punish the person complained about in any way before a finding.

## 6. THE DUTY TO ANSWER

A member who receives a notice **must respond in writing within 5 working
days**. A member who refuses to respond is not presumed guilty — but the
hearing proceeds on the evidence available, and the refusal is recorded. No one
can stall a case by staying silent.

## 7. NO RETALIATION FOR FILING

Filing a grievance is a right, not a provocation. Any adverse action against a
member because they filed — water cut, charges raised, duty assigned unfairly,
boycott, rumours — is retaliation and doubles the penalty of the original
offence (`03-Protection-Mechanisms/04-No-Retaliation-Rule.md`).

*Example:* A member files a grievance that the treasurer's figures do not add
up. The next day she finds her water slot moved to 2 a.m. and is told "that is
what happens to complainers". This is retaliation on top of the original issue.
The slot is restored immediately, the offender faces the ladder at Level 3+, and
the original grievance proceeds independently.

## 8. THE GRIEVANCE LOG

The CRB maintains a public grievance register: case number, parties (or
anonymised), nature, status, and outcome. The register is open to every member
(P6). The file itself is confidential to the parties and the CRB, except that
final reasoned decisions are published — stripped of anything identifying
vulnerable witnesses where the CRB orders it.
