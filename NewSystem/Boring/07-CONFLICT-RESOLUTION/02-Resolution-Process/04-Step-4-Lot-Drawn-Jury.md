# STEP 4 — LOT-DRAWN JURY

**Folder:** `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/02-Resolution-Process/`  
**Cross-reference:** `02-GOVERNANCE/05-Rotation-and-Fairness/02-Lot-Draw-System.md`, `02-GOVERNANCE/02-Committees/05-Conflict-Review-Board.md`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

Step 4 is the **appeal** from a CRB decision. Any party may appeal within
**10 days** of the decision. The appeal is heard by a **fresh 5-person jury
drawn by lot** — deliberately a different body from the CRB, so that no one can
predict or influence who will decide.

The jury performs a **full rehearing**: it is not limited to the CRB's record.
It may accept new evidence, re-examine witnesses, and reach its own findings.

## 2. WHY A LOT-DRAWN JURY

| Design | Why |
|--------|-----|
| Drawn by lot | No one can recruit, lobby, or intimidate the panel in advance. P2. |
| Five members | Large enough to pool judgment, small enough to hear carefully. |
| Fresh body | The dispute is looked at with new eyes, not defended by its first decider. |
| No relatives or friends of the parties | Excluded at the draw, exactly as in `02-GOVERNANCE/05-Rotation-and-Fairness/02-Lot-Draw-System.md`. |
| Reasons published | The jury must write a reasoned decision, so it cannot decide by friendship or pressure. |

## 3. THE DRAW

1. The CRB clerk draws 5 names by lot from the eligible pool (all current
   members with training, excluding both parties, their households, relatives,
   and named friends of either side).
2. Two reserves are drawn alongside.
3. Any juror may state a conflict at the draw and is replaced. Hiding a known
   conflict and serving anyway voids the jury's decision and is an offence at
   Level 3+.
4. The draw is witnessed by at least two members who are not parties.

## 4. THE REHEARING

The jury follows the same hearing discipline as the CRB
(`03-Step-3-Conflict-Review-Board.md`):

1. The appellant states why the CRB decision is wrong — on facts, on rules, or
   both.
2. The other side responds.
3. The jury may recall the CRB panel's file, call new witnesses, and ask for
   new evidence (logs, receipts, records).
4. Both parties appear and speak. Neither side is ever decided against without
   being heard (presence rule).
5. The hearing is completed within **14 days** of the appeal being filed.

## 5. STANDARD OF REVIEW

The jury is not bound to agree with the CRB. It applies the same burden of
proof — preponderance of credible evidence — and may:

- **Affirm** the CRB decision in full.
- **Vary** it (change the remedy, reduce or increase a fine within the rules).
- **Reverse** it where the CRB's factual finding was wrong or the rule was
  misapplied.
- **Remand** only for new evidence that could not reasonably have been
  produced earlier — not to give a party a second chance to argue.

## 6. THE FINAL INTERNAL DECISION

- The jury's written, reasoned decision is issued within **14 days** of the
  hearing.
- It is the **final internal decision** of the system on the facts.
- It is binding on both parties and enforceable through `05-Enforcement/`.
- The only remaining internal recourse is the Step 5 appeal to the General
  Assembly, and that is limited to **rule interpretation** — the facts decided
  here are not re-tried.

## 7. PROTECTIONS DURING THE APPEAL

- **Stay of penalties:** a penalty imposed by the CRB is stayed while the
  appeal is pending. Any order that stops ongoing harm (Cease and Desist) stays
  in force.
- **No retaliation:** a party who appeals is not penalised for appealing.
- **Status quo:** no new adverse step against a party while the appeal is
  pending.

*Example:* The CRB ordered House 3 to pay ₹2,000 for a private-line repair.
House 3 appeals. While the jury hears the case, the ₹2,000 is not collected and
House 3's water is untouched. The jury, looking at the asset register, finds the
line is a common line, reverses the CRB, and orders the ₹2,000 already noted in
House 3's account to be cleared. Both parties receive the written reasons.

## 8. COST AND ABUSE

- The appeal costs a nominal filing fee (refunded if the appeal succeeds), to
  discourage pure delay.
- Repeated appeals without basis attract a warning, then obstruction
  consequences — but a genuine appeal is never penalised.
