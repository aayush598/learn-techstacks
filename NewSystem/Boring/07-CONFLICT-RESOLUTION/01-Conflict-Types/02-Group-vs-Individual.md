# 02 - GROUP VS INDIVIDUAL

**Folder:** `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/01-Conflict-Types/`  
**Cross-reference:** `00-CONSTITUTION/02-Fundamental-Rights/04-Right-to-Protection-from-Targeting.md`, `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/03-Protection-Mechanisms/01-Protection-from-Targeting.md`

---

## 1. WHAT THIS CASE IS

A dispute between **one member and several others**, or between **a small
group and a larger group**, about the water system. Examples:

- Ten members complain that one member's family is "taking too much water".
- One member complains that five neighbours are draining the line together.
- Six members demand that a lone member pay a "special levy" for a pipe that
  runs past their house.

## 2. THE STRUCTURAL DANGER

A group brings numbers, memory of past grievances, and the ability to talk to
each other in private. One member brings only themselves. Without special
protections, the group will win every dispute by sheer volume — and the old
system's worst abuses happened precisely when a crowd "all agreed" against one
person.

The protections below are not favouritism toward the individual. They are the
only way to keep the dispute about **facts and rules** instead of about **who
has more friends**.

## 3. SPECIAL PROTECTIONS FOR THE INDIVIDUAL SIDE

| Protection | Rule |
|------------|------|
| **Notification** | The individual must be personally notified of every complaint against them in writing, with the complaint text, before any step. |
| **Right to be heard** | The individual speaks at every hearing, in their own voice, before any decision. |
| **Written reasons** | Any adverse outcome must state reasons in writing, citing evidence and rules. |
| **No outvoting into loss** | No group vote can strip the individual of water, refunds, credits, or rights. Adverse water outcomes require the full enforcement ladder. |
| **Status quo** | Nothing changes (no slot cut, no charge, no duty change) until the individual has been heard. |
| **Not forced to face the crowd** | The individual may be heard separately from the group, and never has to confront the whole group at once (`03-Protection-Mechanisms/02-Protection-from-Coalitions.md`). |

## 4. HOW A GROUP COMPLAINT IS PROCESSED

1. **Consolidation.** If several members complain about the same individual,
   the complaints are consolidated into one case with one record — the
   individual is not tried twelve separate times.
2. **Written complaint.** Each complainant's claim is taken down in writing
   with facts: dates, times, evidence. Vague statements ("everyone knows")
   are not a complaint.
3. **Notification and hearing.** The individual receives the consolidated
   complaint, then a hearing per `02-Resolution-Process/03-Step-3-Conflict-Review-Board.md`.
4. **Group evidence counts as evidence, not as votes.** Ten members saying
   "she wastes water" is worth the same as one member saying it — nothing, until
   there is a log, a meter reading, or a photograph.
5. **Decision and reasons.** The CRB decides on the evidence and writes reasons.

## 5. THE REVERSE CASE — ONE AGAINST MANY

When one member accuses a group (for example, "five houses are stealing water
from the line"), the same protections apply in reverse:

- The accuser's evidence is examined seriously, never dismissed as "one person's
  word".
- Each accused member is heard individually, not as a block.
- If the group is proven to have coordinated, liability is individual
  (`03-Protection-Mechanisms/02-Protection-from-Coalitions.md`).

*Example:* Member A claims four neighbours routinely bypass their meters at
night. The four neighbours dismiss it as "one crank's story". The CRB checks
the night logs and the meter seals; photos and a meter-installation record
confirm the claim. The four are each liable, and Member A's report is recorded
as accurate — no one is punished for speaking the truth.

## 6. IF THE GROUP IS TARGETING

When the group's real purpose is to punish the individual — higher charges for
one house, water cut "as punishment", false rumours — the case is reclassified
as **targeting** and handled under
`03-Protection-Mechanisms/01-Protection-from-Targeting.md` with Level 3+
consequences for the offenders.

*Example:* Ten members demand that House 7 pay double the common charge "because
he opposed the new tariff". There is no rule permitting individual tariffs. The
demand is targeting in the form of a coalition, each participant is separately
liable, and House 7's charge remains the common charge.

## 7. THE BURDEN NEVER SHIFTS

An individual is never required to "prove their innocence" against a group.
The complainant bears the burden of proof under
`04-Right-vs-Majority-Procedures/01-Fact-Establishment.md`. Silence, isolation,
or unpopularity is never evidence of wrongdoing.
