# 01 - INTERPERSONAL CONFLICTS

**Folder:** `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/01-Conflict-Types/`  
**Cross-reference:** `00-CONSTITUTION/01-Preamble/03-Definitions.md`, `00-CONSTITUTION/02-Fundamental-Rights/05-Right-to-Voice-and-Objection.md`

---

## 1. WHAT THEY ARE

Interpersonal conflicts are personal disputes between members that touch the
water system only incidentally: noise during an evening slot, a pipe laid across
a neighbour's wall, harsh words spoken at a meeting, two families who stopped
speaking years ago and now must share a meter.

These conflicts are real and must be taken seriously — but they must **not** be
allowed to leak into water decisions.

## 2. THE DANGER OF MIXING PERSONAL AND SYSTEM ISSUES

When a personal grudge is wrapped inside a water dispute, the system becomes a
weapon. The old system worked exactly this way: the person who disliked you
also controlled your water, so a personal argument became a water crisis.

The rule is therefore absolute: **a personal dispute between members is never
settled by changing a member's water, duty, charges, or privileges.** Those are
system decisions, decided by system rules. A grudge is never evidence.

## 3. SEPARATING THE TWO QUESTIONS

For any dispute, ask two separate questions and answer them separately:

| Question | Decided by | Never decided by |
|----------|------------|------------------|
| "Is the water system operating correctly for this member?" | Logs, meters, rules | Personal history, who is friends with whom |
| "Is there a personal grievance between these members?" | Mediation between the members | Water pressure, slot cuts, charge increases |

*Example:* Mr. Ramesh and Mr. Suresh fight because Mr. Suresh's children kicked
a ball through Mr. Ramesh's window. At the next meeting, someone proposes
"punishing" Mr. Suresh by moving his water slot to 2 a.m. That proposal is
**void**: it is a personal dispute being settled through system power, which is
exactly the abuse this constitution forbids. The window is a personal matter
for mediation; the slot is governed by the roster.

## 4. THE PROCESS FOR PERSONAL DISPUTES

1. **Mediation first.** A neutral facilitator (trained in the colony, or an
   elder, or a CRB mediator) brings both members together under
   `02-Resolution-Process/01-Step-1-Open-Dialogue.md`.
2. **Agreement in writing.** If they reach a settlement (payment, apology,
   repair, changed behaviour), it is written and signed, and it is binding
   between them.
3. **If mediation fails,** the dispute is a private matter between the members.
   It may go to Step 2–5 **only** if it crosses into water-system conduct
   (harassment of duty members, threats, tampering, abuse at meetings).
4. **System claims are handled separately.** Any water-system component (a
   meter read, a repair bill, a duty slot) is handled as an operational or
   financial conflict, not inside the personal feud.

## 5. WHEN A PERSONAL DISPUTE BECOMES THE SYSTEM'S BUSINESS

| Conduct | Becomes a system matter | Handled as |
|---------|-------------------------|------------|
| Verbal abuse at a meeting | Yes | Disruption under assembly conduct rules |
| Threatening a duty operator | Yes | Targeting / intimidation — Level 3+ |
| Damaging a neighbour's connection | Yes | Tampering — Enforcement Ladder |
| Spreading false stories about a member | Yes | Defamation — see Whistleblower-Protection for the distinction from honest reporting |
| Refusing to share the common terrace line | Yes | Operational conflict |
| Not talking to a neighbour | No | Private |

## 6. PROTECTIONS FOR BOTH SIDES

- The member who complains is never told "keep quiet, it's personal".
- The member complained about is never assumed wrong merely because they are
  outnumbered.
- Neither side's water or money can be touched while a personal dispute is
  pending. Status quo applies (`00-Conflict-Resolution-Model.md`, section 8).
- If either member feels the dispute is being used as cover for targeting, they
  may invoke `03-Protection-Mechanisms/01-Protection-from-Targeting.md`
  immediately.

## 7. DIGNITY (P10) IS NOT OPTIONAL

Mediation never includes public shaming, shouting matches, or forcing one side
to apologise on command. An apology is voluntary and genuine, or it is nothing.
A member who cannot mediate without contempt is replaced as facilitator.
