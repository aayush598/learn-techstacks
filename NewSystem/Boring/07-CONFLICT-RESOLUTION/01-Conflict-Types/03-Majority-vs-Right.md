# 03 - MAJORITY VS RIGHT

## 1. WHAT THIS CASE IS

The signature case of this system: a **majority of members supports an action
that is factually wrong, rule-violating, or right-violating** — and one member
is right.

This is not a rare, exotic case. It is the exact pattern that destroyed the old
system:

- A crowd in anger votes to cut one member's water.
- A group agrees to raise one house's charges because they dislike the owner.
- Everyone "knows" the motor was run out of turn, because the operator said so —
  until a log proves otherwise.
- A majority decides a member's complaint is "troublemaking" and punishes the
  complaint instead of examining it.

## 2. THE CONSTITUTIONAL ANSWER

**The right member wins. Always.** One member with a provable fact or a cited
rule defeats any majority. P5, Truth over Majority, is a basic-structure
provision and can never be amended away.

The practical machinery has two parts, both in this folder:

1. **Truth Veto** — freezes the wrong decision the moment it is raised
   (`04-Right-vs-Majority-Procedures/02-Truth-Veto.md`).
2. **Fact review** — the frozen decision is examined by evidence
   (`04-Right-vs-Majority-Procedures/01-Fact-Establishment.md`).

## 3. THE PROCESS IN THREE MOVES

| Move | What happens | Time |
|------|--------------|------|
| **Freeze** | The lone member files a Truth Veto (or the CRB intervenes). The decision is suspended immediately. No implementation, no "just for now". | Same moment |
| **Review** | Evidence is gathered: logs, receipts, photos, witnesses. The panel answers: Is the fact true? Is the rule cited correctly? Is a right engaged? | Within 7 days |
| **Decide** | If the veto is upheld, the decision is void and the member's position stands. If rejected, the decision may proceed — but only after review, with the objector's dissent recorded without penalty. | Within 7 days |

## 4. THE TWO CLASSIC EXAMPLES

**Example A — majority votes to cut X's water.**
Nine of ten members vote to cut Member X's water for three days because she
"filed too many complaints". Member X files a Truth Veto citing
`00-CONSTITUTION/02-Fundamental-Rights/01-Right-to-Fair-Water.md` and the rule
that water denial is only possible through the full enforcement ladder
(`05-Enforcement/04-Disconnection-Protocol.md`). The vote is frozen, the CRB
confirms the rule, and the resolution is **void ab initio**. X's water is never
touched.

**Example B — majority votes to raise only Y's charges.**
A group of seven members votes to increase House Y's monthly contribution
because "he is difficult to deal with". Member Y vetoes on the ground that no
rule permits individual tariffs (P1: water is common property; equal charges).
The CRB upholds the veto. The resolution is void, and the attempt is treated as
targeting — the instigators face the Enforcement Ladder, not Y.

## 5. WHY THE MAJORITY LOSES WITHOUT BEING CRUSHED

Defeating a wrong majority is not about humiliating the majority. It is about
protecting the system:

- The majority is corrected by evidence and rules, not punished for voting.
- Members who voted in good faith on wrong information pay nothing.
- Members who *deliberately* engineered a wrong vote (spread false facts,
  pressured others, coordinated the targeting) are liable individually —
  `04-Right-vs-Majority-Procedures/04-Cost-of-Defying-Truth.md`.

## 6. WHAT IS NOT "RIGHT AGAINST MAJORITY"

The Truth Veto does not protect preference. One member cannot veto a genuine
choice the majority is entitled to make:

- Can the schedule be moved 30 minutes later? → A genuine choice; a vote decides.
- Is the common tariff going up this year? → A genuine choice subject to the
  finance rules; a vote decides.
- Is the motor log lying? → A fact; the log and witnesses decide, never a vote.

## 7. THE REMINDER

This section exists because the colony's worst memories are of crowds prevailing
over truth. Every time a majority feels certain, it must ask itself: *would we
still act if we were the one, and the crowd stood against us?*
