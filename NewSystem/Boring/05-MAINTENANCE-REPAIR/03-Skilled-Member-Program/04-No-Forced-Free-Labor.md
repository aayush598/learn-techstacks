# 04 - NO FORCED FREE LABOR

**Folder:** `05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/03-Skilled-Member-Program/`

---

## 1. THE RULE

> **No member may be asked, pressured, shamed, or expected to work free.
> Skilled work for the colony is compensated at the published rate or in
> time-credits. Free work may be given voluntarily, but it is a recorded gift,
> never an expectation.**

This rule is the direct opposite of the old system, where skilled members were
told "it is your duty as a colony member" and worked for years unpaid — and
then were called ungrateful when they stopped.

## 2. WHAT "WORK" MEANS HERE

This rule covers **skilled and discretionary labor** performed for the system:

| Covered | Not covered |
|---------|-------------|
| Motor/electric repairs by a skilled member | Routine duty-roster operation (already compensated by credits) |
| Plumbing, welding, tank cleaning by a skilled member | Ordinary chores attached to one's own household |
| Time spent diagnosing or verifying repairs | Membership meetings and assembly attendance |
| Time spent sourcing parts for a repair | — |
| Compensation for a skill the colony asked for | — |

The line is: if the colony asks for a member's **skill**, it pays. If the work
is a shared routine duty on the roster, it is compensated under the duty system
(`03-ROTATION-DUTY/`).

## 3. THE VOLUNTARY GIFT

A member **may** choose to work free. The choice is theirs alone, and it must
be handled so it cannot be turned into a precedent:

1. The member declares the free work **before or while doing it**: "I will do
   this repair without charge."
2. The declaration is recorded in the fault record as a **voluntary gift**,
   with the member's signature and the hours.
3. The recorded gift counts toward the member's service record if they want it
   to — but it creates **no duty** for them, and **no expectation** for others.
4. No other member may say "X did it free, so you should too." Each gift is
   individual and final.
5. If a member does not declare free work, the work is presumed **paid**. The
   colony owes the published rate until the member records a waiver. Payment is
   never withheld on the guess that "they looked happy to help".

## 4. FORBIDDEN PHRASES

These sentences are forbidden in colony discussions and are an offence if used
as pressure:

- "It is your duty to the colony."
- "Everyone contributes, you should too."
- "You have the skill, it takes you only 10 minutes."
- "We will remember your help" (when it means unpaid help).
- "X did it free last time."

Any of these said to pressure a member is a Level 2 offence under the
Enforcement Ladder (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/05-Enforcement/01-Enforcement-Ladder.md`),
and the pressured member's work is still paid.

## 5. WHO ENFORCES THIS

1. Every member — skilled or not — is entitled to be paid for agreed work, and
   may raise a grievance if payment is withheld or refused
   (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/`).
2. The Repair Committee records all engagements and gifts, so "we didn't pay
   because they never asked" cannot be said of a recorded job.
3. The assembly reviews the compensation ledger quarterly to confirm every
   skilled hour was either paid or recorded as a gift. Unpaid hours that are
   neither are a red flag and are corrected.

## 6. SCENARIO — THE FREE-LABOR TRAP

A member who "knows electric work" is asked every month to "just check the
starter". Each month they do it free, and each month the colony says "thank
you". After a year, the check is demanded as routine, and the member is called
"not cooperative" when they ask to be paid. Under this rule, the first check
would have been a recorded paid engagement at the published rate, or a recorded
gift — and the colony would have budgeted for the true cost instead of building
a hidden dependency on unpaid labor.

## 7. P8 AND P10 SERVED

| Principle | How this rule serves it |
|-----------|-------------------------|
| P8 Work rewarded | All skilled work is paid or credited; gifts are recorded, not expected |
| P10 Dignity | No member is shamed into work; skill is treated as valuable, not as an obligation |
| P3 No single point of failure | Paid work spreads across members and vendors; no unpaid "hero" is made indispensable |
