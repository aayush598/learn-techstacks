# 01 - SKILL REGISTRY

**Folder:** `05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/03-Skilled-Member-Program/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

The colony will always need people who can read a panel, fix a pipe, weld a
bracket, or check an account. In the old system, skill was a trap: the same
few skilled people were silently forced to work free, and their skill made
them the target of endless unpaid calls. This program makes skill a
**declared, valued, compensated, and replaceable** resource.

## 2. WHAT THE REGISTRY IS

The Skill Registry is a voluntary list of members and their skills, kept in
the format `12-TEMPLATES/12-Skill-Registry.md`. It records:

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Member name and connection ID | Identifies the member |
| Skills | Electrical, plumbing, welding, motor repair, accounts, legal, IT, driving, construction, health care, others |
| Experience / certification | What they have done, certificates if any |
| Availability | Days/hours typically available (e.g., evenings, weekends) |
| Preferred compensation | Time-credits or cash (see `03-Credit-or-Payment-Choice.md`) |
| Contact | Updated phone number |
| Last verified | Date the entry was last confirmed |

## 3. VOLUNTARY ONLY

1. Registration is **voluntary**. No member can be listed without their
   consent, and no member is forced to register.
2. Registration is **not a commitment to do work**. It says "I have this skill
   and may be available" — it is not a standing order to be summoned.
3. Every engagement is a separate, agreed, compensated assignment
   (`02-Time-Value-Estimation.md`). Registration creates no duty to work free.
4. A member may withdraw from the registry at any time with a simple written
   notice. Withdrawal has no penalty and raises no questions.
5. The registry is **never** used to shame, pressure, or single out anyone
   (P10, P8).

## 4. HOW THE REGISTRY IS USED

1. When a fault needs a skill, the Repair Committee consults the registry and
   invites the member — with the terms of the assignment stated in advance
   (skill, hours, rate, credits).
2. The member is free to accept or decline. Declining is not an offence and
   needs no excuse.
3. If no member is available, the work goes to an approved vendor
   (`04-Vendor-Management/01-Approved-Vendor-List.md`). The registry never
   forces a skill that is not there.

## 5. KEEPING IT CURRENT

1. The Repair Committee verifies the registry **once a year** — every member
   confirms their availability and contact in person or in writing.
2. Members report changes (moved away, changed phone, lost certification) to
   the committee at any time; the entry is updated within 7 days.
3. A yearly refresh day is fixed with the assembly; unconfirmed entries are
   marked "unconfirmed" rather than silently kept.

## 6. SKILL DISCOVERY AND TRAINING

1. At the annual skill refresh, members are also invited to **declare new
   skills** learned during the year.
2. The colony may sponsor training (e.g., a member's fee for a short
   electrical-safety or pump-maintenance course) as a PM-budget line, because
   skill spread is part of P9 (self-sufficiency).
3. Skills learned through colony-sponsored training belong to the member; the
   colony does not bind them by any forced service contract. The colony
   benefits through availability, not through bondage.

## 7. PROTECTION OF REGISTERED MEMBERS

1. Registered skilled members are paid the published rate or credited the
   published credits for every hour of work — including travel and waiting time
   if the assignment requires it (`04-No-Forced-Free-Labor.md`).
2. Pressure to "just do it for free since you're registered" is an offence
   under the enforcement ladder.
3. A skilled member's refusal to work is never treated as betrayal; the system
   has vendors and trained backups so it never depends on any one person
   (`05-No-Over-Reliance-on-Individual.md`).
