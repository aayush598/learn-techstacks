# 04 - NO PERSONAL VENDOR KICKBACKS

**Folder:** `05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/04-Vendor-Management/`

---

## 1. THE RULE

> **No member may accept any money, gift, favour, or commission from a vendor
> in connection with the colony's business. No member may steer colony work to
> a vendor from which they personally benefit. Any such arrangement is an
> offence under the Enforcement Ladder and may void the vendor's contract.**

A vendor paying a member to be "recommended" is exactly how inflated repair
costs survive in the old system: the colony pays more, the member pockets the
difference, and the higher bill looks like a normal market price.

## 2. WHAT IS A KICKBACK

| Form | Example |
|------|---------|
| Cash commission | Vendor gives 10% of a ₹15,000 bill to the member who recommended him |
| Gifts | Festival gifts, groceries, gold, discount on the member's own work |
| Favours | Free wiring at the member's house, a "loan", a job for a relative |
| Profit share | A member co-owns or part-owns the vendor's shop |
| Barter | Vendor's services in exchange for the member steering colony work |
| Undisclosed referral fees | Any fee for the referral itself |

## 3. DISCLOSURE RULE — "MY BROTHER'S SHOP"

1. Any member who has a **personal or family relationship with a vendor**
   (owner, employee, relative, partner, or has a financial interest) must
   disclose it in writing to the Repair Committee **before** the vendor is
   proposed or quoted.
2. The disclosure is recorded in the vendor file and the fault record.
3. Once disclosed, the relationship does not ban the vendor — it triggers the
   safeguards:
   - the interested member takes **no part** in collecting, opening, or
     comparing that vendor's quotes;
   - the interested member **cannot approve** payment to that vendor
     (`02-Breakdown-Repair/03-Repair-Approval-Matrix.md`);
   - the vendor's rating is reviewed by an independent committee member.
4. **Non-disclosure is the offence**, not the relationship. A member who hides
   "it's my brother's shop" and then recommends it has already broken this rule.

## 4. SEVERITY

1. Accepting a kickback, or failing to disclose an interest, is a **Level 3-4
   offence** on the Enforcement Ladder
   (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/05-Enforcement/01-Enforcement-Ladder.md`):
   - recorded finding and public naming;
   - recovery of the kickback value to the colony;
   - removal from any committee/role for a fixed period;
   - for the vendor: immediate removal from the Approved Vendor List and the
     contract voided.
2. If the amount is large or involves fraud, the colony reports it to the
   police/consumer authority as a criminal matter.

## 5. PROTECTION FOR WHISTLEBLOWERS

1. A member who reports a suspected kickback is protected under P7 — no
   retaliation, no targeting.
2. Reports may be made confidentially to the Conflict Review Board
   (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/`) if the member fears the Repair Committee itself.
3. A false accusation made maliciously is itself an offence, but an honest
   report is never punished even if the suspicion turns out wrong.

## 6. SCENARIO — THE COMMISSION MAN

A member recommends a pump shop for the winding job. The quote is accepted,
and later members notice the member's house was rewired "free" the same week.
Another member reports the observation. Investigation finds the pump shop gives
the member 10% of every colony job. The finding is recorded; the kickback value
is recovered; the member is removed from the Repair Committee and rated down on
the enforcement ladder; the vendor is removed from the list and blacklisted for
a year. The colony's future winding work goes to the other approved shop.

## 7. PREVENTION IS CHEAPER THAN DETECTION

1. Sealed quotes and public comparison (`02-Multiple-Quotes-Rule.md`) make
   kickbacks hard to hide, because the money trail is visible.
2. Rotating committee members each year reduces the "friendship bonus".
3. An annual reminder at the assembly states the rule plainly; every member
   knows what "my brother's shop" triggers.
