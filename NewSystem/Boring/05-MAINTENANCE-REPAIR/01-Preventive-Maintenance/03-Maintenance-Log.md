# 03 - MAINTENANCE LOG

**Folder:** `05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/01-Preventive-Maintenance/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

The maintenance log is the **continuous, dated history of every check performed
on the motor and distribution system**. It exists so that:

1. Preventive maintenance can be proven to have happened — and not just claimed.
2. Trends can be seen: yield falling, current creeping up, the same capacitor
   replaced twice in a year — each a warning that a breakdown is coming.
3. Future disputes are settled by evidence, not by who shouts loudest
   (P5, P6). "The motor failed because we never checked it" becomes a question
   answerable from the log.

## 2. WHAT MUST BE RECORDED

Every PM round gets one log entry with the following fields:

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Date and time | Exact date, start and end time |
| Quarter / season | e.g. "Summer pre-check" |
| Items checked | Checklist reference (`02-Checklist.md`) |
| Results summary | PASS count, FAIL count, OBSERVATION count |
| Failures / observations | Each one with its fault number |
| Actions taken | What was done during the round |
| Cost | PM fee, compensation, consumables used |
| Persons | Electrician, skilled member, witness — all named |
| Next due date | Set from the schedule |
| Logged by | Repair Committee member of the quarter |

## 3. LOGGING RULES

1. The log is written **within 48 hours** of the round, never from memory
   weeks later.
2. Each entry is signed by the person who performed the work and by a second
   member as witness. Two signatures minimum.
3. The log lives in one bound register (or one named spreadsheet that is
   backed up at least monthly). It is never kept privately in one member's
   phone.
4. Any member may read, copy, or photograph the log at any time without asking
   permission (P6). Refusing access to the log is a transparency offence.
5. The log is reviewed by the Repair Committee quarterly and by the auditor
   during the annual audit.

## 4. TREND WATCHING

The Repair Committee must watch for warning patterns:

| Pattern in the log | Likely meaning | Action |
|--------------------|----------------|--------|
| Winding reading drops quarter over quarter | Winding degrading | Plan rewinding / motor service in the PM budget |
| Yield down 20%+ from the baseline | Bore/impeller problem | Drawdown test, consult pump shop |
| Same part replaced repeatedly | Wrong part or root cause | Full diagnosis before next replacement |
| Repeated overload trips | Protection or load problem | Immediate electrical diagnosis |
| PM dates slipping later each quarter | Process decay | Escalate to assembly |

A trend is reported to the assembly at least once a year with the log as
evidence.

## 5. DISPUTES AND EVIDENCE

1. In any dispute about maintenance or repairs, the **log is the first
   evidence** consulted.
2. A claim about what was "done" that is not in the log is treated as
   unproven. Conversely, an entry that is signed and witnessed is treated as
   fact unless a member provides evidence to the contrary.
3. If a member believes an entry is false, they may file a written objection
   under the Truth Veto process
   (`00-CONSTITUTION/04-Truth-Supremacy/02-Single-Member-Truth-Veto.md`). The
   entry is then examined before a review, not simply trusted.

## 6. RETENTION

1. Logs are retained for a minimum of **5 years**.
2. Old logs are archived with the books and are part of the audit trail
   (`06-FINANCE/04-Audit/`).
3. When a new member asks how the system is maintained, the log is shown, not
   described.
