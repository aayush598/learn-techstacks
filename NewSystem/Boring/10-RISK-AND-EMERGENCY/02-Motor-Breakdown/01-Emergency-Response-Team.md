# 01 - EMERGENCY RESPONSE TEAM

**Folder:** `10-RISK-AND-EMERGENCY/02-Motor-Breakdown/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

A motor breakdown is the colony's most predictable crisis — and the old
system's favourite weapon. The Emergency Response Team (ERT) exists so that
when the motor stops, there is a **named, on-duty, trained group** that acts
immediately, records everything, and reports to the members. The motor down
is a process event, not a mystery.

## 2. THE TEAM

1. The ERT is **2-3 members drawn by lot from the duty roster**
   (`03-ROTATION-DUTY/`), on duty for **one month**, then rotated.
2. At least one ERT member each month should be a registered skilled member or
   a member who has completed the operation training, so the team is not
   helpless.
3. The current ERT is published on the notice board and in the group at the
   start of each month, with phone numbers.
4. No member serves consecutive months; no household holds the ERT twice in a
   row, so the team never becomes a clique (P2).

## 3. ROLES ON THE TEAM

| Role | Duty |
|------|------|
| **Contact / informer** | Notifies all members of the breakdown within 2 hours (`04-Communication-to-Members.md`); keeps the 12-hour update schedule |
| **Diagnoser / coordinator** | Opens the fault number (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/02-Breakdown-Repair/01-Fault-Reporting.md`), arranges diagnosis, coordinates with the Repair Committee |
| **Logger / recorder** | Logs every action, time, cost, and decision in the crisis log under the fault number |

In a 2-person month, one member covers contact+log and the other covers
diagnosis+coordination. The roles are written on the ERT page so nobody
invents a role mid-crisis.

## 4. WHAT THE TEAM DOES — THE SEQUENCE

| Time | Action |
|------|--------|
| 0 h | Breakdown observed or reported; fault number opened |
| 0-2 h | All members informed (group + notice board + calls to elderly); ERT roles confirmed |
| 0-24 h | Diagnosis arranged (electrician + skilled member) per `05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/02-Breakdown-Repair/02-Diagnosis-and-Quoting.md` |
| Same time | Rental-pump contingency considered (`02-Rental-Pump-Contingency.md`); municipal backup checked (`03-Municipal-Backup.md`) |
| 24-72 h | Quotes, approval, and repair pushed through the pipeline deadlines |
| After repair | Verification attended (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/02-Breakdown-Repair/05-After-Repair-Verification.md`); members informed; fault closed |
| 30 days | Post-crisis review at the assembly |

## 5. POWERS AND LIMITS OF THE TEAM

1. The team **runs the process**; it does not replace the Repair Committee or
   the assembly. Approvals and spending follow the Repair Approval Matrix
   (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/02-Breakdown-Repair/03-Repair-Approval-Matrix.md`).
2. In a genuine emergency the team may act for safety and informing only, with
   strict spending limits ratified later
   (`05-Communication-Emergency/03-Decision-During-Communications-Breakdown.md`).
3. The team may never: hide a breakdown, delay a repair for a favour, or
   decide "who needs water most" on its own — that is the priority order's
   job (`01-Drought-and-Scarcity/02-Priority-Allocation.md`).

## 6. SCENARIO — THE 2 A.M. BURNOUT

On a Thursday midnight the motor hums and dies. The duty operator reports it.
The ERT contact informs all members by 1:30 a.m.; the diagnoser opens fault
F-2026-021 at 7 a.m. and books the electrician and a skilled member for 9 a.m.
By noon the diagnosis is written ("winding burned, overload relay failed") and
by Friday evening a rental pump is running. The colony loses less than a day
of supply — not a week — because the response did not depend on waking up the
right person and begging.

## 7. HANDOVER

1. On the last day of the month the outgoing team holds a 30-minute handover
   with the incoming team: open faults, ongoing vendors, crisis log, lessons.
2. The handover is recorded in the crisis log.
3. The incoming team's names and numbers are published the same day.
