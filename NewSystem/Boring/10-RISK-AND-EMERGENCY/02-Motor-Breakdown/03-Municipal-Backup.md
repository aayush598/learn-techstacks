# 03 - MUNICIPAL BACKUP

**Folder:** `10-RISK-AND-EMERGENCY/02-Motor-Breakdown/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

During a prolonged failure — motor down for days, or bore yield collapsing —
the colony's best external backup is the municipal/piped water supply or a
government tanker. The municipal connection must be established, maintained,
and fairly administered **before** it is needed, so a crisis never becomes a
queue of panicking individuals competing for the public tap.

## 2. THE MUNICIPAL CONNECTION

1. The colony maintains its municipal/panchayat/water-board connection (where
   available) even if the bore is primary. The connection is a shared asset
   (P1), and its maintenance is a small standing cost.
2. One committee member per year is the **municipal liaison**, responsible
   for the connection record, bill, and renewal — rotating yearly so no one
   "owns" the municipality (P3).
3. Where grants or subsidies apply to such connections, the colony uses them
   (`08-GOVERNMENT-INTERACTION/02-Grants-and-Subsidies/`).

## 3. WHEN MUNICIPAL BACKUP ACTIVATES

| Trigger | Action |
|---------|--------|
| Bore/motor down for > 48 hours with no rental option | Municipality/tanker line activated |
| Rationing declared and yield below the "municipal supplement" threshold | Supplemental municipal supply begins |
| Contamination of the bore confirmed | Bore stopped; municipal/tanker becomes the sole supply until cleared (`04-Health-and-Safety/03-Contamination-Response.md`) |

Activation is declared by the Repair Committee with the ERT, published, and
ratified at the next assembly.

## 4. WHO COORDINATES

1. The **municipal liaison** arranges the connection/valve, timing, and any
   official permissions, working with the ERT.
2. Two members are always involved in operating the municipal valve and
   recording volumes — never one.
3. If the municipality requires a formal application or letter, the letter is
   a colony letter with two signatures and a copy in the file.

## 5. PER-HOUSEHOLD ALLOCATION

1. Municipal supply is distributed through the **public distribution points**
   under the same limits as the rationing plan
   (`01-Drought-and-Scarcity/01-Rationing-Plan.md`).
2. The priority order (`01-Drought-and-Scarcity/02-Priority-Allocation.md`)
   governs the order of access.
3. Volumes received and distributed are logged daily during the activation,
   published with the 12-hour updates.

## 6. NO HOARDING

1. During municipal/tanker activation, household storage is limited to the
   same cap as rationing. A household already full does not fill again while
   others wait.
2. Hoarding or private resale of municipal water is a Level 3-4 offence
   (P1) under the Enforcement Ladder
   (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/05-Enforcement/01-Enforcement-Ladder.md`).
3. A member found diverting the municipal line for a private connection is
   treated as theft of common property.

## 7. SCENARIO — THE PROLONGED FAILURE

The winding repair is delayed because the part is not in town (a documented,
published extension). On day 3, municipal backup activates: the liaison has the
valve ready, two members manage the distribution twice a day, volumes are
logged, and the priority order is followed. Households get their published
share throughout the week. The delay is an inconvenience, not a hostage
situation — and when the part arrives, the rental pump and municipal line are
wound down together, witnessed.
