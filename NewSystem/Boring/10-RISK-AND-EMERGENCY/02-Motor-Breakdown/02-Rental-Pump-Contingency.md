# 02 - RENTAL PUMP CONTINGENCY

**Folder:** `10-RISK-AND-EMERGENCY/02-Motor-Breakdown/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

When the borewell motor is being repaired — winding can take days — the
colony need not be dry. A rental pump keeps water flowing from the boring (or
from an alternate source) while the permanent motor is out of service. The
rental option is **pre-planned**, so a breakdown never automatically means
tankers and rationing.

## 2. PRE-APPROVED RENTAL SUPPLIERS

1. The colony keeps **1-2 approved rental-pump suppliers** on the Approved
   Vendor List (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/04-Vendor-Management/01-Approved-Vendor-List.md`).
2. The record for each supplier includes: available pump sizes, matching
   phases/HP, daily rental rate, security deposit, and delivery time.
3. Rates are reviewed yearly with the vendor list. The recorded normal rate is
   the ceiling the colony accepts in a crisis.
4. Supplier contact is on the emergency contacts list
   (`05-Communication-Emergency/02-Emergency-Contacts.md`).

## 3. WHEN TO RENT

| Situation | Decide to rent? |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Motor down, repair expected > 24 hours, bore usable | Yes — rent while repairing |
| Motor down, repair expected < 24 hours, stock/supply can cover | Optional — committee judgment, recorded |
| Motor down and bore itself failed (yield zero) | No — rental pump cannot help; use tanker/municipal plans |
| Power unavailable | Consider generator/inverter option (`03-Power-Failure/`) |

The decision is made by the Repair Committee (2 members) at the diagnosis
stage, recorded under the fault number, and published with the breakdown
notice. In a true emergency the ERT may order within its spending limit
(`01-Emergency-Response-Team.md`).

## 4. COST AND PAYMENT

1. Rental cost is paid from the **Repair Fund** (`06-FINANCE/02-Fund-Management/03-Repair-Fund.md`),
   and is part of the breakdown's total cost, recorded under the fault number.
2. Payment is by voucher, never cash (`06-FINANCE/03-Bookkeeping/02-Expense-Voucher-Rule.md`).
3. A security deposit is paid from the Repair Fund and tracked until refunded;
   the refund is recorded.
4. The daily rental is a published number; if the supplier asks for a higher
   crisis rate, the recorded normal rate is cited and the supplier is rated
   down (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/04-Vendor-Management/03-Vendor-Performance-Rating.md`).

## 5. INSTALLATION AND OPERATION

1. Installation is supervised by **two members** — never one — at least one of
   whom has completed the operation training. Two pairs of hands and eyes
   (P3).
2. The rental pump is connected with proper protection (overload, dry-run
   guard) and earthed, per `04-Health-and-Safety/02-Electrical-Safety.md`.
3. The duty roster operates it; the run is logged in the motor run log just
   like the permanent motor.
4. The rental pump is returned when the permanent motor passes verification
   (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/02-Breakdown-Repair/05-After-Repair-Verification.md`).
   Return is witnessed by two members and the supplier, with the pump checked
   for damage so the deposit is refunded cleanly.

## 6. DISTRIBUTION WHILE RENTED

1. Water from the rental pump follows the normal schedule and limits. If the
   rental pump yields less than the permanent motor, the rationing plan
   (`01-Drought-and-Scarcity/01-Rationing-Plan.md`) and priority order apply.
2. No member's slot is silently cut; any reduction is published.

## 7. SCENARIO — THE THREE-DAY WINDING

The winding burns in April. The pump shop says 3 days. The committee calls the
pre-approved rental supplier, confirms a matching pump at the recorded daily
rate, and two members supervise the installation that evening. The colony runs
on the rental pump for three days at normal supply. Meanwhile the winding
repair follows the two-quote rule. Total disruption: one evening. The colony
never hears the sentence "you will have to wait for the motor".
