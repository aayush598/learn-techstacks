# 04 - COMMUNICATION TO MEMBERS

**Folder:** `10-RISK-AND-EMERGENCY/02-Motor-Breakdown/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

In the old system, the first casualty of a breakdown was information: members
heard "motor not working" and nothing else — no reason, no date, no progress,
no honesty. Communication rules turn every breakdown into an **open, dated,
updated public record** (P6). Silence is not a management style.

## 2. THE IMMEDIATE NOTICE

When a breakdown occurs or a fault is reported:

1. **Within 2 hours**, all members are informed through:
   - the colony group (WhatsApp/other);
   - the notice board at the distribution point;
   - phone calls to households that do not use the group — elderly,
     disabled, and non-digital members (P7);
   - the loudspeaker/announcement channel if it is a supply window
     (`05-Communication-Emergency/01-Offline-Notice-Protocol.md`).
2. The notice states:
   - the fault number;
   - what is known (and what is not yet known);
   - whether supply is interrupted, reduced, or on backup;
   - the next update time.
3. If supply is unaffected, the notice is still given — members must know the
   system is being worked on.

## 3. THE 12-HOUR UPDATE RULE

1. From the first notice until supply is restored, updates are published
   **at least every 12 hours**, even if the update is "no change; waiting for
   the vendor".
2. Each update carries the same fault number and the date-time.
3. An update that has nothing new is still an update. "No update" is
   forbidden; no-news is itself a failure.

## 4. CONTENT OF UPDATES

| Field | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Fault number | F-2026-021 |
| Status | Diagnosis / quoting / approved / repair in progress / verification |
| What was done since the last update | Diagnosis written; two quotes received |
| Next action and by when | Vendor to start at 9 a.m.; deadline 72h from approval |
| Current supply arrangement | Rental pump running; municipal valve open |
| Expected restoration | Within 48 hours (per the approved timeline) |

## 5. NOTHING HIDDEN

1. Faults are never hidden "until we know more". Members are told what is
   known and what is unknown — in plain words, not in soothing phrases.
2. If the diagnosis was wrong and the repair will take longer, the correction
   is published the same day with the reason.
3. Delays, vendor failures, and cost increases are published as they happen.
   The post-crisis assembly reviews the full record
   (`00-Risk-Framework.md`), so hiding now is pointless anyway.
4. A member who deliberately withholds a breakdown notice, or orders others
   not to inform, commits a transparency offence (P6) under the Enforcement
   Ladder (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/05-Enforcement/01-Enforcement-Ladder.md`).

## 6. WHO SPEAKS

1. The ERT contact and the Repair Committee are the named spokespersons for a
   breakdown. Their names are in the first notice.
2. Any member may share the notices, but no one invents information; rumors
   are corrected by the spokespersons' updates.
3. Members are asked to address queries to the spokespersons, not to the
   electrician directly — one channel, one truth.

## 7. SCENARIO — THE QUIET WEEK

In the old system a motor was "being fixed" for a week while members got
nothing but silence and evasion. Under this rule, the same week looks like: a
2-hour notice, twelve 12-hour updates, each naming the stage and the next
deadline, with the fault number constant throughout. When the repair finishes,
the verification is published and the fault is closed. The members were never
in the dark, so nobody could use the darkness.
