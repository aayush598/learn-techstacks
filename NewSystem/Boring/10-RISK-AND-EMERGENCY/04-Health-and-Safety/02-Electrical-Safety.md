# 02 - ELECTRICAL SAFETY

**Folder:** `10-RISK-AND-EMERGENCY/04-Health-and-Safety/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

A borewell motor, a panel, and water make a lethal combination when handled
carelessly. Every electrical rule here is a **standing safety law** of the
colony — not advice, not a suggestion. Non-compliance is an offence, because a
single short can cost a life (P7).

## 2. THE HARD RULES

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Earthing** | Motor body, panel, and generator/inverter must be earthed; earth continuity is checked at every PM round (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/01-Preventive-Maintenance/02-Checklist.md`) |
| **MCB / ELCB (RCCB)** | The panel is protected by MCB and a working earth-leakage device; the trip test is part of the PM checklist |
| **No wet hands** | No one touches switches, plugs, or the panel with wet hands or wet feet, or while standing in water |
| **Panel locked** | The control panel is locked; only duty-trained members and approved electricians open it |
| **Cable integrity** | Cables are dry, un-frayed, and free of joints in water; damaged cable is replaced, not taped "temporarily" |
| **Motor room dry** | No standing water in the motor room; ventilation clear; the room is locked |
| **Isolate before work** | Before any electrical work, the main is switched off and locked; a "work in progress" tag is hung |
| **No amateur wiring** | Only approved electricians and trained skilled members do wiring; "I know a bit" is not qualification |

## 3. THE OPERATOR HANDBOOK

1. The Operation Handbook (`13-HANDBOOKS/`) contains the do/don't list for
   switching, emergencies, and first response. A copy is laminated and mounted
   **inside the motor room** and a copy in the notice area.
2. Every duty operator reads and signs the handbook's understanding page once
   a year, after the annual safety briefing.
3. The handbook is updated by the Repair Committee with electrician's advice;
   changes are published.

## 4. SAFETY DRILLS

1. At least **one electrical-safety drill per year**: practice the shutdown
   sequence, the ELCB trip test, and the "person in contact with live wire"
   response (switch off first — never touch the person directly).
2. The drill is recorded with the maintenance log; weaknesses found are
   corrected.
3. New members attend the safety briefing within 3 months of joining
   (`03-ROTATION-DUTY/02-Duty-Training/`).

## 5. WHO MAY TOUCH THE PANEL

| Person | May do |
|--------|--------|
| Trained duty operator | Switch on/off from the external switch; read indicators; report anomalies |
| Approved electrician | Open the panel, test, repair |
| Skilled member (registered electrical) | Test and assist under the PM rules |
| Everyone else | Touch nothing electrical in the motor room |

The panel key follows the dual-key rule (`03-ROTATION-DUTY/05-Key-and-Control/`).

## 6. SCENARIO — THE PANEL WITH A SHOCK

A member reports "the panel gives a light shock when touched". In the old
system this could be waved off ("it is always like that"). Under this rule it
is an immediate fault: the line is isolated, the electrician checks earth
continuity and the ELCB, the fault is logged (F-number), and the panel is
cleared only after the test passes. Nobody "tests it with their hand" — and a
member who suggests touching the wire to check is corrected on the spot, as a
safety offence.

## 7. NON-COMPLIANCE = OFFENCE

1. Any violation of Section 2 — bypassing the ELCB, blocking the earth,
   operating the panel untrained, working on live wires, opening the panel
   without the key — is an offence under the Enforcement Ladder
   (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/05-Enforcement/01-Enforcement-Ladder.md`).
2. A member who disables a safety device to "keep the motor running" commits a
   Level 3-4 offence, even if nothing bad happened. The act, not the outcome,
   is the violation.
3. A safety violation that causes injury is treated with the highest severity
   and reported to the authorities as required by law.
