# 01 - WATER QUALITY TESTING

**Folder:** `10-RISK-AND-EMERGENCY/04-Health-and-Safety/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

Clean water is the whole point of the system. In the old system, water quality
was a rumor: "the water is fine" said the person who wanted no questions. This
rule makes quality a **measured, scheduled, published fact** (P5, P6). The
colony tests, records, and publishes — before anyone drinks, and after any
event that could change the water.

## 2. THE TESTING SCHEDULE

| When | Test | Minimum |
|------|------|---------|
| **Before the rainy season** (May-June) | Physical + chemical + basic bacterial | 1 test round |
| **After any contamination event** (muddy water, flood, sewer incursion) | Targeted re-test of the affected parameter | Immediate, per `03-Contamination-Response.md` |
| **Yearly lab test** | Full panel: pH, TDS, hardness, iron, fluoride, nitrate, coliform, as per local norms | 1 test |
| **After major repairs** (motor pulled, bore work, pipe replacement) | Turbidity + bacterial check | 1 test round |
| **Post-monsoon** (Sep-Oct) | Bacterial + turbidity | 1 test round |

The schedule is fixed by this rule; skipping a scheduled test is a reportable
process failure, not a saving.

## 3. WHO TAKES THE SAMPLES

1. Samples are taken by **two members plus a witness** — three people, none
   of whom is the person who last handled the distribution line (P3).
2. Sample points: one at the motor room / rising main and one at a far end
   tap, so the whole network is represented.
3. Sample bottles are clean, labelled with date/time/point, and carried cold
   to the lab. The chain is recorded: who took it, who carried it, who
   submitted it.

## 4. WHERE TO TEST

1. Tests go to the **approved lab** on the vendor list
   (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/04-Vendor-Management/01-Approved-Vendor-List.md`) —
   ideally a government/NABL-accredited lab or a recognized private lab.
2. Cost is a standing line in the PM budget
   (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/01-Preventive-Maintenance/04-PM-Budget.md`).
3. If the approved lab is unavailable, a second lab is used and the change is
   recorded — the result, not the logo, is what matters.

## 5. RESULTS ARE PUBLISHED

1. Lab reports are **published in full** — notice board and group — within 7
   days of receipt, with a plain-language summary beside the technical terms
   (e.g., "coliform present — do not drink untreated").
2. A year's reports are kept on file with the maintenance log and are part of
   the audit trail.
3. Results are the facts for any decision; they cannot be "adjusted" by any
   member (P5).

## 6. READING THE RESULTS

| Parameter | Watchpoint |
|-----------|-----------|
| Turbidity | Above the norm → filter/settle, investigate source |
| pH / TDS | Out of range → suitability for drinking assessed |
| Iron / hardness | High → staining, pipe deposits; plan treatment |
| Nitrate / fluoride | Above norms → health risk, alert members |
| Coliform / E. coli | **Present → stop drinking, contamination response** |

Any result outside the applicable norms triggers the response in
`03-Contamination-Response.md` — not a discussion, a response.

## 7. SCENARIO — MUDDY AFTER RAIN

After a heavy July rain, members see muddy water. The ERT stops the bore for
drinking, samples are taken the same day by three members, the lab report
comes back with high turbidity and a positive coliform count. The notice goes
up the same day: do not drink; municipal/tanker water in use; chlorination
scheduled. Retests after chlorination and flushing come back clean, the
results are published, and supply resumes. The muddy water was an event with
evidence — not a rumor that "the water will be fine by evening".
