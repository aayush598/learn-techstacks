# 03 - CONTAMINATION RESPONSE

**Folder:** `10-RISK-AND-EMERGENCY/04-Health-and-Safety/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

Contamination is the one crisis where minutes matter and hiding kills. In the
old system, a member who complained about bad water was dismissed, and the
colony drank on. This rule makes the response **mechanical and fast**: a
change in water is never a rumor — it is a trigger. When water looks, smells,
or tastes wrong, or people fall ill, the colony acts on a script, not on an
argument.

## 2. THE TRIGGERS

| Trigger | Example |
|---------|---------|
| **Visual** | Muddy, cloudy, discolored water at the tap |
| **Smell / taste** | Odour (sulphur, chlorine, sewage), odd taste |
| **Sickness** | Two or more households report stomach illness within days of each other |
| **Event** | Flooding near the bore, sewage/channel water visible near the casing, chemical spill nearby |
| **Lab result** | Any parameter above the norm in a scheduled test |

Any member may trigger the response by reporting under
`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/02-Breakdown-Repair/01-Fault-Reporting.md`. The ERT
validates and declares contamination — the reporter is never blamed.

## 3. THE RESPONSE SEQUENCE

| Step | Action | Timeline |
|------|--------|----------|
| 1. **Stop using** | The ERT stops the bore from drinking use; if evidence is strong, stops pumping entirely | Within 1 hour |
| 2. **Test** | Samples taken by 3 members, sent to the approved lab (`01-Water-Quality-Testing.md`) | Same day |
| 3. **Flush** | Distribution lines flushed to clear contaminated standing water (with the lab's or a professional's guidance, so flushing does not worsen) | After assessment |
| 4. **Notify all** | Notice: do not drink; what is known; alternative supply | Within 2 hours |
| 5. **Medical alert** | Households with symptoms are directed to care; the nearby clinic is informed of a possible waterborne outbreak | Same day |
| 6. **Government complaint** | Formal complaint to the water board / municipal authority / district health office, with the lab report attached | Within 48 hours |
| 7. **Remediate** | Chlorination or other treatment **by an approved person** (licensed/authorized), never by an amateur | Per guidance |
| 8. **Retest and resume** | Re-test clean before drinking use resumes; results published | After treatment |

## 4. RULES WITHIN THE RESPONSE

1. **No drinking the bore water** until the retest is published clean. "It
   smells a little, but we have been drinking it for years" is forbidden as a
   reason.
2. Alternative supply runs per `03-Municipal-Backup.md` and the tanker
   contingency (`01-Drought-and-Scarcity/03-Bulk-Purchase-Contingency.md`).
3. **Chlorination is done only by an approved person** — a certified operator
   or an authorized official. The dose is written down and the log kept.
4. Nothing is said to members that the lab report does not support (P5). No
   "don't worry" and no "it's a disaster" — only facts.
5. A member who continues to drink knowingly during the ban, or who tells
   others the water is "actually fine", is not only penalized — the colony
   actively counter-notifies.

## 5. RECORDS FOR THE HEALTH DEPARTMENT

The colony keeps, for this event:

| Record | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| Fault number and declaration | The event's identity |
| Lab reports (before, during, after) | Evidence of contamination and clearance |
| Response log (times, actions, persons) | Proof of due diligence |
| Member symptom reports | For the health authority's outbreak data |
| Government complaint copy | Follow-up reference |

These records are public and are handed to the health/water authorities on
request. The colony's defence against "you hid it" is the complete, dated file.

## 6. SCENARIO — THE SEWER SMELL

A member reports the water smells like sewage. The ERT stops drinking use in
an hour, samples go to the lab the same day, the notice goes up: do not drink;
municipal backup running. The lab confirms coliform. The medical alert reaches
two households already unwell, who are treated. A complaint goes to the water
board with the report. Chlorination by an approved operator is done, lines are
flushed, and the retest comes back clean. The full record is published and
filed. The colony drank contaminated water for one day — not a silent month.
