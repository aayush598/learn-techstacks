# 02 - INVERTER SHARING

**Folder:** `10-RISK-AND-EMERGENCY/03-Power-Failure/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

A generator is heavy, loud, and costly to run for a few hours of supply. An
inverter system (batteries + inverter) can carry **critical equipment** — a
small motor or the control/communication devices — through short cuts,
quietly and cheaply. This protocol covers colony-owned inverter capacity: who
pays, what it may run, and what it never runs.

## 2. WHAT THE COLONY MAY KEEP

1. The assembly may buy and maintain a colony inverter-battery set for
   critical uses, from the Common/Reserve Fund by resolution.
2. "Critical equipment" means:
   - the control panel's protection and monitoring circuits;
   - a communication device (phone/router) for the colony spokespersons;
   - a small water pump where the generator is not run (e.g., for a short cut
     or for the priority households' fill).
3. The inverter is inventoried and logged like other assets
   (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/05-Parts-and-Inventory/`).

## 3. COST SPLIT

| Cost | Paid by |
|------|---------|
| Purchase and installation | Common/Reserve Fund by assembly resolution |
| Battery replacement and upkeep | Common Fund (maintenance line) |
| Electricity to recharge | Common Fund |
| Private use by a member | Member pays the recorded per-hour cost plus penalty |

1. The cost split is a **standing published rule**; there is no negotiation
   per use.
2. A member who draws the inverter for private use without a log entry pays
   the penalty per `01-Generator-Protocol.md`.

## 4. RUNNING RULES

1. The inverter runs **only the listed critical loads**. It is not a colony
   power bank for fans, fridges, or TVs.
2. During a cut, the ERT decides the sequence: protection first, then
   communication, then the small-pump use (if any).
3. Battery depth-of-discharge is respected: the duty member logs the run, and
   the inverter is not run flat for a non-critical convenience.
4. Use is logged in the run log: date, time, load, battery state, recharge.

## 5. NO PERSONAL USE

1. The inverter is for the colony's critical loads, not for a member's
   household — including the member who "helped install it".
2. A member who connects a private load to the colony inverter commits an
   offence under the Enforcement Ladder
   (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/05-Enforcement/01-Enforcement-Ladder.md`); the
   recharged cost is recovered.
3. Households are expected to manage their own personal inverter needs from
   their own resources; the colony's set exists for the system, not for
   comfort (P1).

## 6. MAINTENANCE

1. Batteries are checked at each PM round
   (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/01-Preventive-Maintenance/01-PM-Schedule.md`):
   terminals, electrolyte level (if applicable), charge retention.
2. A battery failing retention is flagged and replaced from the Common Fund,
   not "used till it dies".
3. The inverter is kept dry and locked in the motor room with the panel.

## 7. SCENARIO — THE SHORT CUT, THE QUIET FILL

A 90-minute cut hits in the morning. The generator would be noise and fuel for
one fill. The ERT runs the inverter: panel protection is live, the spokespersons'
phone stays charged, and the small pump fills the priority households' tanks
in sequence. The cut passes quietly, the log shows the use, and the recharge is
counted in the Common Fund. No member expected their fridge to run — and no
member ran it.
