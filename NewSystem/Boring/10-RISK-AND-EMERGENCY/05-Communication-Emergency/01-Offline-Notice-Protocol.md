# 01 - OFFLINE NOTICE PROTOCOL

**Folder:** `10-RISK-AND-EMERGENCY/05-Communication-Emergency/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

The colony's daily communication runs on phones and groups — which go dead in
a network failure, a power cut, or a flood. In the old system, the moment
phones failed, information became the property of whoever knocked on the right
door. This rule guarantees an **offline channel that always exists**, so no
crisis can silence the colony (P3).

## 2. THE CHANNELS, IN ORDER

| # | Channel | Used when | Owner |
|---|---------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | **Notice board at the distribution point** | Always the master channel; every notice also goes here | Duty member posts every notice |
| 2 | **Loudspeaker / announcement** | Network down, or notice must reach everyone at once | ERT / duty member |
| 3 | **Door-to-door** | Announcing urgent info to households that cannot be reached by 1 or 2 | Duty members in pairs |
| 4 | **Colony group** | Normal times; treated as a convenience, never as the only channel | All |

The rule: **no notice exists only on the phone.** Anything important goes to
the notice board within the same hour it is sent digitally.

## 3. STANDING ITEMS ON THE NOTICE BOARD

The notice board always carries, in print:

| Item | Updated |
|------|---------|
| Current month's ERT names and phones | Monthly |
| Emergency contacts (`02-Emergency-Contacts.md`) | Quarterly |
| Current supply schedule and any rationing limits | When changed |
| Any active fault number and its status | Daily during an event |
| The offline-protocol itself (so members know the rules) | Yearly |

## 4. WHEN THE NETWORK GOES DOWN

1. The duty member or ERT detects/is informed of the outage and posts a
   printed notice: "Network down; updates on board at 8 a.m. and 8 p.m."
2. The **loudspeaker** is used for urgent items: breakdown, contamination,
   emergency meetings. Its hours are limited to avoid nuisance — except for a
   genuine emergency, when it may run as needed.
3. **Door-to-door** rounds are done by **two duty members together** (never
   one alone, P7 and P10): each household gets the fact sheet (fault number,
   what to do, next update time). A checklist of doors visited is signed.
4. Households with elderly, disabled, or chronically ill members are visited
   **first** and marked on the checklist (`02-Priority-Allocation.md`).

## 5. RECORDING

1. Every offline notice is copied into the event log with the time posted.
2. The door-to-door checklist (which houses were told) is kept with the
   crisis log — proof that no one was left uninformed (P7).
3. When the network returns, a summary of what was announced offline is
   posted digitally, so nobody is told twice or not at all.

## 6. ALWAYS HAVE AN OFFLINE CHANNEL — THE TEST

1. The ERT verifies the offline channel **once a month**: a practice notice is
   posted, announced, or walked — at minimum the notice board is checked and
   photographed.
2. Once a year the colony runs a **no-phone drill**: for 24 hours all notices
   use only offline channels. Weaknesses found are fixed and recorded
   (`00-Risk-Framework.md`).

## 7. SCENARIO — THE NETWORK DEAD WEEKEND

A storm kills phone and internet for two days. The motor fails on day one.
The ERT posts the printed breakdown notice, uses the loudspeaker for the
evening announcement, and two members walk the colony with the fact sheet,
visiting the three elderly households first. The notice board carries the
12-hour updates. When the network returns, the summary is posted and the
records are filed. Not one household was uninformed, because the colony never
depended on the phone to begin with.
