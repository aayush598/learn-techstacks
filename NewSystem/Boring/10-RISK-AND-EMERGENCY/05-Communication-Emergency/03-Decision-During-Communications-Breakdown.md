# 03 - DECISION DURING COMMUNICATIONS BREAKDOWN

**Folder:** `10-RISK-AND-EMERGENCY/05-Communication-Emergency/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

When the phones die and members cannot be reached, the colony must still
decide — or rather, it must **not need to decide**: the default rules apply,
and only narrowly bounded emergency powers exist. The principle:

> **No communications breakdown may become a power grab. When members cannot
> be consulted, the written rules decide. The on-duty team may act for safety
> and information only, spend only up to a small limit, and must be ratified
> later by the assembly.**

## 2. THE DEFAULT RULE STANDS

1. When members cannot be reached, **the published schedule and plans
   continue as they are**: supply slots, rationing limits, priority order,
   PM dates, fault deadlines. Nothing stops because the phone is down.
2. The motor runs per the roster; the duty operator logs as usual. A
   communications failure is not a reason to suspend supply or a plan.
3. The plans that stand without any decision: `01-Drought-and-Scarcity/`,
   `02-Motor-Breakdown/`, `04-Health-and-Safety/`. Their rules are written to
   run without consultation.

## 3. THE BOUNDED EMERGENCY POWERS OF THE ON-DUTY TEAM

While members are unreachable, the ERT may do only:

| Power | Boundary |
|-------|----------|
| **Safety** | Make a dangerous site safe (isolate power, stop contaminated water) per `04-Health-and-Safety/` |
| **Informing** | Announce, walk, and post per `01-Offline-Notice-Protocol.md` |
| **Spending** | Up to **₹2,000** per event, only for the above (e.g., a quick isolation repair, a tanker if people are dry), **never** beyond |
| **Activating backup** | Put the rental pump / municipal / tanker plan into motion per its own written limits |

The team may **never**: hire a large repair, approve a big spend, change the
rationing limits, change the priority order, amend the schedule, or sign any
contract.

## 4. WHAT THE TEAM RECORDS

1. Every action taken during the breakdown is written into the crisis log the
   same day: time, action, cost, who decided, why.
2. The log is the evidence for ratification. An action not in the log is
   treated as unauthorized.

## 5. RATIFICATION

1. When communications recover — or at the next scheduled assembly, whichever
   comes first — the assembly **ratifies, adjusts, or rejects** the team's
   actions within 30 days.
2. Ratification is by simple majority with the crisis log as the record.
3. Consequences:
   - **Within power + ratified**: recorded as valid, costs paid from the
     appropriate fund.
   - **Within power + rejected**: costs reviewed; team members not penalized
     for a good-faith bounded action.
   - **Beyond power**: the excess cost is recovered from the member(s) who
     acted beyond their limit, and the enforcement ladder applies
     (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/05-Enforcement/01-Enforcement-Ladder.md`).
4. A team that stayed within the limits is thanked in the record — the bounds,
     not the improvisation, are the system.

## 6. SCENARIO — THE FLOOD NIGHT

A flood cuts power, phones, and the motor dies. Members are unreachable for
30 hours. The ERT acts within its bounds: it makes the motor room safe
(isolates the flooded panel), posts and walks the notices, and — seeing two
priority households with no water at all — orders one tanker at ₹1,800 against
the ₹2,000 limit. When the network returns, the assembly ratifies the actions
within 10 days. The colony survived the blackout with the same rules it
followed in the light.
