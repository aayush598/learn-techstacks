# GENERAL ASSEMBLY 4 — MEETING CONDUCT

**Folder:** `02-GOVERNANCE/01-General-Assembly/`

---

## 1. THE ROTATING FACILITATOR

- Each meeting is chaired by a **rotating facilitator** drawn by lot from
  members, **not** a permanent person (`05-Rotation-and-Fairness/02-Lot-Draw-System.md`).
- The facilitator is a **servant of procedure**, not a leader. They cannot
  decide, overrule, or direct the outcome.
- The facilitator's duties are limited to: calling the meeting to order,
  checking quorum, keeping to the agenda, giving speaking turns, keeping
  order, and declaring the result of votes as the votes actually are.
- A facilitator who is a party to an agenda item hands over the chair for that
  item to the next lot-drawn facilitator.

## 2. ORDER OF SPEAKING

1. The facilitator opens with the quorum check and the agenda.
2. For each item: the proposer explains (time limited), then members speak in
   turn, then the facilitator declares discussion closed and the vote is taken.
3. Speaking turns are given **oldest registered speaker first among those who
   have not yet spoken** on that item, so one person cannot speak repeatedly
   while others wait.
4. A member who has already spoken may speak again only after all other
   members who wish to speak have had their turn.
5. No member speaks on an item without being recognised by the facilitator;
   the facilitator recognises speakers in the order they raised their hand.

## 3. TIME LIMITS

| Action | Time |
|--------|------|
| Proposer's opening | 5 minutes |
| Each member's first turn per item | 3 minutes |
| Rebuttal (after all turns) | 2 minutes per speaker |
| Closing by proposer | 2 minutes |
| Total discussion per ordinary item | 30 minutes maximum, extendable only by a Class A vote |

- Time limits apply to **everyone equally**, including the proposer and any
  influential member. P2 applies inside the meeting too.
- Time limits do not apply to a member presenting a grievance about
  themselves; that member gets as long as the assembly needs to hear them
  (P10).

## 4. NO SHOUTING, NO INSULTS, NO TARGETING

- Shouting, insults, caste/gender slurs, mocking, or threats are prohibited
  in any assembly under P10.
- If a member shouts, the facilitator warns them once. On a second
  occurrence, the facilitator records it in the minutes and the member loses
  their speaking turn for that item.
- Targeting a member's family, occupation, religion, or background is not
  "discussion" — it is an offence under the Enforcement Ladder
  (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/`), and the CRB may be asked to review it.
- A member who feels personally attacked may invoke a short "dignity
  objection"; the facilitator pauses the item until the remark is withdrawn
  or recorded and set aside.

## 5. FACTS vs VOTES AT THE MEETING

Before any vote, the facilitator runs the test from
`00-CONSTITUTION/04-Truth-Supremacy/01-Principle-of-Truth-over-Majority.md`:

1. Is this a question of **fact**? → Evidence review, no vote.
2. Is this a question of **applying an existing rule**? → Apply the rule, no
   vote.
3. Is this a question of a **fundamental right**? → The right stands, no vote.
4. Is this a genuine **choice between valid options**? → Vote, under the
   classes in `03-Decision-Making/01-Decision-Classes.md`.

Any member may stop a vote that violates this test by filing a Truth Veto
(`03-Decision-Making/04-Truth-Review-Override.md`).

## 6. RECORDING OF VOTES

- Every vote is recorded: the item, the class, the counts for/against/
  absent, the method (show of hands or ballot), and the names of the two
  counters (from different households).
- The facilitator **declares the result exactly as counted**; they have no
  power to alter it.
- Any member may request a recount, which is done immediately and openly.

## 7. DECIDING WHEN DISCUSSION IS OVER

- Discussion is closed when: the time limit is reached, or no more speakers
  are registered, or a Class A vote of the assembly calls the question.
- A member who is cut off while holding a turn on a **rights-affecting** item
  may file their view in writing, and it is read into the record before the
  vote.

## 8. DISRUPTION AND UNRULY MEETINGS

- If a member prevents the meeting from functioning, the facilitator first
  warns, then records the disruption, then may ask the CRB to review
  obstruction. The meeting itself is not abandoned because of one person.
- If the whole meeting becomes unruly, the facilitator may adjourn for 15
  minutes and reconvene. Minutes of the disruption are kept.
- A decision taken by physically intimidating others is void.

## 9. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

At one meeting, two neighbours began shouting at each other over a leak. The
lot-drawn facilitator paused the item, gave each a single minute to state the
fact, then noted that "who caused the leak" is a question of evidence, not a
vote. The CRB reviewed the photographs and logs later that week. The meeting
then moved on to genuinely votable items. Everyone, including the two
neighbours, went home knowing the system had handled their fight without a
crowd deciding guilt.

---

*The meeting is a court of procedure, not a market of noise. Order is kept not
to silence people but to ensure every voice — especially the quiet and the
weak — is heard once.*
