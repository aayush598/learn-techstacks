# GENERAL ASSEMBLY 5 — MEETING RECORDS

**Folder:** `02-GOVERNANCE/01-General-Assembly/`

---

## 1. WHY MINUTES MATTER

Minutes are the **memory of the system**. A decision that is not recorded does
not exist; a recorded decision is binding on everyone, including those who
were absent. Minutes protect members from the old habit of "we never agreed to
that". They are legal records under `11-LEGAL-AND-COMPLIANCE/03-Records-Retention.md`.

## 2. THE MINUTES TEMPLATE

- All minutes follow the standard template:
  `12-TEMPLATES/08-Meeting-Minutes.md`.
- The template requires, at minimum:

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| Meeting ID | e.g. GA-2026-003 |
| Type | Monthly / AGM / Emergency / Special |
| Date, time, venue | As published |
| Facilitator | Name of the lot-drawn facilitator |
| Minute-taker | Name (different household from facilitator) |
| Attendance | Present, ballots, absent; quorum threshold and whether met |
| Agenda | Every item, in order, with its class |
| For each item | Discussion summary (facts cited, objections), the vote method, the counts, the decision text |
| Truth Vetoes | Any veto filed, its suspension effect, and the CRB reference |
| Corrections | To previous minutes, noted with dates |
| Next meeting | Date, time, venue |

## 3. WHO TAKES THE MINUTES

- The minute-taker is a **lot-drawn member**, from a household different from
  the facilitator's, so no one writes the record of their own meeting alone.
- The minute-taker records what was **said and decided**, not their opinions.
- At the close of the meeting, the facilitator reads a summary and members may
  point out obvious errors on the spot.

## 4. PUBLICATION WITHIN 48 HOURS

- Draft minutes are published **within 48 hours** of the meeting's close, on
  the notice board and in the members' group and the digital archive.
- Publication makes the minutes available for correction before they become
  final.
- Failure to publish within 48 hours is a transparency failure and is
  reported at the next meeting (P6).

## 5. CORRECTIONS AT THE NEXT MEETING

- At the start of the next meeting, the previous minutes are read for
  corrections (the standard agenda order, Section 6 of
  `03-Agenda-Fixing.md`).
- Corrections may be proposed by any member. A correction that changes a
  **decision** (not just a typo) is treated as a proposal to reconsider, which
  follows the normal decision classes — minutes cannot be quietly edited to
  change an outcome.
- Once corrected and approved, the minutes are signed by the facilitator and
  the minute-taker of that meeting and are final.

## 6. MINUTES ARE LEGAL RECORDS

- The final signed minutes are the authoritative record of the assembly.
- They are kept in the physical register and the digital archive, both
  covered by `04-Transparency/01-Open-Records.md`.
- Every member may inspect and copy minutes at any time
  (`04-Transparency/02-Right-to-Inspect.md`).
- A dispute about what was decided is resolved by the final minutes; a
  dispute about whether the minutes are accurate is resolved by the
  correction process, not by argument.

## 7. SPECIAL RECORDS IN MINUTES

| Event | Required record |
|-------|-----------------|
| Truth Veto filed | The text of the veto, the suspension, the CRB reference, the outcome |
| Secret ballot | The counts and the method; names of counters; ballot papers sealed |
| Rights-affecting decision | The notification and hearing of the affected member per the Absence Rule |
| Refusal of quorum | The count, the second-call notice date |
| Objection to a resolution | `00-CONSTITUTION/04-Truth-Supremacy/03-Objection-to-Wrong-Resolution.md` |

## 8. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

A member later claimed the assembly had approved refunding him ₹800 for a pipe
he bought. The minutes of that meeting showed the item was discussed but that
the motion failed, and the counts were recorded. The written record settled
the dispute without a shouting match — which is precisely why every meeting,
however small, is minuted in full.

---

*An unrecorded meeting is a rumour. A recorded meeting is a law. This system
trusts paper, not memory.*
