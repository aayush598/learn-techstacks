# DECISION-MAKING 5 — NO DECISION WITHOUT PARTICIPATION

**Folder:** `02-GOVERNANCE/03-Decision-Making/`

---

## 1. THE RULE

No decision may be taken **behind closed doors**, and no decision about a
specific member or a small minority may be taken **without that member or
minority present or heard**. The assembly is the only decision-maker, and the
assembly decides in the open, with the people concerned in the room.

## 2. NO DECISIONS BEHIND CLOSED DOORS

| Place | May decide? |
|-------|-------------|
| A formal assembly with quorum and minutes | Yes |
| A "committee meeting" called to decide policy | No — committees execute only |
| A gathering at a member's house "to sort things out" | No — decisions there are not decisions |
| An informal WhatsApp group discussion | No — announcements only, never decisions |
| The notice box / written ballot | Yes, only for the approved absentee procedure |

- Only the assembly decides, and it decides **in the open**. Anyone may
  observe any committee or CRB proceeding
  (`02-Committees/01-Committee-Formation.md`).
- A decision taken in private is void, and anyone who relied on it acted
  without authority.

## 3. DECISIONS AFFECTING ONE MEMBER

When a decision concerns a specific member — their conduct, dues, exemption,
penalty, or entitlements:

1. The member is **notified in writing** at least 7 days in advance
   (`02-Quorum-Rules.md` Section 5 — the Absence Rule).
2. The member is **heard first**, before general discussion, at the meeting.
3. The member may bring a supporter or may ask for the item to be heard by the
   CRB instead if they fear the room.
4. No penalty decision is valid without the member's notice and hearing on
   record.

## 4. DECISIONS AFFECTING A SMALL MINORITY

- A decision that disproportionately affects a minority — a street, a set of
  households, a particular group — requires that the affected members be
  **invited, notified, and heard**, and their objections recorded.
- If the affected minority is **not present at all** and was not notified, the
  decision is void.
- The minority's objection does not veto the decision, but it **must** be
  answered in the minutes with reasons; an unaddressed objection voids the
  decision's reasonableness.

## 5. ABSENTEE COERCION — THE SECRET BALLOT

- If a member cannot attend, they may vote by written ballot
  (`02-Ordinary-Voting.md` Section 3), so absence never silences them.
- If a member fears that voting publicly would bring retaliation, **one
  request turns the vote secret**, and that request cannot be refused.
- No member may be punished for how they voted — after a secret ballot nobody
  knows; after a public ballot, retaliation is an offence under the
  Enforcement Ladder (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/`).
- This combination — open meetings, written ballots, secret-on-request — makes
  it impossible for a strongman to "collect votes" by pressure.

## 6. COERCION AND INTIMIDATION

| Act | Consequence |
|-----|-------------|
| Threatening a member to change their vote | Offence; the affected vote may be re-run by secret ballot |
| Bribing a member for a vote | Offence; the bribe is forfeit to the common fund |
| Tracking who raised a hand to punish them later | Offence; CRB review on complaint |
| Holding a "decision meeting" without notice | The decision is void |

## 7. THE RECORD PROOF

- Minutes must show, for each affected-person item: notice given (date),
  member present or written response on file, what was heard, and the
  disposition.
- A "decision" whose minutes cannot show the hearing is treated as if it never
  happened.

## 8. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

Three houses at the end of the colony's lowest street were getting weak
pressure. The assembly was about to vote "no change — the schedule is fine."
One of those houses' members was present and explained the ground reality:
the tap was at the pipe's far end. The minutes recorded their testimony. The
decision was not simply "no change"; the assembly instead referred the matter
to the Repair Committee to check the pipe for leaks — a decision made with the
affected member in the room, not about them behind their back.

---

*Participation is not a courtesy; it is a condition of validity. A decision
made about a person without that person is a decision made against them.*
