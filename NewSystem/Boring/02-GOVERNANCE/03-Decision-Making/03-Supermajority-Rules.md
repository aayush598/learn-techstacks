# DECISION-MAKING 3 — SUPERMAJORITY RULES

**Folder:** `02-GOVERNANCE/03-Decision-Making/`

---

## 1. WHEN A SUPERMAJORITY IS REQUIRED

A simple majority is not enough when the decision changes the rules everyone
lives under. The following require a supermajority:

| Matter | Threshold | Class |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Water schedule change (timing, duration, seasonal) | 2/3 of all members | B |
| Contribution formula change | 2/3 of all members | B |
| Duty system change | 2/3 of all members | B |
| Annual Budget adoption | 2/3 of all members | B |
| Event water fees | 2/3 of all members | B |
| A one-time project or new expenditure line | 2/3 of all members | B |
| Emergency measures lasting beyond 14 days | 2/3 of all members | B |
| Any rights-adjacent change | 3/4 of all members + CRB certification | C |
| Basic structure changes | FORBIDDEN | D |

This mirrors `00-CONSTITUTION/05-Amendment-Process/03-Voting-Rules.md`.

## 2. THE COUNTING RULE: THE WHOLE MEMBERSHIP, NOT THE ROOM

- For Classes B and C the denominator is **ALL registered members** — not
  merely those present.
- **Silence counts as "against".** A member who does not vote, or who is
  absent without a ballot, is counted as having voted no.
- Why: these decisions bind the absent too. The rule prevents a small, loud
  meeting from imposing change on the silent majority.

## 3. THE MATH IN PRACTICE

For a colony of **20 members**:

| Class | Requirement | Passes only if |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| B | 2/3 of 20 ≈ 14 | At least **14 of 20** members vote yes |
| C | 3/4 of 20 = 15 | At least **15 of 20** members vote yes, plus CRB certification |

Even if 14 of the 15 people in the room raise their hands for a Class B
change, it **fails** if the colony has 20 members — the five absent are counted
as five no-votes (14 < 14.67). This is deliberate.

## 4. EMERGENCY MEASURES

- In a genuine emergency, the assembly may adopt **interim measures** by 2/3
  of members present, but only for a maximum of **14 days**, only for the
  purpose of preserving water, safety, or the system, and subject to the Truth
  Review Override.
- An interim measure lasting longer than 14 days must be confirmed as a
  regular Class B decision with full-membership counting.
- No emergency measure may violate a Fundamental Right, even temporarily
  (`00-CONSTITUTION/06-Supremacy-and-Conflict/03-Interim-Rules-During-Gaps.md`).

## 5. SUPERMAJORITY DOES NOT TRUMP TRUTH

- A supermajority can decide between **valid options**. It cannot:
  1. Decide a **fact** ("the motor was running at 5pm") — evidence decides.
  2. Override a **rule** — only a valid amendment can change a rule.
  3. Override a **right** — rights stand.
  4. Bypass the **basic structure** — forbidden even unanimously.
- Every supermajority decision still passes the Truth Gate
  (`04-Truth-Review-Override.md`).

## 6. PUBLICATION OF HIGH-CLASS VOTES

- Class B and C votes are published with: the full member count, the yes/no/
  absent tally, and the notice history.
- The tally makes the "silence is against" rule visible and fair — a member
  can see that their absence was counted, and can object to a mis-count within
  15 days.

## 7. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

A group of 12 members attended a meeting and voted 10–2 to change the morning
slot. The colony has 20 members. The facilitator declared: "Class B requires
14 of 20. Ten yes votes are recorded. The motion fails." Several members were
surprised, so the tally was shown on the board: 10 yes, 2 no, 8 absent
(counted as no). Nobody could say the schedule was changed by a roomful of
people; the whole colony's consent was required and was not there.

---

*Super-majority is the arithmetic of consent. When change binds everyone,
silence cannot mean consent.*
