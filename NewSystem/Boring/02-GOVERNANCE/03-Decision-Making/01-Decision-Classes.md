# DECISION-MAKING 1 — DECISION CLASSES

**Folder:** `02-GOVERNANCE/03-Decision-Making/`

---

## 1. WHY CLASSES EXIST

Different decisions affect the colony differently. A typo fix and a change to
the water schedule are not the same weight. Decision classes assign each
decision the right threshold, so that trivial matters are not blocked and
important matters are not rushed.

## 2. THE FOUR CLASSES

| Class | Nature | Threshold | Examples |
|-------|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Class A — Ordinary** | Routine execution, no change to rights or structure | Majority (50% + 1) of those present AND ≥ 40% of all members | Approving minutes, choosing a repair vendor, forming an ad-hoc committee, agenda procedure |
| **Class B — Substantive** | Changes that affect everyone's water, money, or duty | **2/3 (66.7%) of all members** (not just those present) | Schedule changes, contribution formula changes, duty system changes, event fees, annual budget adoption |
| **Class C — Rights-adjacent** | Touches a Fundamental Right or a protection mechanism | **3/4 (75%) of all members**, PLUS no upheld Truth Veto, PLUS CRB certification | Changing a protection rule, altering the quorum rule, extending term limits, amending rights language |
| **Class D — Basic structure** | Water as common property, one-member-one-vote, truth supremacy, audit right, contribution duty | **FORBIDDEN — void regardless of votes** | Any attempt to create a permanent controller, remove a right, or exempt a group from contribution |

This is the same ladder as
`00-CONSTITUTION/05-Amendment-Process/03-Voting-Rules.md`. The two documents
are identical by design; nothing in this folder may loosen the constitutional
thresholds.

## 3. CLASSIFICATION PROCESS

1. The proposer states the proposed class on the agenda item
   (`01-General-Assembly/03-Agenda-Fixing.md`).
2. The CRB confirms the class at the agenda stage. **When in doubt, the higher
   class applies** — safer is better.
3. The proposer or any member may appeal the classification to the assembly
   before the vote.
4. A vote taken under the wrong (lower) class is void and must be re-run.

## 4. WHAT CANNOT BE VOTED ON AT ALL

Regardless of class:

1. **Facts** — decided by evidence (`00-CONSTITUTION/04-Truth-Supremacy/04-No-Voting-on-Proven-Facts.md`).
2. **Applying an existing rule** — the rule is applied, not voted.
3. **Fundamental rights** — they stand.
4. **Basic structure** — forbidden.

A motion attempting any of these is stopped by the facilitator or by any
member's Truth Veto (`04-Truth-Review-Override.md`).

## 5. CLASS AND QUORUM LINK

- Class A requires standard quorum (60%) with the floor of 40% at a second
  call (`01-General-Assembly/02-Quorum-Rules.md`).
- Class B and C require the **standard 60% quorum**; the second-call floor of
  40% does **not** apply to them. If a Class B/C meeting cannot reach quorum,
  the item is carried to the next month with a fresh notice.
- Class B and C also count **absentees as "against"**, per
  `03-Supermajority-Rules.md`.

## 6. RECORD

- Every decision records its class, notice dates, discussion summary, vote
  method, counts, and objections — in the minutes
  (`01-General-Assembly/05-Meeting-Records.md`).

## 7. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

The colony wanted to move the evening supply from 5–6 pm to 5:30–6:30 pm.
Because a schedule change touches every household's water, it was classified
Class B. Only 12 of 20 members voted yes — that is 60%, below the required
2/3 of all members — so the change failed. The minority had blocked a change
that would have hurt them, without even needing to argue: the class rule did
the protecting.

---

*The class of a decision is as important as the decision itself. A vote under
the wrong class is a vote that never happened.*
