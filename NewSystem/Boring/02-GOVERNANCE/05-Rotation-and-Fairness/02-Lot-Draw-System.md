# ROTATION AND FAIRNESS 2 — LOT DRAW SYSTEM

**Folder:** `02-GOVERNANCE/05-Rotation-and-Fairness/`

---

## 1. WHY LOTS, NOT CHOICE

Choosing roles by lot is the fairest method that exists: it removes
favouritism, "volunteer cliques", and the pressure to vote for influential
people. A lot cannot be bribed, persuaded, or threatened. The system uses lots
for every duty, committee, and jury because choice is where domination begins.

## 2. WHERE LOTS ARE USED

| Purpose | Lot draw at |
|---------|-------------|
| Duty roster (operators, backups) | Per roster cycle (`03-ROTATION-DUTY/01-Duty-Roster/02-Roster-Generation-by-Lot.md`) |
| Committee seats | AGM + staggered mid-year draws |
| CRB members | AGM + replacement draws |
| Meeting facilitator | Before each meeting |
| Minute-taker | Before each meeting |
| Counters for votes | At each vote |
| Jury/panel for reviews | When the CRB is conflicted |
| Committee convenors | First AGM draw |

## 3. THE DRAW PROCEDURE

| Step | Rule |
|------|------|
| 1. Names | All eligible members' names on identical slips or numbered balls |
| 2. Public | The draw happens at a meeting or a announced public time, never in private |
| 3. Witnessed | At least **two members from different households** witness and sign |
| 4. Recorded | The result is written on the draw sheet, photographed, and posted |
| 5. Declared | The result is announced and entered in the minutes |

- The draw container is transparent or opened fully so everyone sees the slips.
- Members draw for **themselves** as far as possible (self-draw makes
  tampering impossible: a person cannot accuse others of picking their name).
- Names are not put back after drawing, so the same person cannot win twice in
  one draw (unless serving more than one role is permitted by the Separation
  Matrix, which it usually is not).

## 4. NO RIGGING

| Guard | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Photos/records of the draw | The draw sheet + a photo of the drawn slips are posted the same day |
| Two witnesses | Sign the sheet; both are drawn from households other than the drawer |
| Right to witness | Any member may stand at the draw and watch; refusal to allow watchers voids the draw |
| Challenge window | A member may challenge a draw within 7 days, with evidence (photo, witness, sequence) |

- Forging, influencing, or secretly re-running a draw is a **serious offence**
  (obstruction + possible targeting, per `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/`).

## 5. DRAWS REDO IF FLAWED

A draw is **redone** (fresh, in public, with all names back in) when:

1. A procedural step was missed (no witnesses, no record).
2. A member was wrongly included or excluded (wrong slips in the box).
3. The result violates the Separation Matrix (one household holding two
   linked roles).
4. The draw was not public.
5. Evidence shows the slips were tampered with.

- The first draw's record is kept alongside the redo, so the flaw is visible,
  not hidden.

## 6. DECLINING AND SWAPPING

- A member drawn for a duty may decline only by: (a) arranging a **swapped
  volunteer** of the same eligibility, or (b) paying the published **buy-out
  amount** that funds a replacement's stipend (P4, P8)
  (`03-ROTATION-DUTY/03-Duty-Compensation/`).
- A member who repeatedly declines without swap/buy-out is handled by the
  Duty Failure procedure (`03-ROTATION-DUTY/04-Duty-Failure/`).
- Swaps are recorded so the roster stays balanced — one willing person cannot
  quietly take everyone's turns (P3).

## 7. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

The colony drew its duty roster for the month. A member stood at the draw,
watched every slip, and saw his neighbour's name drawn for duty on the day of
his neighbour's shop sale. The neighbour swapped with a volunteer within 48
hours, and the swap was posted on the board. No one had "arranged" anything;
the lot had simply spoken, and the rules had handled the rest.
