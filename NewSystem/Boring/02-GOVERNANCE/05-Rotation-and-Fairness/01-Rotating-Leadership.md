# ROTATION AND FAIRNESS 1 — ROTATING LEADERSHIP

**Folder:** `02-GOVERNANCE/05-Rotation-and-Fairness/`

---

## 1. THE PRINCIPLE

Leadership in this system is a **task, not a rank**. Whoever facilitates a
meeting, runs a committee, or keeps a log is doing a duty — and that duty
moves on. Rotation guarantees that no member accumulates the influence that
comes with being "the person in charge".

## 2. NO PERSON BEYOND TERM LIMITS

- Every role has a fixed term and a mandatory gap
  (`02-Committees/06-Term-Limits-and-Rotation.md`).
- A member who has served a full term cannot simply continue; the next lot
  draw names a replacement.
- A member who tries to hold a role beyond its term has **no authority** after
  the term expires, and their acts are void.

## 3. FACILITATOR AND CHAIR ROTATE EVERY MEETING

| Meeting | Chair | Why rotation matters |
|---------|-------|----------------------|
| Monthly assembly | Lot-drawn facilitator, fresh each meeting | No one becomes "the chairman" |
| AGM | Lot-drawn facilitator, fresh | The most important meeting is chaired by chance, not by ambition |
| Committee meetings | Rotating convener among members | No permanent convenor |
| Emergency assembly | First member on the day's lot list | Speed without power |

- The facilitator has **no casting vote and no deciding authority** — they
  count, they do not rule (`01-General-Assembly/04-Meeting-Conduct.md`).
- If the drawn facilitator cannot serve, the next name on the lot list serves
  for that meeting only.

## 4. POWER IS DELIBERATELY DILUTED

Rotation is designed so that at any moment the colony has **many hands, no
master**:

| Control | Held by | Turns over |
|---------|---------|------------|
| The gavel (meeting) | Lot-drawn facilitator | Every meeting |
| The pen (minutes) | Lot-drawn minute-taker | Every meeting |
| The key (motor) | Duty roster | Per roster cycle |
| The box (cash) | Finance custodian | Per term |
| The ledger | Finance ledger keeper | Per term |
| The record vault | Record keepers | Per term |

No two of these are ever in the same household at the same time
(`02-Committees/06-Term-Limits-and-Rotation.md` Section 4).

## 5. RESISTING THE "EFFICIENT EXPERTS" TRAP

- The most common objection to rotation is: "Why change? The current people
  know how." This is exactly the argument that created the old system.
- Answer: skill is **transferred**, not kept. Each outgoing role-holder
  trains the incoming one during handover
  (`00-CONSTITUTION/03-Fundamental-Duties/02-Duty-to-Learn-and-Participate.md`).
- Rotation is not inefficiency; it is **insurance** (P3). A system that runs
  on one skilled person stops when that person leaves. A system that runs on
  everyone never stops.

## 6. THE EFFECT OF ROTATION ON BEHAVIOUR

- A member who will not hold the same role next year has less reason to
  accumulate favours or debts.
- A member who knows they will one day be a **ledger keeper or a duty
  operator** treats the records and the motor with care.
- Everyone governs; therefore everyone is governed by the same rules.

## 7. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

For a year, the same member facilitated every meeting "because no one else
knew the rules". Under rotation, each meeting's facilitator is drawn by lot,
and a printed one-page facilitation card sits at the notice board
(`13-HANDBOOKS/`). A vegetable vendor facilitated her colony's meeting one
month; a retired teacher the next. Neither gained power — each simply did the
job, and the meeting ran because the rules ran it.

---

*In a rotating system there are no leaders to fall in love with and no
leaders to fear. There are only members taking their turn.*
