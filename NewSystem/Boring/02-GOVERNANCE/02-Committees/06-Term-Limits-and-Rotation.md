# COMMITTEES 6 — TERM LIMITS AND ROTATION

**Folder:** `02-GOVERNANCE/02-Committees/`

---

## 1. THE PRINCIPLE

No role in this system may become a person's **possession**. Every role has a
fixed term, a fixed maximum of consecutive terms, and a mandatory gap. This is
P2 and P3 made concrete: power must circulate or it will crystallise.

## 2. STANDARD TERM STRUCTURE

| Role | Term | Max consecutive terms | Mandatory gap |
|------|------|-----------------------|---------------|
| Committee member | 12 months | 1 term | 12 months |
| CRB member | 3–6 months | 1 term | 6 months |
| Meeting facilitator | 1 meeting | — (drawn fresh each meeting) | — |
| Minute-taker | 1 meeting | — (drawn fresh each meeting) | — |
| Duty operator | Per roster cycle | As the lot draw distributes | Roster balance enforced by the draw |
| Any ad-hoc role | Task duration | 1 task | — |

- "Max consecutive terms: 1" means: after serving a full term, a member must
  wait one full term-length before serving the same role again.
- A partial term (e.g., replacing someone who resigned) counts as **half a
  term**; two partial terms = one full term.

## 3. ROTATION BY LOT, NOT BY POPULARITY

- All roles are filled from the **lot draw** at the AGM
  (`05-Rotation-and-Fairness/02-Lot-Draw-System.md`).
- No election, no "volunteers only", no "experienced ones stay". Experience is
  shared by training (P3), not by permanent tenure.
- A member who wants to serve puts their name in the draw like everyone else;
  the draw decides.

## 4. SEPARATION OF CONTROL — NO TWO ROLES IN ONE HOUSEHOLD

A single household may **not simultaneously hold**:

| Group | Roles that must be split |
|-------|--------------------------|
| Money | Finance signatory, cash custodian, ledger keeper |
| Water | Duty operator holding keys and a seat on the Water Committee |
| Records | Minute-taker and the facilitator of the same meeting |
| Oversight | A CRB member and any committee whose acts may come before the CRB |
| Vendor | Repair Committee member and the same household's vendor business |

The assembly publishes a **Separation Matrix** each year, listing which role
pairs are forbidden for one household. This is the practical enforcement of
"no single point of failure" (P3) and "equal power" (P2).

## 5. WHY SEPARATION MATTERS

- One household holding the keys, the ledger, and the bank passbook
  reproduces the old single-owner system even without a "president" title.
- Separation guarantees that **at least two independent people** must
  cooperate for any abuse: to steal, you need the cash, the keys, and the
  records — held by three different rotating people.
- See the power-concentration test in `00-Governance-Model.md` Section 7.

## 6. HANDOVER OBLIGATION

- Every outgoing role-holder hands over within **7 days**: documents, keys,
  contacts, and a written status note.
- The handover is witnessed and signed; nothing may be withheld "because it is
  mine".
- Failure to hand over is a transparency offence and is escalated per
  `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/`.

## 7. SAVINGS CLAUSE: EXCEPTIONS ARE NOT ALLOWED

- The assembly **cannot extend a term** by ordinary vote. Extending a term is
  a Class C rights-adjacent change (it touches equal power), needing 3/4 of
  all members plus CRB certification — and even then it may not touch the
  basic structure prohibition on permanent roles.

## 8. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

For three years, the same three "helpful" men ran the Finance Committee
"because they were already there". Under these rules, each served one year,
then a mandatory gap. The AGM's lot draw placed new members with a fresh
handover. One newcomer discovered the old committee had never given out
receipts. That discovery, made possible by rotation, exposed a decade-long
pattern of loose books. Rotation is not just fairness — it is the colony's
auditor.

---

*Rotation is the constitutional answer to the human habit of getting used to
power. The system does not rely on people being good; it relies on no one
holding power long enough to become bad.*
