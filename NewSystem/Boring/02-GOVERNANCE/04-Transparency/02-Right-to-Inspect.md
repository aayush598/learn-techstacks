# TRANSPARENCY 2 — RIGHT TO INSPECT

**Folder:** `02-GOVERNANCE/04-Transparency/`

---

## 1. THE RIGHT

Every member has an **absolute right** to inspect and copy any record of the
system at any time. This right is part of the basic structure
(`00-CONSTITUTION/00-MASTER-CONSTITUTION.md` Section 4) and cannot be
amended, waived, conditioned, or voted away (P6).

## 2. THE PROCEDURE

| Step | Rule |
|------|------|
| Request | Inform the record-keeper (any member) — in person, in writing, or via the notice box. No form is required |
| Timing | Access must be given **within 24 hours** for routine records, immediately at meetings, and within 7 days for records not yet finalised (e.g., pending minutes) |
| Location | At a public place in the colony (notice board area, common room) |
| Supervision | A record-keeper may sit with the member, but may not obstruct reading or copying |
| Copying | The member may copy (photograph) freely, or request a photocopy at cost price (photocopying charge only) |

## 3. WHAT MAY BE INSPECTED

- All minutes, ledgers, cash books, receipts, bank statements, logs, rosters,
  registers, CRB findings, vendor comparisons, and the amendment register —
  anything listed in `01-Open-Records.md` Section 2.
- Any record referenced in any decision the member is affected by.

## 4. LIMITS (MINIMAL AND NARROW)

The only records not open to all members, and the reasons:

| Withheld | Reason | Who may see it |
|----------|--------|----------------|
| Whistleblower identity | Protection from retaliation (P7) | Only the CRB, sealed |
| A member's private medical/health data | Privacy | The member and the CRB |

- Nothing else may be withheld. "The accounts are sensitive", "the minutes are
  not final", and "it is in the chairman's house" are **not** valid reasons.

## 5. CONSEQUENCES OF REFUSAL

| Refusal | Consequence |
|---------|-------------|
| One-time refusal without cause | CRB warning; mandatory disclosure within 24h |
| Repeated refusal | Transparency offence, escalated in the Enforcement Ladder |
| Destruction/hiding of a record | Serious offence; CRB review; the system's record is reconstructed from copies with the CRB's certification |
| Refusal combined with retaliation against the inspector | Treat as targeting (P7) — the most serious class of offence |

- A member whose inspection is refused may file a grievance to the CRB and may
  raise it at the next assembly; both routes are free of charge.

## 6. INSPECTION WITHOUT PENALTY

- No member may be punished, mocked, or questioned about why they want to see
  a record. "Why do you want to look?" is not a valid question; P10 protects
  the inspector.
- A member who inspects records frequently is doing exactly what the system
  wants. Discouraging inspection is itself a transparency violation.

## 7. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

A member suspected the diesel used for the generator was being overstated.
He walked to the finance file, photographed the receipts and the log for the
last three months, compared the litres against the run hours, and found the
generator running 40 minutes longer per day than the log said. The discrepancy
went to the CRB. No one could stop him from looking, because looking is his
constitutional right.

---

*Inspection is not an audit by professionals; it is the everyday habit of
every member. A system inspected daily needs no grand inquiry.*
