# OPERATOR HANDBOOK

**Folder:** `13-HANDBOOKS/`  
**Cross-reference:** `03-ROTATION-DUTY/`, `04-MOTOR-OPERATIONS/04-Monitoring-and-Logs/`

---

## 1. YOUR ROLE

You are the **current duty operator**. You do not own the motor — you serve the
collective for your shift. Your three jobs are: **operate** (start/stop on
schedule), **log** (record everything), and **check** (verify the system is
safe and sound). Follow this handbook exactly; it is your authority and your
protection.

## 2. BEFORE YOU START YOUR SHIFT

1. Check the roster for your slots (`12-TEMPLATES/02-Duty-Roster-Sheet.md`).
2. Take the key(s) from the previous operator. Note handover time in the duty
   log.
3. Inspect the motor room: panel closed, no burn smell, no water on the floor,
   no open wiring.
4. Note the current gauge reading and the panel indicator state.

## 3. STARTING THE MOTOR (SCHEDULED RUN)

1. Confirm the slot: date, time, and which group/zone the roster says.
2. Record in the Motor Run Log (`12-TEMPLATES/04-Motor-Run-Log.md`):
   - Scheduled start time
   - Actual start time
   - Gauge reading at start
3. Switch on. Watch for 2 minutes: does pressure build? does the pump sound
   normal?
4. If anything is abnormal, STOP the motor and go to Section 8.

## 4. STOPPING THE MOTOR

1. Stop at the scheduled time — not early, not late (early stopping shortchanges
   a household; late stopping is theft of another's slot).
2. Record actual stop time and gauge reading at stop.
3. Lock the motor room and confirm the key's next handover.

## 5. DURING YOUR WHOLE SHIFT

- The log is the truth. If you did not write it, it did not happen.
- Every single start must be logged — even unscheduled or event runs.
- Keep the motor room locked when you leave. Never leave the key hanging.

## 6. FAULT INDICATORS — THE PANEL

| Indicator | What it means | Action |
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| Overload / trip | Motor drawing too much current | Stop; do NOT restart repeatedly. Call repair coordinator. |
| Dry run / low level | Borewell level too low | Stop immediately; note it; inform coordinator (risk of pump damage). |
| Single-phase / voltage fault | Power supply problem | Stop; note; inform; do not restart until supply is stable. |
| Leakage / earth fault | Wiring or motor insulation fault | STOP, do not touch with wet hands, lock room, call coordinator. |
| No indicator, motor dead | No supply / blown fuse | Check main switch and fuse (only if trained); otherwise call for help. |

Never "force" the motor. Two restarts in a row without diagnosis is misuse of
common property.

## 7. SAFETY — YOUR LIFE AND OTHERS'

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| Dry hands, dry floor, dry shoes near the panel | Electricity |
| Never open the panel unless trained | Live terminals |
| Never put hands near the coupling while running | Rotating parts |
| Never let children into the motor room | Common sense, and the law |
| Never run the motor unattended for long periods | A fault while unattended can burn the pump |
| Report any exposed wire or wet panel immediately | It will kill someone eventually |

If you are unsure, you stop and call. Stopping safely is never a mistake.

## 8. ABNORMAL SITUATIONS — YOUR ACTION TABLE

| Situation | Immediate action | Then report to |
|-----------|------------------|----------------|
| Motor trips once | Wait, check panel, restart once if normal | Log it |
| Motor trips twice | Stop, do not restart | Repair coordinator (fault ticket) |
| Power cut during run | Note time; resume when power returns if slot remains; log both | Log |
| Borewell dry / low water | Stop; protect the pump | Coordinator + treasurer (shortage alert) |
| Burst / leaking pipe visible | Shut the relevant valve if you can do so safely; stop run | Coordinator immediately |
| Someone demands you cut a member's water | **Refuse.** Only a written Level 5 order can do this. Tell the CRB. | CRB — this is targeting |
| Someone pressures you to log falsely | Refuse; log the truth; report the pressure | CRB — the pressure is an offence |
| Someone threatens you | Stop work, lock room, leave safely | CRB immediately; this is retaliation/targeting |
| Fire / smoke in motor room | Cut power at the main switch, evacuate, call 101 and neighbours | Everyone |

## 9. HANDOVER

1. Clean and complete the log for your whole shift.
2. Brief the next operator: what ran, what was odd, what is pending.
3. Hand over the key; the next operator signs the handover line.
4. If no one arrives, do NOT pass the key to a stranger or leave it — call the
   backup list. You may hold the key until a proper handover.

## 10. YOUR PROTECTION

You cannot be punished for following this handbook. If following it upsets
someone, that person is the problem, not you. If you are ever punished for
logging the truth or refusing a false order, file a grievance immediately —
you are a whistleblower and are protected
(`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/03-Protection-Mechanisms/03-Whistleblower-Protection.md`).

Remember: you are one of several trained members (P3). If you are unavailable,
the backups cover you. The system never depends on you alone — and you never
have to shoulder it alone.
