## 1. YOUR ROLE

You are a member of the **Conflict Review Board (CRB)**, drawn by lot, not
chosen by anyone's favour. Your job is to decide disputes **on evidence and
rules** — never on popularity, friendship, anger, or pressure. You are the
mechanical heart of the promise: *the right member wins even alone.*

## 2. NEUTRALITY — YOUR FIRST DUTY

| You must | You must never |
|----------|----------------|
| Recuse if the parties are relatives, friends, or your own opponents | Decide on a case where you have a stake |
| Treat both sides as innocent until evidence says otherwise | Assume the complainant is right because they are upset |
| Apply the same standard to the popular and the isolated | Soften a finding because the offender is well-liked |
| Decide against the majority when the evidence says so | Hide behind "everyone feels that way" |

If you feel you cannot be neutral, say so **before** the hearing. Recusal is
honourable; hiding a conflict is an offence (Level 3+) and voids the decision.

## 3. EVIDENCE GATHERING — BEFORE THE HEARING

1. List what each side claims. For each claim, ask: *what evidence could prove
   or disprove this?* (logs, receipts, photos, timestamps, witnesses, meters —
   `04-Right-vs-Majority-Procedures/01-Fact-Establishment.md`).
2. Order preservation: logs, meters, pipes, and records are preserved the
   moment a case is filed.
3. Collect copies of the relevant documents from both sides.
4. Do **not** collect opinions. Collect facts. "She is always arguing" is not
   evidence; "the log shows her slot ran on 3 of 4 Sundays" is.
5. Note who can verify what. A member's own statement is evidence, but a
   receipt corroborates it.

## 4. THE HEARING — HOW TO CONDUCT IT

1. **Open:** state the issue, the burden, and the ground rules (no shouting, no
   attacks, no interruptions).
2. **Complainant:** full uninterrupted account, then evidence, then witnesses.
3. **Respondent:** same right, same time.
4. **Questions:** panel asks; each side asks through the chair. No direct
   cross-fire.
5. **Witnesses** are heard separately, one at a time, so no one is intimidated.
6. **Protect the vulnerable:** if one side fears the other, hear them
   separately or by phone. The victim of an alleged targeting is **never** made
   to face the whole group at once.
7. **Close:** each side gets two minutes on what they want decided and why.

If the hearing turns abusive, warn once, then continue without the abuser —
a member who will not obey the hearing cannot claim to be heard.

## 5. REASONED DECISIONS — THE MINIMUM CONTENT

Your written decision must contain:

1. **The issue** — what is being decided.
2. **The facts found** — and the evidence each finding rests on. Cite the log
   page, the receipt, the witness.
3. **The rules applied** — cited precisely, not "the rule about water".
4. **The remedy** — what is restored, paid, corrected, or penalised.
5. **The appeal right** — Step 4 (jury) and its 10-day deadline.

A decision without reasons is not a decision; it is an opinion with a
signature. Publish it in the register (P6).

## 6. TIMELINES — YOUR BINDING CLOCK

| Step | Time |
|------|------|
| Register grievance | 1 working day |
| Notice to other side | 2 working days |
| Status-quo / C&D order when targeting alleged | 24 hours |
| Hearing | Within 10 working days of registration |
| Written decision | Within 7 days of the hearing |
| Water restoration where targeting cut water | 24 hours |

Missing a timeline is a failure of the system, not a personal favour anyone
granted you. If you cannot meet it, escalate to the full CRB and find a
replacement — never quietly drop a case.

## 7. THE DECISION ITSELF — HOW THE PANEL DECIDES

- The panel deliberates alone, in confidence.
- **Facts:** by preponderance of credible evidence. Where the panel disagrees
  on a fact, it says so in the decision and resolves it by the evidence's
  weight, not by majority mood.
- **Rules:** applied as written. If a rule seems wrong or unfair, note it for
  the assembly to amend — do not bend it to make a popular outcome.
- **Rights:** if a fundamental right is engaged, the right wins. No outcome
  that targets a member, cuts water as punishment, or punishes a complainant is
  ever a valid decision.

## 8. PRESSURE — HOW TO SURVIVE IT

| Pressure | Your response |
|----------|---------------|
| A group visits you "to explain the facts" | Listen, but record it. Pressure on a panel member is an offence (Level 3+). |
| "Everyone in the colony wants X" | Irrelevant. The decision is evidence + rule. Write it. |
| "Your decision will make you enemies" | Write the decision. The published, reasoned record is your shield. |
| A party threatens you | Record the threat, report to the CRB clerk, proceed. You are protected; the threatener escalates themselves. |
| "We'll make sure you don't get re-drawn" | You are lot-drawn; there is no re-drawing. The threat is empty and is itself an offence. |

Deliberate alone, decide together, write everything. Pressure cannot survive
the light of a published, reasoned record.

## 9. APPEAL AWARENESS

- Every decision you make may be appealed to the Lot-Drawn Jury
  (`02-Resolution-Process/04-Step-4-Lot-Drawn-Jury.md`).
- An appeal is not an insult to you. It is the system checking itself.
- Cooperate fully: release the file, answer the jury's questions, never
  resent the appellant.
- If the jury reverses you, learn the reason, record it, and apply it next
  time. Being reversed is data, not defeat.

## 10. THE EASY SITUATIONS, HANDLED RIGHT

**The 10-members case:** ten members complain about one house's charges. You do
not count heads. You check the tariff rule and the register. One rule, one
answer.

**The lone member's veto:** one member raises a Truth Veto against a crowd. You
freeze it, review the evidence, decide on the evidence. If the lone member is
right, you say so — loudly, in writing.

**The pressure campaign:** a party mobilises "supporters" to influence you.
You record it, proceed, and note it in the decision.

## 11. YOUR REMINDER

You are not a judge of who is liked. You are the guardian of one promise: in
this colony, no one — powerful, popular, or alone — is decided by numbers.
When you are on this board, you are the reason that promise holds.
