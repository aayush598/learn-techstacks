# REPAIR COORDINATOR HANDBOOK

**Folder:** `13-HANDBOOKS/`  
**Cross-reference:** `05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/`, `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/01-Conflict-Types/05-Operational-Conflicts.md`

## 1. YOUR ROLE

You are the **repair coordinator** (rotating role, P3). Faults will come to you
from operators, members, and inspections. Your job is to run every fault
through one orderly, documented flow so that nothing is forgotten, no one is
favoured, and no money vanishes. The flow below is the whole job.

## 2. THE REPAIR FLOW — 10 STEPS

| Step | What you do |
|------|-------------|
| 1. **Receive fault** | Take the report (in person, phone, log entry, grievance). Record who reported, what, when. |
| 2. **Number it** | Open a fault ticket: **F-YYYY-NNN**. Log it in the repair register. |
| 3. **Inform** | Tell the affected members and the treasurer (cost implications) within 24 hours. |
| 4. **Diagnose** | Inspect (or have the trained member inspect) and record the suspected cause. If a member caused it deliberately, note the evidence — do not accuse. |
| 5. **Quotes** | Get at least **2 written quotes** for any job above the small-repair ceiling. Keep both. |
| 6. **Approve** | Below ceiling: proceed. Above ceiling: approval per `05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/` (assembly or CRB) in writing, with the quote attached. |
| 7. **Supervise** | Oversee the work. The vendor works on the written quote, nothing extra without a written addendum. |
| 8. **Verify** | Test the repair works before paying. A paid-but-still-broken repair is a failed step. |
| 9. **Pay & log** | Pay only against the voucher + bill + verification (treasurer does the money; you certify the work). Log the ticket complete. |
| 10. **Rate & inventory** | Rate the vendor in the vendor register. Update parts inventory (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/05-Parts-and-Inventory/`). |

## 3. THE QUEUE — WHO GOES FIRST

The repair queue is **first-in, first-out, adjusted only by severity**:

| Severity | Example | Priority |
|----------|---------|----------|
| Critical | Motor dead, line burst, unsafe wiring | Immediate (stop-the-world) |
| High | Meter leaking, pressure low | Within 48 hours |
| Normal | Slow drip, cosmetic | Within 7 days |

Never move a member up or down the queue as favour or punishment. If a member
thinks their repair is being delayed to hurt them, that is a grievance —
support it; do not be defensive. Delayed repairs as leverage is targeting.

## 4. THE COST RULES

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Common line / common asset | Cost from the common fund, per the asset register |
| Private line / private meter | Cost to the member, per the asset register |
| Disputed ownership | Do not guess — refer to the CRB with the asset register and site photo (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/01-Conflict-Types/04-Financial-Conflicts.md`) |
| Emergency after hours | You may authorise only the minimum to stop damage; full paperwork next morning |

*Example:* Two members fight over a burst pipe's ₹8,000 cost. You do not
decide. You preserve the site, photograph it, pull the asset register, and hand
both to the CRB. Your neutrality is what keeps your role trustable.

## 5. VENDORS

- Use the vendor register; prefer rated vendors.
- Never take a commission, gift, or "discount for you personally" from a
  vendor. It is a bribe, even at ₹200.
- If a vendor is slow, overpriced, or shoddy, rate them down and say so in the
  register. Ratings are public (P6).
- A member's cousin who is "good with motors" is fine **if** the quoted
  quality and price beat the alternatives and the rules are followed. Favour is
  never the reason.

## 6. PARTS AND INVENTORY

1. Record every part bought and used: date, ticket, part, cost, vendor.
2. Keep slow-moving parts (fuses, couplings, starters) in the store; keep the
   store list updated.
3. A part that disappears without a ticket is a financial issue — report to
   the treasurer and CRB; never "adjust" it yourself.

## 7. THE ABNORMAL SITUATIONS

| Situation | Your action |
|-----------|-------------|
| Two members both claim their repair is urgent | Apply severity rules; if it is a tie, first-in wins; log the tie honestly |
| A member demands to know why their repair waits | Show the queue — it is public. Reasons, not excuses |
| A powerful member pressures you to jump the queue | Refuse, log the pressure, report to CRB — that is interference |
| A member threatens you over a repair | Stop work safely, report to CRB immediately (targeting/retaliation) |
| A vendor inflates a bill | Withhold payment pending verification; log the vendor |
| A repair keeps failing | Do not keep paying the same vendor; get a second opinion; check the diagnosis |
| A member is hurt during a repair | Emergency first, then full incident report to the CRB |

## 8. YOUR RECORDS

Keep the **repair register**: ticket number, date, fault, diagnosis, quotes,
approval reference, vendor, cost, completion date, verification. This register
answers most operational conflicts before they become conflicts
(`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/01-Conflict-Types/05-Operational-Conflicts.md`).

## 9. YOUR PROTECTION

You are protected for doing this job honestly: refusing a bribe, refusing to
jump a queue, reporting a false bill. If you are pressured or punished for
honesty, you are a whistleblower and the full protection applies
(`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/03-Protection-Mechanisms/03-Whistleblower-Protection.md`).

## 10. REMEMBER

Repairs are where the colony's money meets members' daily lives — the exact
place the old system favoured friends and punished critics. Your discipline is
what proves this system is different: every ticket, every quote, every rupee
on the record, everyone treated by the same queue.
