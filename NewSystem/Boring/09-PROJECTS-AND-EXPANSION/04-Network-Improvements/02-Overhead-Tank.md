# NETWORK IMPROVEMENTS — OVERHEAD TANK

**Folder:** `09-PROJECTS-AND-EXPANSION/04-Network-Improvements/`

---

## 1. WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

Without a tank, the first to open the tap drains the line and everyone after
fights for the tail. A tank stores the pump's output and lets every house fill
on equal terms — but only if who-fills-when is governed. An ungoverned tank
just moves the first-tap advantage to the first-bucket advantage.

## 2. PURPOSE OF THE TANK

1. A tank smooths pressure across the network (it is the standard remedy for
   the low-pressure houses in `01-Tap-Height-Compensation.md`).
2. It stores the pump's run, so the motor runs fewer, longer cycles instead of
   many short ones.
3. It eliminates the "first-tap advantage": the water in the tank is common,
   and every house takes its share from a level surface (P1).

## 3. EVERYONE FILLS FROM THE TANK — FAIRLY

1. All member connections draw from the tank on the **published schedule**
   (`04-MOTOR-OPERATIONS/01-Daily-Schedule/`).
2. When demand exceeds one fill cycle, slots are assigned by **rotation or by
   lot**, following the duty-roster principles
   (`03-ROTATION-DUTY/01-Duty-Roster/`). A member's position, wealth, or
   friendship does not decide their place in the order.
3. The schedule is on the notice board and in the members' group; a change
   requires assembly notice, not a tap-owner's decree.

## 4. TANK CLEANING SCHEDULE

| Task | Frequency |
|------|-----------|
| Drain, scrub, and disinfect | At least twice a year — before summer and before monsoon |
| Inlet/outlet valve inspection | With each cleaning |
| Overflow and vent guard check | Quarterly |
| Cleaning record (date, who, photos) | Filed with maintenance records |

Cleaning dates are published at least 15 days ahead so members can store water
and no one is surprised. The cleaning log is public (P6).

## 5. LEVEL GAUGE READ PUBLICLY

1. A level gauge or dipstick is fitted; the reading is taken at **fixed times**
   (e.g., 6 am and 6 pm) by the duty member and posted daily to the members'
   group.
2. An abnormal drop — level falling far faster than the schedule predicts — is
   investigated within 24 hours: leak, overdraw, or motor issue
   (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/02-Breakdown-Repair/`).
3. The level log is a record of truth; it shows the system's actual behaviour
   instead of complaints about it.

## 6. UNAUTHORISED USE OF THE TANK

1. Filling private drums or roof tanks for sale, or drawing beyond one's slot,
   is unauthorised use under the Water Usage Agreement
   (`11-LEGAL-AND-COMPLIANCE/01-Binding-Agreement/02-Water-Usage-Agreement.md`).
2. The level log is the evidence; the Enforcement Ladder
   (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/05-Enforcement/`) applies.

## 7. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

The tank fills to the brim by 6 am. The level log shows a daily drop of three
feet by noon — far beyond the schedule. The duty investigation finds house A
filling drums all day to sell water. The log and photos are presented; the
unauthorised use is penalised, and the tank's level returns to the curve the
schedule predicts.

## 8. TANK AS A PROJECT

- The tank itself is a Class C project: needs assessment, cost estimate,
  funding from the Reserve Fund, 3+ quotes, written contract, close-out, and
  entry in the Asset Register
  (`09-PROJECTS-AND-EXPANSION/00-Project-Overview.md`).

---

*The tank is the great equaliser of pressure — provided the level is read, the
schedule is obeyed, and no one fills before their turn.*
