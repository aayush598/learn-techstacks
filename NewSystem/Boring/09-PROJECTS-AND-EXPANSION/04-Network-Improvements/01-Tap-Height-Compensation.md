# NETWORK IMPROVEMENTS — TAP HEIGHT COMPENSATION

**Folder:** `09-PROJECTS-AND-EXPANSION/04-Network-Improvements/`

---

## 1. WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

Water runs downhill and loses pressure with distance. In the old system the
house on the hill drank first and drank the most — its taps ran strong while
the low-end houses waited, and the "tall house gets everything" was treated as
a law of physics rather than an injustice. This file turns geometry into a
matter of fairness (P1, P7).

## 2. THE FAIR-SURVEY

1. A survey maps every connection: elevation relative to the boring or tank,
   distance from the main line, pipe size, and observed flow.
2. The survey is conducted by **two or more members drawn by lot** plus the
   current duty/technical member, using a bucket-and-stopwatch or a pressure
   gauge — not by asking who "feels" low.
3. The result is a **published table**: for every house, its fill time for a
   fixed container or its measured pressure, ranked from best to worst.

## 3. COMPENSATION OPTIONS

For every connection whose documented flow is below the system's fairness
standard, the assembly chooses one or more remedies:

| Option | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Longer water slot** | Extended fill time in the schedule for the affected house(s) |
| **Booster pump** | Installed on the common line at system cost, or approved on the member's private line |
| **Network fix** | Bigger main, relocated tap, shortened run, or an intermediate line |
| **Credit** | A documented credit against consumption charges if meters exist |

The choice is made on the evidence of the survey, by assembly resolution, and
is **not** a private favour — the affected houses are entitled, not begging.

## 4. RESURVEY WHEN THE NETWORK CHANGES

1. Any change to the network — new boring, overhead tank, bigger pipes, new
   connections, relocated main — triggers a **re-survey within 60 days**.
2. Compensation is re-assigned on the new measurements. A house that was low
   may improve; a house that was fine may become the new low house. Both
   outcomes come from the tape measure, not from the assembly's mood.

## 5. DISPUTES BY EVIDENCE (P5)

1. A member who disputes their ranking brings **measurements**: fill-time for a
   fixed bucket, gauge readings, photographs, timed slot records.
2. A claim of "my water is weak" without a measurement is not a dispute; the
   duty team measures on request within 7 days.
3. Unresolved disputes follow `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/02-Resolution-Process/`;
   the evidence, not seniority or loudness, decides.

## 6. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

The house at the hill end fills a bucket in 3 minutes; the house at the low end
takes 40 minutes for the same bucket. The survey documents both. The assembly
gives the low house a longer slot and, after the tank is built, a booster. The
hill house is not punished for its position — it simply loses the unearned
advantage it had from being tall.

## 7. RELATION TO OTHER RULES

- If the survey shows the whole network is weak, the remedy is a network
  project (`02-Overhead-Tank.md`, `03-Pipe-Replacement.md`), not patch
  compensation.
- The survey feeds the motor-sizing head calculation
  (`02-New-Motor-Installation/01-Motor-Sizing.md`).

---

*Elevation decides where water is easy; it must not decide who gets it. The
survey is the level on which the system stands.*
