# INFRASTRUCTURE ASSET REGISTER — ASSET VALUATION

**Folder:** `09-PROJECTS-AND-EXPANSION/05-Infrastructure-Asset-Register/`

---

## 1. WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

"How much is the boring worth?" The answer changes with the question being
asked: insurance, refunds, and buy-in each need a different number. This file
fixes which number is used for which purpose — and forbids the "it is worth a
crore because it is mine" style of valuation that ruled the old system.

## 2. THE THREE VALUES

| Value | Meaning | Example (motor ₹18,000, 3 years old) |
|-------|---------|----------------------------------------|
| **Purchase price** | Historic cost at acquisition | ₹18,000 |
| **Replacement cost** | What it costs to replace with an equivalent today | ₹14,000 |
| **Depreciated value** | Replacement cost minus wear per the depreciation schedule (`03-Depreciation-and-Reserve.md`) | ₹9,000 |

All three columns are maintained in the Asset Register and published in the
annual asset statement.

## 3. WHEN EACH VALUE IS USED

| Purpose | Value used | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| Insurance cover | **Replacement cost** | Insurance must restore the asset, not merely refund its age |
| Buy-in formula | **Depreciated replacement cost** of shared assets | A late joiner pays for the network's current value, not its age (`03-Late-Joiner-Buy-in/01-Backdated-Contribution.md`) |
| Refund on exit | **The member's recorded paid share**, not asset value | Refunds return money contributed; the boring is not for resale (`01-MEMBERSHIP/03-Exit-and-Transfer/03-Refund-Procedure.md`) |
| Reserve target | **Replacement cost** of motor + boring share | The reserve must be able to rebuild the system (`03-Depreciation-and-Reserve.md`) |

## 4. VALUATION RULES

1. Figures are produced by the Finance Committee together with the Asset
   Register Keeper and published.
2. Replacement cost is based on 2-3 market quotes or the maker's list price,
   filed and dated.
3. Depreciation follows the adopted schedule
   (`03-Depreciation-and-Reserve.md`), applied mechanically.
4. No member's opinion replaces a recorded number. A member who believes a
   value is wrong raises it with evidence (a quote, a dated invoice) through
   `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/02-Resolution-Process/`.

## 5. THE BORING IS NOT FOR SALE

1. The boring is common property (P1). Its valuation is used for insurance,
   buy-in, and reserve planning — never as a price at which a member can buy
   it, sell it, or claim a "share."
2. An exiting member gets their money back per the refund rules; they do not
   take a chunk of the boring, and they do not sell their "connection" to the
   highest bidder outside the transfer rules (`01-MEMBERSHIP/03-Exit-and-Transfer/`).

## 6. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

A member leaving the colony claims "my share of the boring is worth ₹50,000."
The register shows his paid contributions were ₹15,000, and the refund rules
return his ₹15,000 (less any dues). The valuation of the boring — used for the
insurance and the next buy-in — is untouched by his claim, because the system
is not a partnership selling its equipment; it is a collective whose money
returns are defined by records.

---

*Valuation serves the system's needs: to insure, to reserve, and to buy-in
fairly. It never serves as a price tag for common property.*
