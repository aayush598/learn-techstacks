# INFRASTRUCTURE ASSET REGISTER — DEPRECIATION AND RESERVE

**Folder:** `09-PROJECTS-AND-EXPANSION/05-Infrastructure-Asset-Register/`

---

## 1. WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

Assets wear out. A motor dies, a tank corrodes, a meter goes bad. In the old
system the day of failure meant a sudden, large collection from every house —
which the weak could not pay, and which the strong turned into leverage. This
file converts sudden bills into planned savings: every year the system sets
aside a little, so the day of replacement is already paid for.

## 2. ANNUAL DEPRECIATION

1. Each depreciable asset is assigned a **useful life** in the Asset Register:

| Asset | Typical life (example) |
|-------|------------------------|
| Motor and pump | 7-10 years |
| Control panel / starter | 10 years |
| Cables | 8-12 years |
| Overhead tank | 20-25 years |
| Distribution pipes | 15-25 years |
| Meters | 10 years |

2. Annual depreciation = **replacement cost / useful life**, computed from the
   valuation rules (`02-Asset-Valuation.md`).
3. The amount is transferred from the Common Fund to the **Reserve Fund** each
   year as a budget line, until the asset's replacement cost is fully covered.

## 3. THE RESERVE TARGET

1. The **Reserve target** is the sum of: the replacement cost of the motor set
   plus the boring's shared renewal share, per the assembly-adopted plan.
2. The target is published; progress toward it is reported in the bimonthly
   statement (`06-FINANCE/03-Bookkeeping/04-Bimonthly-Statement.md`).
3. Once the target is reached, depreciation still accrues for the next
   replacement cycle — the reserve keeps pace with ageing.

## 4. PROTECTION AGAINST SUDDEN COLLECTIONS

| Situation | Rule |
|-----------|------|
| Replacement within the reserve target | Paid from the Reserve Fund; no special levy |
| Emergency beyond the target | Crisis call per `06-FINANCE/02-Fund-Management/05-Fund-Raise-Crisis-Calls.md`, with installments for hardship |
| Insurance payout received | Credited to the Reserve Fund before any new call |

No member is ever hit with a full replacement cost on the day the motor dies —
that is the entire purpose of this file (P4, P7).

## 5. DEPRECIATION IS REAL, NOT BOOKKEEPING THEORY

- A Reserve Fund that exists only on paper protects no one. The Reserve Fund
  rules in `06-FINANCE/02-Fund-Management/02-Reserve-Fund.md` require the
  money to be held and audited — not "remembered" and not lent to members.

## 6. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

The motor costs ₹18,000 with a 9-year life; the system saves ₹2,000 per year.
In year eight the motor burns out. The Reserve Fund already holds ₹16,000 plus
the insurance payout; the replacement is ordered the same week, and no member
receives a surprise bill. The family that could never pay a sudden ₹18,000
never has to.

## 7. RELATION TO OTHER RULES

- The reserve is the first source for new projects
  (`01-New-Boring-Installation/02-Funding-and-Shares.md`).
- A late joiner's buy-in includes a reserve share
  (`03-Late-Joiner-Buy-in/01-Backdated-Contribution.md`).

---

*The system saves a little every year so that no member is crushed by a big
bill on a bad day. Depreciation is honesty about wear; the reserve is the
saving that honesty makes.*
