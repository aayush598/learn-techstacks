# NEW BORING INSTALLATION — WATER TESTING AND APPROVAL

**Folder:** `09-PROJECTS-AND-EXPANSION/01-New-Boring-Installation/`

---

## 1. WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

A borewell that yields brackish, iron-laden, or contaminated water is a
permanent and costly mistake. In the old system the contractor said "water is
fine," the pump went in, and the colony discovered the truth a year later.
Acceptance here is decided by three tests, not by the contractor's word.

## 2. LAB WATER TEST — QUALITY

1. A water sample is drawn by **two members together** (never by the contractor
   alone) into a clean, sealed bottle, and sent to an accredited laboratory.
2. Parameters tested include: TDS, pH, hardness, iron, fluoride, nitrate,
   chloride, and bacteriological (coliform) count.
3. Results are compared against BIS drinking-water standards and any state norms.
4. The laboratory report is **published to all members** — including when the
   water fails. A failed report is not hidden; it becomes a treatment or
   re-drill decision.

## 3. YIELD TEST — PUMPING TEST

1. A constant-discharge pumping test runs for the period set in the contract
   (typically 6-8 hours), recording static level, dynamic level, discharge, and
   recovery after the pump stops.
2. The minimum acceptable yield is stated in the contract before drilling; the
   test result is compared against it.
3. The pumping test log is signed by the oversight members and filed.

## 4. DEPTH AND CASING RECORDS

1. The driller must produce: total depth, casing diameter and material, depth
   of the screen, and the depth of the water-bearing zone.
2. The oversight members verify the depth log against the drilling day-record
   and, where possible, the casing pipe delivered.
3. Casing depth protects the bore from surface contamination and collapse;
   a boring without verified casing records is not accepted.

## 5. ACCEPTANCE ONLY AFTER PASSING

| Test | Pass condition | If it fails |
|------|----------------|-------------|
| Water quality | Meets the chosen drinking standard (or an agreed, documented lower standard for non-drinking use) | Treatment estimate, or reject |
| Yield | Meets the contract's minimum over the full test period | Contractor remedy under contract |
| Depth/casing records | Complete and verified | Withhold final payment until produced |

1. The boring is **accepted** only when all three pass. Acceptance is a written
   certificate signed by the two oversight members and one witness, dated, and
   filed in the project file.
2. If tests fail, the contractor's warranty and the payment milestones govern:
   remedy, re-drill terms, or withheld final payment
   (`01-New-Boring-Installation/03-Contractor-Selection.md`).

## 6. THE ACCEPTANCE RECORD

- The acceptance certificate, lab report, pumping test log, and depth/casing
  records are copied into the Infrastructure Asset Register
  (`09-PROJECTS-AND-EXPANSION/05-Infrastructure-Asset-Register/01-Asset-List.md`).
- Copies are kept in the records system (`11-LEGAL-AND-COMPLIANCE/03-Records-Retention/`).

## 7. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

The contractor says "the water is sweet, install the pump." The lab report
shows iron at three times the standard and high TDS. The system does not
accept. It obtains a treatment and filtration estimate, and renegotiates the
contract for a treatment package — because the evidence, not the contractor's
assurance, decided the outcome.

## 8. REJECTION AND RIGHTS

- If the boring is rejected, the project file keeps the test records; the
  assembly decides the next step (re-drill, another site, treatment) on the
  evidence and the contract terms.
- A member who believes acceptance or rejection was decided wrongly may
  challenge it with the test records through
  `07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/02-Resolution-Process/`.

---

*No boring enters the system's life on a promise. It enters with a lab report,
a pumping log, and a depth record — all public.*
