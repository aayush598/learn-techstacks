# LATE JOINER BUY-IN — BACKDATED CONTRIBUTION

**Folder:** `09-PROJECTS-AND-EXPANSION/03-Late-Joiner-Buy-in/`

---

## 1. WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

Ten houses pay ₹1,20,000 to drill a boring; twenty more houses later ask for
water "for free," because the boring already exists. In the old system the late
joiners either got free water (robbing those who paid) or paid whatever the
founders demanded (becoming servants). This file replaces both with a published,
fair formula.

## 2. THE PRINCIPLE

A late joiner pays a **fair share of the past capital costs** plus a **share of
the reserve**. This is not a fine, a penalty, or a price for friendship. It is
repayment of the newcomer's share of the common investment that others already
made — the same amount the early members effectively paid, adjusted honestly.

## 3. THE FORMULA — ONE, PUBLISHED, NOT RE-INVENTED

The assembly adopts **one formula** and publishes the arithmetic. It is applied
identically to every late joiner. Two acceptable versions:

```
Simple (capital basis):
  Buy-in = ( Total original project cost + major renewals since )
           / ( Number of member connections at the time of the original funding )

Indexed (current-value basis):
  Buy-in = ( Depreciated replacement cost of boring + network + installations )
           / ( Current number of member connections )
           + ( Reserve Fund target / current number of member connections )
```

- The **simple** version is easy to check: it makes the late joiner's rupee
  equal to the early member's rupee.
- The **indexed** version reflects today's replacement cost and a reserve share,
  and is preferred once assets have been renewed.
- The arithmetic of the adopted version is written out on the notice board with
  every new buy-in.

## 4. A TRANSPARENT STATEMENT FOR EVERY JOINER

1. Each late joiner receives a written statement showing: the formula, the
   figures used, the computation, and the amount due.
2. The statement is signed by the Finance Committee, copied to the member and
   to the records (`11-LEGAL-AND-COMPLIANCE/03-Records-Retention/`), and is
   open to every member (P6).
3. The member may ask any other member to check the arithmetic. A buy-in figure
   that no member can reproduce from the published formula is void.

## 5. NO FREE ENTRY — AND NO FAVOURS

| Situation | Rule |
|-----------|------|
| A relative of a founder | Pays the same formula. Blood does not discount water. |
| A poor widow in hardship | Goes through the published hardship path (`02-Installment-Options.md`) — not a silent zero. |
| A rich late joiner | Pays the same formula, on the same terms. Wealth buys no speed. |
| A family that helped with labour | Time served is credited per the work-compensation rules (`03-ROTATION-DUTY/03-Duty-Compensation/`), not by a wink. |

## 6. WHERE THE BUY-IN GOES

1. The buy-in is credited between the **Reserve Fund** and the **asset-fund /
   project account** as the assembly decides when adopting the formula.
2. It is recorded in the Share Ledger
   (`01-New-Boring-Installation/02-Funding-and-Shares.md`) and counts toward
   the member's refund on exit (`01-MEMBERSHIP/03-Exit-and-Transfer/03-Refund-Procedure.md`).

## 7. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

Twelve houses built the boring and first motor; the effective cost per house
was ₹10,000. Eight new houses join. Under the adopted simple formula each pays
the ₹10,000-equivalent share (indexed for renewals since), plus a reserve
share. The statement shows the figures. No newcomer enters free; no founder is
robbed; and when the new members later leave, their paid buy-in follows the
refund rules.

## 8. WHEN THE FORMULA IS REVISED

- The formula is revised only by a **two-thirds (2/3) assembly vote**, with the
  new arithmetic published 15 days before the vote
  (`02-GOVERNANCE/03-Decision-Making/03-Supermajority-Rules.md`).
- A revision applies to joiners after its adoption; it never re-charges members
  already settled.

*Buy-in is the bridge between those who paid for yesterday's boring and those
who will drink from it tomorrow. The bridge has a published span — never a
tollman's whim.*
