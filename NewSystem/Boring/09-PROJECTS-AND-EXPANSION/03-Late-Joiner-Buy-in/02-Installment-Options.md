# LATE JOINER BUY-IN — INSTALLMENT OPTIONS

**Folder:** `09-PROJECTS-AND-EXPANSION/03-Late-Joiner-Buy-in/`

---

## 1. WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

A daily-wage family cannot hand over a full buy-in in one month — yet the
system must not let the well-off avoid paying. Installments are the middle
path: the buy-in is fully payable, but over time, on published terms. This is
how the system protects the weak (P7) without creating a free class (P4).

## 2. THE INSTALLMENT PLANS

1. **Default plan:** the buy-in is paid in 3-6 monthly installments, chosen by
   the member from the published schedule.
2. **Extended plan:** for documented hardship (job loss, illness, disaster),
   the assembly may extend the period — for example to 12 months — by a simple
   majority, recorded with the reason.
3. The first installment is due **before connection**
   (`03-Access-Only-After-Buy-in.md`); the remainder on the agreed monthly
   dates.
4. Payment is by bank transfer, UPI, or cheque, or against a numbered receipt
   (`06-FINANCE/01-Contributions/01-Monthly-Contribution.md`). No cash to any
   person.

## 3. RIGHTS WHILE OWING — WATER, DUTY, VOICE

While the buy-in is unpaid, the member holds:

| Right | Full | Restricted |
|-------|------|------------|
| Water | Full share (P1, P7) | — |
| Duty roster | Full duties (P4) | — |
| Voice | Speak, object, ask questions | — |
| Vote on water schedules, maintenance, daily matters | Full | — |
| Vote on financial matters | | Restricted (see Section 4) |
| Refund entitlement | Buy-in paid counts toward refund | — |

The member is not a second-class citizen. The restriction is narrow and
temporary.

## 4. VOTING RESTRICTED ON FINANCE MATTERS ONLY

1. While buy-in is outstanding, the member **may not vote** on: budgets,
   spending, new projects, fund-raise calls, or the buy-in formula itself.
2. On every other matter the member votes in full.
3. The restriction ends the day the buy-in is settled. It is recorded in the
   minutes so it can never be applied to non-finance votes by mistake.

## 5. INTEREST — DEFAULT IS INTEREST-FREE

1. **Default:** the plan is interest-free, so that cost does not bury a poor
   family (P7).
2. The assembly reviews this annually and may adopt a small interest rate by a
   two-thirds vote; a decision to charge interest must be published with the
   formula (`01-Backdated-Contribution.md`).
3. Whether interest-free or not, **identical terms apply to every member in the
   same situation**. A discount for one member is void.

## 6. BUY-IN COUNTS AGAINST REFUNDS

1. Every paid installment is recorded in the Share Ledger
   (`01-New-Boring-Installation/02-Funding-and-Shares.md`).
2. On exit, the member's paid buy-in counts in their refund exactly as early
   members' capital does (`01-MEMBERSHIP/03-Exit-and-Transfer/03-Refund-Procedure.md`).
3. The unpaid balance at exit is recovered per the exit rules — it does not
   vanish with the member.

## 7. DEFAULT AND RECOVERY

1. If an installment is missed, the member is notified in writing within 7 days
   with a reminder of the remaining schedule.
2. The system does **not** cut a member's water while they are paying part of
   the schedule (P7), but a total abandonment of the plan is handled under the
   enforcement rules (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/05-Enforcement/`): the plan ends,
   the member may lose the connection until the balance is secured, and the
   paid amount is accounted for.
3. Every recovery action is recorded and public — no quiet coercion.

## 8. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

A family joins with a buy-in of ₹12,000. They choose 4 installments of ₹3,000.
They are connected after the first ₹3,000, get full water and duties, and vote
fully on schedules. At the budget meeting they may not vote on the new project
fund — that is the finance restriction. Two installments later their father
loses his job; they ask the assembly for the 12-month extended plan, which is
granted with the reason recorded. They are protected, not penalised, and the
system is not cheated.

---

*Installments are a bridge, not a gift. The full buy-in is owed by every
joiner; the bridge only decides the pace of payment.*
