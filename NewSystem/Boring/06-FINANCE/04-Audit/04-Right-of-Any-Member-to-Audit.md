# RIGHT OF ANY MEMBER TO AUDIT

**Folder:** `06-FINANCE/04-Audit/`

---

## 1. THE CONSTITUTIONAL RIGHT

Every member has an **absolute, personal right to inspect the books of the
system at any time** — not through a committee, not with permission, not at a
convenient time chosen by the Finance Committee. This right comes directly
from the Constitution:

> "Every member has the absolute right to see, copy, and verify every record
> of the system: money, duty, motor logs, schedules, minutes, vendor quotes,
> contracts, government documents, and this constitution itself."
> — `00-CONSTITUTION/02-Fundamental-Rights/02-Right-to-Information-and-Audit.md`

## 2. WHAT THE MEMBER MAY DO

| Action | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Inspect** | Open the register box, read the ledger, vouchers, receipts, bank statements, and minutes at any working hour |
| **Copy** | Photograph or photocopy any page; take the copies home |
| **Verify** | Cross-check ledger vs bank vs vouchers vs receipts themselves |
| **Question** | Ask the Finance Committee for an entry-by-entry explanation in writing |
| **Escalate** | Raise a formal review of any discrepancy found |

No appointment, no request form, no committee approval, and no majority vote
is required. Denial of access is itself a Level 2 offence and a red flag.

## 3. HOW THE MEMBER AUDITS

A member performing a self-audit follows a simple discipline:

1. Tell the Finance Committee in one line, in the ledger margin or in the
   group: "Member X is auditing, date." (A note, not a request.)
2. Work through the books using the same four-way check as the regular audit
   (`01-Regular-Audit.md`): ledger vs bank, ledger vs vouchers, ledger vs
   receipts, books vs minutes.
3. Note any discrepancy on a dated sheet: what was expected, what was found,
   the amount, the entry number.
4. Hand the sheet to the Finance Committee and to the Conflict Review Board
   or the next assembly.

## 4. WHAT HAPPENS TO A DISCREPANCY

1. The Finance Committee responds in writing within **7 days**: proof that
   the record is correct, or a corrected entry by the strike-through rule.
2. If the member is not satisfied, the discrepancy becomes a **formal
   review**: it goes to the Conflict Resolution process
   (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/02-Resolution-Process/`) and, if it involves
   possible misuse, directly to `05-Misuse-Consequences.md`.
3. A confirmed discrepancy is corrected publicly; the correction and its
   cause are recorded in the ledger and the next statement.
4. A member who is found right in a formal review is entitled to a written
   acknowledgement in the minutes — the system thanks its watchdogs rather
   than resenting them.

## 5. PROTECTION OF THE AUDITING MEMBER

1. A member who audits, questions, or finds a problem is **protected from
   retaliation**: no fine, no duty punishment, no water restriction, no
   verbal targeting (P5, P7, P10).
2. A committee that punishes a member for auditing commits a Level 4 offence.
3. Audit findings are facts, and "No Voting on Proven Facts" applies
   (`00-CONSTITUTION/04-Truth-Supremacy/04-No-Voting-on-Proven-Facts.md`): a
   majority cannot vote a discrepancy away.

## 6. THE LIMITS OF THE RIGHT

The right to audit is absolute for **records**. It does not include:

- carrying books home overnight (the member may photograph/copy instead);
- altering the books (only the Finance Committee may correct, and only by the
  strike-through rule);
- disrupting a required use of the box (the member waits minutes, not days).

These limits protect the record; they never require permission or
justification.

## 7. THE RIGHT IS NON-NEGOTIABLE

The right to information is part of the basic structure of the Constitution
and can never be restricted by amendment, emergency, or "committee secrecy"
(`00-CONSTITUTION/02-Fundamental-Rights/02-Right-to-Information-and-Audit.md`,
Section 6).
