# SURPRISE AUDIT

**Folder:** `06-FINANCE/04-Audit/`

---

## 1. WHY SURPRISE

A yearly audit catches what is visible at year end. But the old system's
worst abuse was **slow theft** — a little from here, a little from there,
bills rounded up, receipts "misplaced", a sip from every cup. A surprise
audit exists to catch the drip that a scheduled audit never sees coming.

## 2. HOW OFTEN

1. The system holds **1-2 unannounced audits per year**, in addition to the
   annual regular audit (`01-Regular-Audit.md`).
2. Their dates are a secret held by the audit authority (the Conflict Review
   Board, or the assembly-designated audit convenor). No committee, no
   signatory, and no bookkeeper is told in advance.
3. At least one surprise audit falls **between** two regular audits.

## 3. HOW THE AUDITOR IS DRAWN BY LOT

1. At an assembly meeting (without prior notice of timing), slips naming
   eligible members are placed in a box. Eligible means: not on the Finance
   Committee, not a signatory, not a bookkeeper this year, and not auditing
   the regular audit.
2. Two slips are drawn: the **lead auditor** and the **witness**. Both names
   are recorded in sealed minutes, and the auditor is instructed not to
   reveal anything until the day.
3. The chosen members may decline once per year; a member who declines twice
   in a row must serve.

## 4. WHAT THE SURPRISE AUDIT DOES

On the day, without warning, the auditor:

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Appears at the register box and takes the ledger, receipt book, vouchers, and bank statement file |
| 2 | **Counts cash** — there must be **none** in any member's or office's possession; any cash found is immediately suspicious |
| 3 | Reconciles the ledger against the **latest bank statement** row by row |
| 4 | Checks the **receipt book** for gaps, unsigned stubs, and undated entries |
| 5 | Spot-checks vouchers against ledger rows and attachments |
| 6 | Verifies the three sub-account balances against the budget positions |
| 7 | Records findings on a dated, numbered sheet and files it |

## 5. THE STANDARD

A surprise audit applies the same four-way check as the regular audit
(`01-Regular-Audit.md`) but on the current, unrehearsed state of the books.
A book that is clean on audit day is clean because it is kept clean — not
because it was cleaned.

## 6. FINDINGS AND AFTERMATH

1. Findings are presented at the next assembly, whatever they are.
2. Minor errors are corrected by the strike-through rule with initials.
3. Anything that suggests **slowly-building misuse** — small repeated
   rounding, missing receipt numbers, late entries that "always get fixed" —
   is escalated to `05-Misuse-Consequences.md` and the Conflict Resolution
   process.
4. If a surprise audit finds the books clean, that fact is recorded as an
   assurance, and no one is treated as guilty for having been audited.

## 7. THE VALUE OF THE ELEMENT OF SURPRISE

1. Because the date is unknowable, the books must be kept audit-ready **every
   day** — which is exactly the habit the rules demand
   (`03-Bookkeeping/01-Ledger-Format.md`).
2. A member who is tempted to "adjust" knows that a stranger may open the box
   tomorrow morning.
3. Surprise audits also test the **receipt book discipline** and the
   **cash-free rule**, the two places slow theft most often lives.

---

*The surprise audit is the quiet watchman. Its power is not in what it finds;
it is in the certainty that it may come at any time.*
