# AUDITOR ROTATION

**Folder:** `06-FINANCE/04-Audit/`

---

## 1. WHY ROTATION

An auditor who audits the same books with the same faces for years becomes,
in effect, part of the furniture. In the old system the "auditor" was the
money-keeper's brother or his cousin from the same street, and the "audit"
was a handshake. Rotation breaks that chain: the auditor is always new enough
to be genuinely unfamiliar, and never obligated to anyone.

## 2. HOW THE AUDITOR IS CHOSEN

Two methods, either of which the assembly may adopt each year:

| Method | How |
|--------|-----|
| **By lot** | Names of willing, qualified members and neighbours are written on slips; the auditor is drawn publicly at an assembly meeting |
| **From another colony** | The assembly requests an auditor from a neighbouring colony water system with the same rules (a friendly exchange: they audit us, we audit them) |

The chosen person accepts in writing, is told the rules, and is paid the
budgeted honorarium.

## 3. THE ROTATION RULES

1. **Never the same person twice in a row.** An auditor who audited this year
   cannot audit next year.
2. **No family connection.** The auditor must not be from the same household
   as any Finance Committee member, any signatory, or any person who entered
   or verified the books in the audit period. This includes spouse, parents,
   children, and in-laws.
3. **No recent committee service.** A person who served on the Finance
   Committee in the audited year cannot audit that year.
4. **Preference to a fresh face** over a repeat one; the goal is maximum
   unfamiliarity, not maximum convenience.

> **Scenario:** The committee proposes the previous treasurer's brother as
> auditor. The assembly must reject it: the auditor's independence is a
> structural rule, not a preference, and a "friendly" auditor is no auditor.

## 4. EXCHANGE BETWEEN COLONIES

1. A colony with the same constitution rules may enter an **audit exchange**
   with the neighbouring colony: each audits the other every alternate year.
2. The exchange is recorded in both colonies' minutes, and the honorarium is
   matched in the budgets.
3. Exchange auditors are especially valuable because they have no personal
   stake in the colony's friendships or grudges.

## 5. WHO MAY NOT AUDIT — THE FULL LIST

| Cannot audit | Reason |
|--------------|--------|
| The same person twice in a row | Rotation rule |
| Household member of a Finance Committee member | Conflict of interest |
| Household member of a signatory | Conflict of interest |
| A person who entered or verified the books | Audit of self is no audit |
| A person holding any system office that year | Structural independence |
| A person owed favours or money by the committee | Real or perceived bias |

## 6. WHAT IF NO ONE IS AVAILABLE

1. If no independent person can be found locally, the assembly must
   **invite from outside** — another colony, a students' group, a retired
   accountant, a bank staff member — rather than relax the independence rule.
2. If even that fails, the books are audited by a **two-member team of
   ordinary members** chosen by lot, with the explicit note in the report
   that independence was partial.
3. The independence rule itself is never relaxed for convenience.

## 7. THE AUDITOR'S PROTECTION

1. The auditor has full access to every record, box, and passbook, and may
   interview any member without a committee member present
   (`00-CONSTITUTION/02-Fundamental-Rights/02-Right-to-Information-and-Audit.md`).
2. Any attempt to influence, threaten, or bribe the auditor is a Level 4
   offence and is itself an audit finding.
3. The auditor's findings are read at the AGM even when they are
   uncomfortable.

---

*Rotation is not about finding the smartest auditor; it is about guaranteeing
the most honest one. A stranger who owes nothing is worth a genius who owes a
favour.*
