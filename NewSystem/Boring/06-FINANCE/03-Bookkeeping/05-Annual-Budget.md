# ANNUAL BUDGET

**Folder:** `06-FINANCE/03-Bookkeeping/`

---

## 1. WHAT THE ANNUAL BUDGET IS

The Annual Budget is the **financial plan of the whole system for one
financial year**, adopted before the year begins. It fixes the monthly
contribution, the fund provisions, the fine schedule, and every spending
ceiling. In the old system spending was invented on the day. Here the year is
decided in advance, in public, by the assembly.

## 2. WHO PREPARES IT

The Finance Committee prepares the draft from:

- the previous year's actuals (ledger totals);
- verified quotations for the year's needs;
- the maintenance schedule (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/01-Preventive-Maintenance/`);
- the duty compensation rates (`03-ROTATION-DUTY/03-Duty-Compensation/01-Time-Credits.md`);
- the Reserve Fund target estimate (`02-Fund-Management/02-Reserve-Fund.md`).

## 3. THE LINE ITEMS

The budget contains at least these lines, each with an amount and a note on
how it was estimated:

| Line | Contents |
|------|----------|
| Power | Electricity charges (current tariff x expected units, seasonal) |
| Preventive maintenance | PM schedule costs |
| Repair provision | Expected breakdown allowance |
| Stipends / compensation | Duty payments, skilled-member rates |
| Reserve Fund top-up | Monthly provision to the target |
| Administrative | Stationery, bank charges, board, audit honorarium |
| Other income estimate | Event fees, fines, grants, interest |
| Contingency | Small buffer, shown and capped |

## 4. THE DEBATE

1. The draft budget is published **at least 30 days before the new financial
   year**.
2. An assembly meeting is called to debate it. Members may question any line;
   the committee answers with the calculation behind the figure.
3. Amendments are moved and voted in the same meeting. Every adopted line is
   recorded in the minutes.

## 5. ADOPTION

1. The budget is adopted by the **2/3 vote** of member connections present
   and voting, in a meeting with the quorum fixed by the General Assembly
   rules (`02-GOVERNANCE/01-General-Assembly/`).
2. The vote adopts the budget **and** the monthly contribution derived from
   it (`01-Contributions/01-Monthly-Contribution.md`).
3. No budget, no year: if the assembly fails to adopt, the previous budget's
   structure continues with figures revised only for tariff changes, until a
   new budget is adopted.

## 6. THE BUDGET IS A CEILING, NOT A SUGGESTION

1. Each line item is a **ceiling**. Spending beyond a line is allowed only:
   - by a written **assembly note** at the next assembly (for overspend under
     10% of the line), or
   - by a 3/4 vote (for overspend over 10%), or
   - for genuine emergency power payments, ratified at the next assembly.
2. Unspent money is not automatically spent "because it is in the budget"; a
   line's unspent balance returns to the Common Fund and is shown in the
   year-end surplus (`05-Distribution-and-Refunds/01-Surplus-Distribution.md`).
3. A line cannot be raided to pay another line without an assembly note.

> **Scenario:** Mid-year, the committee wants to spend ₹8,000 more on repairs
> than the repair line allows, moving money from the stipend line. Both
> actions need an assembly note at the next meeting — the committee cannot
> quietly re-classify spending.

## 7. MONITORING THE BUDGET

1. The bimonthly statement (`04-Bimonthly-Statement.md`) shows each line's
   **budgeted vs spent** figures, so drift is visible at 2 months, not at 12.
2. The auditor checks actuals against the budget
   (`04-Audit/01-Regular-Audit.md`).
3. At year end, the Finance Committee presents the **actuals vs budget**
   report to the assembly; unexplained deviations are noted and answered.

## 8. THE BUDGET IS PUBLIC

1. The adopted budget, the underlying calculations, and the amendment
   minutes are public records from the day of adoption.
2. Any member may ask "where did this figure come from?" and receive the
   calculation — that is what makes the contribution a formula and not a mood.

---

*The budget is the colony deciding its own year on paper, in the open, before
a single rupee is spent.*
