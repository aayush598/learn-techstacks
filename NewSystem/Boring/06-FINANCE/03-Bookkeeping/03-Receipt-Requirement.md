# RECEIPT REQUIREMENT

**Folder:** `06-FINANCE/03-Bookkeeping/`

---

## 1. THE RULE IN ONE LINE

> **Every rupee coming in has a numbered receipt or a bank record. No
> receipt, no collection.**

In the old system cash was collected and "everyone trusts me". This rule
replaces trust with paper: money that is not receipted was never collected —
or worse, it was collected and kept.

## 2. WHAT COUNTS AS A RECEIPT

Two acceptable forms, and nothing else:

| Form | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Bank record** | The member's own transfer/UPI/cheque deposit record showing the account credited; matches the ledger |
| **Numbered receipt** | From the system's receipt book, one per payer, with: date, payer name, amount in words and figures, purpose, receipt number, and the collector's name + signature |

For bank transfers, the bank record is the receipt. For cash, a numbered
receipt is mandatory and the cash is banked **the same working day**.

## 3. THE RECEIPT BOOK

1. The receipt book is a **numbered, pre-printed book controlled by the
   Finance Committee** — not by any single person.
2. Receipt numbers run consecutively. A missing receipt number must be
   explained in writing and initialled; an unexplained gap is a red flag.
3. The carbon/stub copy stays in the book; the book stays in the common
   register box, open to any member.
4. At least two members witness the handing over of the receipt book at the
   start of each financial year, and its state is checked at each audit.
5. The book may be issued to a collector only for a defined duty (for
   example, the monthly collection counter) and is returned the same day,
   checked.

## 4. WHEN A RECEIPT IS ISSUED

1. Every cash payment received — contribution, event fee, fine, crisis call,
   buy-in installment, sale of scrap — gets an immediate receipt.
2. Every bank transfer is receipted in the ledger with the bank reference;
   a paper receipt is issued to the payer on request.
3. No payment is "too small" to receipt.

## 5. THE MISSING-RECEIPT RULE

1. If a member claims they paid and there is **no receipt and no bank
   record**, the payment is treated as **suspected misappropriation** until
   proven otherwise: the claim goes to the audit and the conflict process,
   not to a shrug.
2. If a collector received money and did not issue a receipt, the collection
   is treated as misappropriated; restitution is demanded
   (`04-Audit/05-Misuse-Consequences.md`).
3. The payer is not punished for the collector's missing receipt if the payer
   can show the money left their hands; the collector answers.

> **Scenario:** A member pays ₹800 in cash for an event water fee. The
> collector pockets it and issues nothing. The member later asks for a
> receipt; the collector denies receiving anything. The missing receipt number
> in the book, plus the member's record, triggers the audit and misuse
> proceedings. The payer is not blamed for being honest with cash.

## 6. CASH IS THE EXCEPTION, NOT THE RULE

1. The **default** is bank transfer (UPI, NEFT, cheque). Cash is accepted
   only at the Finance Committee collection counter, against an immediate
   receipt, and is banked the same working day.
2. No member collects cash door-to-door. Any door-to-door cash collection is
   void, and the collector is immediately suspected of misappropriation.
3. Members are encouraged to pay by transfer so that the bank record and the
   ledger match without any paper in between.

## 7. RECONCILIATION

- At month end, the receipt book's totals are reconciled against the ledger
  and the bank statement: every receipt number is accounted for, and every
  deposit is explained.
- The reconciliation note is signed by two Finance Committee members and
  filed.

---

*Trust built a boring; only paper protects it. The receipt is the fingerprint
of every rupee that enters the system.*
