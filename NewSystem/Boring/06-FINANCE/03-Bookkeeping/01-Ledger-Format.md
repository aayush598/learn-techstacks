# LEDGER FORMAT

**Folder:** `06-FINANCE/03-Bookkeeping/`

---

## 1. WHY A STANDARD LEDGER

The ledger is the **single book of truth** for money. In the old system money
was collected "somewhere", recorded "somehow", and remembered "roughly". This
rule fixes the format, the order, and the integrity rules so that a missing
entry is a visible wound, not a memory.

## 2. THE STANDARD COLUMNS

Every transaction is a **single numbered row**. The standard columns:

| # | Column | What goes here |
|---|--------|----------------|
| 1 | Date | Date of the transaction |
| 2 | Entry No. | Running serial number, never reused, never skipped |
| 3 | Description | What happened: payer/payee, reason, related fund, receipt/voucher link |
| 4 | Income (₹) | Amount received (with source) |
| 5 | Expense (₹) | Amount paid (with payee) |
| 6 | Balance (₹) | Running balance after the row |
| 7 | Voucher / Receipt No. | The matching voucher or numbered receipt |
| 8 | Entered by | Name + signature/initial of the member who wrote the row |
| 9 | Verified by | Name + signature/initial of the second member who checked it |

A printable/electronic copy is in the template at
`12-TEMPLATES/07-Finance-Ledger.md`.

## 3. THE RULES OF THE BOOK

1. **Chronological:** entries run strictly in date order; no back-dating
   except to correct a same-day error, marked as such.
2. **Numbered:** entry numbers run consecutively. A gap is a red flag and
   must be explained in the margin.
3. **No erasures:** nothing is erased, whitened, or overwritten. A wrong entry
   is **struck through with a single line, corrected beside it, initialled by
   the person who wrote it and by one verifier**, and the correction is
   numbered.
4. **Every row signed:** "entered by" and "verified by" are two different
   people from two different households, where possible.
5. **Nothing outside the book:** a rupee that is received or paid without a
   ledger row does not exist. If it happens, it is recorded immediately as a
   correction, or it is suspected misappropriation
   (`04-Audit/05-Misuse-Consequences.md`).
6. **One ledger for all:** the ledger covers Common, Reserve, and Repair
   sub-accounts through a "fund" tag in the description or a fund column; it
   is not three separate secret books.

## 4. HOW ENTRIES ARE WRITTEN

1. Income is entered the **same day** it is received (transfer, deposit, or
   receipt issued).
2. Expense is entered the **same day** the payment is authorized, with the
   voucher number (voucher first: `02-Expense-Voucher-Rule.md`).
3. Bank charges, interest, and fund transfers are entered as rows with their
   own descriptions and the bank statement reference.
4. At month end, the ledger is totalled (income, expense, balance) and
   reconciled against the bank statement; the reconciliation note is initialled.

## 5. THE LEDGER AS PUBLIC PROPERTY

1. The ledger lives in the **common register box** (unlocked) with a digital
   backup updated weekly.
2. Any member may read it, copy it, or photograph it at any time without
   permission (`04-Audit/04-Right-of-Any-Member-to-Audit.md`).
3. The Finance Committee does not "own" the ledger; it maintains it on
   behalf of all members.
4. The ledger is produced, with vouchers and receipts, for every audit
   (`04-Audit/`).

## 6. COMMON VIOLATIONS AND THEIR TREATMENT

| What someone does | What it is |
|-------------------|------------|
| Writes "₹500 collected" with no name | Unverifiable entry; void until the name and receipt exist |
| Tears out a page | Tampering; Level 4 offence |
| Keeps a second private notebook of "real" figures | Two sets of books; Level 4 offence |
| "Forgets" to enter a payment until next month | Late entry, marked and explained; repeat is an offence |
| Leaves blank spaces between entries | Suspicious; filled with a struck-through "not used" line and initials |

---

*The ledger is the mirror in which every rupee is seen. A clean book and a
clean account are the same thing.*
