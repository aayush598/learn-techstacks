# DIVIDEND BY CONTRIBUTION

**Folder:** `06-FINANCE/05-Distribution-and-Refunds/`

---

## 1. THE PRINCIPLE

> **Those who contribute more benefit proportionally. Those who contribute
> less benefit proportionally. No one's contribution is pooled and given to
> someone else as a reward.**

The old system's hidden rule was that everyone paid the same but one family
benefited the most. The dividend rule makes the benefit follow the
contribution, so favouritism has nothing to hide behind.

## 2. WHAT COUNTS AS A CONTRIBUTION

For distribution purposes, a member's contribution in a period includes:

| Contribution | Detail |
|--------------|--------|
| Monthly contributions paid | Actual money received and receipted |
| Buy-in / installment payments | Per `01-MEMBERSHIP/01-Enrollment/02-Buy-in-Contribution.md` |
| Event and extra charges paid | Actual fees paid |
| Crisis-call payments | Special collections paid |
| **Time-credits** | Duty and skilled work credited per `03-ROTATION-DUTY/03-Duty-Compensation/01-Time-Credits.md` — counted at their money value |

Fines and penalties are **not** contributions; they are restitution and do
not earn a share.

## 3. THE DIVIDEND FORMULA

When the assembly declares a distribution (`01-Surplus-Distribution.md`),
each member's share is:

```
Member's share =
  Total surplus to distribute
  x ( Member's total contribution in the period )
  / ( Sum of all members' total contributions in the period )
```

Every member's contribution total is already in the ledger and public
(`03-Bookkeeping/01-Ledger-Format.md`). The arithmetic is published with the
distribution list, so each member can check their own share with a
calculator.

## 4. THE WORKING EXAMPLE

Three members, a ₹1,500 surplus:

| Member | Contributed | Share |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| A | ₹6,000 | ₹750 |
| B | ₹4,500 | ₹562.50 |
| C | ₹1,500 | ₹187.50 |
| Total | ₹12,000 | ₹1,500 |

The surplus is returned **in the proportion contributed** — not split equally
as ₹500 each, which would have transferred money from C to A.

## 5. TIME-CREDITS COUNT AS CONTRIBUTIONS

1. A member who cannot pay money but serves extra duty earns time-credits at
   the published rate. Those credits are valued and added to their
   contribution total for distribution.
2. This honours P8 (work rewarded): the poor member's labour is as real a
   contribution as the richer member's rupees.
3. Time-credits are valued at the same rate used for settlement
   (`03-ROTATION-DUTY/03-Duty-Compensation/01-Time-Credits.md`).

## 6. WHAT DIVIDEND BY CONTRIBUTION DOES NOT MEAN

- It does **not** mean unequal voting power — every member still has exactly
  one vote (P2). Money never buys votes.
- It does **not** mean more water — water share stays equal (P1). The surplus
  is about money returned, not water taken.
- It does **not** mean the hardship tier is punished — hardship members'
  alternate contributions count too, and their water and voice are
  untouched (P7).

## 7. DISTRIBUTION RULES

1. Distribution is declared only by the assembly, at year end, per
   `01-Surplus-Distribution.md`.
2. The distribution list (member, contribution, share) is published with the
   statement before payment.
3. Payments are made by transfer to each member, or credited against future
   contributions, as the assembly decides. No cash handouts.
4. A share left unclaimed after 90 days is held on the member's ledger and
   shown as a credit — it is never reabsorbed.

---

*Dividend by contribution is the money-side mirror of equal water: water is
shared by need and right; surplus is shared by what each member put in. The
two rules together are fairness itself.*
