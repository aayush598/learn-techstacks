# FUND-RAISE — CRISIS CALLS

**Folder:** `06-FINANCE/02-Fund-Management/`

---

## 1. WHAT A CRISIS CALL IS

A crisis call is a **special, published, one-time collection** when the
system's funds are genuinely short — for example, a major repair beyond the
Repair Fund, an emergency beyond the Reserve Fund, or a sudden price shock.
In the old system "crisis" was the favourite word of the money-keeper: any
excuse, any amount, collected door to door in cash, recorded nowhere. This
rule makes a crisis call a rare, transparent, pre-calculated act — and
nothing else.

## 2. WHEN A CRISIS CALL IS ALLOWED

A crisis call may be made only when **all** of the following are true:

1. The need is real and urgent (supply threatened or a declared project);
2. The Common Fund cannot absorb it without breaching the minimum balance
   rule (`01-Common-Fund.md`); and
3. The Repair Fund and Reserve Fund cannot cover it (`02-Reserve-Fund.md`,
   `03-Repair-Fund.md`).

Routine expenses are **never** a crisis. Using the crisis label to fund
ordinary items is a Level 4 offence.

## 3. THE PROCESS

### 3.1 The notice

1. The Finance Committee publishes a **Crisis Call Notice** in writing, on
   the notice board and in the members' group, before any collection begins.
2. The notice contains: what happened, why existing funds are short, the
   total needed, and the calculation.

### 3.2 The calculation — published arithmetic

```
Crisis share per member =
  ( Total cost, with quotes/invoices attached
    - available fund balances )
  / ( Number of member connections )
```

- The total is backed by quotes or invoices (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/04-Vendor-Management/`).
- The available balances are taken from the latest statement and are public.
- Every member can check the arithmetic with a calculator.

### 3.3 Assembly approval

1. The notice is discussed at an assembly meeting. Approval requires a
   **3/4 vote of member connections present and voting**, except in a
   genuine emergency where the cost is below the medium-repair threshold and
   delay would stop supply — then the call may begin on publication alone, and
   must be ratified at the next assembly.
2. If the assembly rejects the call, **no collection happens**. The committee
   must find the money inside the budget or seek other means.

### 3.4 The collection

1. Collection is by **bank transfer or against numbered receipts** — never
   door-to-door cash.
2. Installment option: any member may pay in up to **three installments**
   over the timeline below, on request; no penalty for installments in a
   crisis call.
3. Hardship: a member in the hardship tier (`01-Contributions/02-Contribution-Tiers.md`)
   pays **half** the share, on application, with the alternate-contribution
   rule applied by the same standard.
4. The Finance Committee records every payment against the member's ledger
   account (`03-Bookkeeping/01-Ledger-Format.md`) and issues a receipt for
   every rupee.

### 3.5 Timeline

| Step | Deadline |
|------|----------|
| Notice published | Day 0 |
| Assembly discussion and vote | Within 7 days of notice |
| Collection window | 14 days from approval |
| Installments | Up to 45 days from approval |
| Accounts closed and over-collection refunded | Within 7 days of window close |

## 4. NO EMERGENCY COLLECTIONS WITHOUT PUBLICATION

1. **Any** collection of system money, in any amount, for any reason, must be
   published in advance. A collection that begins in secret, even if the
   purpose is genuine, is void and the collector faces misuse proceedings.
2. A member who is told "pay ₹2,000 now, explain later" may refuse, and must
   report it; that report is protected (P5, P7).

## 5. OVER-COLLECTION IS REFUNDED

1. If the collected amount exceeds the approved total (for example, the
   repair cost came lower than the quotes), the surplus is **refunded
   proportionally to those who paid**, within 7 days of the window closing.
2. The refund is by transfer to the paying members, with a refund list
   published in the statement.
3. If the member cannot receive a transfer (no account), the credit is held
   and shown on their ledger account until claimed — never absorbed silently.

> **Scenario:** A crisis call collects ₹60,000 from 30 members (₹2,000 each).
> The repair actually costs ₹45,000. The excess ₹15,000 is refunded at ₹500
> per member within a week — it is not "kept as a buffer" and it is not kept
> at all.

## 6. RECORD AND PUBLICITY

- The entire crisis call — notice, calculation, approvals, payments, refunds —
  is recorded in the ledger and summarized in the next bimonthly statement
  (`03-Bookkeeping/04-Bimonthly-Statement.md`).
- The crisis call file is a public record; any member may audit it
  (`04-Audit/04-Right-of-Any-Member-to-Audit.md`).

## 7. CONSEQUENCES OF ABUSE

Collecting a fake crisis, keeping collected money unrecorded, or refusing to
refund over-collection is **misuse of funds** (`04-Audit/05-Misuse-Consequences.md`),
a Level 4 offence, with restitution and removal from all roles.

---

*The crisis call exists to save the system in rare moments of genuine need —
published, calculated, voted, receipted, and refunded. It is never a routine,
and never a surprise.*
