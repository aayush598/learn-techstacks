# WINTER AND RAINY SEASON

**Folder:** `04-MOTOR-OPERATIONS/02-Seasonal-Rotation/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

In winter and the rainy season, demand falls (households store less, gardens are
watered less) and the borewell recharges naturally. The motor should not run
full normal-season hours just "because that is the habit". This file defines how
runs are reduced or stopped **by process**, saving power, money, and wear.

## 2. CONDITIONS THAT TRIGGER REVIEW

| Condition | Indicator |
|-----------|-----------|
| Demand falls | Measured stored volume at slot end exceeds demand for 7 consecutive days |
| Borewell recharges | Water level rises and the pump rests more often; yield exceeds normal season |
| Rainfall | Sustained rain, groundwater recharge reported by the water committee |
| Power costs | Seasonal tariff or diesel/power usage becomes a significant cost |

## 3. HOW RUNS ARE REDUCED — BY PROCESS, NEVER BY CHOICE

1. The water committee presents the **seasonal measurement** (yield, storage,
   demand) to the assembly.
2. The assembly passes a **seasonal resolution** that either:
   - **Reduces duration** of each slot (for example, 60 minutes → 40 minutes), or
   - **Reduces frequency** (for example, evening window skipped on alternate
     days, morning window retained), or
   - **Suspends one window** entirely (for example, no evening run in the peak
     monsoon week) with a published end date.
3. The reduced schedule is **published 7 days in advance**
   (`01-Daily-Schedule/03-Schedule-Publication.md`) and applied by the duty
   operator exactly like any schedule.
4. **No individual may decide** to skip a run "because it rained". The operator
   runs the schedule until a resolution changes it.

## 4. STOPPING RUNS ENTIRELY

1. Full stoppage (all runs suspended, for example in a flood week) is only by
   **assembly resolution**, issued through the emergency protocol
   (`01-Daily-Schedule/04-Notification-Protocol.md`).
2. Stoppage must carry a **review date** (maximum 7 days), after which either
   the resolution is renewed or the schedule resumes.
3. During stoppage, the borewell level is recorded daily; the readings are the
   basis for resumption.

## 5. SAVING POWER — THE REASON IS COMMON, NOT PERSONAL

1. Reduced runs save power and motor wear; the savings belong to the Common
   Fund and reduce future contributions.
2. Savings are **logged and reported** at the assembly quarterly
   (`06-FINANCE/01-Contributions/`).
3. A member may not hoard the "saved" water for private storage; the saving is
   collective. Hoarding beyond normal storage is investigated as wastage or
   tanker misuse (`05-Irregularities/03-Water-Wastage-Detection.md`).

## 6. WINTER-SPECIFIC RULES

1. Morning slots may start 30 minutes later to avoid freezing/early-dark issues
   where relevant, by the same assembly resolution.
2. In winter, households typically need less daily volume; the fair-share
   formula (`01-Daily-Schedule/02-Slot-Definition.md`) is recalculated on the
   measured demand, not kept static.

## 7. SCENARIO: MONSOON WEEK

During heavy rain, every roof is full of stored water and households stop
drawing. The water committee observes two consecutive days where slots end with
tanks still full. It presents the data; the assembly resolves to skip the
evening run for 5 days, ending on a published date, with a review on day 5. The
duty operator does not run the evening window during those 5 days, logs "evening
run suspended by resolution dated ___", and records the borewell level each
morning. On day 6 the schedule resumes. **The saving belongs to the fund; the
decision belongs to the assembly; the execution belongs to the log.**

## 8. RESUMPTION TO NORMAL SEASON

1. When the measured yield and demand return to normal-season levels, the water
   committee reports it and the assembly resolves to restore the Standard
   Schedule (`01-Daily-Schedule/01-Standard-Schedule.md`).
2. Resumption also follows the 7-day publication rule.

---

*The rainy season is an opportunity to rest the motor and the fund — but only by
resolution, only by measurement, and only in the log. The motor never stops
because someone felt like it; it stops because the colony decided it should.*
