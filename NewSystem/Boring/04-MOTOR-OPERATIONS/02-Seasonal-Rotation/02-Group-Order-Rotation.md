# GROUP ORDER ROTATION

**Folder:** `04-MOTOR-OPERATIONS/02-Seasonal-Rotation/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

Not all slots are equal. The first slot of a run window usually enjoys higher
pressure and a fuller borewell; the last slot may see lower yield and leftover
pressure. **Group order rotation** ensures this advantage is shared, so no group
is always first and no group is always last.

## 2. THE RULE — ROTATE WEEKLY

1. During any grouping season (and during normal season when groups exist), the
   **order in which groups are served rotates every week**.
2. The rotation moves **one position forward each week**: the group that was
   first becomes last, and every other group moves up one.
3. The rotation applies to **both windows** together: if Group A is first in the
   morning window, it is first in the evening window too for that week.

## 3. EXAMPLE ROTATION CALENDAR

| Week | Morning order | Evening order |
|------|---------------|---------------|
| Week 1 | G1 → G2 → G3 | G1 → G2 → G3 |
| Week 2 | G2 → G3 → G1 | G2 → G3 → G1 |
| Week 3 | G3 → G1 → G2 | G3 → G1 → G2 |
| Week 4 | G1 → G2 → G3 | G1 → G2 → G3 |

1. The calendar is published **with the grouping schedule**, 7 days in advance
   (`01-Daily-Schedule/03-Schedule-Publication.md`), covering the whole season.
2. Week boundaries are fixed (Monday 06:00) and are not changed mid-season
   except by assembly resolution.
3. If grouping is revised by lot (`01-Summer-Grouping.md`), the rotation restarts
   from Week 1 with the new groups.

## 4. WHY ROTATION, NOT "FAIR SLOTS"

1. Some physical inequalities cannot be engineered away — the borewell is at one
   point, the far house is at the end of the line. Rotation shares the **cost of
   that inequality** equally over time.
2. Rotation is **automatic and impersonal**: it does not require a committee to
   judge "who deserves the good slot this week".
3. Rotation supports the principle of **equal power and no single point of
   failure** (P2, P3): no negotiation, no favour, no anger — only the calendar.

## 5. ROTATION AND COMPENSATION

1. Rotation reduces, but may not eliminate, inequality. Where a group still
   measurably receives less over a full cycle, the logs are the basis for
   additional compensation (longer duration or credits) under
   `03-Slot-Fairness-Compensation.md`.
2. Claims are assessed on **log evidence** (Pressure and Quality Log, Motor Run
   Log), never on how convincing the complaint sounds.

## 6. NO SWAPPING WITHOUT PROCESS

1. A group that wants a different order (for example, to avoid a clash with a
   local festival) may **propose** a swap at the assembly.
2. A swap must be **agreed by both affected groups** and approved by assembly
   resolution, published 7 days in advance.
3. A swap that disadvantages a group which did not agree is void. One group
   cannot "swap itself" into the best slot by pressuring another group.

## 7. SCENARIO: "WHY IS MY STREET ALWAYS LAST?"

A member from the last-served street complains in Week 2 that "the powerful
families always get first water". The answer is on the board: the published
calendar shows their street was first in Week 1, will be first again in Week 4,
and the log confirms their storage filled in Week 1. The complaint is recorded,
the facts shown, and the calendar continues. **Feelings do not stop the rotation;
facts govern it.**

---

*The rotation calendar is the assembly's pre-commitment to fairness. It is
published once, applied mechanically, and changed only by the same published
process that created it.*
