# PRESSURE AND QUALITY LOG

**Folder:** `04-MOTOR-OPERATIONS/04-Monitoring-and-Logs/`

---

## 1. PURPOSE

Water must not only arrive — it must arrive **at sufficient pressure and safe
quality**. The Pressure and Quality Log records both, periodically and
consistently, so that fairness compensation and repair decisions rest on
measured facts (P5) rather than complaint volume.

## 2. WHAT IS RECORDED

| Record | Where | Frequency |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| Flow/pressure at **sample taps** | A fixed set of representative connections (at least: first house of line, mid-line, last house, highest tap) | Each slot, at slot middle |
| Borewell discharge / pump pressure | Motor room gauge (`03-Pump-Gauge-Recording.md`) | Each run, start and stop |
| Water clarity | Visual check at the sample tap (clear / turbid / muddy / with particles) | Each slot, daily |
| Taste/odour anomaly | Visual and reported odour | When observed |
| Stored volume reached | Representative tanks at slot end | Each slot (feeds fairness) |
| Any repair/network change | Note in the log | When made |

## 3. THE SAMPLE TAP SET

1. The sample taps are chosen by the assembly, published, and **rotated monthly**
   so no single street carries the recording burden or the advantage.
2. A sample tap is a member's tap only by agreement, with their consent
   recorded. A member may decline, and another sample point replaces it.
3. The sample set must cover the network fairly: a high tap, a distant tap, and
   a low tap always included, reflecting `09-PROJECTS-AND-EXPANSION/04-Network-Improvements/01-Tap-Height-Compensation.md`.

## 4. HOW READINGS ARE TAKEN

1. Readings are taken by the **duty operator** during their duty period and
   written into the log immediately.
2. Flow is measured with the simple test (fill a 10-litre can at the tap and
   time it) or with a cheap pressure gauge where fitted — the method is fixed
   and published so readings are comparable.
3. Clarity is recorded in plain words; a photo of the sample is added where
   a change is noticed.

## 5. ANOMALIES AND REPORTS

| Anomaly | Meaning | Next action |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| Pressure below the published minimum at a sample tap for 2 consecutive slots | Network fault or compensation need | Record; report via `05-Irregularities/04-Reporting-Violations.md`; repair track if it is a fault |
| Turbid/muddy water | Borewell silt, pump surge, or pipe ingress | Stop drinking advisory via the emergency protocol; test and repair |
| Odour or particles | Contamination risk | Immediate notice to all, water-quality check, `10-RISK-AND-EMERGENCY/04-Health-and-Safety/` |
| Pressure high (above safe limit) | Risk of burst pipes | Reduce/shorten per fault report; `05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/02-Breakdown-Repair/01-Fault-Reporting.md` |

Every anomaly gets a **number** in the log and is tracked to closure — an open
anomaly is visible in the log until it is resolved or formally closed.

## 6. THE BASIS FOR FAIRNESS COMPENSATION

1. The weekly summary of the Pressure and Quality Log is the evidence for
   `02-Seasonal-Rotation/03-Slot-Fairness-Compensation.md`: a group whose
   sample taps show persistently low pressure or low stored volume is
   compensated on these numbers.
2. The same numbers **defend** a group against baseless claims: if the log
   shows normal readings, a complaint of "no water" points to a household-side
   fault, not a network injustice.

## 7. THE BASIS FOR REPAIR DECISIONS

1. A pattern of low pressure at one street, while other streets are normal,
   indicates a **network problem** on that branch — the repair track is opened
   (`05-MAINTENANCE-REPAIR/02-Breakdown-Repair/`).
2. A colony-wide pressure drop indicates a **borewell or pump problem** — gauge
   and discharge checks under `03-Pump-Gauge-Recording.md`.
3. Repair priority is argued from the log, not by who shouts first.

## 8. SCENARIO: THE HIGH-TAP HOUSEHOLD

A member with the highest tap in the colony claims the system "never gives them
water". The log's sample readings show their street's delivered pressure is
normal for the network but insufficient to lift to their tank. The answer is
not a favour — it is the published **tap-height compensation**
(`09-PROJECTS-AND-EXPANSION/04-Network-Improvements/01-Tap-Height-Compensation.md`):
a longer effective fill, a booster option, or a documented credit. The log
proved the cause; the rule chose the remedy.

---

*Pressure and quality are measured on a schedule, recorded in the log, and used
by the rules. Water that cannot be measured cannot be argued about — and water
that is not safe is never served silently.*
