# DISPUTE ADJUDICATION — ARBITRATION CLAUSE

**Folder:** `11-LEGAL-AND-COMPLIANCE/04-Dispute-Adjudication/`

---

## 1. WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

Not every dispute can be solved inside the colony. But a dispute between
neighbours should not require a district court. The Membership Agreement
carries an **arbitration clause**: disputes that survive the internal process
go to a neutral third party whose decision is binding — faster, cheaper, and
fairer than a court, and far better than a fight in the street.

## 2. THE CLAUSE

1. Any dispute arising from membership, water use, contributions, projects,
   or the constitution that is not resolved internally
   (`01-Internal-Exhaustion.md`) is referred to arbitration.
2. Arbitration proceeds under the Indian Arbitration and Conciliation Act (or
   the law in force), with a seat and language agreed in the clause.
3. The arbitration clause is part of the **signed Membership and Water Usage
   Agreements** (`01-Binding-Agreement/`) — a member has accepted it in
   writing before any dispute arises.

## 3. CHOOSING ARBITRATORS — AGREED, OR BY LOT

| Situation | How the arbitrator is chosen |
|-----------|------------------------------|
| Both parties agree | One arbitrator agreed within 15 days |
| No agreement | A 3-member panel drawn **by lot** from a standing list |
| The standing list | Retired engineers, advocates, or respected residents of a nearby colony who are **not members** and have no interest |

1. A standing list of 5-10 neutral names is maintained by the assembly and
   reviewed annually.
2. A person who is a party's relative, or has an interest, **recuses**;
   a challenge to an arbitrator is decided by the other arbitrators or the
   agreed appointing rule.

## 4. THE AWARD IS BINDING

1. The arbitration award is binding on the parties, and enforceable per law.
2. The system treats the award as final; the parties comply within the time the
   award sets.
3. The award is filed in the records (`03-Records-Retention/`) and is public
   (P6) except for genuinely personal details.

## 5. COSTS

| Cost | Rule |
|------|------|
| Arbitrator fees and expenses | Shared equally, or as the award directs |
| Each party's own lawyer/expenses | Borne by each side, unless the award says otherwise |
| System's share | Advanced from the Common Fund by assembly resolution |
| Member in hardship | May apply for support from the system fund (P7) |

Costs are predictable, published, and never used to crush a poorer member.

## 6. THE RELATION TO COURT

1. Arbitration replaces court for system disputes; a party cannot run to court
   instead of arbitrating once the clause binds.
2. Court involvement in arbitration is limited to what the law provides
   (challenging or enforcing an award) — see `03-Court-Last-Approach.md`.

## 7. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

Two members dispute the boundary of a common pipe line. Internal steps finish
without agreement. They draw one arbitrator from the standing list; he hears
both, inspects the line with both present, and issues an award defining the
line and the cost share. Both comply; the award is filed. No court, no feud,
and the evidence made the award predictable.

## 8. TEMPLATE AND RECORD

- A short arbitration agreement (statement of the dispute, the chosen
  arbitrator, the timetable, the cost rule) is drawn from the templates
  (`12-TEMPLATES/`) and signed by both parties before the hearing begins.
- The timetable mirrors the internal deadlines: hearings within 30 days, award
  within 60 days of appointment, unless the parties agree longer.

---

*Arbitration is the colony's own court — chosen by the parties or by lot,
bound by evidence, and finished on time. It is the middle rung of the ladder
between a neighbour's quarrel and a judge's docket.*
