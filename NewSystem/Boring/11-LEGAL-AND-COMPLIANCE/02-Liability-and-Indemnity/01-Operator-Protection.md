# LIABILITY AND INDEMNITY — OPERATOR PROTECTION

**Folder:** `11-LEGAL-AND-COMPLIANCE/02-Liability-and-Indemnity/`

---

## 1. WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

Duty operators run the motor, open valves, and work on the network. In the old
system, when something went wrong the individual was blamed: "you pressed the
button, you pay for the motor." That is why nobody wanted to serve, and why the
same few families ended up doing everything. Operator protection is system
protection — a system that punishes its volunteers ends up with no volunteers.

## 2. THE PRINCIPLE

> A member acting for the system, following the system's rules, is not
> personally liable for the outcome. The system — through the Common Fund or
> its insurance — bears the loss.

The operator is the system's hands. The system owns the consequences of its
own hands.

## 3. WHEN THE OPERATOR IS PROTECTED

The operator is covered when all of the following are true:

| Condition | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| Acting on system duty | On the roster, or called for an emergency |
| Following the rules | Checklist, schedule, safety procedure (`03-ROTATION-DUTY/`) |
| Logging honestly | Entry made in the run log / fault log |
| Reporting irregularities | Flagged in the log or to the duty committee |

If those conditions hold and damage still occurs — the motor burns because the
voltage dipped in a storm, a valve cracks under age — the system covers it.

## 4. WHAT IS NOT COVERED — DELIBERATE NEGLIGENCE

| Excluded | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| Intentional wrongdoing | Deliberate damage, theft, sabotage |
| Disobedience of a written rule | Running the motor despite a written stop order |
| Incapacity | Operating while drunk or grossly unfit |
| Personal use | Using the system to serve private business without approval |

The boundary between an **honest error** and **deliberate negligence** is
decided by the Conflict Review Board or the assembly **on evidence** — the
logs, the checklist, the witnesses — never by the loudest voice and never by
who is popular (P5).

## 5. THE PROCEDURE AFTER AN INCIDENT

1. The incident is logged and reported the same day.
2. Photos and any insurance notice are taken within the policy window
   (`03-Insurance.md`).
3. If the operator is protected, the system files the claim or pays from the
   Common Fund/Repair Fund and records the loss.
4. If deliberate negligence is alleged, the CRB hears the evidence first; the
   operator is presumed protected until the evidence proves otherwise.

## 6. NO QUIET COLLECTION FROM THE OPERATOR

- No member may demand that an operator "pay quietly" to avoid a dispute. Such
  a demand is itself a violation of the protection rule and is dealt with under
  the enforcement ladder (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/05-Enforcement/`).
- The operator's identity is never a reason to settle privately; the system's
  records are public (P6).

## 7. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

During a thunderstorm the voltage dipped and the motor burned out. The duty
operator had started the motor per schedule, ticked the checklist, and logged
the start time. The system files an insurance claim, tops up from the Reserve
Fund, and replaces the motor. The operator is thanked and credited for duty —
not fined, not asked to pay, and not dropped from the roster.
