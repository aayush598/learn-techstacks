# RECORDS RETENTION — RETENTION PERIODS

**Folder:** `11-LEGAL-AND-COMPLIANCE/03-Records-Retention/`

---

## 1. WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

In the old system, records were kept when convenient and "lost" when not: the
receipt vanished, the minutes page was torn, the ledger "burned." Retention
periods make two guarantees: **facts are never lost**, and **history is never
"forgotten."** Every record has a minimum life, and its disposal requires a
recorded decision.

## 2. THE RETENTION PERIODS

| Record | Minimum period |
|--------|----------------|
| Constitution and all amendments | **Permanent** |
| Assembly minutes | **Permanent** |
| Member registry, agreements, entry/exit documents | **Permanent** (life of system + 10 years) |
| Financial ledgers | **Permanent** |
| Vouchers, receipts, invoices | 10 years |
| Share Ledger and buy-in records | **Permanent** |
| Duty logs, motor run logs, reading sheets | 5 years |
| Incident, fault, and repair reports | 10 years |
| Project files (boring, motor, tank, pipes) | **Permanent** |
| Warranty documents | Life of warranty + 10 years |
| Insurance policies and claim files | Policy life + 10 years |
| Routine notices (schedule changes, reminders) | 3 years |

"Permanent" means for as long as the system exists, and in practice for as
long as any member affected by the record may live.

## 3. WHY THE CORE IS PERMANENT

1. Ownership, rights, and money cannot be allowed to expire into memory.
   A ledger that is "lost" is exactly the file a corrupt office-holder would
   lose.
2. Permanent retention is the guarantee behind the Truth rules (P5): a fact
   recorded thirty years ago still decides a dispute today.

## 4. DISPOSAL RULES

1. A record is disposed of **only after** its period expires, and **only by an
   assembly resolution** with the disposal list recorded.
2. Anything tied to a live dispute, audit, or legal proceeding is **frozen**
   until the matter closes — no disposal during a dispute.
3. Disposal means destruction in a way that cannot be recovered (shredding),
   or transfer to a member/authority if the record has continuing value; the
   method is recorded.

## 5. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

Ten years after a buy-in, a member asks, "Did I ever actually pay my buy-in?"
The permanent ledger shows the payment, and the ten-year vouchers still exist.
The answer is a page, not a shrug — nothing about the member's money was
"forgotten," because the periods forbade forgetting.

## 6. RESPONSIBILITY

- The rotating Records Keeper maintains the retention calendar
  (`03-Digital-and-Physical-Backup.md`); the Finance Committee answers for
  financial records; the Registry Keeper for membership records.
- The quarterly backup check verifies that old records still exist and open.

---

*Retention periods are the system's promise that no one can delete the past.
What was recorded stays recorded — until a recorded decision says otherwise.*
