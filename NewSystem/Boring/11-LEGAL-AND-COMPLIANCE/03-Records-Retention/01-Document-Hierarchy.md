# RECORDS RETENTION — DOCUMENT HIERARCHY

**Folder:** `11-LEGAL-AND-COMPLIANCE/03-Records-Retention/`

---

## 1. WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

Disputes are won by documents, not by memory. When a document is missing,
"we all agreed" replaces truth. When two documents contradict each other,
someone must know which one wins. This file fixes the hierarchy of documents —
so the paper trail has an order, and history cannot be rewritten by whoever
holds the loudest file.

## 2. THE HIERARCHY

| Rank | Document | Authority |
|------|----------|-----------|
| 1 | **Constitution** (Master + its parts) | Supreme law of the system |
| 2 | **Assembly resolutions** (minutes, decisions) | Made under the constitution |
| 3 | **Binding agreements** (membership, water usage, contribution) | Signed commitments of members |
| 4 | **Contracts** (contractors, vendors, insurers) | Obligations of outsiders to the system |
| 5 | **Registers and logs** (ledgers, asset register, duty logs, readings) | Records of facts and events |

This mirrors the Norm Hierarchy in
`00-CONSTITUTION/06-Supremacy-and-Conflict/01-Norm-Hierarchy.md`.

## 3. WHAT WINS WHEN DOCUMENTS CONFLICT

| Conflict | Result |
|----------|--------|
| Resolution contradicts the constitution | The resolution is void (`00-CONSTITUTION/06-Supremacy-and-Conflict/02-Conflict-Between-Rules.md`) |
| Contract contradicts an assembly resolution | Internally, the resolution governs; the contract was not validly approved if it conflicts |
| Log entry contradicts a resolution | The resolution wins as a norm; the log stands only as a record of fact |
| Two minutes contradict each other | The later valid minutes govern, unless the later one violated the constitution |

## 4. PUBLIC VERSUS CONFIDENTIAL

| Category | Access |
|----------|--------|
| Minutes, ledgers, registers, reading sheets, project files | **Public** — any member may inspect (P6) |
| Medical and hardship letters, personal details in claims | **Confidential** within the file; only summaries are published |

A confidential note does not make a decision secret — the decision itself is
public, and the personal detail is protected.

## 5. HOW THE HIERARCHY IS USED

1. When a dispute arises, the parties first identify the **rank** of each
   document involved; the higher rank governs.
2. When a document is missing, its rank cannot be assumed — a missing register
   is not evidence that an event did not happen, and a missing minutes page is
   not evidence that a resolution never passed.
3. The hierarchy is cited in the internal resolution process
   (`07-CONFLICT-RESOLUTION/02-Resolution-Process/`) and in arbitration
   (`04-Dispute-Adjudication/02-Arbitration-Clause.md`).

## 6. EXAMPLE FROM COLONY LIFE

A member claims the assembly "decided" to refund him ₹5,000. The minutes show
no such resolution, and no agreement clause produces that refund. The claim
fails — the minutes, not memory, rule. Conversely, a member cannot be fined
₹5,000 because "everyone remembers the rule" when the fine schedule is
published and shows no such fine.

## 7. RELATION TO RETENTION

- The hierarchy tells you which document wins; the retention periods
  (`02-Retention-Periods.md`) tell you how long each document must be kept so
  that the hierarchy can be applied.

---

*The constitution rules, the assembly decides, the agreements bind, the
contracts promise, and the registers witness. Each document knows its place —
so no document can be used to rewrite another.*
