1. **How do you build vocabulary efficiently for placements?**
   - Learn roots/prefixes/suffixes plus word families, review with spaced repetition, and read actively — meaning comes from usage, not lists alone.

2. **What is the difference between synonyms and antonyms questions?**
   - Synonyms test closest meaning; antonyms the opposite — both need precision over approximate recall ('abandon' ≠ 'consign').

3. **What is a word in context strategy?**
   - If you don't know a word, use the surrounding context (contrast like 'but', definition clauses, examples) to infer the sense before guessing.

4. **What does 'meticulous' mean and its context?**
   - Showing great care/attention to detail — a synonym for scrupulous/precise; often appears in character-description vocabulary items.

5. **What is the difference between 'recipient' and 'precipient' trap pairs?**
   - Groups of similar-looking words (recipient vs recipient, quite vs quiet) are designed to trap — read the exact spelling and match the intended meaning.

6. **What are the most tested word families in placement papers?**
   - Adjectives of quality (conscientious, pragmatic), formal verbs (facilitate, mitigate), and abstract nouns (paradigm, inference) — the interview-grade vocabulary set.

7. **How do you answer 'odd one out' vocabulary questions?**
   - Compare meanings precisely, not first impressions; the odd word usually differs in nuance (e.g., a concrete vs abstract term) — deliberate over rushing.

8. **What is collocation and why does it matter?**
   - Words that naturally co-occur (heavy rain, strong coffee) — matching common collocations avoids awkward-sounding answers in fill-in-the-blank sections.

9. **State the formula used to solve 03 vocabulary problems quickly in aptitude tests.**
   - Every aptitude topic rests on a small set of formulas and unit-adjustments; write the formula, plug the numbers, and always sanity-check the units (hours/minutes, percent, ratio) before finalising — most errors are unit errors.

10. **Describe a shortcut technique for 03 vocabulary.**
   - Look for symmetric/divisible structure, cancellation in fractions, or base-value anchoring (e.g., assume a convenient total like 100 or LCM). Shortcuts reduce arithmetic but should not skip the conceptual check.
