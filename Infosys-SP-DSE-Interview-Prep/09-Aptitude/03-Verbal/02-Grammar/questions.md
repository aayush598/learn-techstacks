1. **What are the eight parts of speech?**
   - Noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection — the taxonomy every grammar question references.

2. **What is the difference between a subject and a predicate?**
   - Subject: who/what the sentence is about; predicate: the verb + everything said about the subject — every complete sentence needs both.

3. **What is subject-verb agreement?**
   - A singular subject takes a singular verb; plural takes plural — the classic trap: phrases between subject and verb ('a box of apples IS') confuse the number.

4. **What is the difference between past perfect and simple past?**
   - Past perfect (had done) signals an action completed BEFORE another past action; simple past states a past fact — order matters for choosing.

5. **What is an article and the a/an/the rules?**
   - a/an for indefinite (first mention, sound-based 'an hour'), the for definite (already known/specific); omit for generality — the three-article system.

6. **What is the difference between direct and indirect speech?**
   - Direct quotes the exact words; indirect reports them shifting tense, pronouns, and time words ('I am' → 'he said he was') — shifting rules are frequently tested.

7. **What are common pronoun errors?**
   - Ambiguous antecedents, and case errors: 'between you and me' (object), 'he and I went' (subject) — the me/I selection is the classic one.

8. **What is the difference between 'affect' and 'effect'?**
   - affect is usually a verb (to influence); effect is usually a noun (result) — the confusable pair most vocab/grammar sections use.
