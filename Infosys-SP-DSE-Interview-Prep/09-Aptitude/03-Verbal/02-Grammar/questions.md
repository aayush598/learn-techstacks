# Verbal — Grammar Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What are the eight parts of speech?
**A:** Noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection — the taxonomy every grammar question references.

## Q2: What is the difference between a subject and a predicate?
**A:** Subject: who/what the sentence is about; predicate: the verb + everything said about the subject — every complete sentence needs both.

## Q3: What is subject-verb agreement?
**A:** A singular subject takes a singular verb; plural takes plural — the classic trap: phrases between subject and verb ('a box of apples IS') confuse the number.

## Q4: What is the difference between past perfect and simple past?
**A:** Past perfect (had done) signals an action completed BEFORE another past action; simple past states a past fact — order matters for choosing.

## Q5: What is an article and the a/an/the rules?
**A:** a/an for indefinite (first mention, sound-based 'an hour'), the for definite (already known/specific); omit for generality — the three-article system.

## Q6: What is the difference between direct and indirect speech?
**A:** Direct quotes the exact words; indirect reports them shifting tense, pronouns, and time words ('I am' → 'he said he was') — shifting rules are frequently tested.

## Q7: What are common pronoun errors?
**A:** Ambiguous antecedents, and case errors: 'between you and me' (object), 'he and I went' (subject) — the me/I selection is the classic one.

## Q8: What is the difference between 'affect' and 'effect'?
**A:** affect is usually a verb (to influence); effect is usually a noun (result) — the confusable pair most vocab/grammar sections use.

## Q9: State the formula used to solve 02 grammar problems quickly in aptitude tests.
**A:** Every aptitude topic rests on a small set of formulas and unit-adjustments; write the formula, plug the numbers, and always sanity-check the units (hours/minutes, percent, ratio) before finalising — most errors are unit errors.

## Q10: Describe a shortcut technique for 02 grammar.
**A:** Look for symmetric/divisible structure, cancellation in fractions, or base-value anchoring (e.g., assume a convenient total like 100 or LCM). Shortcuts reduce arithmetic but should not skip the conceptual check.

## Q11: Give a practice problem for 02 grammar and solve it step by step.
**A:** Illustrative solved step-by-step with setup -> arithmetic -> final answer and a one-line verification; solving aloud with structured steps is exactly what the assessment expects.

## Q12: What are the typical mistakes students make in 02 grammar?
**A:** Misreading what is asked, unit mix-ups, ignoring 'approximate' vs 'exact', and arithmetic slips under time pressure. Mitigate by re-reading the question and estimating the answer before computing.
