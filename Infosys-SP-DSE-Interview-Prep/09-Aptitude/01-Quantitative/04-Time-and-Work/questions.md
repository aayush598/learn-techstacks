1. **What is the time-and-work formula?**
   - Work done per unit time is the rate: if a person does a job in T days, they complete 1/T per day; combined rates add. Total work is usually set to 1 (or LCM to avoid fractions).

2. **A finishes work in 12 days, B in 18. Working together, how long?**
   - Rates: 1/12 + 1/18 = (3+2)/36 = 5/36 per day; time = 36/5 = 7.2 days. Combined-rate addition is the core technique.

3. **Pipes: a pipe fills a tank in 8h, another in 12h. Together?**
   - Fill rates 1/8 + 1/12 = 5/24 per hour → 24/5 = 4.8 hours. Pipes fusing rates is the classic time-and-work variant.

4. **If 4 men do work in 9 days, how many days for 6 men?**
   - Total work = 4×9 = 36 man-days; 6 men → 36/6 = 6 days. Constant total work: men × days stays fixed (inverse proportion).

5. **A does 1/3 of work in 4 days; how long for the whole job alone?**
   - 1/3 work in 4 days → 1 work in 12 days. Fraction-of-work-to-time conversions are the identification step in Time and Work.

6. **What is the LCM method in time-and-work?**
   - Set total work = LCM of the day-counts; each person's per-day = total/days; combine and divide — avoiding fractional rates speeds up arithmetic.

7. **If A+B complete in 6 days and A alone in 10, how long for B?**
   - B's rate = 1/6 - 1/10 = (5-3)/30 = 2/30 = 1/15 → B alone 15 days. Difference-of-rates recovery.

8. **What are the common traps?**
   - Converting days to rates correctly, adding reciprocals (NOT adding days), and misreading 'A works alone for a while then both' set-ups — read the timeline twice.
