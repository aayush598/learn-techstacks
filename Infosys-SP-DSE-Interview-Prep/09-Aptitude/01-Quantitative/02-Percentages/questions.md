# Quantitative — Percentages Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What is a percentage and its conversion rules?
**A:** A percentage is a fraction with base 100: x% = x/100. Convert by moving the decimal two places; fractions like 25% = 1/4, 33.33% = 1/3 are the anchors.

## Q2: What is 20% of 150 plus 10% of 250?
**A:** 20% of 150 = 30; 10% of 250 = 25; sum = 55. Compute percentages separately then combine — never 'add percentages of different bases'.

## Q3: A price increases 25%, then decreases 25%. Net change?
**A:** Let price = 100; after increase 125, after decrease 93.75 → net -6.25%. Equal % up then down always nets a LOSS — the classic trap answer (net is not 0).

## Q4: What is the difference between percentage and percentage points?
**A:** Going from 10% to 15% is an increase of 5 percentage points but a 50% increase (relative) — the two vocabularies must be kept distinct.

## Q5: Successive discounts 20% and 10% — single equivalent discount?
**A:** Let price=100 → 80 → 72, so single discount of 28%. Successive discount formula: a + b - ab/100 = 20+10-2 = 28%.

## Q6: If a number increases 12%, what is the multiplier?
**A:** Final = original * 1.12; for a decrease of 12%, multiplier 0.88. Multipliers chain directly for multi-step changes (1.12 * 0.88 ≠ 1).

## Q7: A student scores 85% in an 80-mark paper. How many marks?
**A:** Marks = 85% × 80 = 68. Percentage as fraction of total is the fundamental conversion in score-based aptitude.

## Q8: What is 33⅓% of 90?
**A:** 33⅓% = 1/3, so 1/3 × 90 = 30. Common fraction-percent pairs (1/3, 2/3, 1/6, 5/6) are worth memorising.

## Q9: State the formula used to solve 02 percentages problems quickly in aptitude tests.
**A:** Every aptitude topic rests on a small set of formulas and unit-adjustments; write the formula, plug the numbers, and always sanity-check the units (hours/minutes, percent, ratio) before finalising — most errors are unit errors.

## Q10: Describe a shortcut technique for 02 percentages.
**A:** Look for symmetric/divisible structure, cancellation in fractions, or base-value anchoring (e.g., assume a convenient total like 100 or LCM). Shortcuts reduce arithmetic but should not skip the conceptual check.

## Q12: What are the typical mistakes students make in 02 percentages?
