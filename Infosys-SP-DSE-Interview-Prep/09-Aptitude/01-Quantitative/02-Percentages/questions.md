1. **What is a percentage and its conversion rules?**
   - A percentage is a fraction with base 100: x% = x/100. Convert by moving the decimal two places; fractions like 25% = 1/4, 33.33% = 1/3 are the anchors.

2. **What is 20% of 150 plus 10% of 250?**
   - 20% of 150 = 30; 10% of 250 = 25; sum = 55. Compute percentages separately then combine — never 'add percentages of different bases'.

3. **A price increases 25%, then decreases 25%. Net change?**
   - Let price = 100; after increase 125, after decrease 93.75 → net -6.25%. Equal % up then down always nets a LOSS — the classic trap answer (net is not 0).

4. **What is the difference between percentage and percentage points?**
   - Going from 10% to 15% is an increase of 5 percentage points but a 50% increase (relative) — the two vocabularies must be kept distinct.

5. **Successive discounts 20% and 10% — single equivalent discount?**
   - Let price=100 → 80 → 72, so single discount of 28%. Successive discount formula: a + b - ab/100 = 20+10-2 = 28%.

6. **If a number increases 12%, what is the multiplier?**
   - Final = original * 1.12; for a decrease of 12%, multiplier 0.88. Multipliers chain directly for multi-step changes (1.12 * 0.88 ≠ 1).

7. **A student scores 85% in an 80-mark paper. How many marks?**
   - Marks = 85% × 80 = 68. Percentage as fraction of total is the fundamental conversion in score-based aptitude.

8. **What is 33⅓% of 90?**
   - 33⅓% = 1/3, so 1/3 × 90 = 30. Common fraction-percent pairs (1/3, 2/3, 1/6, 5/6) are worth memorising.
