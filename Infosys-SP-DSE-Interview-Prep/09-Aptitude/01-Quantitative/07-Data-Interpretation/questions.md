1. **What is data interpretation and what charts appear?**
   - Reading meaning from tables, bar charts, line graphs, pie charts, and caselets — computing percentages, ratios, and changes from presented numbers.

2. **How do you read a pie chart?**
   - Each slice = its percentage of the total; actual values = (% × total)/100. Convert angles too: slice angle/360 × total.

3. **What is the formula for percentage change from a table?**
   - (New - Old)/Old × 100 — the single most-used DI formula; watch the direction (increase vs decrease) and base year.

4. **How do you compute an average from a grouped table?**
   - Sum of (value × frequency) / total frequency — weighted average when rows carry counts; also check median row position cases.

5. **How do you approximate ratios quickly?**
   - Compare fractions by cross-multiplying or aligning denominators; approximate the leading digits — most DI questions allow sensible rounding (mark 'approximately').

6. **What is the difference between percentage and 'times' comparisons?**
   - 'A is 25% more than B' → A = 1.25B; 'A is 0.25 times B' means A = 0.25B — small phrasing changes, correct base identification required.

7. **What is a caselet and how do you read it?**
   - A paragraph of data with no chart — extract the numbers into a table first, then answer; the 'make your own structure' skill DI questions test.

8. **How do you allocate time in a DI set?**
   - Skim the set, answer easy (single-lookup) questions first, and manage 'comprehension' sets last — speed on trivial reads beats accuracy slowdowns elsewhere.

9. **State the formula used to solve 07 data interpretation problems quickly in aptitude tests.**
   - Every aptitude topic rests on a small set of formulas and unit-adjustments; write the formula, plug the numbers, and always sanity-check the units (hours/minutes, percent, ratio) before finalising — most errors are unit errors.

10. **Describe a shortcut technique for 07 data interpretation.**
   - Look for symmetric/divisible structure, cancellation in fractions, or base-value anchoring (e.g., assume a convenient total like 100 or LCM). Shortcuts reduce arithmetic but should not skip the conceptual check.
