1. **What is profit and loss?**
   - Profit = SP - CP (when SP > CP); Loss = CP - SP. Percentages computed on the cost price — the base is always CP unless stated otherwise.

2. **A shopkeeper sells at 20% profit on an item costing 500. Find SP.**
   - Profit = 20% × 500 = 100; SP = 600 — or directly 500 × 1.20 = 600 (the multiplier shortcut).

3. **How do you compute profit percentage?**
   - Profit% = (SP - CP)/CP × 100. Loss% = (CP - SP)/CP × 100 — same base (CP) for both.

4. **If SP = 440 with 10% loss, what is CP?**
   - SP = 90% of CP → CP = 440/0.90 = 488.89. Recover CP from SP when loss% is known using CP = SP / (1 - loss%).

5. **What is a mark-up and discount chain?**
   - Marked price = CP × (1 + markup%); selling price = marked × (1 - discount%) — profit computed on the final SP, not the marked price. Multipliers chain.

6. **Two successive discounts over a 1000 item — 10% and 20%?**
   - 1000 → 900 → 720; net 28% off (10+20-2). Always apply successive discounts multiplicatively, never add.

7. **What is the difference between profit on SP vs CP?**
   - Profit% is on CP; some questions phrase profit as a % of SP (e.g., 'sold at 20% profit on sales') — read the base; converting: profit/SP = p → profit/CP = p/(1-p).

8. **If an article sold at 1125 gives 25% profit, find CP.**
   - CP = SP / 1.25 = 900. The SP×(multiplier) and CP×(multiplier) conversions are the two directions to master.
