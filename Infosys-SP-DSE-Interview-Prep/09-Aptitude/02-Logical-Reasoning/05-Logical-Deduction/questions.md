# Logical Reasoning — Logical Deduction Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What is logical deduction?
**A:** Deriving a conclusion that necessarily follows from given facts/conditions — using modus ponens, contrapositives, and elimination over premises.

## Q2: What is modus ponens and modus tollens?
**A:** MP: If P then Q; P; therefore Q. MT: If P then Q; not Q; therefore not P. The two valid deductive forms — invalid forms are the catchy distractors.

## Q3: What is the difference between 'if' and 'only if'?
**A:** 'P if Q' = Q → P; 'P only if Q' = P → Q — reversing them is the classic error; master the direction convention.

## Q4: What is the contrapositive of 'If it rains, the ground is wet'?
**A:** 'If the ground is not wet, it didn't rain' — the contrapositive is always logically equivalent; the converse ('wet ground → rain') is NOT.

## Q5: What is the difference between conditional and biconditional?
**A:** Conditional (→) one direction only; biconditional (↔) both directions — 'if and only if' phrases signal equivalence.

## Q6: What is the principle of least commitment?
**A:** Never infer more than the premises force — a conclusion must hold in every model of the premises, so only necessary consequences are valid.

## Q7: Give an example of an invalid conclusion that sounds correct.
**A:** All cats are mammals; some mammals are pets => 'some cats are pets' is possible but NOT guaranteed — the quantifier ambiguity trap.

## Q8: How do you approach multi-premise deduction?
**A:** Chain conditionals (A→B→C gives A→C), collect contrapositives, and mark consistent inconsistent literals — structured chaining yields the conclusions.
