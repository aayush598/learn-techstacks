# Logical Reasoning — Puzzles Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What is the approach to logical puzzles?
**A:** Convert statements into a structured grid/table (possessions, order, relationships), then apply elimination via definite facts — most puzzles fall to systematic constraint propagation.

## Q2: How do you solve 'who lives in which house' (Einstein-like) puzzles?
**A:** Build a table dimensioned by categories, fill definite placements, and chain implications; keep 'either/or' rows until a contradiction eliminates them.

## Q3: There are 3 boxes: one has gold, one silver, one both. Labels are all wrong. You open one box. How do you find all?
**A:** All labels wrong → open the box labelled 'both': it is silver or gold. If silver, the box labelled 'gold' must be 'both' (since it can't be gold) and 'silver' is gold — the classic wrong-label logic.

## Q4: A ferry can hold 2; a wolf, goat, and cabbage need safe crossing. How?
**A:** Take goat over; return; take wolf; bring goat back; take cabbage; return; take goat — the state-space solution to the classic crossing problem.

## Q5: How do you solve a scheduling/order puzzle (A sits left of B)?
**A:** Translate each clue into inequalities/adjacency marks; build the sequence by fixing the most constrained person first; backtrack on contradictions.

## Q6: What is the difference between deductive and inductive puzzle solving?
**A:** Deductively you PROVE with facts (all clues are certain); inductively you hypothesise and test. In puzzles the clues are usually deductive constraints.

## Q7: How do you verify a puzzle solution?
**A:** Check every clue against the final solution explicitly — each statement should be satisfied; one violated clue invalidates the answer.

## Q8: What is the role of 'at least/at most' phrasing in puzzles?
**A:** 'At most one' permits zero; 'exactly one' fixes one; 'at least one' forbids zero — subtle quantifiers change the constraint sets entirely.

## Q9: State the formula used to solve 02 puzzles problems quickly in aptitude tests.
**A:** Every aptitude topic rests on a small set of formulas and unit-adjustments; write the formula, plug the numbers, and always sanity-check the units (hours/minutes, percent, ratio) before finalising — most errors are unit errors.

## Q10: Describe a shortcut technique for 02 puzzles.
**A:** Look for symmetric/divisible structure, cancellation in fractions, or base-value anchoring (e.g., assume a convenient total like 100 or LCM). Shortcuts reduce arithmetic but should not skip the conceptual check.

## Q12: What are the typical mistakes students make in 02 puzzles?
