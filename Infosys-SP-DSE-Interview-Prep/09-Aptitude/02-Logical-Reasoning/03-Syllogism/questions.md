# Logical Reasoning — Syllogism Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What is a syllogism?
**A:** A deductive argument with two premises (with a common 'middle' term) and a conclusion; classic form: All/most/some statements with a quantifier structure.

## Q2: What are the four basic statement types?
**A:** All A are B (universal affirmative); No A are B (universal negative); Some A are B (particular affirmative); Some A are not B (particular negative) — shown in Venn/Euler diagrams.

## Q3: What conclusions follow from 'All A are B' and 'All B are C'?
**A:** All A are C (valid); also Some A are B follows from All A are B if A is non-empty — distinguish guaranteed vs possible conclusions.

## Q4: What is the difference between a definite and a possible conclusion?
**A:** A definite conclusion holds in every Venn diagram consistent with the premises; a 'possible' conclusion holds in at least one — syllogism tests accept only definite ones.

## Q5: Why do candidates over-infer?
**A:** People import assumptions beyond the premises (e.g., assuming existence or distribution). In syllogisms, use ONLY the premises — the classic 'fanatic of the given' trap.

## Q6: What is the 'No A are B → No B are A' conversion rule?
**A:** "No" statements are symmetric (conversion is valid); "All A are B" does NOT imply "All B are A"; "Some" statements convert only if the terms are existential.

## Q7: How do Venn diagrams help?
**A:** Draw overlapping circles for the three terms; place entities from premises; the conclusion must be true in every consistent placement — diagramming beats mental juggling.

## Q8: Give an example where a tempting conclusion is invalid.
**A:** All dogs are animals; all cats are animals => 'all dogs are cats' is invalid (the common term 'animals' is the shared class, not a subset relation) — the classic trap.

**A:** Every aptitude topic rests on a small set of formulas and unit-adjustments; write the formula, plug the numbers, and always sanity-check the units (hours/minutes, percent, ratio) before finalising — most errors are unit errors.

## Q10: Describe a shortcut technique for 03 syllogism.
**A:** Look for symmetric/divisible structure, cancellation in fractions, or base-value anchoring (e.g., assume a convenient total like 100 or LCM). Shortcuts reduce arithmetic but should not skip the conceptual check.

## Q12: What are the typical mistakes students make in 03 syllogism?
