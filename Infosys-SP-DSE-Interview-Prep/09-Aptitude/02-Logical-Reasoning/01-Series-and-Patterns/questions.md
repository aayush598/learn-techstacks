1. **How do you approach a number series?**
   - Check differences first; then ratios; then alternating (two interleaved series); then prime/square/cube patterns; then difference-of-differences — one systematic ladder.

2. **Find the next term: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ...?**
   - Differences 4,6,8,10 → next +12 → 42. Alternatively n(n+1): next = 6×7 = 42 — both give the same term (pattern recognition can overlap).

3. **Find the missing term: 3, 9, 27, 81, ...?**
   - Each term ×3 → next 243. Multiplicative series are read by ratio, not difference — the quickest detection heuristic.

4. **What is an alternating series and how do you spot it?**
   - Two interleaved sequences where odd positions follow one rule and even positions another — spot it by comparing positions 1,3,5 and 2,4,6 separately.

5. **Find the next letter: A, C, F, J, O, ...?**
   - Gaps of 2,3,4,5 → next gap 6 → U (positions 1,3,6,10,15,21). Letter series mirror number gaps with the alphabet positions.

6. **What is the difference between prime, square, and Fibonacci sequences?**
   - Prime: 2,3,5,7,11 (no simple gap); squares: 1,4,9,16 (gaps 3,5,7); Fibonacci: each term = sum of previous two — identify the family before extending.

7. **How do you handle 'difference of differences'?**
   - When the first difference isn't constant, difference the differences (second differences) — quadratic series have constant second differences (e.g., 1,4,9,16).

8. **What are the common pitfalls?**
   - Multiple valid continuations exist for short series — choose the most natural rule, and verify the rule on at least two transitions before committing.
