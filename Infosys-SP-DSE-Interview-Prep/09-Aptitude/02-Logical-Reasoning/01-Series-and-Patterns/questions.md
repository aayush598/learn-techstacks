# Logical Reasoning — Series And Patterns Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: How do you approach a number series?
**A:** Check differences first; then ratios; then alternating (two interleaved series); then prime/square/cube patterns; then difference-of-differences — one systematic ladder.

## Q2: Find the next term: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ...?
**A:** Differences 4,6,8,10 → next +12 → 42. Alternatively n(n+1): next = 6×7 = 42 — both give the same term (pattern recognition can overlap).

## Q3: Find the missing term: 3, 9, 27, 81, ...?
**A:** Each term ×3 → next 243. Multiplicative series are read by ratio, not difference — the quickest detection heuristic.

## Q4: What is an alternating series and how do you spot it?
**A:** Two interleaved sequences where odd positions follow one rule and even positions another — spot it by comparing positions 1,3,5 and 2,4,6 separately.

## Q5: Find the next letter: A, C, F, J, O, ...?
**A:** Gaps of 2,3,4,5 → next gap 6 → U (positions 1,3,6,10,15,21). Letter series mirror number gaps with the alphabet positions.

## Q6: What is the difference between prime, square, and Fibonacci sequences?
**A:** Prime: 2,3,5,7,11 (no simple gap); squares: 1,4,9,16 (gaps 3,5,7); Fibonacci: each term = sum of previous two — identify the family before extending.

## Q7: How do you handle 'difference of differences'?
**A:** When the first difference isn't constant, difference the differences (second differences) — quadratic series have constant second differences (e.g., 1,4,9,16).

## Q8: What are the common pitfalls?
**A:** Multiple valid continuations exist for short series — choose the most natural rule, and verify the rule on at least two transitions before committing.

## Q10: Describe a shortcut technique for 01 series and patterns.
**A:** Look for symmetric/divisible structure, cancellation in fractions, or base-value anchoring (e.g., assume a convenient total like 100 or LCM). Shortcuts reduce arithmetic but should not skip the conceptual check.
