# Logical Reasoning — Seating Arrangement Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What is a seating arrangement puzzle?
**A:** Placing people in a line or circle from directional clues (left/right, next to, opposite) — the constraint-satisfaction classic of logical reasoning.

## Q2: What is the difference between linear and circular arrangement?
**A:** Linear has definite left/right extremities; circular has no start/end (rotate freely) and 'opposite' meaning across the circle — approach the base differently.

## Q3: How do you start a seating problem?
**A:** Find the most specific clue (a fixed position or a direct adjacency), place it first, then attach the next-most-constrained clue — fixing anchors beats guessing.

## Q4: What does 'A is second to the right of B' mean vs 'immediately right'?
**A:** 'Second to the right' leaves one person between; 'immediately/next to' means adjacent — one-step vs two-step phrasing changes placement.

## Q5: How do you handle 'either/or' clues?
**A:** Explore both branches; derive consequences; discard the branch that contradicts a later clue — systematic case analysis is the technique.

## Q6: What is front-facing vs back-facing in circular puzzles?
**A:** If everyone faces the centre, 'right' and 'left' are reversed for people across the table — the directionality detail that flips placements.

## Q7: How do you verify a complete arrangement?
**A:** Re-check every clue against the final diagram; a correct arrangement satisfies all of them simultaneously — efficiency from correct first placement.

## Q8: What is the common mistake?
**A:** Drifting from the diagram's directions or imagining ambiguity where there is none; redraw cleanly with an arrow for 'front' and re-verify orders.
