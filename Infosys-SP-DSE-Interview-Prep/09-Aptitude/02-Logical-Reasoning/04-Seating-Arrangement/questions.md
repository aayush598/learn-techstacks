1. **What is a seating arrangement puzzle?**
   - Placing people in a line or circle from directional clues (left/right, next to, opposite) — the constraint-satisfaction classic of logical reasoning.

2. **What is the difference between linear and circular arrangement?**
   - Linear has definite left/right extremities; circular has no start/end (rotate freely) and 'opposite' meaning across the circle — approach the base differently.

3. **How do you start a seating problem?**
   - Find the most specific clue (a fixed position or a direct adjacency), place it first, then attach the next-most-constrained clue — fixing anchors beats guessing.

4. **What does 'A is second to the right of B' mean vs 'immediately right'?**
   - 'Second to the right' leaves one person between; 'immediately/next to' means adjacent — one-step vs two-step phrasing changes placement.

5. **How do you handle 'either/or' clues?**
   - Explore both branches; derive consequences; discard the branch that contradicts a later clue — systematic case analysis is the technique.

6. **What is front-facing vs back-facing in circular puzzles?**
   - If everyone faces the centre, 'right' and 'left' are reversed for people across the table — the directionality detail that flips placements.

7. **How do you verify a complete arrangement?**
   - Re-check every clue against the final diagram; a correct arrangement satisfies all of them simultaneously — efficiency from correct first placement.

8. **What is the common mistake?**
   - Drifting from the diagram's directions or imagining ambiguity where there is none; redraw cleanly with an arrow for 'front' and re-verify orders.
