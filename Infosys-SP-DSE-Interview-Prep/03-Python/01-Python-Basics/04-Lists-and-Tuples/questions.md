1. **What is a list?**
   - A mutable, ordered sequence of heterogenous objects — O(1) append/pop at end, O(n) insert/remove, supports slicing, indexing, and iteration.

2. **What is a tuple and when prefer it over a list?**
   - Immutable ordered sequence — used as dict keys, to return multiple values, and to signal fixed data; slightly cheaper and hashable.

3. **What is the difference between list.append, extend, and insert?**
   - append adds one element; extend merges an iterable's items; insert(index, x) places x at a position shifting the rest.

4. **How does list slicing work?**
   - lst[start:stop:step] returns a new list (shallow copy of references); negative indices count from the end; lst[::-1] reverses.

5. **What is the time complexity of common list ops?**
   - Index O(1), append/pop O(1) amortised, insert/delete middle O(n), membership `in` O(n), copy O(n), sort O(n log n).

6. **What is list comprehension vs map/filter?**
   - Comprehensions are idiomatic and faster than map/filter with lambdas; `[str(x) for x in nums]` reads better than `map(str, nums)` for most developers.

7. **How do you flatten a nested list?**
   - [x for sub in nested for x in sub]; generic recursion or itertools.chain.from_iterable for one level.

8. **What is a namedtuple?**
   - A tuple subclass with named fields — tuple immutability plus attribute access; used for lightweight records (Point(x=1,y=2)).

9. **What are sorting keys?**
   - sorted(lst, key=len) sorts by the key function without touching items; use reverse=True and operator.itemgetter for multi-field sorts.

10. **What is a deque and when use it over a list?**
   - collections.deque gives O(1) appendleft/popleft — ideal for queues/undo stacks; lists are O(n) at the front.
