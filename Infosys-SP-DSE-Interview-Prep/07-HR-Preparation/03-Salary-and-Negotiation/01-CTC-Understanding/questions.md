1. **How is CTC different from in-hand salary?**
   - CTC includes employer-paid components — basic, HRA, allowances, PF/gratuity, insurance, and variable pay; in-hand is what actually lands monthly after deductions — citing this distinction shows you understand offer letters.

2. **What is a variable pay or bonus component?**
   - Performance-linked pay paid periodically (often annual) based on individual and company performance — not guaranteed monthly; treat it as upside, not base.

3. **What fixed vs variable split should you expect?**
   - Fresher offers are mostly fixed with a small variable/performance component; I'd ask the recruiter for the exact split and the variable's frequency and threshold before signing.

4. **What deductions are standard in Indian offers?**
   - Employer PF + employee PF, gratuity, and professional tax reduce gross; the rest largely reaches your account — a quick deduction walkthrough shows financial literacy.

5. **What is the difference between gross and net salary?**
   - Gross = total earnings before deductions; net = what's banked after PF/gratuity/professional tax — always compute net before committing to expenses.

6. **How would you evaluate an offer beyond CTC?**
   - Effective hourly rate, training quality, role growth, project exposure, work location, and benefits (insurance, learning budget) — CTC alone overvalues less-critical-per-kg offers.

7. **What is joining bonus vs retention bonus?**
   - Joining bonus is paid at onboarding (usually clawed back if you leave within a window); retention bonus rewards staying — two different incentives to read carefully.

8. **What salary range would be acceptable for your role?**
   - State a researched, confident-but-flexible range for SP DSE — anchor on market rates for Infosys specialist freshers and say the primary driver is learning and role fit — never say 'whatever you give'.

9. **How do you plan your expenses with a variable income?**
   - Budget on fixed in-hand, treat variable pay as savings/investment, and keep an emergency buffer — a disciplined answer shows maturity in stability and planning.

10. **What is the employer's perspective on Fresher compensation?**
   - They price in training cost, deployment velocity, and market bands — so negotiating leverage comes from demonstrated skills, not needs; I'd negotiate on evidence, not urgency.
