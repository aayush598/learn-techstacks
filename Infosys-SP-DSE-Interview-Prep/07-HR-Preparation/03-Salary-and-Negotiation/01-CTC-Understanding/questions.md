# Salary And Negotiation — Ctc Understanding Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: How is CTC different from in-hand salary?
**A:** CTC includes employer-paid components — basic, HRA, allowances, PF/gratuity, insurance, and variable pay; in-hand is what actually lands monthly after deductions — citing this distinction shows you understand offer letters.

## Q2: What is a variable pay or bonus component?
**A:** Performance-linked pay paid periodically (often annual) based on individual and company performance — not guaranteed monthly; treat it as upside, not base.

## Q3: What fixed vs variable split should you expect?
**A:** Fresher offers are mostly fixed with a small variable/performance component; I'd ask the recruiter for the exact split and the variable's frequency and threshold before signing.

## Q4: What deductions are standard in Indian offers?
**A:** Employer PF + employee PF, gratuity, and professional tax reduce gross; the rest largely reaches your account — a quick deduction walkthrough shows financial literacy.

## Q5: What is the difference between gross and net salary?
**A:** Gross = total earnings before deductions; net = what's banked after PF/gratuity/professional tax — always compute net before committing to expenses.

## Q6: How would you evaluate an offer beyond CTC?
**A:** Effective hourly rate, training quality, role growth, project exposure, work location, and benefits (insurance, learning budget) — CTC alone overvalues less-critical-per-kg offers.

## Q7: What is joining bonus vs retention bonus?
**A:** Joining bonus is paid at onboarding (usually clawed back if you leave within a window); retention bonus rewards staying — two different incentives to read carefully.

## Q8: What salary range would be acceptable for your role?
**A:** State a researched, confident-but-flexible range for SP DSE — anchor on market rates for Infosys specialist freshers and say the primary driver is learning and role fit — never say 'whatever you give'.

## Q9: How do you plan your expenses with a variable income?
**A:** Budget on fixed in-hand, treat variable pay as savings/investment, and keep an emergency buffer — a disciplined answer shows maturity in stability and planning.

## Q10: What is the employer's perspective on Fresher compensation?
**A:** They price in training cost, deployment velocity, and market bands — so negotiating leverage comes from demonstrated skills, not needs; I'd negotiate on evidence, not urgency.
