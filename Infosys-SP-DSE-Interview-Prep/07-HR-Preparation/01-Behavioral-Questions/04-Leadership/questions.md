1. **Have you ever taken a leadership role?**
   - Technically yes — I led the AI/backend workstream in my SIH team and drove open-source PRs to completion: I set the architecture, assigned tasks, and owned integration risk end-to-end.

2. **What does leadership mean to you?**
   - Raising the team's output by removing ambiguity: clarity of goal, clarity of ownership, and psychological safety to fail fast. Not rank — responsibility.

3. **How do you motivate teammates?**
   - By making the work make sense — connecting tasks to the end goal, giving early wins, and letting people own their modules; motivation is mostly autonomy and clarity.

4. **What would you do as a new lead in your first week?**
   - Listen before deciding: one-on-ones, map the existing codebase and pipelines, find the top bottleneck (usually the CI/deploy pain), and fix one visible thing quickly to earn trust.

5. **How do you assign work to a team you don't know yet?**
   - Start with a skills/stakes mapping conversation, assign by strengths and growth goals, and add code-reviews as a safety net until I learn each person's quality bar.

6. **Describe a time you influenced without authority.**
   - In the SIH team I convinced peers to adopt a shared Git workflow for integration — I demonstrated the benefit with a five-minute demo and got buy-in voluntarily, without being the 'leader'.

7. **How do you handle a hard deadline as a lead?**
   - Work backwards from the deadline: freeze scope, parallelise by module boundaries, gate promotions by tests, and communicate risk honestly earlier — panic doesn't ship software.

8. **What is the difference between a leader and a manager?**
   - A manager administers scope, process, and performance; a leader sets direction, removes blockers, and models the standards — I'd aim to blend both, weighted by situation.

9. **How do you celebrate a win as a lead?**
   - Attribute it loudly to the team by name and log what worked (retro note) so the next project benefits — wins are fuel and documentation at once.

10. **What would your team say about you as a lead?**
   - That I'm clear about what 'done' means, that I code beside them rather than above them, and that I take the final responsibility for integration failures — that's the reputation I build.
