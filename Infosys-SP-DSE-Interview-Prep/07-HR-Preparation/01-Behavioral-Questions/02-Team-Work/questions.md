1. **Describe a time you worked in a team.**
   - Use my SIH 2024 finalist project: a 4-member team built a full solution from ideation to demo under a deadline — my role covered the AI/backend, and we soured by merging modules daily (Git), not at the end.

2. **How do you handle disagreements in a team?**
   - I separate ego from evidence: discuss the constraint (time vs correctness vs cost) rather than the person, write down both options' trade-offs, and if it's not urgent, prototype the cheaper option to decide with data — e.g., choosing between prompt engineering vs fine-tuning for a demo.

3. **How do you handle a teammate who isn't pulling their weight?**
   - First: clarify if it's a blocker or motivation (ask, don't accuse). Next: reassign scope to their strengths and offer pair sessions. If it persists, escalate with facts — a professional, non-personal approach interviewers want to hear.

4. **What role do you usually take in a group?**
   - I'm naturally the systems person — I build the integration layer (APIs, data flow, CI) that makes everyone else's work composable; but I've also learned to step back and let specialists lead their part to avoid being a bottleneck.

5. **How do you divide tasks in a project?**
   - By ownership boundaries, not arbitrary files: each person owns a module with a defined interface, integrations happen on a shared branch with CI tests — the git workflow is itself the coordination mechanism.

6. **Tell me about a team achievement you're proud of.**
   - ScriptVector shipped end-to-end by turning the 'suggest relevant snippets' idea into a RAG pipeline (Gemini embeddings + Agno + SQLite) — the team contribution was my embedding/index design that made it fast enough to feel usable.

7. **How do you give constructive feedback?**
   - Situation-Behaviour-Impact: specific example, the behaviour, the consequence — then a concrete suggestion. Framed as 'let's improve the outcome', never as blame.

8. **How do you onboard a new team member?**
   - A short architecture walkthrough, a starter issue with a clear contract, and pairing on the first merge — I've done this informally at internships and it accelerates everyone.

9. **How do you keep the team motivated under crunch?**
   - Visible progress: a shared board with small wins, slicing tasks into shippable chunks, and celebrating checkpoints — momentum beats coercion on a deadline.

10. **Have you led a group? How?**
   - I've led technical workstreams — I set the architecture, distributed tasks by strengths, and unblocked failures — while remaining hands-on; leadership for me is coordination + accountability, not authority.
