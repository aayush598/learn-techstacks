1. **What is a convolution operation?**
   - Sliding a small filter over the input computing dot products — detecting local patterns (edges, textures) with weight sharing; the CNN building block.

2. **What is a convolutional layer?**
   - Multiple filters produce feature maps; strides control step size; padding preserves spatial dims. Each filter learns one local pattern.

3. **What are the components of a CNN?**
   - Conv layers (feature extraction) + pooling (downsampling) + fully-connected head (classification); batchnorm and dropout interleave — the LeNet-AlexNet lineage.

4. **What is pooling and its types?**
   - Max pooling (activations, noise-robust, default) and average pooling (smoothing) downsample, adding translation invariance and shrinking compute.

5. **What is the receptive field?**
   - The input region a neuron sees — deeper layers aggregate wider context; stacking convolutions grows it without huge kernels.

6. **What is stride and padding?**
   - Stride: filter step (controls output size); padding 'same' preserves spatial dims, 'valid' shrinks them — both tune downsampling.

7. **What are known CNN architectures?**
   - LeNet (1998), AlexNet, VGG (deep small kernels), ResNet (residuals), Inception, EfficientNet — name ResNet's skip connections as the modern turning point.

8. **What is the difference between a fully-connected and convolutional layer?**
   - FC: full connections, spatially agnostic, parameter heavy. Conv: local receptive fields, weight sharing, translation-equivariant — ideal for images.

9. **What data augmentation for CNNs?**
   - Rotation, flip, crop, colour jitter, noise — teaching invariance and extending the effective dataset cheaply.

10. **What is transfer learning with CNNs?**
   - Fine-tune pre-trained ImageNet backbones (ResNet, EfficientNet) — freezing early layers and retraining the head for new datasets efficiently.

11. **What is a 1x1 convolution?**
   - A point-wise conv mixing channels while keeping spatial dims — cheap dimensionality mixing used in Inception/ResNet bottleneck blocks.

12. **What are limitations of CNNs?**
   - Local receptive fields (hard to capture global context without depth), fixed grid assumption, and large data needs; attention models address some of these.
