# Nlp — Bert And Transformers Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What is BERT?
**A:** Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers — an encoder-only transformer pre-trained with masked language modelling + next-sentence prediction; the understanding model.

## Q2: What is the masked language modelling (MLM) objective?
**A:** Random tokens are masked; BERT predicts them from full bidirectional context — unlike GPT's causal left-to-right objective.

## Q3: What is the difference between BERT and GPT in attention?
**A:** BERT: bidirectional attention (sees both sides). GPT: causal/masked attention (left only). Encoder vs decoder — the two dominant pre-training paradigms.

## Q4: What are BERT's input embeddings?
**A:** Token embedding + segment embedding (sentence A/B) + positional embedding, headed by special [CLS]/[SEP] tokens — the encoding recipe.

## Q5: What is the CLS token and [CLS] pooling?
**A:** The first special token whose final embedding summarises the sequence — commonly fed to a classifier head for sentence-level tasks.

## Q6: What is fine-tuning BERT?
**A:** Adding a task head (classification/QA) and training with a low learning rate — task-specific adaptation of pre-trained language knowledge.

## Q7: What are the sizes of BERT (base/large)?
**A:** Base: 12 layers of 12 attention heads / 768 hidden (110M params); Large: 24 layers / 16 heads / 1024 hidden (340M) — the canonical parameter map.

## Q8: What is DistilBERT / knowledge-distilled BERT?
**A:** A 40% smaller student distilled to retain 97% of BERT's performance at ~60% speed — efficient-deployment NLP.

## Q9: How does BERT handle sentence-pair tasks?
**A:** Two segments joined with [SEP] and segment embeddings; NSP/classification heads decide entailment or similarity.

## Q10: What is a tokenizer's role specific to BERT?
**A:** WordPiece splitting with [UNK]/[MASK]/[CLS]/[SEP]; attention mask distinguishes real tokens from padding — the encoding that transformers consume.
