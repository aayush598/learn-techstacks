# Nlp — Text Preprocessing Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What are the typical text cleaning steps?
**A:** Lowercasing (task-dependent), Unicode normalisation, expanding contractions, removing URLs/HTML/emails, and controlling for noise — keep only what helps the model.

## Q2: What is stopword removal and when is it safe?
**A:** Dropping frequent function words (the, and) for bag-of-words classifiers — but harmful for neural/contextual models; token-tuned models like having them.

## Q3: What is stemming vs lemmatisation?
**A:** Stemming chops suffixes crudely (running -> runn); lemmatisation reduces to dictionary root (running -> run) using POS/lexicon — lemmatisation is cleaner but slower.

## Q4: What is tokenisation at the word vs subword level?
**A:** Word tokens blow up vocabularies and leave unknowns; BPE/WordPiece split unknown words into known pieces — the standard for sequence models.

## Q5: What is a vocabulary and OOV handling?
**A:** The finite set of known tokens; out-of-vocabulary words either become <UNK> or split into subwords — large-vocab coverage reduces information loss.

## Q6: What is whitespace tokenisation?
**A:** Splitting on spaces — simplest but breaks on punctuation/split verbs; the baseline to compare smarter tokenisers against.

## Q7: What is padding/truncation for batching?
**A:** Sequence models need fixed-length batches — pad short texts to max length and truncate long ones (with [PAD] tokens masked in attention).

## Q8: What are special tokens (<CLS>, <SEP>, <PAD>, <MASK>)?
**A:** Reserved tokens carrying structural information: CLS aggregates, SEP separates sentences, PAD preserves shape, MASK highlights the training target.

## Q9: What is sentence splitting?
**A:** Splitting documents into sentences on punctuation/newlines — but abbreviations and decimals break naive rules; spaCy/NLTK handle edge cases.

## Q10: What is normalisation of emojis/hashtags?
**A:** Mapping emojis to keywords or dropping them; splitting hashtags — the noisy-social-text step that measurably helps social-media models.
