1. **What is NLP?**
   - Natural Language Processing: the field making machines understand, generate, and manipulate human language — from spam filters to LLM assistants.

2. **What is a language model?**
   - A probability distribution over token sequences — P(w_n | w_1..w_{n-1}); n-gram counts historically, neural transformers now.

3. **What is tokenisation?**
   - Splitting text into tokens (words, subwords) — WordPiece/BPE for modern LLMs balances vocab size and unknown-word handling.

4. **What is a bag-of-words representation?**
   - A vector of word counts/booleans ignoring order — simple, sparse, loses syntax; the baseline for classical text classification.

5. **What is TF-IDF?**
   - Term-frequency × inverse-document-frequency reweighting: common-words down, distinctive-words up — a classical information-retrieval feature.

6. **What are word embeddings (Word2Vec/GloVe)?**
   - Dense vectors where semantically similar words are close (king - man + woman ≈ queen) — learned from co-occurrence statistics.

7. **What is the difference between BOW, TF-IDF, word2vec, and contextual embeddings?**
   - BOW: orderless counts. TF-IDF: adds corpus-level importance. Word2Vec: static dense vectors. Contextual (BERT): meaning shifts by sentence — each step more expressive.

8. **What is a n-gram and an n-gram language model?**
   - Chains of n consecutive tokens; counts-based next-token probability with smoothing — the classical predecessor beat by neural LMs.

9. **What is perplexity?**
   - Exponential of average negative log-likelihood — how 'surprised' the model is; lower perplexity = better language modelling.

10. **What is a corpus, and why does data dominate NLP?**
   - The collected text a model trains on; vocabulary, domain, and size decide what the model can express — 'garbage in, patternless out' applies doubly.
