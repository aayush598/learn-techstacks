# Ml Fundamentals — Regularization Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What is regularisation and why use it?
**A:** Penalising large weights to reduce overfitting by biasing toward simpler models — the model-complexity lever in the bias-variance trade-off.

## Q2: What is L1 (Lasso) regularisation?
**A:** Adds |w| penalty — drives some weights exactly to zero yielding sparse, feature-selecting models; great for high-dimensional inputs.

## Q3: What is L2 (Ridge) regularisation?
**A:** Adds w^2 penalty — shrinks weights toward zero without sparsity; handles multicollinearity and smooths coefficients.

## Q4: What is the difference between L1 and L2?
**A:** L1 clips coefficients to exact zero (feature selection); L2 only shrinks them — L1 sparsity vs L2 stability; elastic net combines both.

## Q5: What is early stopping?
**A:** Halting training when validation loss stops improving (patience) — avoids obvious overfitting cheaply; the pragmatic DL regulariser.

## Q6: What is dropout?
**A:** Randomly zeroing neurons during training acts as ensemble averaging — the deep-learning regularisation workhorse (rate ~0.2-0.5).

## Q7: What is batch normalisation?
**A:** Normalising layer inputs per batch stabilises and speeds training, adding slight regularisation — it reshapes the optimisation landscape.

## Q8: What is data augmentation as regularisation?
**A:** Adding transformed copies (rotation, noise, cropping) — enlarging effective training data and teaching invariance; especially in vision/audio.

## Q9: What is weight decay vs L2?
**A:** Weight decay shrinks weights each update — mathematically near L2 for standard SGD but distinct with adaptive optimisers (AdamW) — worth distinguishing.

## Q10: What is the difference between regularisation and hyperparameter tuning?
**A:** Regularisation changes the effective model family via constraints; tuning picks the strength (lambda, drop rate) — tuning searches a regularisation schedule.
