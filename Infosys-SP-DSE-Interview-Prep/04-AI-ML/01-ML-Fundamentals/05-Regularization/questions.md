1. **What is regularisation and why use it?**
   - Penalising large weights to reduce overfitting by biasing toward simpler models — the model-complexity lever in the bias-variance trade-off.

2. **What is L1 (Lasso) regularisation?**
   - Adds |w| penalty — drives some weights exactly to zero yielding sparse, feature-selecting models; great for high-dimensional inputs.

3. **What is L2 (Ridge) regularisation?**
   - Adds w^2 penalty — shrinks weights toward zero without sparsity; handles multicollinearity and smooths coefficients.

4. **What is the difference between L1 and L2?**
   - L1 clips coefficients to exact zero (feature selection); L2 only shrinks them — L1 sparsity vs L2 stability; elastic net combines both.

5. **What is early stopping?**
   - Halting training when validation loss stops improving (patience) — avoids obvious overfitting cheaply; the pragmatic DL regulariser.

6. **What is dropout?**
   - Randomly zeroing neurons during training acts as ensemble averaging — the deep-learning regularisation workhorse (rate ~0.2-0.5).

7. **What is batch normalisation?**
   - Normalising layer inputs per batch stabilises and speeds training, adding slight regularisation — it reshapes the optimisation landscape.

8. **What is data augmentation as regularisation?**
   - Adding transformed copies (rotation, noise, cropping) — enlarging effective training data and teaching invariance; especially in vision/audio.

9. **What is weight decay vs L2?**
   - Weight decay shrinks weights each update — mathematically near L2 for standard SGD but distinct with adaptive optimisers (AdamW) — worth distinguishing.

10. **What is the difference between regularisation and hyperparameter tuning?**
   - Regularisation changes the effective model family via constraints; tuning picks the strength (lambda, drop rate) — tuning searches a regularisation schedule.
