1. **What is feature engineering?**
   - Transforming raw data into predictive inputs: encoding, scaling, binning, interactions, and derived quantities — often worth more than model choice.

2. **What is one-hot encoding vs label encoding?**
   - One-hot: k columns for k categories (no ordinality, works for trees/linear). Label encoding: integers — only for ordinal data or targets; avoid imposing false order.

3. **What is standardisation vs normalisation?**
   - Standardisation: zero-mean/unit-var (z-scores) for Gaussian-ish and distance models; min-max normalisation: scale to [0,1]. Choose per algorithm.

4. **What is handling of missing values in features?**
   - Drop (if random/tiny), mean/median/mode impute, model-based imputation, or flag the missingness as a feature — the safer approaches than silently dropping rows.

5. **What are the datetime/date features you'd extract?**
   - Day-of-week, month, hour, is_weekend, season, elapsed time — turning timestamps into pattern-capturing columns for forecasting/ranking.

6. **What is feature scaling's effect on gradient descent?**
   - Unscaled features make the loss surface elongated, slowing convergence; scaling features speeds up optimisation and improves distance metrics.

7. **What is an interaction feature?**
   - A synthetic column combining two variables (product/sum) — capturing effects a linear model can't express, e.g., price × quantity = revenue.

8. **What is a text-to-feature pipeline?**
   - TF-IDF/bag-of-words, word embeddings, or LLM encodings followed by dimensionality reduction — depending on the downstream model.

9. **How do you remove redundant features?**
   - Correlation/VIff inspection, mutual-information scoring, tree-importances, and backward elimination — reducing multicollinearity and overfitting.

10. **What is the curse of dimensionality?**
   - As feature count grows, data becomes sparse so distances lose meaning — models overfit and clusters dissolve; dimensionality reduction counters it.
