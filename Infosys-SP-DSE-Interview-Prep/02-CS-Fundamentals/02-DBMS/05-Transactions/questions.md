# Dbms — Transactions Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What is a transaction?
**A:** A logical unit of work composed of one or more statements executed with ACID guarantees; it either commits fully or rolls back fully.

## Q2: How do you start/end a transaction in SQL?
**A:** BEGIN (or BEGIN TRANSACTION) starts; COMMIT ends successfully; ROLLBACK aborts. Nested via savepoints (SAVEPOINT sp; ROLLBACK TO sp).

## Q3: What is a savepoint?
**A:** A named checkpoint inside a transaction; you can roll back to a savepoint without aborting the whole transaction — fine-grained error recovery.

## Q4: What is a lock and why needed?
**A:** A mechanism restricting concurrent access to a row/table while a transaction holds it, ensuring isolation — shared (read) and exclusive (write) locks are the basics.

## Q5: What is a deadlock?
**A:** Two transactions each hold a lock the other needs and wait forever. DBs detect cycles and abort one offender (rollback) — retry logic on the app side is expected.

## Q6: What is a lost update?
**A:** Two concurrent transactions both read a value and write back, the second overwriting the first's change without seeing it — avoided by locking or MVCC.

## Q7: How does MVCC prevent blocking reads?
**A:** Each transaction works on its own snapshot/version of rows; writers don't block readers and vice versa (PostgreSQL). Conflicts surface as version conflicts at commit.

## Q8: What is optimistic vs pessimistic locking?
**A:** Optimistic: read without locks, verify the version at write (raise conflict if changed). Pessimistic: acquire locks upfront (SELECT ... FOR UPDATE). Optimistic scales better under low conflict.

## Q9: What happens if the app loses connection mid-transaction?
**A:** The DB (or driver) rolls back the uncommitted transaction; app-level retry should re-execute idempotently — a distributed-systems interview point.

## Q10: How do you test transaction behaviour?
**A:** Open two sessions; one inserts without committing; assert the other can't see the change (READ COMMITTED); commit; assert it appears — this is the practical isolation check.
