# 02 — Discounts, Offers & Scarcity (Use Sparingly & Correctly)

Discounts attract money fast, but used wrongly they destroy your value. Use them as **launch and conversion tools only**.

## When To Discount (good)

- **Launch offer** to get your first 1–3 testimonials/reviews.
- **Referral bonus** — discount/reward for customers who bring new business.
- **Friends & family** early adopters (to get proof).
- **Bundle discount** when they buy multiple add-ons at once.
- **Founding pre-sale** — early customers get lifetime-locked lower price (builds goodwill + cash now).
- **Annual prepay discount** — reward paying the annual license upfront.

## When NOT To Discount (bad)

- To every customer who haggles (train them to expect discounts).
- As your default pricing.
- Before you have proof — you'll anchor too low forever.
- For unlimited free work disguised as "discount."

## How To Frame Discounts (Psychology)

- Frame as **value**, not price drop: "For this month, the Complete package includes free data migration (worth ₹1,500) at no extra cost."
- Use **deadlines honestly**: "This launch price is for the first 3 customers this month."
- Offer **extras** (free training, spare pendrive) instead of cutting core price — protects your rate.

## The Referral Discount System (RECOMMENDED)

- **Referrer reward:** Give existing customer ₹/$, or free months of license, when their referral buys.
- **Referee reward:** Small discount on setup for the new customer.
- Keep it **simple & tracked** (Part 10, Part 14 templates).

## Sample "Founding Customer" Offer (First 3–5)

> "I'm onboarding my first 3 customers this month at a founding launch price, and I'll lock that price for you for life as long as you stay with me. After that, prices go up."

This creates **scarcity + urgency + loyalty** and gets your first cash fast.

## Price Anchoring In Practice

When presenting:
- Show Premium first, then Complete, then Starter.
- Recommend Complete: "Most shop owners choose Complete — it's the best value and does everything you need."
- Always give your **recommendation** — owners want guidance, not choice-paralysis.
