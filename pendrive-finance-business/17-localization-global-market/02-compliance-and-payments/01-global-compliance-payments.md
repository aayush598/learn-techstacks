# 01 — Global Compliance & Cross-Border Payments (Sell Anywhere Safely)

When you sell to other countries, three things change: legal/compliance, payments, and language/time. Manage them properly.

## Cross-Border Selling Checklist

### 1. Legal & Tax
- **Know your home-market rules** for exporting services; keep a CA/tax advisor.
- **Foreign customers:** You generally invoice them; you may owe home-country tax on foreign income (report it honestly, Part 07).
- **Local registration abroad:** For occasional foreign sales you usually DON'T need to register there. If you build real sustained revenue in a country, get local advice (sales tax/VAT, e.g., EU VAT on digital services — thresholds may apply).
- **Data transfers:** Ensure you handle customer data per both your privacy rules and theirs (Part 07). Keep encryption + minimal data.

### 2. Payments (international)
Open accounts/services that accept foreign money:
- **Wise / Payoneer / PayPal / Stripe** — receive USD/EUR/GBP etc.
- **Bank transfer (SWIFT)** for large amounts.
- Check **fees & FX** — factor into price or show "pay in your currency."
- For emerging markets, local wallets (GCash, M-Pesa, etc.) may be needed if you target there.

### 3. Pricing & Currency
- Price in a stable currency (USD/EUR) for online work, or the customer's local currency if you have local payment rails.
- Watch **exchange rates** — decide whether to lock prices in USD vs local.
- Be clear about **taxes/fees** in your invoice.

### 4. Language & Time
- Provide UI + support in the customer's language where feasible (platform i18n).
- Set clear support hours/timezone expectations.
- Use **video demos + recorded walkthroughs** so language barriers are lower (Part 06).

### 5. Contract & Delivery
- Use a **cross-border-friendly agreement** (English; clear governing law) (Part 07).
- Ship pendrives with **tracking + insurance** for value.
- Offer **digital delivery option** (they provide their own drive / a flashable image / an installer) to reduce shipping cost — designed around delivery, Part 09.

## Recommended "Going Global" Order (low-risk)

1. **Master your home market first** (India) — prove the playbook (Part 15).
2. **Add online/remote sales** within your country (Part 13).
3. **Expand to nearby/geographically-easy countries** with similar needs (English/regional).
4. **Then English + Western markets** for higher-ticket remote sales.
5. **Add local payment rails + compliance** as each market proves itself.

> Never spread thin across 10 countries early. Master one market at a time.

## The "Remote Delivery" Model (reduces friction)

- Customer order online → you build/customize → produce a **pendrive image or installer** → ship physically OR guide them to prepare a drive.
- Provide **video onboarding** (Part 14) instead of in-person.
- Charge: setup fee + annual license + shipping/hardware.
- This is how you scale beyond your city without leaving home.

## Digital Goods / Installer Option (advanced)

- If you productize a **template installer** the customer can run, selling becomes lighter (Part 12/13).
- Balance: shipping a ready drive = higher value + more reliable; installer = cheaper/faster. Offer both, price accordingly.
