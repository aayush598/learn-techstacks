# Launch Strategy: Making a Splash as a Solo Founder

## The Launch Mindset

A launch is not the end of your journey. It's the beginning. The goal of a launch is not to "go viral" or hit #1 on Product Hunt. The goal is to build momentum — to get your first customers, your first feedback, and your first believers.

For solo founders, a successful launch means:
- 50-200 signups (pre-seed customers)
- 3-10 paying customers (validation + early revenue)
- A list of people who believe in what you're building
- Feedback that makes your product better

Anything above that is a bonus.

## Pre-Launch: The 90-Day Buildup

A launch doesn't start when you hit "Publish." It starts 90 days before.

### 90 Days Before Launch: Audience Building

```
Goal: Have 500+ people who know you're building something

Actions:
- Start building in public on Twitter/X
  "I'm building [product] to solve [problem]. Follow along."
- Write about the problem (not the solution) on LinkedIn
- Join communities where your ICP hangs out
- Start an email list (even 50 people counts)
- Share your journey on Indie Hackers

Metrics to hit by launch day:
- Twitter/X: 200+ followers
- LinkedIn: 300+ relevant connections
- Email list: 200+ subscribers
- Indie Hackers thread: 50+ followers
```

### 60 Days Before Launch: Tease the Solution

```
Goal: People know what you're building and why it matters

Actions:
- Share screenshots of early development
- Write about your approach to the problem
- Share your pricing philosophy
- Ask for input on features (makes people feel invested)
- Start a waitlist (collect emails early)

Teaser examples:
- "Here's the dashboard for [product]. What metrics matter most to you?"
- "I'm pricing [product] at $X/month. Too much? Too little?"
- "My approach to [problem] is different because [reason]."
```

### 30 Days Before Launch: Build Anticipation

```
Goal: People are looking forward to your launch

Actions:
- Announce launch date: "Launching [product] on [date]"
- Share launch page/landing page
- Create a "Coming Soon" page with email capture
- Give early access to a few people for feedback
- Share testimonials from beta testers
- Create countdown posts

Launch date checklist:
□ Product is stable (no critical bugs)
□ Onboarding flow works
□ Payment processing works
□ Support system in place (email, chat)
□ Pricing page is live
□ FAQ page is live
□ Analytics are tracking
```

### 14 Days Before Launch: The Pre-Launch Sprint

```
Week before launch:
- Finalize all launch materials
- Test everything (onboarding, payments, email flows)
- Warm up your audience with daily posts
- Recruit launch supporters (friends, beta users, community)
- Prepare your Product Hunt/HN/BetaList submissions
- Write your launch post/comments

Day before launch:
- Send "Launching TOMORROW" email to list
- Final post: "Tomorrow is the day"
- Get 8 hours of sleep
- Prepare to be responsive all day
```

## The Launch Channels

### Channel 1: Product Hunt

**When:** Launch day (Tuesday-Thursday are best)

**Preparation (2-4 weeks before):**

```
1. Create a Product Hunt maker account
2. Find a hunter (someone with PH followers to feature your launch)
   - Ask your network
   - Look for hunters in your space
   - The hunter's followers will see your launch
3. Prepare your assets:
   - Product name and tagline (test with friends)
   - Logo and images (154x154 minimum)
   - GIF demo (best-performing asset — show key workflow)
   - First comment (tell your story)
   - Description (focus on problem, not features)
4. Prepare your team:
   - Ask friends/followers to upvote on launch day
   - Ask customers to leave reviews
   - Prepare to respond to every comment
```

**Launch day on Product Hunt:**

```
Your schedule:
- 12:01 AM PT: Product launches (your post goes live)
- Morning: Respond to every comment (within 1 hour)
- Mid-day: Engage with questions
- Afternoon: Thank supporters
- Evening: Share results with your community

What NOT to do:
- Ask for upvotes (against PH rules)
- Ignore negative comments (respond gracefully)
- Spam your community (share once, don't beg)

What to track:
- Upvotes (goal: 100-500)
- Comments (goal: 20-50 meaningful discussions)
- Website visits from PH
- Signups from PH (goal: 50-200)
- Pays from PH (goal: 3-10)

After PH:
- Email everyone who signed up (within 24 hours)
- Thank your supporters
- Share results (transparency builds trust)
- Follow up with leads
```

**Product Hunt results by quality:**

```
Top results (top 5 product of day):
- 500-2,000 upvotes
- 2,000-10,000 visits
- 100-500 signups
- 10-50 paid customers

Good results (featured but not top):
- 100-500 upvotes
- 500-3,000 visits
- 50-200 signups
- 3-20 paid customers

Modest results (not featured):
- 20-100 upvotes
- 100-500 visits
- 10-50 signups
- 0-5 paid customers
```

### Channel 2: Hacker News

**Best for:** Developer tools, technical founders, products with hacker appeal

**Preparation:**

```
1. Create a HN account with some history (comments, submissions)
2. Know the culture (journalists, hype, marketing spin are disliked)
3. Prepare a technical story that's interesting

Your submission:
- Title: Should be intriguing, not promotional
  - Good: "Show HN: I built a tool that makes [process] 10x faster"
  - Bad: "Stop struggling with [process] — try [product]"
- URL: Your product landing page
- First comment: Tell your story (how you built it, challenges, lessons)

Timing:
- Best: Weekday mornings (US time)
- After your Product Hunt launch (if relevant)
- When you have something interesting to share
```

**What HN looks for:**

```
- Technical depth (how did you build it?)
- Honesty (no marketing spin, no "growth hacking")
- A story (why did you build this?)
- Unique approach (what's different?)
- Independence (solo founder, bootstrapped = bonus points)
```

**HN results expectations:**

```
Front page:
- 50-300 upvotes
- 500-5,000 visits (can spike server)
- 50-500 signups
- 5-30 paid customers

Not front page:
- 5-30 upvotes
- 50-500 visits
- 5-30 signups
- 0-3 paid customers

Pro tip: A detailed, interesting first comment is often more valuable
than the product itself for getting upvotes.
```

### Channel 3: BetaList

**Best for:** Pre-launch / beta signups

**Preparation:**

```
1. Submit 2-4 weeks before launch
2. Complete profile (logo, description, screenshots)
3. Highlight what makes your product unique
4. Offer beta access (free or discounted)

After listing:
- BetaList sends daily emails to subscribers
- Expect 50-500 visits from their newsletter
- 10-50 signups typical
```

### Channel 4: Social Media (Twitter/X, LinkedIn)

**Best for:** Continuous launch support

**Timing:**

```
Launch day posting schedule:

6 AM: "Today's the day. [Product] is live on Product Hunt."
8 AM: "What I learned building [Product] in [X] months as a solo founder"
10 AM: "Live on Product Hunt — come say hi!"
12 PM: "X upvotes on Product Hunt! Thank you amazing community."
2 PM: "Here's the story behind [Product]"
4 PM: "[Product] is trending on Product Hunt!"
6 PM: "The response to [Product] has been incredible. X signups in Y hours."
8 PM: "Going to sleep. 3 things I learned from launching today."
```

**LinkedIn specific:**

```
Format: Long-form post with personal story
- "I quit my job to build [product]. Here's what happened."
- Include: The problem, the build, the launch, early results
- Tag: people who helped, beta users, supporters
- End: Clear CTA (link to product)
```

**Twitter/X specific:**

```
Format: Thread
- Tweet 1: "Today I launched [product] on Product Hunt. Here's the story..."
- Tweet 2: The problem
- Tweet 3: The solution
- Tweet 4: How I built it (technical)
- Tweet 5: Launch results
- Tweet 6: Lessons learned
- Tweet 7: Link to product
```

### Channel 5: Email Launch

**Your email list is your most valuable launch asset.**

**Launch sequence:**

```
Email 1 (Launch morning):
Subject: 🚀 [Product] is live!

Content:
- What you built
- Why it matters
- Link to Product Hunt
- Ask to support (upvote, share)
- Personal story

Email 2 (Launch day recap):
Subject: Launch day results + what's next

Content:
- Thank supporters
- Share early results (transparency)
- What happens next
- Offer to try the product

Email 3 (3 days after launch):
Subject: You asked about [feature] — here's what we're building

Content:
- Feedback summary
- Roadmap update
- Special offer for early adopters
```

**Email list expectations:**

```
200-person list:
- 40-60% open rate (personal relationship)
- 10-20% click rate
- 10-30 signups from launch email

500-person list:
- 30-40% open rate
- 8-15% click rate
- 25-75 signups
```

### Channel 6: Communities

**Where to share (if rules allow):**

```
- Indie Hackers (build in public thread)
- Your niche communities (Slack, Discord, Facebook)
- Reddit (relevant subreddits — check rules)
- LinkedIn groups
- Niche forums

How to share:
"I built [product] to solve [problem]. It does X, Y, and Z.
I'd love feedback from this community who knows this space well.

[Link]

Happy to answer questions!"
```

### Channel 7: Press / Bloggers

**Best for:** Products with a compelling story

**Who to reach out to:**

```
- Journalists covering your space
  ("I built [product] to solve [problem]" — is this story-worthy?)
- Newsletter writers in your space
  ("I think your readers would love [product] because [reason]")
- Bloggers / content creators
- Podcast hosts (especially indie SaaS podcasts)

Outreach template:

Subject: [Product] — [Interesting angle]

Hi [Name],

I'm the founder of [Product], a [description] that solves [problem].

I thought this might be interesting to you because [reason]:
- We took a unique approach to [specific aspect]
- We're seeing [early traction/interest]
- [Interesting angle specific to their audience]

Would you be interested in:
- A quick demo?
- Early access for your audience?
- A story about [specific angle]?

Thanks,
[Your Name]
[pitch limit: 5 sentences max]
```

## The Launch Day Playbook

### Before Launch Day

```
□ Product is stable (tested, no critical bugs)
□ Onboarding works (first-time user experience tested)
□ Payment flow works (test the entire purchase flow)
□ Email sequences are ready (welcome, onboarding)
□ Support is ready (you will respond to everything)
□ Launch assets prepared (images, GIF, description)
□ Product Hunt submission ready
□ Hacker News submission ready
□ BetaList submission done (2-4 weeks ago)
□ Launch email written
□ Social media posts scheduled
□ Launch supporters lined up
□ Analytics tracking implemented
□ Server can handle traffic spike (test with load tool)
□ Get good sleep
```

### Launch Day Schedule

```
Morning (6-9 AM):
- Product Hunt listing goes live (midnight PT)
- Send launch email to your list
- Post on Twitter/X
- Post on LinkedIn

Mid-day (9 AM - 12 PM):
- Respond to every Product Hunt comment (within 1 hour)
- Engage with Twitter mentions
- Monitor signups and issues
- Thank early supporters

Afternoon (12-5 PM):
- Continue responding to comments
- Post progress updates
- Fix any critical issues that come up
- Help with onboarding questions
- Post in communities (if rules allow)

Evening (5-10 PM):
- Post recap on social media
- Send thank you to supporters
- Analyze first-day data
- Respond to all remaining comments/questions
- Get ready for tomorrow
```

### After Launch Day

```
Day 2:
- Follow up with trial signups
- Thank your hunter and supporters
- Share results with your community
- Start working on feedback

Week 1:
- Onboard new users (personal welcome emails)
- Collect and organize feedback
- Fix critical issues
- Post launch retrospective
- Continue engaging with leads

Month 1:
- Convert trial users to paid
- Ship quick wins (fixes from launch feedback)
- Measure launch ROI
- Plan ongoing growth channels
```

## Launch Metrics

### What to Track

| Metric | Good | Great | Amazing |
|--------|------|-------|---------|
| Product Hunt upvotes | 100+ | 300+ | 500+ |
| Email signups | 50+ | 200+ | 500+ |
| Trial signups | 30+ | 100+ | 300+ |
| Paying customers | 3+ | 10+ | 30+ |
| Revenue (first month) | $300+ | $1,000+ | $3,000+ |
| Unprompted testimonials | 1+ | 5+ | 10+ |
| Community engagement | 20+ comments | 50+ comments | 100+ comments |

### What Real Success Looks Like

```
Realistic launch results (for a solo founder's first launch):

Total visits from launch: 500-5,000
Email signups: 50-300
Free trial signups: 30-150
Paying customers: 3-20
First month revenue: $100-2,000
Churn after 90 days: 30-50% (of trial users)

Note: Most of your customers will NOT come from the launch.
They'll come from ongoing content, SEO, word-of-mouth.
The launch is the START, not the END.
```

## Common Launch Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Launching to an Empty Room

```
Problem: You launch but nobody knows about it
Solution: Build an audience BEFORE launching
Minimum: 200 email subscribers, 200 Twitter followers
```

### Mistake 2: Perfecting Instead of Launching

```
Problem: "It's not ready yet" → never launches
Solution: Launch when it's good enough, not perfect
You can always improve after launch
```

### Mistake 3: Not Building in Public

```
Problem: Nobody knows who you are when you launch
Solution: Share your journey for 30-90 days before launch
Familiarity builds trust
```

### Mistake 4: Over-Engineering the Launch

```
Problem: 20 tools, 10 automations, 30 scheduled posts
Solution: Launch is simple: have a product, tell people about it
Don't optimize before you have data
```

### Mistake 5: Ignoring Post-Launch

```
Problem: All energy goes into launch day, nothing after
Solution: The first 30 days after launch matter more than launch day
Onboard users, collect feedback, iterate
```

### Mistake 6: Pricing Too Low

```
Problem: Fear of rejection → low price → low revenue
Solution: Price based on value, not fear
You can always offer discounts, you can never raise prices easily
```

### Mistake 7: Getting Discouraged by Low Results

```
Problem: 50 signups "not enough" → give up
Solution: Most successful SaaS started with 50 signups
$2,000 MRR is the start, not the failure
```

## Post-Launch: The Real Work Begins

### The 30-Day Post-Launch Sprint

```
Week 1: Onboarding optimization
- Personal welcome emails to every new user
- Fix onboarding friction points
- Offer help to struggling users

Week 2: Feedback collection
- Survey new users (what's working, what's not?)
- Analyze churn patterns (where do people drop off?)
- Identify quick wins (features that would retain users)

Week 3: Feature shipping
- Ship 2-3 quick improvements based on feedback
- Communicate changes to users
- Ask: "Is this better?"

Week 4: Conversion optimization
- Analyze trial → paid conversion
- Improve pricing page (if data suggests changes)
- Launch referral program
- Plan ongoing growth
```

### Month 2-3: First Iteration

```
- Based on launch feedback, update product
- Continue content marketing (2 posts/week)
- Nurture trial users toward conversion
- Expand to 1 new channel
- Ship feature improvements

Goal: 2x the launch results (100+ signups, 10+ customers)
```

### Month 4-6: Growth Mode

```
- SEO content strategy (long-tail keywords)
- Referral program
- Partnership development
- Paid ads testing (if unit economics work)
- Hire first contractor if needed

Goal: Sustainable growth without launches
```

## The Solo Founder Launch Philosophy

### What Launching Teaches You

```
1. People care about the PROBLEM, not your product
   - If your launch resonates, people see the problem too
   - If it doesn't, either the problem isn't real or your message is wrong

2. Your first 100 users are your most valuable
   - They'll tell you what to build
   - They'll tell their friends
   - They'll tell you what's wrong

3. Launching is a learning experience, not a validation moment
   - Even a "failed" launch teaches you something
   - The product that fails at launch can succeed 6 months later
   - Many successful products launched and nobody cared initially
```

### The Truth About Launches

```
The best SaaS products often DON'T have great launches:
- Basecamp didn't launch on Product Hunt (it didn't exist)
- ConvertKit's first launch was mediocre
- Canva took YEARS to get traction
- Slack grew slowly at first

The correlation between launch day success and long-term success is WEAK.

What matters more:
- Do users who find you stick around?
- Do they tell others?
- Do you keep improving the product?
- Do you keep finding ways to reach customers?
```

## Remember

Your launch is one day. Your business is forever. Don't put too much pressure on launch day. Focus on getting better every day after.

**The best founders don't launch once. They launch over and over — new features, new markets, new channels. Launch is a mindset, not an event.**
