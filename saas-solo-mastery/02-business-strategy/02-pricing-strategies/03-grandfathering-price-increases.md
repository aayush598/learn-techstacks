# Grandfathering and Price Increases: Raising Prices Without Losing Customers

## Why Price Increases Are Inevitable

If you're not raising prices, you're leaving money on the table. As your product improves, your value increases, and your price should reflect that. But raising prices is scary — especially for solo founders who worry about losing customers.

The reality: **A well-executed price increase grows revenue and strengthens your business. A poorly executed one destroys trust and causes churn.**

This document covers how to raise prices while keeping customers happy.

## When to Raise Prices

### The Right Reasons

```
Reason 1: Your value has increased significantly
- More features, better performance, more integrations
- Customer outcomes have improved
- You're delivering 2x the value at the same price

Reason 2: Your costs have grown unsustainably
- Infrastructure costs increased
- Support costs per customer rising
- You need to invest more in the product

Reason 3: Your market position has changed
- You're now the market leader (not the new entrant)
- Your brand commands a premium
- Competitors have raised prices too

Reason 4: You've validated higher willingness to pay
- Customers consistently say they'd pay more
- You're leaving money on the table
- Free tier is too generous
```

### The Wrong Reasons

```
- You're desperate for cash (fix operations, not prices)
- You want to match competitor's price (copying ≠ strategy)
- You want to punish unprofitable customers (segment them instead)
- You haven't added value yet (raise value first, then price)
```

## The Psychology of Price Increases

### Loss Aversion

Customers feel losses 2x more intensely than equivalent gains.

```
Losing $10 feels worse than gaining $10 feels good.
A price increase of $10/month feels like a loss.
Adding $10 of value doesn't feel equivalent.

Mitigation:
- Frame the price increase alongside new value
- Give ample notice (reduces surprise = reduces negative reaction)
- Offer options (annual discount, grandfathering)
```

### The Endowment Effect

Customers feel they "own" their current price.

```
"Grandfathered" customers feel they have a special privilege.
Losing grandfather status = losing something they own.

Mitigation:
- Never take away grandfather status
- Frame the new price for NEW customers only
- Existing customers keep their price — forever
```

### Fairness Perception

Customers need to feel the increase is fair.

```
Fair: "We're adding significant value and adjusting prices accordingly."
Unfair: "We need to make more money."

Fair: "New customers will pay more. Existing customers keep their rate."
Unfair: "Everyone pays more starting next month."

Fair: "We're aligning with market rates."
Unfair: "We're increasing prices because we can."
```

## Grandfathering Strategy

### What to Grandfather

```
Always grandfather:
- Current subscription price (never take away their rate)
- Current feature set (never downgrade their plan)
- Current billing terms (annual vs monthly, discount codes)

Optional grandfather:
- Current integrations (if you change integration pricing)
- Current limits (if you change usage limits)
- Current support level (if you tier support)
```

### The Grandfathering Offer

```
Standard offer for existing customers:

"We're increasing prices for NEW customers starting [date].
Your current price of $X/month stays the same as long as
you keep your account active.

As a thank you for being an early customer, you'll also get:
- [New feature that's on the new plan]
- Priority access to [future feature]
- [Something extra to make them feel valued]"
```

### How Long to Grandfather

```
Option 1: Lifetime grandfathering
"They keep their current price forever."
Pros: Maximum goodwill, zero churn risk
Cons: Revenue growth is slower from existing customers
Best for: Early adopters, first 100 customers, founding members

Option 2: Time-bound grandfathering
"They keep current price for 12 months, then gradual increase."
Pros: Eventual revenue increase from all customers
Cons: Churn spike when grandfather ends
Best for: When you need to eventually align pricing

Option 3: Never grandfather
"Everyone pays the new price."
Pros: Immediate revenue increase
Cons: Maximum churn risk, trust damage
Best for: Only if absolutely necessary (rare)
```

**Recommendation:** Lifetime grandfathering for the first 1-2 years of your business. It's a small revenue cost for enormous goodwill and word-of-mouth marketing.

## Price Increase Playbooks

### Playbook 1: The "New Customers Only" Increase

**What it is:** Raise prices for new customers only. Existing customers stay at their current rate.

**Best for:** When you want zero churn risk and want to test price elasticity.

```
Timeline:
Week 1: Announce internally, prepare systems
Week 2: Email existing customers about the change
Week 3: Update pricing page for new customers
Week 4+: Monitor new customer conversion rate at new price

Template:

Subject: Important pricing update (good news inside!)

Hi [Name],

We're writing to let you know that we'll be updating our
pricing for new customers starting [date].

Here's what it means for you: Nothing changes. Your current
plan price of $[X]/month continues as long as your account
remains active. You're grandfathered in.

This change affects only new customers signing up after [date].

Why are we doing this? Since you joined, we've added:
- [Feature 1]
- [Feature 2]
- [Feature 3]

The value we deliver has grown significantly, and our new
pricing reflects that. But as an early customer, your loyalty
means everything to us. Your price stays the same.

Thanks for being part of our journey.

[Your Name]
```

**Expected outcomes:**
- Existing customer churn: < 1%
- New customer conversion: Depends on new price point
- Revenue impact: Immediate increase from new customers
- Trust impact: Positive (you took care of existing customers)

### Playbook 2: The "Added Value" Increase

**What it is:** Add significant new features, then raise price alongside the added value. Existing customers get the new features at their current price (or a smaller increase).

**Best for:** When you've built genuinely valuable new features.

```
Timeline:
Month 1-3: Build significant new features
Month 4: Launch new features to everyone
Month 4: Announce new pricing alongside new features
Month 5: New pricing takes effect

Template:

Subject: Huge new features + a small change

Hi [Name],

We just launched [Major New Feature] — something our customers
have been asking for since day one.

[Brief description of the feature and its value]

Because of these major additions, we're updating our pricing
for the first time in [X] years.

For existing customers:
- Option A: Lock in your current rate for another year
  ($X/month, no changes)
- Option B: Switch to the new plan (more features, $Y/month)

Your account won't change unless you choose to upgrade.

For new customers after [date]:
- Pricing will increase to $Y/month

You've been with us since [join date], and we want to make
sure you feel taken care of. Your current rate is secured
through [date] as long as you stay on your current plan.

Thanks for being an early supporter.

[Your Name]
```

**Expected outcomes:**
- Existing customer churn: 1-3%
- Some customers may UPGRADE (positive revenue)
- New customer revenue increases
- Trust impact: Positive (value before price)

### Playbook 3: The "Gradual" Increase

**What it is:** Phase in price increases over 3-6 months with advance notice.

**Best for:** Large price increases (>30%) or when you can't grandfather everyone.

```
Timeline:
Month 1: Announce price increase (effective in 3 months)
Month 2: Follow-up reminder
Month 3: Final notice, early action incentive
Month 4: New price takes effect

Template (Month 1):

Subject: A note about our pricing

Hi [Name],

I want to give you a heads-up about a change coming to
[Product]'s pricing.

On [date 3 months away], our subscription prices will be
increasing by $X/month for all plans.

Here's why: Since you joined, we've:
- [Major improvement 1]
- [Major improvement 2]
- [Major improvement 3]

Our costs have also increased as we invest in:

- [Infrastructure investment]
- [Support team/hours]
- [New development]

We wanted to give you plenty of notice, and a way to
lock in your current rate:

→ Lock in your current price by switching to annual billing
($X/month, billed annually — save Y%)

Your current monthly price is guaranteed through [date].
After that, the new price of $Y/month will apply.

We know price changes aren't fun, and we appreciate your
understanding. We're committed to making [Product] worth
every penny.

Questions? Reply to this email — I read every one.

[Your Name]
```

**Expected outcomes:**
- Existing customer churn: 3-8%
- Some convert to annual (positive cash flow)
- Revenue per customer increases
- Trust impact: Neutral to slightly negative (handled well)

### Playbook 4: The "Plan Restructure" Increase

**What it is:** Change plan structure (features, limits, tiers) rather than just raising prices. Existing customers get grandfathered into their current plan.

**Best for:** When you need to fundamentally change your pricing model.

```
Example:
Old: Starter ($29), Pro ($79), Business ($199)
New: Free ($0), Pro ($99), Pro Plus ($149), Enterprise ($299)

How to handle:
1. Create new plans alongside old plans (run both)
2. Give existing customers option to stay on old plan
3. Encourage move to new plan with incentives
4. Eventually sunset old plan (give 12+ months notice)

Template:

Subject: Important changes to [Product] plans

Hi [Name],

We're evolving our plans to better serve our customers.

What's new:
- [New plan structure description]
- [New features and limits]
- [New pricing]

What this means for you:
You're currently on the [Old Plan Name] plan at $X/month.
You can stay on this plan as long as you'd like — nothing
changes.

OR, you might like our new [New Plan Name] plan:
[Comparison table showing differences]

If you switch to the new plan:
- Your price will be $Y/month
- You get [new features]
- And [another benefit]

If you stay on your current plan:
- Your price stays $X/month
- You keep all current features
- No changes

Take your time deciding. There's no rush.

[Your Name]
```

**Expected outcomes:**
- Existing customer churn: 2-5%
- Many customers will upgrade to new plans voluntarily
- New customers pay more from day one
- Trust impact: Positive (you gave them choice)

### FAQ for Customers

```
Q: Why are you raising prices?
A: Since launch, we've added [features]. We're also investing
more in [infrastructure, support, etc.]. The new pricing
reflects the increased value we deliver.

Q: Will my price change?
A: [If grandfathered:] No. Your current price is locked in
as long as your account stays active.
[If not grandfathered:] Yes, but we're giving [X months]
notice to prepare.

Q: What if I can't afford the new price?
A: [Grandfathered:] Your current price doesn't change.
[Not grandfathered:] We offer annual billing at a discount,
and we can discuss options.

Q: Will I lose any features?
A: Absolutely not. You keep everything you have now.

Q: When does this take effect?
A: [Date] for new customers. Existing customers:
[grandfathering timeline].

Q: Can I lock in my rate?
A: Yes! Switch to annual billing before [date] and lock in
your current rate for another year.

Q: What if I want to cancel?
A: We'd be sorry to see you go, but you can cancel anytime.
Your account will continue through the end of your billing
period.
```

## Handling Customer Reactions

### Positive Reactions

```
Some customers will be fine with the increase — they see the value.
- Thank them for their understanding
- Reiterate the value they'll continue to receive
- Consider offering them an extra perk

Response: "Thank you for understanding! As a token of
appreciation, we've added [bonus feature] to your account."
```

### Neutral Reactions

```
Most customers won't love it but won't leave.
- They may not respond at all
- They accept the change silently
- The key factor: Did you communicate well?

Best outcome: They barely notice because you handled it well.
```

### Negative Reactions

```
Some customers will complain. This is normal.

Response framework:
1. Listen and acknowledge
2. Explain the reasoning
3. Offer options (annual discount, feature downgrade, etc.)
4. If they threaten to leave: Offer to discuss
5. If they insist on leaving: Make it easy

Handling specific objections:

"I've been a loyal customer for X years!"
"We appreciate your loyalty. That's why we're grandfathering
your current rate. Your price doesn't change."

"This feels unfair."
"I understand. We tried to make this as fair as possible
by [grandfathering/giving notice/offering options]."

"I can't justify the cost anymore."
"I understand budgets are tight. Would you like to discuss
a different plan that might work better for you?"

"I'm leaving."
"We're sorry to see you go. Your account will continue
through [end of billing period]. If you change your mind,
your current plan will be here for 30 days."
```

## Expected Churn from Price Increases

### By Magnitude

```
Small increase (5-10%):
- Churn: 1-2%
- Revenue impact: +3-8% net

Moderate increase (10-20%):
- Churn: 2-5%
- Revenue impact: +5-15% net

Large increase (20-30%):
- Churn: 5-10%
- Revenue impact: +10-20% net

Very large increase (30%+):
- Churn: 10-20%
- Revenue impact: +5-15% net
- Should only be done with significant value add
```

### By Customer Segment

```
Small customers (under $50/month):
- More price sensitive
- Higher churn from increases
- But lower revenue impact per customer

Medium customers ($50-200/month):
- Moderate price sensitivity
- Moderate churn
- Significant revenue impact

Large customers ($200+/month):
- Lower price sensitivity
- Lower churn
- Very high revenue impact
- BUT: need more communication, relationship management

Enterprise customers (custom pricing):
- Zero surprize — discuss changes in QBR
- Can negotiate new terms
- Longer sales cycle for price changes
```

## Price Increase Case Studies

### Case Study: Basecamp

**The change:** Raised from $79 to $99/month (for new customers)

**How they did it:**
- Existing customers grandfathered at $79/month
- Only new customers paid $99/month
- No timeline — grandfathered indefinitely
- Communication was honest and humble

**Result:**
- Zero existing customer churn
- New customer growth unaffected
- Massive goodwill from "taking care of existing customers"

**Lesson:** Grandfathering turns a negative into a positive marketing message.

### Case Study: ConvertKit

**The change:** Multiple increases as the product matured

**How they did it:**
- Grandfathered early customers at founding member rates
- Each increase was tied to significant new features
- Open communication from founder Nathan Barry
- Existing customers kept their rate but got new features

**Result:**
- Minimal churn during each increase
- Loyal customer base felt valued
- Revenue grew significantly

**Lesson:** Each increase should be tied to new value delivered.

### Case Study: Netflix

**The change:** Multiple price increases over years

**How they did it:**
- Grandfathered existing customers for 1-2 years
- Announced well in advance
- Tied to content investment
- Grandfather period gave customers time to adjust

**Result:**
- Temporary dip in growth, then recovery
- Long-term revenue growth
- Price increases became "normal" event

**Lesson:** Even massive companies need to handle price increases carefully.

## Legal and Contractual Considerations

### Check Your Terms of Service

```
Does your ToS allow price changes?
- Most SaaS ToS allow changes with notice
- Typical notice period: 30 days
- Check: Do you need explicit consent?
- Check: Are there locked-in pricing agreements?
```

### Grandfathering and Contracts

```
Month-to-month customers:
- Can change price with proper notice
- Customer can cancel if they don't accept
- Keep it simple

Annual contracts:
- Price is locked for contract duration
- Cannot change mid-contract
- Changes apply at renewal

Multi-year contracts:
- Price is locked for full term
- Cannot change
- Renegotiate at renewal
```

### Communication Best Practices

```
Legal must-haves:
1. Minimum 30 days notice (check local laws)
2. Clear effective date
3. Option to cancel before new price takes effect
4. Prorated refund if customers cancel after prepaying

Also consider:
- GDPR: If EU customers, proper notification
- California: Auto-renewal laws
- Local consumer protection laws (varies by country)
```

## The Solo Founder's Price Increase Checklist

### Pre-Increase (4-8 Weeks Before)

```
□ Determine price increase amount and justification
□ Decide grandfathering policy
□ Prepare communication materials (email, FAQ, blog post)
□ Update billing system to handle old/new prices
□ Test new pricing flow
□ Prepare customer support responses for common questions
□ Set up monitoring for churn and support volume
□ Plan "lock-in" incentive (annual billing discount)
```

### Announcement (2-4 Weeks Before)

```
□ Send announcement email to existing customers
□ Post in-app notification
□ Update pricing page for new customers
□ Publish blog post explaining the change
□ Share on social media (optional, based on audience)
□ Prepare for customer questions
```

### During Transition (1-4 Weeks After)

```
□ Monitor support volume — be ready for questions
□ Track churn rate — watch for unusual patterns
□ Monitor new customer signups — check conversion rate
□ Respond to all customer emails personally (solo founder advantage)
□ Consider offering extended grandfathering to at-risk customers
□ Track revenue impact
```

### Post-Increase (After 30 Days)

```
□ Analyze churn data — which segments left?
□ Analyze revenue impact — did it achieve goals?
□ Gather feedback from remaining customers
□ Document lessons for next increase
□ Thank customers for their continued support
□ Evaluate: Is another increase needed?
```

## The Solo Founder Advantage

As a solo founder, you have a unique advantage when raising prices:

```
Personal touch:
- Founders can write personal emails (not corporate communications)
- You can reply to customer concerns directly
- Your story is more authentic

Flexibility:
- You can grandfather more easily (no board approval)
- You can offer custom deals to at-risk customers
- You can change plans if something isn't working

Trust:
- Customers have a relationship with YOU, not a brand
- Your transparency builds trust
- A personal note goes further than a company letter

Use this advantage: 
- Write the price increase email personally (CC: from you)
- Reply to every response (for first 50-100 customers)
- Share the REAL reasons (not corporate speak)
- Be human about it
```

## Final Thoughts

Price increases are a sign of a healthy business. If you're not raising prices, you're either:
1. Not delivering enough value, or
2. Not capturing the value you deliver

The key to successful price increases is:
- **Communicate early and clearly**
- **Grandfather existing customers** (especially early adopters)
- **Tie increases to delivered value**
- **Be human and transparent**
- **Make it easy to stay**

A solo founder who handles price increases well builds a stronger business AND deeper customer trust.

**Price increases don't have to be painful. Handle them with care, and your customers will appreciate your honesty and fairness.**
