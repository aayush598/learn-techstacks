# When and What to Outsource as a Solo Founder

## The Outsourcing Mindset

Solo does not mean doing everything yourself. It means being the single person ultimately responsible for the business. How the work gets done — by you, by AI, by freelancers, or by a VA — is a tactical decision, not an identity statement.

**The core principle:** Your time is more valuable than money. Every hour you spend on a task that someone else could do for $10-50/hour is an hour you're not spending on the high-value work that only you can do.

## The $100/Hour Test

Use this simple calculation to decide whether to outsource a task:

```
Your hourly rate = Your MRR / Hours worked per month

Example:
  MRR = $5,000
  Hours worked = 160 (40 hours/week)
  Your hourly rate = $5,000 / 160 = $31.25/hour
```

**Decision rule:**
- If outsourcing costs LESS than your hourly rate → OUTSOURCE
- If outsourcing costs MORE than your hourly rate → Do it yourself UNLESS:
  - It's a task you hate (burnout prevention is worth the premium)
  - It's a specialized skill that would take you 10x longer to learn
  - It prevents you from doing revenue-generating work

**Example calculations:**

| Task | Your Time Cost (at $31/hr) | Outsourcing Cost | Decision |
|------|--------------------------|-----------------|----------|
| Data entry (2 hours/week) | $62/week | $20/week (VA) | Outsource |
| Writing blog post (4 hours) | $124 | $100 (freelancer) | Outsource |
| Designing landing page (6 hours) | $186 | $500 (designer) | Do it yourself (for now) |
| Bookkeeping (3 hours/month) | $93 | $200/month | Do it yourself (or find cheaper) |
| Customer support (10 hours/week) | $310/week | $250/week (part-time) | Outsource |

## What to Outsource (By Stage)

### Stage 1: Pre-revenue / MVP ($0 MRR)

**Outsource:** Very little. You need to learn every aspect of your business.

**The exception:** Specialized skills you absolutely cannot learn in reasonable time.
- Legal documents (incorporation, terms of service) — one-time cost, high risk if wrong
- Logo/branding (if design will significantly impact fundraising or sales)

**Do yourself:** Everything else. This is your learning phase.

### Stage 2: Early Traction ($0-$3k MRR)

**You should still do most things yourself, but start outsourcing:**

**Outsource now:**
- **Bookkeeping** ($100-200/month) — Categorizing expenses, reconciling accounts. This is easy to outsource and frees up 2-4 hours/month.
- **Logo/visual branding** ($200-500 one-time) — If you're still using a default or DIY logo.
- **Legal setup** ($500-1500 one-time) — If you haven't incorporated yet, pay a lawyer. Don't DIY this.

**Consider outsourcing:**
- **Content writing** ($50-100 per post) — If content marketing is working but you hate writing.
- **Social media management** ($200-500/month) — If social is a meaningful channel.

**Do yourself:** Customer support (you need to understand customer pain), product development (you need to own the code), sales (you need to close the first deals yourself).

### Stage 3: Growth ($3k-$15k MRR)

**Outsource now:**
- **Admin/VA** ($200-600/month, 5-15 hours/week)
  - Email triage
  - Calendar management
  - Expense tracking
  - Data entry
  - Research tasks
  - Travel booking
  - Personal tasks (grocery delivery, appointments)

- **Bookkeeping & accounting** ($200-400/month)
  - Monthly reconciliations
  - Quarterly tax estimates
  - Invoice management
  - Receipt organization

- **Customer support** ($300-800/month, 10-20 hours/week)
  - Tier 1 support (common questions, account issues)
  - Triage and routing
  - Knowledge base maintenance
  - CSAT monitoring

**Consider outsourcing:**
- **Content marketing** ($500-1000/month)
  - Blog posts (2-4/month)
  - Newsletter writing
  - SEO optimization
  - Social media content

- **Development** ($1000-3000/month)
  - Bug fixes
  - Minor features
  - Integration work
  - Mobile app development (if applicable)

### Stage 4: Scale ($15k-$50k+ MRR)

**Full outsourcing team:**

- **VA (part-time)** — Admin, scheduling, research, personal tasks
- **Customer support (part-time/full-time)** — First point of contact, you handle escalations
- **Bookkeeper** — Weekly financial management
- **Content writer** — Blog, social media, email
- **Freelance developer(s)** — Feature work, bug fixes, maintenance
- **Freelance designer** — UI/UX, marketing assets, landing pages

**You focus on:** Product strategy, sales, partnerships, team management, high-value customer conversations.

## Specific Outsourcing Categories

### Virtual Assistant (VA)

**What a VA can do:**
- Email management (triage, respond, archive)
- Calendar management (schedule, reschedule, confirm)
- Travel booking (flights, hotels, transportation)
- Expense tracking and receipt organization
- Internet research (competitors, tools, vendors)
- Social media scheduling
- CRM data entry and cleaning
- Invoice preparation and follow-up
- Personal tasks (doctor appointments, package returns)

**Where to find VAs:**

| Platform | Cost | Quality | Best For |
|----------|------|---------|----------|
| **Belay** | $10-15/hr | High | Vetted, US-based |
| **Time Etc** | $10/hr | High | Professional VAs |
| **Upwork** | $5-15/hr | Variable | Flexible, global |
| **OnlineJobs.ph** | $4-8/hr | Good | Filipino VAs |
| **Fancy Hands** | $4-6/task | Medium | Task-based, not hourly |

**How to vet a VA:**
1. Start with a small paid trial task (2-5 hours)
2. Test: communication, attention to detail, proactive vs reactive
3. Check: do they follow instructions exactly? Do they ask clarifying questions?
4. Look for: written English skills, time zone overlap, reliability

**Onboarding a VA:**

```
Week 1: Trial period (3-5 hours)
  → Give simple, well-defined tasks
  → Provide written SOPs for each task
  → Review work closely

Week 2-3: Ramp up (5-10 hours/week)
  → More complex tasks
  → Introduce to tools (email, calendar, CRM)
  → Start giving more autonomy

Month 2: Regular schedule (10-20 hours/week)
  → Consistent weekly tasks
  → Standing check-in (15 min, daily/weekly)
  → SOP maintenance (they help keep it updated)

Month 3+: Optimization
  → Identify additional tasks to delegate
  → Review processes for efficiency
  → Consider increasing hours
```

**SOP template for VA tasks:**

```markdown
# Task: Email Triage

## Purpose
Ensure important emails are responded to quickly and inbox stays organized.

## Tools
- Gmail (you@yourdomain.com)
- Help Scout (for support emails)
- Todoist (for task creation)

## Process

### Step 1: Scan inbox (2x daily)
1. Open Gmail
2. Sort by newest
3. Scan subjects and previews

### Step 2: Categorize each email

CATEGORY A: URGENT (requires founder within 4h)
  → Forward to founder@yourdomain.com
  → Add [URGENT] to subject
  → Criteria: server issues, major customer complaints, partnership offers

CATEGORY B: SUPPORT (can be answered from KB)
  → Respond with relevant KB article
  → If no KB article → create ticket in Help Scout

CATEGORY C: ADMIN (non-urgent)
  → Respond or archive
  → Examples: newsletters, confirmations, spam

CATEGORY D: TASKS (creates action)
  → Create Todoist task under appropriate project
  → Assign to founder if needed

### Step 3: Documentation
- Track emails handled per session
- Note any recurring questions for KB articles
```

**What NOT to delegate (yet):**

- Features that define your competitive advantage

**Where to find developers:**

| Platform | Cost | Quality | Best For |
|----------|------|---------|----------|
| **Upwork** | $20-80/hr | Variable | All types, vetted options |
| **Toptal** | $60-150/hr | Very high | Pre-vetted, enterprise |

| **Arc.dev** | $40-80/hr | Good | European developers |
| **HackerX** | Free | Variable | Community, referrals |
| **Personal network** | Varies | Best | Referrals from founders |

**Vetting developers:**

```
Technical vetting:
  1. Review portfolio / GitHub
  2. Check: contributions to relevant projects
  3. Give a paid test project (4-8 hours)
  4. Evaluate: code quality, testing, communication
  5. Check: do they use version control? Write clear commits?

Communication vetting:
  1. Do they respond in a reasonable timeframe?
  2. Do they ask clarifying questions or just start building?
  3. Do they provide status updates proactively?
  4. Can they explain technical concepts clearly?

Reliability vetting:
  1. Check references (previous clients)
  2. Test with a non-critical feature first
  3. Evaluate: on-time delivery, budget adherence
  4. Look for: do they test their own work before handing it off?
```

### ROI Calculation for Each Outsourced Role

```python
# ROI of hiring a VA for customer support

cost_per_month = 400  # 20 hours/week at $5/hour (Philippines-based VA)

founder_hourly_rate = 50  # Your effective hourly rate
time_value_per_week = hours_saved_per_week * founder_hourly_rate  # $1000
time_value_per_month = time_value_per_week * 4.33  # $4330
roi = (time_value_per_month - cost_per_month) / cost_per_month * 100

print(f"VA ROI: {roi:.0f}%")  # ~983%

# ROI of hiring a content writer
cost_per_post = 150

estimated_new_customers_per_post = 2
```

## Common Outsourcing Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Under-specifying

**Problem:** You're too busy to write a detailed brief, so the freelancer delivers something wrong. You spend more time fixing it than doing it yourself.

**Solution:** "I don't have time to explain it" means you don't have time to outsource it. Write the brief as if explaining to a smart colleague who knows nothing about your business.

### Mistake 2: Micromanaging

**Problem:** You hire someone and then check every detail, ask for constant updates, and second-guess their work.

**Solution:** If you micromanage, you're not outsourcing — you're creating more work for yourself. Provide clear specs, set check-in points, and let them work.

### Mistake 3: Hiring Cheap = Hiring Twice

**Problem:** You hire the cheapest person on Upwork, they deliver poor quality, you have to redo it, and you've now paid twice (lost time + money).

**Solution:** Pay market rate for quality. When you find a good freelancer, pay them well and treat them well. Retention is cheaper than re-hiring.

### Mistake 4: Not Investing in Onboarding

**Problem:** You expect a freelancer to understand your business, product, and voice on day one.

**Solution:** Spend 2-4 hours onboarding. Create documentation. Record Loom videos. The time investment pays off after the first month.

### Mistake 5: Outsourcing Core Competencies

**Problem:** You outsource product strategy, sales calls, or your key differentiator.

**Solution:** Never outsource what makes your product unique. You can delegate execution but not vision.

### Mistake 6: No Feedback Loop

**Problem:** Freelancer delivers work, you accept it, but never tell them what to improve.

**Solution:** Every piece of work gets feedback. "This is great." "This needs X changed." "Next time, focus on Y." Continuous improvement compounds.

### Mistake 7: Not Planning for Transition

**Problem:** A freelancer quits or becomes unavailable, and you're stuck.

**Solution:** Always have documentation. Every process should be written down. Always know what you'd do if your key freelancer left tomorrow.

## When NOT to Outsource

Core responsibilities to always keep in-house:

1. **Product vision and roadmap** — You must define the "why" behind your product
2. **Customer discovery and validation** — You need direct customer conversations
3. **Key sales conversations** — Especially early stage, you must close deals yourself
4. **Company culture and values** — Even solo, your company has a culture
5. **Strategic decisions** — Pricing, positioning, market selection
6. **Final quality control** — You are responsible for the customer experience
7. **Financial decisions** — Spending, runway, pricing changes
8. **Security and compliance** — You can't delegate liability

### How to Find Great Freelancers

1. **Ask other founders** — This is the #1 source. "Who do you use for [X]?"
2. **Post in communities** — Indie Hackers, MicroConf, SaaS groups
3. **Look at who's writing great content** — Great writers share their work
4. **Hire from Upwork's Top Rated** — More expensive but worth it
5. **Trial multiple, keep the best** — Hire 2-3 for a test, keep the best performer

### Retaining Great Freelancers

Great freelancers are rare. Keep them happy:
- Pay on time (within 24 hours of invoice)
- Pay fairly (don't haggle over small amounts)
- Give consistent work (they'll prioritize you)
- Give positive feedback and testimonials
- Respect their time (clear briefs, timely responses)
- Refer them to others (they'll appreciate it)

## Final Advice

Outsourcing is not about getting rid of work you dislike. It's about focusing your limited time on the work that creates the most value for your customers and your business. Start small, prove the model with one role, then expand.

The best solo founders don't do everything themselves — they build systems where things get done, whether by them, by AI, or by carefully chosen partners.
