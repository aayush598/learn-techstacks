# Step-by-Step Incorporation for Solo Founders

## Why Incorporate?

Incorporation (forming an LLC or corporation) transforms your side project or freelancing activity into a legitimate business. It provides:

- **Personal liability protection** — Your personal assets are separate from business liabilities
- **Tax advantages** — Deduct business expenses, self-employment tax strategies
- **Professional credibility** — Customers and partners take you more seriously
- **Business banking** — Open accounts, accept payments, build credit
- **IP ownership** — Your intellectual property is owned by the company
- **Future flexibility** — Easier to add co-founders, investors, or sell

**When to incorporate:** As soon as you have paying customers or are handling customer data.

## The Incorporation Process: Complete Step-by-Step

### Step 1: Choose Your State

**Decision matrix:**

```
Do you plan to raise VC?
  → Yes → Delaware C-Corporation
  → No → Choose based on where you live:

Do you live in a state with low/no annual LLC fees?
  → Yes (WY, NV, CO, NM, TX, FL, etc.) → Form LLC in your home state
  → No (CA $800/yr, NY expensive + publication) → Consider Delaware LLC + foreign qualify

Do you need Delaware's legal system for contracts?
  → Yes (enterprise B2B, frequent litigation) → Delaware
  → No → Your home state is fine
```

**Cheapest states for LLC registration (no physical presence needed):**
1. **New Mexico** — $50 formation, $0 annual
2. **Wyoming** — $100 formation, $62 annual, privacy
3. **Delaware** — $90 formation, $300 annual minimum
4. **Colorado** — $50 formation, $10 annual

**Important:** If you form an LLC in a state where you don't live, you must "foreign qualify" in your home state. This means paying fees in both states. For most solo founders, forming in your home state is the simplest and cheapest option.

### Step 2: Choose a Business Name

**Requirements:**
- Must be unique in your state (no other LLC/corporation with the same name)
- Must include entity type (LLC, Corp, Inc.)
- Cannot include restricted words (bank, insurance, etc.)

**Naming conventions:**
- [BrandName] LLC
- [BrandName] Technologies LLC
- [BrandName] Software LLC
- [BrandName] Inc. (if forming a corporation)

**Check name availability:**
1. Search your state's Secretary of State business name database
2. Search USPTO trademark database (if you want federal trademark)
3. Search domain name availability
4. Search social media handles

**Trademark note:** Forming an LLC with a name does NOT give you trademark rights. You need a separate federal trademark registration for that (see IP Protection guide).

### Step 3: Choose a Registered Agent

A registered agent is a person or company who receives legal documents on your behalf (service of process, government notices).

**Requirements:**
- Must have a physical address in the state of formation
- Must be available during business hours
- Must forward documents to you promptly

**Options:**

| Option | Cost | Best For |
|--------|------|----------|
| **You (if you live in the state)** | $0 | If you have a stable physical address in the formation state |
| **Northwest Registered Agent** | $125/year | Best overall service, privacy, business-friendly |
| **LegalZoom** | $159/year | Good if you used them for formation |
| **ZenBusiness** | $99/year | Budget option |
| **Registered Agents Inc.** | $99/year | Good nationwide coverage |

**Why use a professional registered agent:**
- Privacy (your home address isn't public record)
- Reliable document handling
- Compliance reminders (annual reports)
- You can switch states without changing your address

### Step 4: File Formation Documents

**For an LLC:** File "Articles of Organization" (called "Certificate of Organization" in some states)

**For a Corporation:** File "Articles of Incorporation" (called "Certificate of Incorporation" in Delaware)

**Information needed:**
- Business name
- Principal address
- Registered agent name and address
- Purpose of business (standard: "any lawful business activity")
- Management structure (member-managed or manager-managed for LLC)
- Stock information (for corporations: number of authorized shares)

**Filing methods:**
- Online (most states): $50-200 fee
- By mail (some states): Slower, same fee
- Via formation service: $0-300 (plus state fees)

**Turnaround time:**
- Online: 1-2 business days (some states same day)
- By mail: 2-6 weeks
- Expedited: $50-200 extra for 24-hour processing

### Step 5: Create an Operating Agreement (LLC)

An Operating Agreement is the internal document that governs how your LLC operates. Even for a single-member LLC, it's important.

**What it should include (single-member):**
- Owner's name and ownership percentage (100%)
- Business purpose
- Capital contributions
- Management structure
- Tax treatment election (default pass-through or S-Corp)
- Indemnification clause
- Succession plan (what happens if you die or become incapacitated)

**What it should include (multi-member/co-founders):**
- All of the above for each member
- Ownership percentages
- Profit/loss sharing ratios
- Voting rights and decision-making process
- Transfer restrictions (can't sell without approval)
- Buy-sell provisions (valuation method, payment terms)
- Dispute resolution (mediation/arbitration first, then litigation)
- Death/disability provisions
- Adding new members
- Dissolution process

**Where to get an Operating Agreement:**
- [Rocket Lawyer](https://www.rocketlawyer.com/) — Free templates for single-member
- [LegalZoom](https://www.legalzoom.com/) — Customizable templates
- [Northwest Registered Agent](https://www.northwestregisteredagent.com/) — Included with formation
- [Nolo](https://www.nolo.com/) — DIY legal books with templates
- Hire a lawyer — For complex situations (co-founders, investors)

### Step 6: Get an EIN (Employer Identification Number)

An EIN is your business's Social Security Number. You need it to:
- Open a business bank account
- File business tax returns
- Hire employees or contractors
- Apply for business credit
- Register with payment processors (Stripe)

**How to get an EIN:**
1. Go to [IRS EIN Assistant](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online)
2. Fill out the online application (10-15 minutes)
3. Receive your EIN immediately (online, during business hours)
4. Download and save your EIN confirmation letter (CP 575)

**Information needed:**
- Legal business name (exactly as on formation documents)
- Entity type (LLC, Corporation, etc.)
- Responsible party (you — name and SSN/ITIN)
- Business address
- Reason for applying (started new business)
- Date business started or acquired
- Closing month of accounting year (December for most)

### Step 7: Open a Business Bank Account

A separate business bank account is essential for:
- Liability protection (commingling funds can pierce the corporate veil)
- Clean accounting and tax preparation
- Professional payment processing
- Building business credit

**What you need to open an account:**
- EIN confirmation letter
- Formation documents (Articles of Organization/Incorporation)
- Operating Agreement (for LLC, may not be required)
- Business license (if applicable)
- Your personal ID (driver's license, passport)
- Initial deposit ($25-1,000 depending on bank)

**Which bank to choose:**

| Bank | Best For | Monthly Fee | Min Balance | Notes |
|------|----------|------------|-------------|-------|
| **Mercury** | Online-first, startups | $0 | $0 | Great for tech companies, no physical branches |
| **Brex** | Startups with VC | $0 | $0 | Requires $50k+ in bank or VC backing |
| **Novo** | Small business, freelancers | $0 | $0 | Integrates with Stripe, QuickBooks |
| **Chase Business** | Full service, branches | $0-15/mo | $0-2,000 | Waivable fee |
| **Bank of America** | Full service, branches | $0-16/mo | $0-5,000 | Waivable fee |
| **Local credit union** | Personal service | $0-5/mo | $0 | Great for relationship banking |

**Set up these accounts:**
1. **Business checking** — Day-to-day transactions, incoming payments
2. **Business savings** — Tax savings, emergency fund
3. **Business credit card** — Expenses, building credit (personal guarantee required early on)

### Step 8: Register for State Taxes

Depending on your state, you may need:

1. **State Tax Registration** — Most states require you to register for state taxes (income tax withholding, sales tax)
2. **Sales Tax Permit** — If you sell taxable products/services. Many SaaS products are exempt from sales tax (varies by state)
3. **Employer Registration** — If you hire employees, you must register for state unemployment insurance and workers' comp

**How to find requirements:**
- Google "[Your State] business tax registration"
- Check your Secretary of State website
- Consult a CPA or formation service

**Sales tax for SaaS:**

This is complex and varies by state. Key points:
- Some states tax SaaS as "tangible personal property"
- Some tax it as a "service" (not taxable)
- Some have specific "digital goods" laws
- Economic nexus: If you sell to customers in a state and exceed a threshold (typically $100k or 200 transactions), you must collect and remit that state's sales tax

**Tools to help:**
- **Stripe Tax** — Automatic sales tax calculation and filing ($0 for first $100k in transactions)
- **TaxJar** — Sales tax auto-filing ($19/month flat for small businesses)
- **Quaderno** — International tax compliance for SaaS

### Step 9: Get Business Licenses

Most SaaS businesses need:

**General Business License:**
- Required by most cities/counties
- Cost: $50-500/year
- Check your local city hall website

**Professional Licenses:**
- Generally not needed for software businesses
- Required for: legal, medical, financial services

**Home Occupation Permit:**
- If you work from home, some cities require this
- Cost: $50-200

**Health Department Permit:**
- Not needed for SaaS (only for food, health services)

**How to check:**
- Google "[Your City] business license requirements"
- Check SBA's local resources: [https://www.sba.gov/local-assistance](https://www.sba.gov/local-assistance)

### Step 10: Set Up Your Financial Infrastructure

**Accounting software:**
- Set up Wave (free) or Xero ($13/mo)
- Connect your business bank account
- Categorize startup expenses (formation fees, registered agent, etc.)
- Track startup costs for tax deductions

**Payment processing:**
- Create a Stripe account (business account)
- Connect your business bank account for payouts
- Set up Stripe Tax if applicable

**Business credit:**
- Apply for a business credit card (Amex SimplyCash, Chase Ink, Capital One Spark)
- Use it for all business expenses
- Pay in full each month
- Build your business credit profile

## Incorporating with a Co-Founder

If you have a co-founder, the incorporation process becomes more complex:

### Step 5a: Vesting Agreement

Standard terms:
- **Vesting:** 4-year schedule with 1-year cliff
- **Cliff:** If a co-founder leaves before 12 months, they get 0% equity
- **Monthly vesting:** After cliff, equity vests monthly over 36 months

### Step 5b: Co-Founder Agreement

Document everything:
- Equity split (based on contributions, not 50/50 by default)
- Roles and responsibilities (CEO, CTO, etc.)
- Decision-making authority
- IP assignment (all IP created before and during the company belongs to the company)
- Non-compete and confidentiality
- What happens if someone leaves (buy-sell provisions)
- Dispute resolution

### Step 5c: IP Assignment

Every co-founder must sign:
- **IP Assignment Agreement:** All IP created before joining is assigned to the company (or clearly excluded)
- **Confidential Information Agreement:** Protection of trade secrets
- **Proprietary Rights Agreement:** All IP created during employment belongs to the company

### Step 5d: Stock Issuance (C-Corp)

If forming a C-Corp:
- Authorize shares (10 million is standard)
- Issue shares to founders (83(b) election is critical — file within 30 days of issuance)
- Set par value ($0.00001 is standard)
- Consider stock classes (common for founders, preferred for investors)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Commingling Funds
- **Problem:** Using personal accounts for business transactions
- **Risk:** Piercing the corporate veil (personal liability for business debts)
- **Solution:** Separate bank account and credit card from day one

### 2. Not Signing Agreements
- **Problem:** Not having signed Operating Agreement, meeting minutes, or contracts
- **Risk:** Court may treat your business as an "alter ego" of yourself
- **Solution:** Sign and date all documents. Keep records.

### 3. Incorrect Entity Choice
- **Problem:** Forming C-Corp when you don't need it, or staying as sole proprietor too long
- **Solution:** Use the decision framework in Entity Comparison guide

### 4. Ignoring Foreign Qualification
- **Problem:** Forming in Delaware but living/operating in California without foreign qualifying
- **Risk:** Fines, inability to sue in your state, back taxes
- **Solution:** Foreign qualify where you have "physical presence" (office, home office, employees)

### 5. Not Filing 83(b) Election
- **Problem:** Receiving stock in a C-Corp but not filing 83(b) within 30 days
- **Risk:** Huge tax liability on future stock appreciation
- **Solution:** File 83(b) election with IRS within 30 days of stock receipt

### 6. DIY on Complex Matters
- **Problem:** Using generic templates for co-founder agreements, stock issuance, or investor documents
- **Risk:** Unenforceable agreements, disputes, tax problems
- **Solution:** Pay a startup lawyer for anything beyond basic LLC formation

## Incorporation by State (Quick Reference)

### Delaware

**Best for:** VC-funded startups, C-Corps
**Formation fee:** $90 (online)
**Annual fee:** $300 minimum (franchise tax)
**Formation time:** 1-2 hours (online), 2-3 weeks (mail)
**Key forms:** Certificate of Incorporation (corporation) or Certificate of Formation (LLC)
**Url:** https://corp.delaware.gov/

### California

**Best for:** Solo founders living in CA (who don't want to foreign qualify)
**Formation fee:** $70
**Annual fee:** $800 minimum franchise tax (LLC or Corp)
**LLC statement of info:** $20 every 2 years
**Formation time:** 5-7 business days (online)
**Key forms:** Articles of Organization (LLC) or Articles of Incorporation (Corp)
**Url:** https://www.sos.ca.gov/business-programs

### Wyoming

**Best for:** Privacy, low costs, anonymous LLC (not for VC)
**Formation fee:** $100
**Annual fee:** $62 (or 0.02% of assets, whichever is less)
**Formation time:** 1-2 business days
**Key forms:** Articles of Organization
**Url:** https://wyobiz.wyo.gov/

### New York

**Best for:** NY residents (everyone else should choose differently)
**Formation fee:** $200
**Annual fee:** $9-$4,500 (based on income)
**Additional:** Must publish notice in two newspapers (expensive, $500-1500)
**Formation time:** 1-2 weeks
**Key forms:** Articles of Organization
**Url:** https://www.dos.ny.gov/corps/

## When to Hire a Lawyer

You can DIY incorporation with online services for basic LLCs. Hire a lawyer when:

1. **You have a co-founder** — Co-founder agreements, equity splits, and buy-sell provisions need professional drafting
2. **You're raising outside funding** — Investors require properly structured C-Corps with clean cap tables
3. **You're in a regulated industry** — Fintech, healthtech, legaltech need specialized advice
4. **You have complex IP** — Multiple inventors, prior work that may belong to former employers
5. **You're forming in a state where you don't live** — Foreign qualification, tax implications
6. **Your situation is non-standard** — Non-US founder, non-profit, multi-entity structure

**How to find a startup lawyer:**
- Ask other founders for referrals
- Search for "startup lawyer [your city]"
- Use [Lawyer for Startups](https://lawyerforstartups.com/) directory
- Look for firms offering "startup packages" (flat fee for formation + standard docs)

**Estimated legal fees:**
- Basic LLC formation (single member): $500-1000
- LLC with co-founder agreement: $1000-2500
- Delaware C-Corp with stock issuance: $2000-5000
- Full legal setup (formation + docs + IP + contracts): $3000-8000
