# Co-Founder Agreements for Solo Founders

## Why This Matters Even If You're Solo Now

You may be solo today, but you might add a co-founder tomorrow. And if you DO have a co-founder, a written agreement is not optional — it's essential. More startups fail from co-founder disputes than from product-market fit problems.

This guide covers:
- How to find and evaluate a co-founder
- Equity split frameworks
- Vesting and cliff structures
- IP assignment (critical)
- Roles and responsibilities
- What happens when things go wrong

## Finding a Co-Founder

### Where to Look

| Source | Quality | Best For |
|--------|---------|----------|
| **Your network** (former colleagues, classmates) | ★★★★★ | Already trust each other |
| **Co-founder matching platforms** | ★★★☆☆ | Connecting with strangers |
| **Community events/meetups** | ★★★★☆ | Building relationships first |
| **Online communities** (Indie Hackers, YC Co-Founder) | ★★★☆☆ | Technical co-founders |
| **Accelerator programs** | ★★★★☆ | Vetted, committed individuals |

**Co-founder matching platforms:**
- [Y Combinator Co-Founder Matching](https://www.ycombinator.com/cofounder-matching)
- [Indie Hackers Co-Founder](https://www.indiehackers.com/cofounders)
- [Founder2be](https://www.founder2be.com/)
- [CoFoundersLab](https://www.cofounderslab.com/)

### What to Look For

**Non-negotiable qualities:**
1. **Trust** — Can you be vulnerable with each other? Do you trust them with your ideas, money, and reputation?
2. **Complementary skills** — You should overlap in some areas but be strong in different ones (business + technical is the classic pair)
3. **Shared values** — Same work ethic, risk tolerance, communication style
4. **Commitment level** — Are they as committed as you are? Full-time or part-time?
5. **Conflict resolution** — Can you disagree constructively and resolve conflicts?

**Questions to ask before committing:**

```markdown
1. "Why do you want to start this company?"
2. "What's your ideal work schedule?"
3. "How do you handle stress and conflict?"
4. "What's your financial situation? Can you afford to not take a salary?"
5. "What's your long-term vision for the company (lifestyle vs unicorn)?"
6. "What happens if one of us wants to leave?"
7. "How do you want to divide responsibilities?"
8. "What scares you about this partnership?"
9. "Have you had a falling out with a business partner before? What happened?"
10. "What's your exit strategy?"
```

### The "Trial Period" — Dating Before Marriage

**NEVER form a legal entity with someone you haven't worked with before.**

Work together on a project for 1-3 months first:
- Build a small feature together
- Work on customer discovery
- Create a landing page and validate the idea
- See how you communicate, handle stress, and resolve disagreements

**During the trial period:**
- Both parties retain full IP rights to their contributions
- No equity is granted
- Use a Simple IP Assignment Agreement if needed
- Have explicit conversations about expectations

## The Co-Founder Agreement

### What It Must Cover

**1. Equity Split**

The most contentious issue. Avoid 50/50 by default — it's rarely the right split.

**Factors that determine equity:**

```
Equity = Contribution × Commitment × Risk

Contribution factors:
  → Who had the original idea? (Minor — ideas are cheap)
  → Who built the MVP? (Significant — execution matters)
  → Who has domain expertise? (Valuable but not critical)
  → Who brings industry connections? (Can be very valuable)
  → Who is funding initial costs? (Should be compensated)

Commitment factors:
  → Full-time vs part-time
  → Salary vs no salary
  → Personal capital investment

Risk factors:
  → Income sacrificed (opportunity cost)
  → Reputation risk
  - Time investment (sunk cost)
```

**Recommended equity splits:**

| Scenario | Split | Rationale |
|----------|-------|-----------|
| Both equal contributors, equal commitment | 50/50 | True partnership |
| One had idea + built MVP, other joins later | 60/40 or 70/30 | Rewards execution |
| One full-time, one part-time | 70/30 or 80/20 | Commitment mismatch |
| One funded initial costs | Adjust 5-15% | Compensate investment |
| One brings key relationships/domain expertise | Adjust 5-10% | Compensate access |

**The "Dynamic Equity Split" (Slicing Pie model):**

Instead of a fixed split at formation, use a dynamic model where equity adjusts based on actual contributions (time, money, resources). This is more complex but fairer over time.

Resources:
- [Slicing Pie by Mike Moyer](https://www.slicingpie.com/) — Dynamic equity model
- [Founder Equity Calculator](https://founderequitycalculator.com/) — Online tool

**2. Vesting Schedule**

**Standard vesting:** 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff

```
Year 1 (cliff): 0% vested. If you leave before 1 year, you get nothing.
Year 1 (after cliff): 25% vests immediately
Years 2-4: Remaining 75% vests monthly (1/48th per month)
```

**Why vesting is important:**

Without vesting, if a co-founder quits after 3 months, they still own 50% of your company. With vesting, they get 0% if they leave within the first year.

**Accelerated vesting:**

| Trigger | Effect | Standard? |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| Death or disability | Full vesting | Usually yes |
| Termination without cause | Full or partial vesting | Negotiated |
| Company acquisition (single trigger) | Full vesting | Rare for founders |
| Company acquisition + termination (double trigger) | Full vesting | Standard |

**3. IP Assignment**

**This is the most legally critical part of your agreement.**

Every co-founder must assign:
- All IP they created BEFORE joining the company (that's relevant to the business)
- All IP they create DURING their time with the company
- This includes: code, designs, business plans, customer lists, branding

**The IP Assignment Agreement should:**

```markdown
1. List all pre-existing IP that belongs to the founder (not the company)
   → "Schedule A: Pre-existing IP" — list everything
   → Anything not listed is automatically assigned to the company

2. Assign all future IP to the company
   → "All IP created during the term of this agreement is the sole property of the Company"

3. Include a "works made for hire" clause
   → Ensures company owns IP for copyright purposes

4. Require cooperation
   → Founders must assist with patent filings, trademark registrations, etc.

5. Perpetual and irrevocable
   → The assignment doesn't expire, even if the founder leaves
```

**IMPORTANT:** If a founder worked on the idea BEFORE the company was formed, and there's no IP assignment, that founder could theoretically take the code and start a competing company. This is a common source of litigation.

**4. Roles and Responsibilities**

Clearly define who does what:

```markdown
Co-Founder A (CEO / Business):
  → Sales and customer acquisition
  → Marketing and growth
  → Fundraising and investor relations
  → Business development and partnerships
  → Finance and administration
  → Product management and strategy

Co-Founder B (CTO / Technical):
  → Software architecture and development
  → Infrastructure and DevOps
  → Technical product decisions
  → Code review and quality
  → Technical hiring (future)
  → Data security and compliance
```

**Decision-making authority:**

| Decision | Who Decides |
|----------|------------|
| Product roadmap | Co-Founder B (CEO) with input from both |
| Pricing | Both — mutual consent |
| Hire/fire employees | Both — mutual consent |
| Raise funding | Both — mutual consent |
| Sell the company | Both — mutual consent |
| Technical architecture | Co-Founder B (CTO) |
| Marketing spend under $1k | Either — no consent needed |
| Marketing spend over $1k | Both — mutual consent |
| Take a loan | Both — mutual consent |

**5. Compensation**

| Co-Founder | Salary | Equity | Notes |
|------------|--------|--------|-------|
| Founder A | $0 (until fundraised or profitable) | 60% | Sweat equity |
| Founder B | $0 (until fundraised or profitable) | 40% | Sweat equity |

**Salary triggers:**
- "Founders will take a market salary once the company reaches $X MRR"
- "Founders will take a minimal salary ($2k/month) upon raising seed round"
- "If one founder needs salary and the other doesn't, salary will be treated as a loan to the company"

**6. Buy-Sell Provisions (Exit and Departure)**

What happens when a co-founder leaves:

**Voluntary departure (founder quits):**
```
  → Company has the right to repurchase unvested shares (at cost)
  → Company has the option (not obligation) to repurchase vested shares
  → Repurchase price: Fair market value determined by appraisal OR formula
  → Payment terms: 3-5 year promissory note
  → Non-compete: 1-2 years (check enforceability in your state)
```

**Termination for cause (founder is fired):**
```
  → Unvested shares: Repurchased at cost
  → Vested shares: Company can repurchase at a discount (50% of FMV)
  → Non-compete: Enforced
```

**Death or disability:**
```
  → Company has the right to purchase all shares (vested and unvested)
  → Price: Fair market value
  → Insurance: Consider key-person life insurance to fund the purchase
```

**Buy-sell funding:**
- **Life insurance** — Key-person insurance on each founder
- **Sinking fund** — Company sets aside money monthly
- **Seller financing** — Company pays over time

**7. Dispute Resolution**

```markdown
Step 1: Informal resolution (14 days)
  → Founders meet and attempt to resolve in good faith

Step 2: Mediation (30 days)
  → Neutral third-party mediator
  → Cost split equally

Step 3: Binding arbitration (if mediation fails)
  → American Arbitration Association or JAMS
  → Arbitrator's decision is final and binding
  → Cost split equally (or loser pays, if agreed)

Step 4: Litigation (last resort)
  → Specific performance for IP issues
  → Court of Chancery (Delaware) for corporate disputes
```

**Shotgun clause (if all else fails):**

A "Texas Shootout" or "Russian Roulette" clause:
```
  1. Founder A offers to buy Founder B's shares at a specific price
  2. Founder B can either:
     a. Sell at that price → Founder A buys
     b. Buy Founder A's shares at the SAME price → Founder B buys
  This ensures the offering price is fair (if it's too low, they get bought out)
```

## Sample Co-Founder Agreement Outline

```markdown
CO-FOUNDER AGREEMENT

1. PARTIES
  [Founder A Name] ("Founder A")
  [Founder B Name] ("Founder B")

2. COMPANY
  [Company Name], a [State] [LLC/C-Corp]

3. EQUITY
  a. Founder A: [X]% of fully-diluted common stock
  b. Founder B: [Y]% of fully-diluted common stock
  c. Total authorized: [e.g., 10,000,000 shares]

4. VESTING
  a. Schedule: 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff
  b. After cliff: 25% vests, then 1/48th monthly
  c. Acceleration: Double-trigger on acquisition
  d. Repurchase right: Company may repurchase unvested shares at cost

5. IP ASSIGNMENT
  a. All IP created before or during is assigned to the Company
  b. Pre-existing IP is listed in Schedule A
  c. Founders agree to sign further documents as needed
  d. This obligation survives termination

6. ROLES
  a. Founder A: CEO — [list responsibilities]
  b. Founder B: CTO — [list responsibilities]
  c. Major decisions require unanimous consent
  d. Day-to-day decisions within each founder's area

7. COMPENSATION
  a. No salary until [trigger event: funding / profitability target]
  b. Salary will be [market rate / below market] after trigger
  c. Expenses: Pre-approved over $[amount] require both signatures

8. BUY-SELL
  a. Voluntary departure: Company may repurchase vested shares at FMV
  b. Termination for cause: Company may repurchase at [50%] of FMV
  c. Death/disability: Company must purchase at FMV
  d. Payment: [3-5] year promissory note at [X]% interest

9. NON-COMPETE & NON-SOLICIT
  a. During + [12-24] months after: No competing businesses
  b. During + [12] months after: No soliciting employees/customers
  c. Geographical scope: Worldwide (reasonable for SaaS)

10. DISPUTE RESOLUTION
  a. 14-day informal resolution period
  b. 30-day mediation
  c. Binding arbitration (AAA rules)
  d. Shotgun clause as final mechanism

11. CONFIDENTIALITY
  a. All non-public company information is confidential
  b. Obligation survives termination
  c. Specific exceptions: public info, required by law

12. TERMINATION
  a. By mutual agreement
  b. By either founder with [30] days notice
  c. For cause (material breach, fraud, criminal activity)
  d. On death or disability

[SIGNATURES]
[Date]
```

## Red Flags to Watch For

Before signing a co-founder agreement, watch for these red flags:

| Red Flag | Why It's Dangerous |
|----------|-------------------|
| **"We don't need a written agreement"** | This is the biggest red flag. They either don't understand the risks or plan to exploit ambiguity. |
| **"I want 50% even though I'm part-time"** | Equity should reflect commitment. Part-time = less equity. |
| **"I'll think about vesting"** | Vesting is standard. Resisting it means they want to leave with equity without earning it. |
| **"My previous code/ideas don't need to be assigned"** | Everything must be assigned or listed. Otherwise future IP disputes are guaranteed. |
| **"We'll figure out roles later"** | Role ambiguity leads to stepping on each other's toes and resentment. |
| **"I can't commit full-time right now"** | This person is not a co-founder yet. They're an advisor or part-time contributor. |
| **"Let's just start and formalize later"** | "Later" becomes "after things get complicated." Formalize early. |
| Rushing | Someone pushing you to sign quickly without proper review is suspect. |

## The "No Co-Founder" Alternative

If you can't find the right co-founder, consider alternatives:

**1. Stay Solo + Hire Contractors**
- Hire freelancers for specific tasks
- You keep 100% equity
- You don't deal with co-founder drama
- Challenge: You must wear all hats

**2. Stay Solo + Hire a "First Employee"**
- Give 1-3% equity instead of 30-50%
- Lower risk, lower commitment
- More control
- Consider this if you can afford to pay salary

**3. Find an Advisor Instead**
- Advisory board (3-5 people)
- 0.25-0.5% equity per advisor
- Monthly or quarterly calls
- No legal commitment beyond advisory agreement

**4. Join an Accelerator**
- Some equity (5-10%) for funding + network + mentorship
- Structured program
- Access to co-founder matching within the cohort

## Legal Checklist

### Before Signing

```
☐ Both founders have read and understand the entire agreement
☐ Each founder has had opportunity to consult with their own lawyer (separate lawyers)
☐ Pre-existing IP is documented in Schedule A
☐ Equity split is fair and mutually agreed
☐ Vesting schedule is clear
☐ Roles and responsibilities are clearly defined
☐ Buy-sell provisions are understood
☐ Dispute resolution process is agreed
☐ Non-compete is reasonable and enforceable
☐ Both founders have signed the agreement
☐ Agreement is dated and witnessed (or notarized if required)
```

### After Signing

```
☐ IP Assignment Agreement signed by each founder
☐ Stock issuance (if C-Corp) completed with 83(b) election filed (within 30 days!)
☐ Board of Directors appointed (if corporation)
☐ Initial board meeting held and minutes documented
☐ Operating Agreement updated (if LLC)
☐ Company bank account opened with both founders authorized
☐ Corporate records stored in secure location (with both founders having access)
```

## If It Goes Wrong

### Preventing Disputes

The best way to handle a dispute is to have clear agreements that make disputes less likely:
- Regular check-in meetings (weekly or biweekly)
- Transparent communication about concerns
- Written documentation of important decisions
- Annual review of the agreement (is it still fair?)

### Mediating Disputes

If a dispute arises:
1. Re-read the agreement (you both agreed to this process)
2. Schedule a structured conversation (not a confrontation)
3. Use "I" statements: "I feel frustrated when..." not "You always..."
4. Focus on interests, not positions: "What outcome do we both need?"
5. Bring in a neutral third party (mentor, advisor, mediator)

### Ending the Partnership

If it can't be resolved:

1. **Follow the agreement** — Buy-sell provisions, non-competes, etc.
2. **Be generous, not spiteful** — A fair exit is cheaper than litigation
3. **Get legal advice** — Both parties should have their own lawyers
4. **Negotiate a clean break** — Sign a release of claims
5. **Communicate to stakeholders** — Employees, customers, investors (carefully)
6. **Learn from it** — What would you do differently next time?
