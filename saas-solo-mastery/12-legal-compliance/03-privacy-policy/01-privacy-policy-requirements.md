# Privacy Policy Requirements for Solo SaaS Founders

## Why Your Privacy Policy Matters

Your Privacy Policy is a legal document that explains how you collect, use, store, share, and protect personal data. It's required by law in most jurisdictions, and failing to have one (or having an inaccurate one) can result in significant fines.

**For solo founders:** Your privacy policy is also a trust signal. When a potential customer visits your pricing page, the next thing they look at is often your privacy policy. A clear, transparent policy builds confidence.

## Legal Requirements by Jurisdiction

### United States (Federal)
- No single federal privacy law (sector-specific)
- FTC Act: Prohibits deceptive practices (including privacy misrepresentations)
- CAN-SPAM Act: Commercial email requirements
- Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA): If you serve children under 13

### California (CCPA/CPRA)
- Applies if you do business in California AND meet any threshold
- Thresholds: $25M+ gross revenue OR 50k+ CA residents' data OR 50%+ revenue from selling data
- Most solo SaaS founders under $25M revenue are NOT subject to CCPA
- BUT if you have even one customer in California, best practice is to comply

### EU/EEA (GDPR)
- Applies if you offer services to individuals in the EU
- No revenue threshold — if you have EU users, GDPR applies
- Applies even if you have no physical presence in the EU

### UK (UK GDPR)
- Post-Brexit, mirrors EU GDPR with minor differences
- Applies if you serve UK users

### Other Countries
- **Canada (PIPEDA):** Applies to commercial activities
- **Brazil (LGPD):** Similar to GDPR, applies if serving Brazilian users
- **Australia (Privacy Act):** Applies to businesses with $3M+ AUD revenue
- **Japan (APPI), South Korea (PIPA), China (PIPL):** Various requirements

## What Your Privacy Policy Must Cover

### 1. Information You Collect

```
Information We Collect

Information You Provide:
  → Account information: name, email address, company name
  → Payment information: collected and stored by Stripe (we don't store card numbers)
  → Profile information: avatar, bio, preferences
  → Content: documents, projects, messages you create using the Service
  → Communications: emails, support tickets, survey responses

Information We Collect Automatically:
  → Usage data: pages visited, features used, time spent, clicks
  → Device information: browser type, operating system, IP address
  → Log data: server logs, error reports, performance data
  → Cookies and tracking technologies (see our Cookie Policy)
  → Location data: approximate location based on IP address

Information from Third Parties:
  → If you sign up via Google/SSO: name, email, profile picture
  → If you integrate with third-party services: data from those services
  → Payment processors (Stripe): payment confirmation, not full card details
```

**What to include:**
- Every data point you collect, with specificity
- How you collect it (directly, automatically, from third parties)
- Include categories of data, not just examples

### 2. How You Use Information

```
How We Use Information

We use your information to:
  → Provide, maintain, and improve the Service
  → Process transactions and send related communications
  → Send technical notices, updates, security alerts, and support messages
  → Respond to your comments, questions, and requests
  → Monitor and analyze usage trends and preferences
  → Detect, investigate, and prevent fraudulent transactions and abuse
  → Comply with legal obligations
  → Communicate with you about products, services, and events (with consent)
  → Develop new products and features

We do NOT sell your personal information to third parties.
```

**What to include:**
- Every use case for the data you collect
- Be specific: "send marketing emails" vs "send transactional emails"
- State clearly if you DO or DO NOT sell data

### 3. How You Share Information

```
How We Share Information

We may share your information with:

Service Providers (Processors):
  → Hosting: AWS / DigitalOcean / [provider]
  → Payment processing: Stripe
  → Analytics: PostHog / Google Analytics
  → Email delivery: SendGrid / Mailgun
  → Customer support: Help Scout / Crisp
  → Error monitoring: Sentry

These providers have access to data only as necessary to perform services
and are contractually bound to protect your data.

Legal Requirements:
  → To comply with applicable law, regulation, or legal process
  → To respond to valid government requests
  → To enforce our Terms of Service
  → To protect our rights, privacy, safety, or property

Business Transfers:
  → In connection with a merger, acquisition, or sale of assets
  → If we are acquired, your data may be transferred

Aggregated Data:
  → We may share aggregated, de-identified data that cannot identify you
  → This is not personal information

We do NOT sell your personal information to third parties.
We do NOT share your data with advertisers.
```

**What to include:**
- List ALL third parties you share data with (not just "service providers")
- Explain WHY you share with each
- State clearly what you DON'T do (no selling, no advertising)
- Include data transfer mechanisms (SCCs for international transfers)

### 4. Cookies and Tracking

```
Cookies and Tracking Technologies

We use the following types of cookies:

Essential Cookies:
  → Authentication (session cookies, login tokens)
  → Security (CSRF tokens, fraud prevention)
  → These are necessary for the Service to function

Analytics Cookies:
  → PostHog: product analytics, session recording
  → Plausible/Google Analytics: website traffic analysis
  → These help us understand how you use the Service

Functional Cookies:
  → User preferences (theme, language, settings)
  → Feature flags and A/B testing

Third-Party Cookies:
  → Stripe: payment processing
  → Intercom/Crisp: live chat

Cookie Management:
  → You can control cookies through your browser settings
  → Blocking essential cookies may affect Service functionality
  → See our Cookie Policy for detailed information
  → [Link to Cookie Policy]

Do Not Track:
  → We do not currently respond to Do Not Track signals
```

**What to include:**
- Categorize your cookies (essential, analytics, functional, advertising)
- Explain purpose of each category
- Provide a way to manage cookies
- Link to detailed Cookie Policy if you have one

### 5. Data Retention

```
Data Retention

We retain your personal information for as long as your account is active
or as needed to provide the Service.

Account Data:
  → Retained while your account is active
  → Upon account deletion: data is deleted within [30] days
  → Residual copies in backups for up to [90] days

Usage Data:
  → Anonymized usage data may be retained indefinitely
  → Personally identifiable usage data: [12-24] months

Communications:
  → Support correspondence: [3] years
  → Marketing communications: until you unsubscribe

Legal Requirements:
  → We may retain data longer if required by law
  → Tax records: [7] years (US requirement)
```

**What to include:**
- Specific retention periods for each data category
- What happens when an account is deleted
- Backup retention
- Legal/regulatory retention requirements

### 6. Data Security

```
Data Security

We implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to protect
your data, including:

Technical Measures:
  → Encryption in transit (TLS 1.2+)
  → Encryption at rest (AES-256)
  → Regular security audits and penetration testing
  → Access controls and authentication requirements
  → Monitoring and logging for unauthorized access
  → Automated backup and disaster recovery

Organizational Measures:
  → Employee training on data protection
  → Access limited to personnel who need it
  → Incident response plan
  → Vendor security assessments

No Guarantee:
  → While we take these measures, no security system is impenetrable
  → We cannot guarantee the absolute security of your data
  → We will notify you of any data breach in accordance with applicable law
```

**What to include:**
- Specific security measures (not just "we take security seriously")
- Encryption standards (TLS, AES)
- Access controls
- Incident response commitment
- Honest disclaimer (no absolute security)

### 7. Your Rights

```
Your Rights

Depending on your location, you may have the following rights regarding
your personal information:

Right to Access:
  → You can request a copy of the personal data we hold about you

Right to Rectification:
  → You can correct inaccurate or incomplete data

Right to Deletion ("Right to be Forgotten"):
  → You can request deletion of your personal data
  → Subject to legal obligations (tax records, etc.)

Right to Restrict Processing:
  → You can request that we limit how we use your data

Right to Data Portability:
  → You can request a copy of your data in a machine-readable format

Right to Object:
  → You can object to processing for direct marketing
  → You can object to processing based on legitimate interests

Right to Withdraw Consent:
  → Where processing is based on consent, you may withdraw at any time

Right to Non-Discrimination:
  → We will not discriminate against you for exercising your rights

To exercise any of these rights, contact us at privacy@[yourdomain].com.
We will respond within [30] days (or the timeframe required by applicable law).
```

**What to include:**
- List ALL applicable rights (GDPR, CCPA, others)
- Explain how to exercise each right
- Include response timeframe
- Include non-discrimination statement (CCPA requirement)
- Provide a clear contact email

### 8. International Data Transfers

```
International Data Transfers

Your data may be transferred to and processed in countries other than
your own, including the United States.

If you are in the EEA, UK, or Switzerland:
  → Data transfers are protected by Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)
  → These are legal agreements ensuring adequate data protection
  → Copies of SCCs are available upon request

Our service providers (AWS, Stripe, etc.) also use SCCs for data transfers.
```

**What to include:**
- Where your servers are located
- What transfer mechanism you use (SCCs, adequacy decisions)
- That your sub-processors also use appropriate transfer mechanisms

### 9. Children's Privacy

```
Children's Privacy

Our Service is not intended for individuals under [13/16/18] years of age.

We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.
If we learn we have collected a child's personal information, we will
delete it promptly.

If you believe a child has provided us with personal information,
please contact us at privacy@[yourdomain].com.
```

**What to include:**
- Minimum age (13 for US COPPA, 16 for GDPR)
- What happens if you discover child data
- Contact for reporting

### 10. Changes to Privacy Policy

```
Changes to This Policy

We may update this Privacy Policy from time to time. We will notify you
of material changes:
  → By email (to the address on your account)
  → Via in-app notification
  → By posting the updated policy on our website

Changes take effect on the date stated in the notice.
Your continued use of the Service after changes constitutes acceptance.

Previous versions of this policy are available upon request.
```

**What to include:**
- How you'll notify users of changes
- Effective date of changes
- Archive of previous versions

### 11. Contact Information

```
Contact Information

For questions about this Privacy Policy or to exercise your privacy rights:

Email: privacy@[yourdomain].com
Address: [Your business address]

Data Protection Officer (if applicable):
  → We have appointed a DPO: [Name, email]
  → (Not required for most solo founders)

For EU/UK users:
  → You have the right to lodge a complaint with your local data protection authority
  → Contact details: [Link to EU DPA list / UK ICO]
```

## Privacy Policy Checklist

### Required Sections

```
☐ Information We Collect (categories, sources, methods)
☐ How We Use Information (every use case)
☐ How We Share Information (all third parties)
☐ Cookies and Tracking Technologies
☐ Data Retention (specific periods)
☐ Data Security (specific measures)
☐ Your Rights (complete list)
☐ International Data Transfers (mechanisms)
☐ Children's Privacy
☐ Changes to Policy (notification method)
☐ Contact Information (specific, responsive)
```

### Jurisdiction-Specific Requirements

```
US:
  ☐ COPPA compliance (if applicable)
  ☐ CAN-SPAM Act compliance
  ☐ State-specific requirements (CCPA, CPA, CDPA, etc.)

GDPR:
  ☐ Identity and contact details of controller
  ☐ Purposes and legal basis for processing
  ☐ Legitimate interests pursued (if applicable)
  ☐ Recipients or categories of recipients
  ☐ International transfer safeguards
  ☐ Retention period or criteria
  ☐ Right to lodge complaint with supervisory authority
  ☐ Whether providing data is a contractual requirement
  ☐ Existence of automated decision-making (if applicable)

CCPA:
  ☐ Categories of personal information collected
  ☐ Categories of sources
  ☐ Business/commercial purpose for collection
  ☐ Categories of third parties shared with
  ☐ Right to know, delete, opt-out
  ☐ Non-discrimination statement
  ☐ Metrics report (if processing 10M+ requests/year)
```

## How to Create Your Privacy Policy

### Options for Solo Founders

| Option | Cost | Quality | Best For |
|--------|------|---------|----------|
| **Generic template** (free) | $0 | Low | Just starting, no users |
| **Iubenda** | $10-30/month | Good | GDPR + CCPA compliance, auto-updates |
| **Termly** | $14/month | Good | Multi-jurisdiction, auto-updates |
| **FreePrivacyPolicy.com** | Free | Basic | Simple, no updates |
| **Avodocs** | Free | Good | Free SaaS-focused templates |
| **Custom lawyer** | $500-2000 | Excellent | Enterprise, complex data processing |

**My recommendation:**
- **Start:** Avodocs (free, SaaS-focused)
- **Grow:** Iubenda or Termly (auto-updates for regulatory changes)
- **Scale:** Custom lawyer (when you have significant revenue or enterprise customers)

### Privacy Policy vs Cookie Policy vs DPA

| Document | What It Covers | Required By |
|----------|---------------|-------------|
| **Privacy Policy** | Overall data handling practices | Most jurisdictions |
| **Cookie Policy** | Specific cookie and tracking details | GDPR (ePrivacy Directive) |
| **DPA (Data Processing Agreement)** | GDPR Article 28 requirements | GDPR (if you process EU personal data) |
| **CCPA Notice at Collection** | Specific CCPA requirements | CCPA |

## Common Privacy Policy Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Dangerous | Fix |
|---------|-------------------|-----|
| **Not listing all third-party services** | You're not being transparent about data sharing | Audit all services and list them |
| **Vague language ("we may collect data")** | Doesn't meet legal requirements for specificity | List exact data categories |
| **Claiming not to use cookies when you do** | Deceptive practice (FTC violation) | Actually audit your cookies |
| **Not updating when you add new features** | Outdated policy is the same as no policy | Review policy quarterly |
| **No DPIA for high-risk processing** | GDPR requirement for certain processing | Assess if you need a DPIA |
| **Using a template that doesn't fit your business** | Missing required sections for YOUR product | Use a tailored template |
| **No contact method for privacy requests** | Required by law and trust-building | Add privacy@ email address |
| **Burying data sale disclosure** | CCPA requires clear "Do Not Sell" link | Add clear opt-out link if selling data |

## Privacy Policy Maintenance

### Regular Review Schedule

```
Monthly:
  → Check if you've added any new features that collect data
  → Verify no new third-party services added

Quarterly:
  → Review all third-party services (are they all still used?)
  → Check if any new cookies have appeared
  → Verify all data collection is documented

Annually:
  → Full review of Privacy Policy against your actual practices
  → Check for regulatory changes (new state laws, GDPR updates)
  → Update if anything has changed
```

### When You MUST Update

- You add new features that collect additional data
- You integrate with a new third-party service
- You change how you use or share data
- A new privacy law takes effect in a jurisdiction you serve
- You receive a privacy request that reveals a gap
- Your business structure changes (acquisition, new entity)
