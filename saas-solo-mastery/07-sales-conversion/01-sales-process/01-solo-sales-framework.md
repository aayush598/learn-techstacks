# Solo Sales Framework

## The Solo Founder's Sales Reality

As a solo founder, you have no dedicated sales team, no SDRs booking meetings, no sales engineers running demos, and no account executives closing deals. You are the entire revenue engine. This framework is designed specifically for the constraints and advantages of solo founder sales.

The core insight: you cannot out-sell a team of ten, but you can out-relationship, out-expertise, and out-speed them. Your competitive advantage is deep product knowledge, founder authority, and the ability to make decisions instantly.

## Inbound Handling System

### Tier 1: Self-Serve Qualified (Score 0-30)
These leads sign up, explore the product, but haven't engaged with sales content. They should be handled by the product experience, not by you.

**Automated handling:**
- Triggered onboarding email sequence (day 0, 1, 3, 7)
- In-app guidance and tooltips
- Product usage monitoring with automated check-ins
- If they hit key activation milestones, keep them in self-serve

**When to escalate to you:**
- They request a demo
- They send a support email asking pricing/sales questions
- They hit trial day 10 without activating
- They invite team members (B2B signal)

### Tier 2: Sales-Qualified Active (Score 31-60)
These leads are actively using the product, have hit activation milestones, and show budget intent through behavior.

**Handling process:**
- Send a personalized video (Loom) from you as founder within 24 hours of qualifying
- Offer a 15-minute "strategy call" — not a demo, but a discussion about their goals
- Follow up with a case study relevant to their use case
- Track their feature usage to tailor your conversation

**Your time investment:** 30 minutes per lead max at this stage.

### Tier 3: High Intent (Score 61-80)
These leads have requested pricing, initiated a demo request, or come through a referral. They are actively evaluating.

**Handling process:**
- Respond within 2 hours (solo advantage — speed)
- Send a calendly link with your availability, including same-day slots
- Before the call, research: company size, industry, LinkedIn profiles of stakeholders, current tech stack
- Prepare a personalized demo agenda based on their signup behavior
- Have pricing ready, including annual discount options

**Your time investment:** 1-2 hours per deal at this stage.

### Tier 4: Enterprise (Score 81-100)
Multiple stakeholders, procurement process, security review required, deal size $10k+ annually.

**Handling process:**
- Identify the economic buyer (may not be the person who signed up)
- Prepare an executive summary document before the first meeting
- Bring a clear implementation timeline and migration plan
- Expect 3-5 touchpoints before close
- Consider using a deal desk tool or CRM with pipeline tracking

**Your time investment:** 5-15 hours per enterprise deal.

### Lead Scoring Criteria

Build a simple scoring model in your CRM:

**Company signals (+10 each):**
- Company size > 50 employees
- Raised funding (check Crunchbase)
- Has dedicated IT/engineering team
- Industry match with your ICP
- Geographic match with your compliance capabilities

**Behavior signals (+5 each):**
- Visited pricing page
- Viewed help docs on integration/security
- Invited team members
- Created > 3 projects
- Used API
- Uploaded data (migration intent)
- Replied to onboarding email

**Negative signals (-10 each):**
- Used free tier for > 60 days
- No login in 14 days
- Used competitor tool listed in their profile
- Student/edu email domain

### CRM Setup for Solo Founders

You need a CRM. Period. Even if it's a simple one.

**Recommended tools by solo founder stage:**
- **Pre-revenue or < $5k MRR:** Spreadsheet + email tracking (Mixmax, Streak)
- **$5k-$20k MRR:** Pipedrive or Close (lightweight, email-centric)
- **$20k+ MRR:** HubSpot Sales Hub (free tier then paid) or Salesforce Essentials

**Required CRM fields:**
- Lead score (0-100, auto-calculated)
- Lead source (organic, referral, paid, content, partner)
- ICP fit (high/medium/low)
- Deal stage (as defined below)
- Next action date
- Estimated deal value
- Close probability (%)

### Pipeline Stages

### Stage 1: New Lead (Inbound)
Lead enters through any channel. No contact yet.

**Exit criteria:** First human touchpoint (email reply, call booked, chat conversation)

**Actions:** Auto-score, send to appropriate tier, add to nurture sequence if not ready

### Stage 2: Connected (Qualification Call Complete)
You've spoken with the prospect. You understand their need.

**Exit criteria:** Mutual agreement that there's a fit worth exploring further

**Questions to answer:**
- What is the specific problem they're trying to solve?
- What have they tried before?
- What is their budget range?
- What is their timeline?
- Who else is involved in the decision?
- What would success look like in 30/60/90 days?

**Red flags:**
- No budget identified
- Timeline > 6 months
- Decision by committee with no champion
- They're "just researching"

### Stage 3: Demo Completed
You've shown the product tailored to their use case.

**Exit criteria:** They've seen the solution and expressed interest in moving forward

**Actions:**
- Send recording of the demo
- Share relevant case studies
- Provide a trial extension if needed
- Send pricing proposal

### Stage 4: Proposal Sent
Pricing and terms have been shared.

**Exit criteria:** Verbal commitment or signed contract

**Proposal components:**
- Executive summary of their needs
- Solution overview (how your product addresses each need)
- Pricing options (monthly vs annual)
- Implementation timeline
- Case studies / social proof
- FAQ addressing common objections
- Next steps with clear call to action

### Stage 5: Negotiation
Prospect has objections or requests modifications.

**Exit criteria:** All objections addressed, terms agreed

**Common negotiations:**
- Price discount for annual commit
- Feature requests for roadmap
- Implementation support
- Contract terms (auto-renewal, notice period)

### Stage 6: Closed Won/Lost

**Closed Won actions:**
- Welcome email sequence triggered
- Onboarding kick-off scheduled
- Billing configured
- Internal handoff notes (even though you're the same person)
- Ask for testimonial/referral

**Closed Lost actions:**
- Log reason for loss
- Schedule follow-up in 90 days
- Add to re-engagement nurture sequence
- Ask for feedback (what almost made them buy? what stopped them?)

## Qualification Framework: BANT Adapted for SaaS

### Budget
- "What budget have you allocated for solving this problem this year?"
- "What are you currently spending on [current solution]?"
- "If this solved your problem completely, what would that be worth to your team?"

Don't ask about budget too early. Earn the right to talk about money.

### Authority
- "Besides yourself, who else would be involved in evaluating a solution like this?"
- "How have purchasing decisions like this been made at your company?"
- "What would the approval process look like if we decided this was the right fit?"

Remember: in enterprise, the user and the buyer are often different people.

### Need
- "What prompted you to look for a solution now?"
- "What happens if you don't solve this problem?"
- "What does your current solution do well? What does it do poorly?"

The pain must be present, personal, and pressing.

### Timeline
- "When would you ideally like to have a solution in place?"
- "What milestones need to happen before you can make a decision?"
- "If we could get you set up by [date], would that work?"

If there's no timeline, there's no deal. Create urgency or move on.

## Sales Methodology: The Solo Spin

Rather than complex methodologies like MEDDIC or Challenger (which assume a sales team), use this simplified solo approach:

### 1. Discover Deep
Your first call is not a demo. It's a discovery conversation.
- 80% listening, 20% talking
- Understand their workflow, not just their problem
- Find the emotional driver (fear of falling behind, desire for promotion, pressure from boss)
- Map their current process step by step

### 2. Diagnose Before Prescribe
Before showing the product, articulate the problem back to them better than they could.

"I've heard you say that [problem] is causing [pain]. What I'm also hearing is that [deeper issue] is the real challenge. Does that resonate?"

When you diagnose accurately, they trust that your solution will work.

### 3. Demo with Purpose
Every feature you show must connect to a pain they expressed.
- No feature dumps
- Show the "before and after" of their workflow
- Let them drive whenever possible
- Pause after each major flow to check understanding

### 4. Close on Value, Not Price
- Quantify the ROI of your solution
- "You mentioned this process costs your team 10 hours per week. Our solution reduces that to 2 hours. At $50/hr burdened cost, that's $400/week or $20k/year savings. Our plan is $2k/year. That's 10x ROI."
- Always frame price in context of value

### 5. Follow Up Relentlessly (Professionally)
Most sales happen between the 3rd and 8th touchpoint.

**Follow-up sequence:**
- Day 0: Thank you + recap + recording
- Day 2: Case study or social proof
- Day 5: Value-add article or tip
- Day 7: Check-in, offer to answer questions
- Day 14: "Should I close your file?"
- Day 30: New feature or update that's relevant
- Day 60: Re-engagement with new angle

Use a tool like Mixmax or HubSpot to automate sequences but personalize them.

## Pricing Conversations

### When to Share Pricing
- Never before you've demonstrated value
- Ideally during or after the demo
- Have pricing visible on your website for self-serve leads

### How to Present Pricing

**The comparison frame:**
"Most of our customers are spending [$X] on [current solution]. Our [plan name] at [$Y/mo] gives them [key benefit] plus [unexpected bonus]. When you look at the ROI, the question isn't whether you can afford it, but whether you can afford not to."

**Anchor with your highest plan first:**
"Most companies your size start on our Scale plan at [$Z/mo]. However, based on your needs, I think [$Y/mo] plan would be the right fit because..."

### Handling "Send me pricing"
This is often a stall tactic. Respond with:
"I'd be happy to send pricing. To make sure I recommend the right plan for you, could we hop on a quick 10-minute call? I want to understand your specific needs so I don't send you something irrelevant."

If they insist on email, send pricing with context:
- Three plan options tiered
- Highlight the recommended one
- Include a case study
- Offer a call to review together

## Building Pipeline as a Solo Founder

### Content-Driven Pipeline
Your content is your sales team when you're asleep.

**Types of content that generate leads:**
- "How we solved [common problem]" posts
- ROI calculators and interactive tools
- Comparison pages (YourProduct vs Competitor)
- "How to [achieve outcome] with your product" tutorials
- Guest posts on industry blogs
- Podcast appearances

### Partnership Pipeline
- Integrate with popular tools in your space
- Offer affiliate commissions (20-30% recurring)
- Build an integration marketplace
- Co-market with complementary products

### Referral Pipeline
- Build a referral program from day 1
- Offer 1-3 months free for successful referrals
- Ask for referrals at the moment of highest satisfaction (after a win, after great support)
- Make referring as easy as sharing a link

### Outbound Pipeline (Manual, High-Touch)
As a solo founder, outbound must be highly targeted.

- Pick 20-50 ideal accounts
- Find a warm intro (LinkedIn mutual connection, shared Slack/Discord)
- Provide value first (feedback on their product, introduction to someone useful)
- Engage with their content on LinkedIn
- Send a personalized video, not a template email

## Metrics to Track

### Leading Indicators
- New qualified leads per week
- Demo requests per week
- Demo-to-proposal conversion rate
- Time from lead to first contact
- Email open/reply rates

### Lagging Indicators
- Close rate (by source, by tier, by deal size)
- Average revenue per deal
- Sales cycle length
- CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
- LTV:CAC ratio

### Solo Founder Metrics
- Hours spent on sales per week (if > 50% of your time, you need to hire or systematize)
- Revenue per sales hour
- Pipeline coverage ratio (pipeline value / revenue goal)

## Common Solo Founder Sales Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Selling to Everyone
You have limited time. ICP definition is survival.
Fix: Say no to prospects outside your ICP. Fire bad customers.

### Mistake 2: Building Before Selling
You don't need a perfect product to start selling. You need a solution to a real problem.
Fix: Sell the vision and the outcome. Build as you go.

### Mistake 3: Underpricing
Solo founders often undervalue their product because they're insecure about lacking a team.
Fix: Double your price. If you lose no customers, triple it.

### Mistake 4: Over-Customizing
Every custom feature request dilutes your product and consumes your time.
Fix: "That's a great idea for our roadmap. For now, here's a workaround."

### Mistake 5: Not Asking for the Sale
Founders often talk forever and never ask for commitment.
Fix: "Based on everything we've discussed, are you ready to move forward?" Just ask.

### Mistake 6: Following Up Too Little
You get busy building and forget to follow up. 80% of sales require 5+ follow-ups.
Fix: Automate your follow-up sequences and block 30 min/day for sales activity.

## Weekly Sales Rhythm for Solo Founders

### Monday: Pipeline Review (60 min)
- Review all leads from the past week
- Score and categorize them
- Move deals through stages
- Plan outreach for the week

### Tuesday: Prospecting/Outbound (90 min)
- Research target accounts
- Send personalized outreach
- Engage on social media
- Nurture existing relationships

### Wednesday: Demos and Calls (2-3 hours)
- Schedule all demos and discovery calls
- Block focus time before calls for preparation
- Block time after for follow-up

### Thursday: Follow-ups and Proposals (90 min)
- Send proposals
- Follow up on pending deals
- Handle objections via email
- Send recordings and case studies

### Friday: Admin and Optimization (60 min)
- Update CRM
- Review sales metrics
- Refine messaging and sequences
- Read and learn sales techniques

This rhythm protects 4+ hours for sales while leaving the rest of the week for building and support. Adjust ratios based on your stage — earlier stage means more sales time, later stage means more building to keep up with growth.

## Conclusion

Solo founder sales is not about being a "natural salesperson." It's about systematizing the process, understanding your customer deeply, and leveraging your unique advantages as the founder. You have authority that no sales rep has. You have product knowledge that no sales rep can match. And you can make decisions — on pricing, on features, on terms — in seconds, not weeks.

Build the system. Trust the process. And ask for the sale.
