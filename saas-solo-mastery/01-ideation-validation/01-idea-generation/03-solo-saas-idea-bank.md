# Solo SaaS Idea Bank: 50+ Validated Ideas for Solo Founders

## How to Use This Idea Bank

This isn't a list of "get rich quick" schemes. Each idea here represents a real, validated market need that a solo founder could realistically build and profit from. The ideas are organized by category with analysis of:

- **Problem:** What pain point this solves
- **Market:** Who the customers are and roughly how many
- **Revenue potential:** Realistic ARR estimates for a solo founder
- **Difficulty:** Technical complexity (1-10)
- **Moat potential:** How defensible the business could be (1-10)
- **Solo suitability:** How well-suited this is for a one-person operation (1-10)
- **Key insight:** The strategic angle that makes this work for a solo founder

### Selection Criteria
Each idea in this bank meets these criteria:
- Can be built by one person (or with minimal contractors)
- Doesn't require massive upfront capital
- Has clear monetization potential ($5K-$20K+ MRR achievable)
- Solves a genuine problem (not a "nice to have")
- Has some defensibility path for a solo founder

## Idea Validation Checklist

Before building anything:

- [ ] 20+ customer interviews confirm the pain
- [ ] At least 10 people said they'd pay
- [ ] Competitors exist but have clear weaknesses
- [ ] You understand the customer acquisition channels
- [ ] The build effort is realistic for a solo founder
- [ ] You have a clear differentiation angle
- [ ] The economics work ($50+ MRR per customer target)
- [ ] You can build an MVP in 4-8 weeks
- [ ] There's a path to $5K MRR within 6 months of launch
- [ ] You're excited enough to work on this for 2+ years

## Final Thoughts

The best SaaS idea for a solo founder is one that:
1. Solves a painful problem (not a nice-to-have)
2. Serves a specific, accessible market
3. Has natural switching costs
4. Can be built by one person in 4-8 weeks
5. Has a clear path to paying customers
6. You're personally interested enough to sustain through the hard parts

The ideas in this bank are starting points — they're validated market opportunities, not guaranteed successes. Your execution, niche selection, and customer intimacy will determine whether any of them becomes a sustainable business.

**Remember:** The best idea is the one you'll actually start. Pick one, validate it, build it, and ship it. The rest is iteration.
