# Concierge MVP: The Manual-First Approach to Building SaaS

## What is a Concierge MVP?

A Concierge MVP is a product validation strategy where you manually deliver the service that your software will eventually automate. Instead of building software first, you perform the service by hand for paying customers — using spreadsheets, email, screenshots, and whatever tools are available.

**The philosophy:** Don't build a robot to solve a problem until you've proven that (a) people will pay for the solution and (b) you understand the solution well enough to automate it.

### Concierge MVP vs. Traditional MVP

| Aspect | Traditional MVP | Concierge MVP |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| **What you build** | Minimal software | Minimal process (manual) |
| **Time to first customer** | 2-6 months | 2-6 days |
| **Code written** | Lots | None (or minimal) |
| **Learning speed** | Slow (build → ship → learn) | Fast (do → learn → improve) |
| **Customer feedback** | After building | During delivery |
| **Revenue timing** | After launch | From day one |
| **Scalability** | Built in | None (until automated) |
| **Risk** | Building the wrong thing | Getting tired of manual work |

## Why Concierge MVP Works for Solo Founders

### Reason 1: Speed
You can start delivering value TOMORROW. No coding required. No waiting for features. Just you, a process, and a customer.

### Reason 2: Learning
You learn the problem better than any interview could teach you. When you DO the work, you discover edge cases, inefficiencies, and opportunities that no customer could articulate.

### Reason 3: Revenue
You can generate revenue from day one. This de-risks everything — you know people will pay before you invest months in development.

### Reason 4: Relationship
You build deep relationships with your first customers. They become co-creators, not just users. They'll evangelize your product because they helped shape it.

### Reason 5: Perfect Product-Market Fit
When you automate, you'll automate the EXACT process that works. Not what you guessed would work. Not what a customer described. What you've done 50+ times manually.

## The Concierge MVP Process

### Phase 1: Identify a Manual Service

Look for problems that have these characteristics:

- **Repeatable process:** The same steps every time
- **Value-adding:** Someone would pay for the outcome
- **Time-consuming:** Takes 1-10 hours per delivery
- **Rule-based:** Follows clear patterns and logic
- **Data-intensive:** Involves collecting, processing, or analyzing data
- **Error-prone:** Manual work leads to mistakes

**Good Concierge MVP candidates:**
- "I'll analyze your CRM data and find duplicate contacts"
- "I'll monitor your webhooks and alert you when they fail"
- "I'll review your contracts for unusual terms"
- "I'll generate your quarterly compliance report"
- "I'll track your competitor pricing and send weekly updates"
- "I'll audit your website for accessibility issues"
- "I'll process your invoices and prepare them for accounting"
- "I'll research and compile a list of prospects for you"

**Bad Concierge MVP candidates:**
- Physical tasks (cleaning, delivery)
- Tasks requiring special equipment
- Tasks that need a team
- Tasks where value depends on automation (real-time processing)
- Tasks that cost you more to do manually than you can charge

### Phase 2: Find Your First Customers

**Method 1: Existing Network**
```
Message to former colleagues/contacts:

"Hi [Name], I'm testing a service idea and thought of you.
I'm offering [specific service] for [companies like yours].
Would you be interested in being my first customer at a
discounted rate? I'll do [specific outcome] for $[price]."

Best for: First 1-3 customers
Conversion rate: 20-50% (existing trust)
```

**Method 2: Cold Outreach**
```
LinkedIn DM template:

"Hi [Name], I noticed you're a [role] at [company]. 
I'm testing a service that [solves specific problem]. 
I'm looking for 5 beta customers to do this for free
in exchange for feedback. Interested?"

Then convert free to paid after delivering value:
"Based on what I did for you for free, would you pay
$[price]/month for me to keep doing this?"

Best for: 3-10 customers after proving concept
Conversion rate: 5-15%
```

**Method 3: Warm Intro**
```
Ask your network for introductions:
"Hey, do you know anyone who struggles with [problem]?
I'm testing a service to help with this and looking for
beta customers."

Best for: High-quality leads
Conversion rate: 30-50%
```

**Method 4: Problem-Solving in Public**
```
Post on LinkedIn/Twitter:
"I'm offering to [service] for free for 5 companies.
I want to understand this problem better before
building a solution. DM me if interested."

Best for: Building audience + finding customers
Conversion rate: Variable
```

### Phase 3: Price Your Concierge Service

**Pricing strategies for concierge MVP:**

**Strategy 1: Cost-Plus**
```
Calculate your time:
- 5 hours per delivery per week
- Your desired hourly rate: $100/hour
- Cost: $500/month per customer
- Price: $500/month (or discount to $250 for beta)
```

**Strategy 2: Value-Based**
```
Calculate value delivered:
- Customer saves 10 hours/week
- Their time is worth $75/hour
- Value: $750/week = $3,000/month
- Price: $500-1,000/month (15-30% of value)
```

**Strategy 3: Competitive**
```
Research what similar services cost:
- Hiring a freelancer: $50-150/hour
- Agency service: $5,000-10,000/month
- Enterprise software: $500-5,000/month
- Your price: slightly below agency, above freelancer
```

**Strategy 4: Beta Discount**
```
Start with a discount for early customers:
- Regular price: $500/month
- Beta price: $200/month
- "In exchange for feedback and a testimonial"
- Raise price for new customers as you automate
```

**Pricing benchmarks for concierge services:**
| Service Type | Typical Monthly Price |
|-------------|----------------------|
| Data analysis/reporting | $200-1,000/month |
| Process outsourcing | $300-2,000/month |
| Research/monitoring | $100-500/month |
| Content creation | $500-3,000/month |
| Compliance/document prep | $200-800/month |
| Lead generation | $500-2,000/month |

### Phase 4: Deliver the Service

**How to set up your manual process:**

```
Step 1: Document the current state
- What data do you need from the customer?
- What format is it in?
- How do you get it?

Step 2: Design the workflow
- Step-by-step process
- Tools you'll use (spreadsheets, email, Google Docs)
- Decision points and rules
- Quality checks

Step 3: Set up your tools
Example for a CRM cleanup service:
- Airtable (tracking progress)
- Google Sheets (data processing)
- Gmail (communication)
- Calendly (scheduling)
- Zapier (automate what you can)
- Notion (documentation)

Step 4: First delivery
- Communicate expectations
- Deliver on time
- Over-deliver on value
- Ask for feedback
- Ask for testimonial
```

**Concierge service delivery checklist:**

```
Daily:
- Check customer data/file submissions
- Process new inputs
- Communicate progress

Weekly:
- Deliver completed work
- Quick call or email summary
- Ask: "What would make this more valuable?"

Monthly:
- Full deliverable
- Review with customer
- Document improvements for automation
```

### Phase 5: Learn and Iterate

**What to track during your concierge MVP:**

| Metric | Why It Matters |
|--------|---------------|
| Time per delivery | Will tell you how much to automate |
| Customer satisfaction | Validates the problem is worth solving |
| Willingness to pay | Validates the business model |
| Repeat requests | Validates recurring need |
| Customer referrals | Validates value perception |
| Edge cases discovered | Informs product requirements |
| Feature requests | Tells you what to build first |
| Error rate | Shows where automation adds value |

**The automation diary:**

After each delivery, note:
```
Task: [Specific step you performed]
Time spent: [Minutes]
Frequency: [How often this task occurs]
Rule-based? [Yes/No — could software do this?]
Complexity: [1-10 — how hard to automate?]
Priority: [High/Medium/Low — should we automate first?]
```

**When you see these patterns, it's time to automate:**
- You've done the same task 10+ times
- You can write clear rules for the task
- The task takes more than 30 minutes per customer
- Multiple customers ask for the same thing
- You're making mistakes due to repetition
- You're bored doing it manually

## The Transition: Manual → Software

### Phase 6: Automate Step by Step

**The automation sequence:**

```
Phase 1: Pure manual (Weeks 1-4)
- You do everything by hand
- 3-5 paying customers
- Validated: people pay for the service

Phase 2: Simple templates (Weeks 4-8)
- Create templates for repetitive work
- Use snippets, spreadsheets with formulas
- Reduce delivery time by 20-30%
- 5-10 customers, maybe raise price

Phase 3: Partial automation (Weeks 8-16)
- Build scripts for repetitive tasks
- Automate data collection and formatting
- Use Zapier/GPT/APIs for common steps
- Reduce delivery time by 50-60%
- 10-20 customers, approaching capacity

Phase 4: Software MVP (Weeks 16-24)
- Build self-service version of your service
- Customers can do some things themselves
- You handle complex cases
- 20-50 customers, hybrid model

Phase 5: Full SaaS (Months 6-12)
- Core service is fully automated
- You focus on complex cases + support
- Customer can self-serve entirely
- 50-200+ customers, scalable
```

### Building the First Automation

**What to automate first:**

```
Priority 1: Data collection (eliminate your manual data entry)
- Build a form/survey for customer input
- Create a shared folder for files
- Set up automatic data import from customer's tools

Priority 2: Data processing (speed up your analysis)
- Write scripts for calculations
- Build spreadsheet formulas
- Use GPT for text analysis
- Create report templates

Priority 3: Delivery (eliminate manual report generation)
- Build report generator
- Set up automated email delivery
- Create customer dashboard

Priority 4: Quality (reduce errors)
- Add validation checks
- Build testing scripts
- Create monitoring alerts
```

### The Hybrid Phase

During the transition, you'll run a hybrid model:

```
Service tier: $500/month
- You handle everything
- Weekly calls
- Custom reports

Software tier: $99/month
- Self-service dashboard
- Automated reports
- Email support only
- Chat/Customer portal for complex needs

Hybrid tier: $249/month
- Self-service for standard tasks
- You handle complex cases
- Monthly check-in call
```

## Concierge MVP Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Baremetrics

**Founder:** Josh Pigford
**Product:** SaaS subscription analytics

**Concierge phase:**
1. Josh manually pulled data from Stripe for 5 companies
2. Sent them weekly reports via email
3. Charged $50/month for the manual service
4. Learned exactly what metrics mattered
5. Automated report generation
6. Replaced manual with software subscription

**Key lesson:** The manual phase taught him which metrics were important. His software only included metrics customers actually cared about.

**Timeline:** 3 months manual → 6 months building → launched to 50+ pre-sold customers

### Case Study 2: Customer.io

**Founder:** JP (single founder at start)
**Product:** Email automation for SaaS

**Concierge phase:**
1. JP manually sent targeted emails for SaaS companies
2. Used Mailchimp + spreadsheets to segment and send
3. Charged $500-1,000/month per client
4. Learned the exact email automation workflows companies needed
5. Built Customer.io to automate what he was doing manually

**Key lesson:** He knew EXACTLY what features to build because he had been doing it manually for paying clients.

**Timeline:** 6 months manual → 12 months building → $10M+ ARR

### Case Study 3: Intercom

**Founder:** Des Traynor, Eoghan McCabe (small team)
**Product:** Customer messaging platform

**Concierge phase:**
Before Intercom was a product, the founders ran a web design agency. They noticed clients kept asking:
- "Can we talk to visitors on our website?"
- "Can we send targeted messages?"

They manually set up chat widgets and targeted emails for clients before building software to do it.

**Key lesson:** The concierge phase funded their development AND validated demand.

### Case Study 4: The Solo Concierge Success (Kitchen Sink)

**Founder:** Single developer
**Product:** Inventory management for restaurants

**Concierge phase:**
1. Contacted 5 local restaurant owners
2. Offered to manage their inventory tracking for $200/month
3. Visited restaurants 2x/week to count inventory
4. Manually entered data into spreadsheets
5. Sent weekly reports with ordering recommendations
6. After 3 months, started building software to automate
7. Converted manual customers to software customers
8. Now serves 50+ restaurants at $79/month

**Result:** Restaurant owners saw the value because they experienced it as a service. Revenue during concierge phase: $1,000/month. After automation: $3,950/month with almost zero overhead.

## Running the Concierge MVP: The Details

### Week-by-Week Plan

```
Week 1: Setup
- Define your service offering
- Identify 20 potential customers
- Set pricing
- Create service agreement (simple)
- Set up tools (Airtable, calendar, communication)

Week 2: First customer
- Offer "beta" pricing to first customer
- Deliver first service
- Document every step
- Time everything

Week 3-4: Refine
- Get feedback
- Improve process
- Add second customer
- Continue documenting

Week 5-8: Scale manual
- Onboard 3-5 customers
- Create templates and shortcuts
- Build SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)
- Measure time per customer

Week 8-12: Start automating
- Identify highest-time tasks
- Build simple automations
- Create self-service components
- Test automated delivery vs. manual

Week 12-16: Prepare for software
- Define feature set (based on manual work)
- Build landing page
- Start waitlist for self-service version
- Transition manual customers to software beta
```

### Scaling Your Time

**The solo founder's time constraint:**

```
Maximum customers in a concierge MVP:
- 5 hours/week per customer (typical)
- 40 hours/week available (you)
- 8 customers maximum (no margin)
- 5 customers comfortable (with buffer)

If you have 5 customers at $500/month:
- Revenue: $2,500/month
- Hours: 25/week
- Sustainable: Yes, for 3-6 months
- This funds 3-6 months of software development
```

**When to stop manual and start building:**

```
Signals:
1. You have 5+ paying customers
2. They've been paying for 2+ months
3. You can articulate the exact feature set
4. You're turning away new customers (capacity)
5. You're repeating the same steps for every customer
6. You have a waitlist of interested customers

If these are true, it's time to build the software.
```

## Concierge MVP Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: You Never Automate
Some founders get stuck in the concierge phase. It's comfortable, paying, and doesn't require building. But it's not scalable.

**Solution:** Set a hard deadline for automation. "I will stop manual work by [date] and switch to software."

### Pitfall 2: You Automate Too Early
You built software after 2 manual deliveries. You didn't learn enough. The software automates the wrong things.

**Solution:** Do at least 20-30 manual deliveries before automating. Learn every edge case.

### Pitfall 3: You Charge Too Little
If you charge $50/month for a concierge service, you'll need 40+ customers to sustain yourself. That's 40x the manual work.

**Solution:** Charge what your time is worth. $200-500/month minimum for concierge B2B services.

### Pitfall 4: You Charge Too Much
$2,000/month might be fair for your time but unsustainable for software pricing (hard to convert to $99/month SaaS).

**Solution:** Design the concierge service so the software version has a clear price path. If your concierge service is $500/month, the software can be $99-199/month.

### Pitfall 5: You Don't Document
You do the work manually but don't write down what you're doing. When it's time to build, you have to re-learn everything.

**Solution:** Record your screen. Take notes. Write SOPs. Document every step.

### Pitfall 6: You Build for One Customer
You automate exactly what your first customer asked for. Turns out it doesn't work for anyone else.

**Solution:** Serve 5+ customers before automating. Build for the PATTERN, not the specific request.

## The Concierge MVP Checklist

### Before Starting

- [ ] I've identified a problem that can be solved manually
- [ ] I've verified someone will pay for the manual solution
- [ ] I have a clear process for delivering the service
- [ ] I know how I'll find my first 3-5 customers
- [ ] I've set a price that covers my time
- [ ] I've defined success criteria (how many customers = I should automate)
- [ ] I've set a timeline for switching from manual to software

### During Delivery

- [ ] I track time per delivery
- [ ] I document every step of the process
- [ ] I collect customer feedback after each delivery
- [ ] I note what can be automated
- [ ] I note what CAN'T be automated (these are your differentiators)
- [ ] I ask for referrals
- [ ] I ask for testimonials
- [ ] I measure customer satisfaction

### Before Building Software

- [ ] I have 5+ paying concierge customers
- [ ] They've been paying for 2+ months
- [ ] I've documented the full process
- [ ] I know the top 3 features to build first
- [ ] I know the price point for the software version
- [ ] My manual customers have agreed to switch to software
- [ ] I have a waitlist of new customers who want software
- [ ] I have 3-6 months of savings to build the software

## The Concierge MVP Mindset

### You Are Not a Service Business
The goal is not to build a service business. The goal is to VALIDATE a product idea. If you're still doing manual work after 12 months, you've failed at the MVP but succeeded at a service business. That's not bad, but it's not the goal.

### Every Manual Task is Product Research
Every time you do something manually for a customer, ask:
- "Would software make this easier?"
- "Could I build this so the customer does it themselves?"
- "Is this step really necessary, or is it because of the manual process?"

### Your Customers Are Your Co-Founders
Treat your concierge customers as product advisors. Give them special access. Ask for their opinions. Share your roadmap. Make them feel invested in your success — because they are.

### The Goal is Learning, Not Earning
Yes, revenue from concierge services is great. But the real value is what you learn. Don't optimize for revenue during this phase. Optimize for learning as much as possible about the problem, the solution, and the customer.

## The Concierge MVP Promise

When you run a concierge MVP, you're making a promise to your customers — and to yourself:

**To your customers:** "I will solve this problem for you. Eventually, I'll build software so you can solve it yourself. Until then, I'll do it manually, and you'll get the best service of your life."

**To yourself:** "I will not build software until I'm certain people will pay for it, I understand the problem deeply, and I know exactly what to build."

Keep these promises, and you'll build a successful SaaS product with minimal risk and maximum learning.

**Don't build until you've served. Don't automate until you've done it manually. Don't scale until you've validated.**
