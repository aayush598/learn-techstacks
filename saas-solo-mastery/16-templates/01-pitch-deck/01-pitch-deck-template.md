# SaaS Pitch Deck Template: 10-15 Slides for Fundraising

## Executive Summary

This template provides a complete pitch deck structure for SaaS companies raising their first institutional round (Seed to Series A). Each slide includes what to include, what investors look for, example content, and design tips. Fill in the [bracketed] sections with your specific information.

---

## Slide Structure Overview

```
1.  Title Slide — Company name, tagline, logo
2.  Problem — The pain you solve
3.  Solution — Your product
4.  Market Size — TAM, SAM, SOM
5.  Why Now — Market timing
6.  Product — Screenshots, demo, key features
7.  Traction — Metrics, revenue, growth
8.  Business Model — Pricing, unit economics
9.  Competition — Landscape, your advantage
10. Go-to-Market — Distribution, sales strategy
11. Team — Founders, key hires, advisors
12. Financials — Projections, assumptions
13. Ask — What you're raising, use of funds
14. Vision — Where you're going
15. Appendix — Supporting data (optional, for Q&A)
```

**Slide count guide:**
- Seed round: 10-12 slides (get to the point faster)
- Series A: 12-15 slides (more detail on traction and team)
- Always leave time for Q&A (most important part)

```
[Product screenshot or logo]
```

**Example:**

```
[Beautiful dashboard screenshot]

TaskFlow - January 2025

```

**Design tips:**
- Clean, professional design
- Your logo prominently displayed
- Product screenshot if visually appealing
- No more than 6-8 words in tagline
- Use company colors consistently

## Slide 2: Problem

**Purpose:** Make investors feel the pain you solve.

**Content:**
```
THE PROBLEM

Headline: [The pain point in one sentence]

[For whom is this a problem?]

Before [your solution]:
- [Pain point 1 - specific and quantifiable]
- [Pain point 2 - specific and quantifiable]
- [Pain point 3 - specific and quantifiable]

[Quote from real customer or statistic]

Cost of the problem:
[Annual cost or time wasted by this problem]
```

**Example:**
```
Remote teams waste 8 hours/week on project coordination

For: Distributed teams of 10-100 people

Before TaskFlow:
- Status update meetings take 4+ hours/week
- 35% of time spent on coordination, not work
- 60% of teams don't know what others are working on

"The Monday status meeting is the biggest waste of my week."
- Engineering Manager, mid-size tech company

$15,000/year lost per employee to coordination overhead
```

**Investor check:**
- Is this a real, painful problem?
- How many people have this problem?
- Are they already paying for solutions?
- Is it a "vitamin" or "painkiller"?

## Slide 3: Solution

**Purpose:** Show how you solve the problem.

**Content:**
```
THE SOLUTION

[Company Name] [description of what you do]

[Key benefit in one sentence]

How it works:
1. [Key feature/capability 1]
2. [Key feature/capability 2]
3. [Key feature/capability 3]

[Simple diagram or visual showing the solution flow]

Why it's better:
- [Differentiator 1]
- [Differentiator 2]
- [Differentiator 3]
```

**Example:**
```
TaskFlow automates project coordination for remote teams

"Projects that run themselves"

How it works:
1. Connect Slack, Jira, Asana, and Google Calendar
2. TaskFlow AI automatically syncs status across tools
3. Daily briefings sent to team, no meetings needed

Why TaskFlow wins:
- 90% reduction in status meetings
- Real-time visibility without manual updates
- Works with existing tools (no migration needed)
```

**Investor check:**
- Is the solution clear and compelling?
- Does it directly address the problem?
- Is it differentiated from existing approaches?
- Can you explain it in 30 seconds?

```
Technology enablers:
```

**Purpose:** Show how you make money.

**Content:**
```
BUSINESS MODEL

Pricing:
- [Tier 1]: $[X]/month for [features]
- [Tier 2]: $[X]/month for [features]
- [Tier 3]: $[X]/month for [features]

- CAC: $[X]

- Payback period: [X] months
- Gross margin: [X]%

[% from subscriptions, % from usage, % from services]

- Net Revenue Retention (NRR): [X]%
- Upgrade rate: [X]%
```

**Example:**
```
Free: $0 - Up to 10 users (limited features)
Pro: $10/user/month - Full features, unlimited projects
Business: $25/user/month - Analytics, integrations, priority support
Enterprise: Custom - SSO, dedicated support, custom SLAs
```

**Investor check:**
- Are unit economics venture-grade? (LTV:CAC > 3:1)

- Is there expansion revenue potential?
- Is NRR above 100%?

## Pitch Deck Best Practices

### Content Guidelines

**The 3-second test:** Can someone understand what you do in 3 seconds?

**The "grandmother test":** Would your grandmother understand the value?

**The 10/20/30 rule:** 10 slides, 20 minutes, 30pt font minimum.

**Story arc:**
```
Problem (emotional hook)
  -> Solution (relief)
    -> Market (opportunity)
      -> Product (how it works)
        -> Traction (proof)
          -> Business Model (how we make money)
            -> Competition (why we'll win)
              -> GTM (how we'll grow)
                -> Team (why we can do it)
                  -> Ask (what we need)
```

### Design Guidelines

- Use your brand colors consistently
- One idea per slide
- No paragraphs (bullets, short sentences)
- Charts and graphs over tables
- Screenshots over descriptions
- 30pt+ font minimum
- High-quality images only
- No clip art, no stock photos of people shaking hands

### Delivery Tips

- Practice until you can do it without notes
- Know your numbers cold (no "umms" when asked)
- Have answers for the 10 most likely questions
- The best decks feel like conversations, not presentations
- Watch for investor energy (skip sections they're excited about)
- Leave time for Q&A (this is where deals are won/lost)

### Common Pitch Deck Mistakes

1. **Too much text** — You're presenting, not sending reading material
2. **No traction** — "We just launched" is not a compelling reason to invest
3. **Ignoring competition** — Hurts credibility
4. **Vague market size** — "We serve the $1T software market" is meaningless
5. **No business model** — At seed, show at least pricing and unit economics
6. **Unrealistic projections** — "We'll get 1% of the $100B market in year 1"
7. **Founder-only team** — Show you can hire/manage a team
8. **Burying the ask** — Be clear about what you need and why
