# Investor Update Template: Monthly & Quarterly Communications

## Executive Summary

Regular investor updates are critical for maintaining trust, building relationships, and setting up future fundraising success. This template provides a complete structure for your monthly or quarterly investor communications, including the metrics to share, narrative to craft, and asks to make.

---

## Section 1: The Philosophy of Investor Updates

### 1.1 Why Updates Matter

**For current investors (building relationships):**
- Trust through transparency (good news + bad news)
- They can help more when they know what's happening
- Makes future communications easier (they're already in the loop)
- They'll be your biggest advocates

**For future fundraising:**
- Investors forward good updates to other investors
- A track record of great updates builds credibility
- Reference calls go better when investors feel informed
- You demonstrate executive communication skills

**For you (the founder):**
- Forces regular reflection on the business
- Helps spot trends early (good and bad)
- Creates a historical record of your journey
- Practice your investor communication skills

### 1.2 When to Send Updates

| Round | Frequency | Best Time |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Pre-seed / Bootstrapped | Monthly | First week of month |
| Seed | Monthly | First week of month |
| Series A | Monthly | First week of month |
| Series B+ | Monthly or Quarterly | Within 2 weeks of period end |
| Pre-IPO | Quarterly | Within 3 weeks of quarter end |

**General rule:** Monthly is best for pre-Series B. Once you have institutional investors, monthly is expected.

**Send on a consistent day:** First Monday of the month. Investors learn to expect it.

## Section 4: Investor Update Best Practices

### 4.1 What to Always Include

```
Always include:
  - Revenue MRR (or closest SaaS metric)
  - Growth rate (MoM)
  - Cash balance
  - Runway (months)
  - Top win (every update should have good news)
  - Top challenge (every update should be honest)
  - At least one ask (keeps investors engaged)
```

### 4.2 What to Never Include

```
Never include:
  - Fluff or filler (be concise, respect their time)
  - Excuses without plans (bad things happen, show solutions)
  - Complaining about investors (this update goes to all of them)
  - Sensitive info you wouldn't want leaked (it may leak)
  - Negative comments about team members
  - Competitor smear campaigns
```

### 4.3 Tone & Style

**Good investor updates do this:**
- Sound like they're written by a confident leader
- Acknowledge problems without being alarmist
- Celebrate wins without being hyperbolic
- Show progress toward stated goals
- Include specific, believable numbers

**Voice examples:**

*Bad:*
"Things are going well. We're growing fast and customers love us. We shipped some stuff and hired some people. We're running out of cash but should be fine."

*Good:*
"This month we hit $52K MRR (12% MoM, above our 10% target). The big win was launching our Slack integration which drove 30% of new signups. Our challenge: churn ticked up to 2.1% from 1.8%, driven by small teams. We're launching a small-team onboarding program next week to address this. Cash is $420K, giving us 8 months of runway."

### 4.4 Timing & Consistency

- Send on the same date every month (e.g., 5th business day)
- Set a calendar reminder to prepare it (3 days before)
- Batch all updates to go out at once
- Use a template (this document!) for consistency
- Track open rates (if using email)

### 4.5 Distribution

**Who gets the update:**
- All current investors (angels, VCs, syndicates)
- Key advisors (they should be as informed as investors)
- Board members (if separate from investors)
- Potential future investors (if you're building relationship)

**How to send:**
- Email with inline summary + link to full version
- Use a simple tool like Mailchimp, ConvertKit, or just Gmail
- Include a "confidential" note at the top
- Use a consistent subject line format

### 4.6 Measuring Success

```
Metrics to track:
  - Open rate: Target > 70% (you want investors reading)
  - Reply rate: Target > 20% (you want engagement)
  - Help received: Number of asks fulfilled
  - Forward rate: Investors forwarding to others
```

## Section 7: Advanced Investor Communication

### 7.1 Bad News Protocol

**When things go wrong, follow this protocol:**

1. **Tell investors before they ask** (don't wait for the monthly update)
2. **State the facts clearly** (no sugar-coating)
3. **Explain root cause** (why it happened)
4. **State your response** (what you're doing about it)
5. **Ask for help if needed** (investors appreciate being brought in)

**Bad news email template:**
```
Subject: [Company] - [Issue] - [Action we're taking]

Hi [Investor],

I wanted to give you a heads up about [issue].

What happened:
[2-3 sentences on the situation]

Impact:
[Revenue impact, timeline impact, customer impact]

Root cause:
[Why it happened]

Our response:
[What we're doing, when it's resolved]

What we need:
[Any asks - intros, advice, resources]

We'll keep you updated. Happy to hop on a call if you want
to discuss further.

[Your name]
```

### 7.2 Asking for Warm Intros

**The right way to ask for an intro:**

```
Bad:
"Can you intro me to [Company]?"

Good:
"I'm looking to connect with [Company]'s Head of Product.
[Specific reason why they're relevant]. Do you know anyone
in your network who might be a good fit?

Specifically, I'm looking for:
- [What you want from the connection]
- [What you offer in return]
- [Any mutual context you've identified]
```

### 7.3 Investor Meeting Prep

**When an investor wants to meet based on your update:**

```
Prepare:
  - Current metrics (memorized)
  - Key narrative points (3 wins, 3 challenges)
  - Specific asks (3-5)
  - Updated deck (if needed)

During:
  - Start with the good news
  - Be honest about challenges
  - Ask for their advice (they love giving it)
  - State specific asks clearly

After:
  - Send thank you within 24 hours
  - Follow up on any promises
  - Add them to your update list (if not already)
  - Note their advice and reference it next update
```
