# Comprehensive SaaS Business Plan Template with Financial Projections

## Executive Summary

This template provides a complete business plan structure specifically designed for SaaS companies. It covers everything from executive summary to detailed financial projections, with fill-in-the-blank sections for each component. Use this to plan your SaaS, raise funding, or clarify your strategy.

---

## Section 1: Executive Summary

**Write this last, but it appears first.**

```
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

[Company Name] is a [description] that [core value proposition].

We serve [target customer] who need [problem/pain point].
Our solution [differentiated approach] enables [key benefit].

Market opportunity: $[TAM] TAM, growing at [X]% CAGR.

Current traction:
- MRR: $___ (growing ___% MoM)
- Customers: ___
- Average NPS: ___
- Monthly churn: ___%

Team: [Founder background and key team members]

We are raising $[X] to [primary use of funds].

Projected ARR:
- Year 1: $___
- Year 2: $___
- Year 3: $___
```

**Example:**
```
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

TaskFlow is an AI-powered project coordination platform that
automates status updates, standups, and meeting scheduling for
remote teams.

We serve distributed engineering teams of 10-100 people who
waste 8 hours/week on coordination overhead. Our solution
automatically syncs across Slack, Jira, Asana, and Linear,
eliminating 90% of status meetings.

Market opportunity: $40B project management software market,
growing at 22% CAGR (remote work tailwind).

Current traction:
- MRR: $52K (12% MoM growth)
- Customers: 187
- NPS: 67
- Monthly churn: 1.8%

Team: CEO (ex-Slack, 5 yrs building project mgmt features),
CTO (ex-OpenAI, AI systems expert).

We are raising $3M to expand our AI capabilities, build
integrations, and scale marketing.

Projected ARR:
- Year 1 (current): $624K
- Year 2: $1.8M
- Year 3: $4.2M
```

## Business Plan Writing Tips

1. **Be specific, not vague** — "We target mid-market companies" vs "Companies with 50-500 employees in SaaS verticals"

2. **Use real data** — "According to Gartner, the market is $40B" vs "The market is huge"

3. **Show traction** — If you have customers, revenue, or usage, lead with it

4. **Be honest about risks** — Every business has risks; acknowledging them shows maturity

5. **Keep it realistic** — Hockey-stick projections are a red flag to experienced investors

6. **Connect every section** — The problem, solution, market, and business model should all be consistent

7. **Write for your audience** — Investors want to see market size and unit economics; partners want to see GTM; team members want to see vision

8. **Update regularly** — A business plan is a living document. Update it quarterly

---

*The best business plan is one that you actually use to run your business, not one that sits in a drawer. Write it to clarify your own thinking, and the fundraising and alignment benefits will follow naturally.*
