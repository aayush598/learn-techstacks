# Day-by-Day Action Plan for the First 30 Days of Your SaaS Journey

## Executive Summary

The first 30 days of your SaaS journey will determine your trajectory. This day-by-day plan takes you from idea to first paying customer in 30 days. It's aggressive but achievable if you stay focused and execute consistently.

---

## Section 1: The 30-Day Philosophy

### 1.1 The Sprint Mindset

```
Days 1-7:  VALIDATE (Prove the problem exists)
Days 8-14: BUILD (Create the minimum solution)
Days 15-21: LAUNCH (Get it in front of real users)
Days 22-30: ITERATE (Get first paying customers)

Each week builds on the last. Don't skip ahead.
Don't get stuck perfecting. Move fast.
```

### 1.2 Success Metrics for 30 Days

```
Primary metric: 1-5 paying customers
Secondary:
  - Validated problem (10+ problem interviews)
  - MVP shipped (ugly but functional)
  - Landing page with conversion tracking
  - First $X in MRR
  - 3-5 pieces of content published

Reality check:
  - 5 paying customers in 30 days = GREAT
  - 0 paying customers but strong validation = GOOD
  - No validation and no MVP = START OVER
```

### 1.3 Daily Time Commitment

```
Week 1 (Validation): 4-6 hours/day
Week 2 (Building): 6-8 hours/day
Week 3 (Launch): 8-10 hours/day
Week 4 (Iterate): 6-8 hours/day

Total: ~200 hours for the month
```

## Section 2: Week 1 — Validate (Days 1-7)

### Day 1: Idea Selection & Hypothesis

```
MORNING (2 hours):
[ ] Brain dump all your SaaS ideas (20+)
[ ] Pick top 3 ideas based on:
    - Personal expertise
    - Market size
    - Willingness to pay
    - Buildability (can I build this solo?)

[ ] For each top idea, write a hypothesis:
    "I believe [target customer] needs [solution]
    because [problem]. They are currently using
    [alternative]. I can reach them via [channel]."

AFTERNOON (2 hours):
[ ] Research each idea:
    - Search Reddit for pain points
    - Check Twitter/LinkedIn for complaints
    - Read competitor reviews (what do people hate?)
    - Look for existing solutions and gaps

[ ] Pick ONE idea to pursue (commit!)

EVENING (1 hour):
[ ] Create a simple landing page (Carrd/Unicorn Platform):
    - Headline: Problem statement
    - Subheadline: Your solution
    - Email capture field
    - 1-2 sentences of copy
[ ] Set up basic analytics (Plausible/Google Analytics)
```

### Day 2: Problem Deep Dive

```
MORNING (3 hours):
[ ] Research the problem in depth:
    - Google search for your problem space
    - Read 10+ articles/blog posts about the problem
    - Find communities where your customers hang out
    - Identify 20+ potential interview candidates

[ ] Create interview script:
    - What's your current process for [task]?
    - What frustrates you about it?
    - What have you tried to solve it?
    - What would your ideal solution look like?
    - Would you pay $[X]/month for a solution?

AFTERNOON (2 hours):
[ ] Reach out to 20+ people for interviews:
    - LinkedIn direct messages
    - Twitter DMs
    - Reddit/community DMs
    - Email outreach

EVENING (1 hour):
[ ] Conduct first 2-3 problem interviews
[ ] Record key insights from each interview
```

### Day 3: Customer Interviews (Part 1)

```
MORNING (2 hours):
[ ] Conduct 3-5 more problem interviews
[ ] Listen for patterns in responses

AFTERNOON (3 hours):
[ ] Continue outreach for more interviews
[ ] Conduct additional interviews
[ ] Document all responses in a spreadsheet:

    | Person | Role | Problem | Current Solution | Pain Level (1-10) | Willingness to Pay |
    |--------|------|---------|------------------|--------------------|--------------------|

EVENING (1 hour):
[ ] Review all interview notes
[ ] Identify top 3 patterns/insights
[ ] Adjust your hypothesis based on learnings
```

### Day 4: Customer Interviews (Part 2)

```
MORNING (2 hours):
[ ] Conduct 3-5 more interviews (aim for 10-15 total)

AFTERNOON (3 hours):
[ ] Analyze interview data:
    - What's the #1 pain point?
    - How bad is it really? (1-10)
    - Are they willing to pay? (How much?)
    - Can you reach them? (Where?)
    - Is the market big enough?

[ ] Go/No-Go Decision:
    - Strong pain + willingness to pay = GO
    - Weak pain or no willingness to pay = PIVOT
    - If pivot, start over with next idea

EVENING (1 hour):
[ ] If GO: Create your "Minimum Viable Product" spec
    - List the absolute minimum features to sell
    - NOT to make users happy — to get them to pay
    - 3-5 core features max
```

### Day 5: Competitive Analysis

```
MORNING (2 hours):
[ ] Identify 5-10 direct and indirect competitors
[ ] Sign up for all of them (use free trials)
[ ] Take notes:
    - What do they do well?
    - What do they do poorly?
    - What's their pricing?
    - What do reviews complain about?
    - What's their positioning?

AFTERNOON (3 hours):
[ ] Create competitive matrix:

    Feature | Competitor 1 | Competitor 2 | Competitor 3 | [Us]
    [Feature 1] | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ | ✓
    [Feature 2] | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | ✓
    [Feature 3] | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ | ✓
    Pricing | $X | $Y | $Z | $?

[ ] Define your unique angle (what will make you win)

EVENING (1 hour):
[ ] Update landing page with competitive positioning
[ ] Write your value proposition statement
[ ] Share your idea with 3-5 people for feedback
```

### Day 6: Pricing & Business Model

```
MORNING (2 hours):
[ ] Research pricing models:
    - Flat rate (one price, all features)
    - Tiered (3-4 plans with different features)
    - Per-user pricing
    - Usage-based pricing
    - Hybrid

[ ] Define your pricing:
    - Best competitor pricing: $[X]
    - Your price: $[X] (consider value-based pricing)
    - Annual discount: [X]%
    - Free trial: [X] days

AFTERNOON (2 hours):
[ ] Create pricing page mockup
[ ] Test pricing with 5-10 potential customers:
    "Would you pay $[X]/month for this?"

EVENING (2 hours):
[ ] Set up Stripe account if not done already
[ ] Define your metric tracking:
    - MRR target for month 1: $[X]
    - MRR target for month 3: $[X]
    - Maximum acceptable churn: [X]%
    - Target ARPU: $[X]
```

### Day 7: Week 1 Review & Week 2 Plan

```
MORNING (2 hours):
[ ] Week 1 retrospective:
    - What worked well?
    - What was harder than expected?
    - What did you learn?
    - Are you still confident in the idea?

[ ] Validate:
    - Number of interviews completed: ___
    - Pain level confirmed? [Yes/No]
    - Willingness to pay confirmed? [Yes/No]
    - Decision: [GO / PIVOT]

AFTERNOON (2 hours):
[ ] Define exact MVP scope:
    - Feature 1: [Description] - Essential? [Yes/No]
    - Feature 2: [Description] - Essential? [Yes/No]
    - Feature 3: [Description] - Essential? [Yes/No]
    - Feature 4: [Description] - Essential? [Yes/No]
    - Feature 5: [Description] - Essential? [Yes/No]

[ ] Cut everything that's not essential

EVENING (2 hours):
[ ] Set up development environment
[ ] Create project board (Linear/Asana/GitHub Projects)
[ ] Plan Week 2 day-by-day
[ ] Get a good night's sleep (Week 2 is intense)
```

## Section 3: Week 2 — Build (Days 8-14)

### Day 8: Core Feature 1 — Authentication & Setup

```
FULL DAY (8 hours):
[ ] Set up project (Next.js/Rails/Django + whatever stack)
[ ] Implement authentication (Auth0/Clerk/Firebase)
[ ] Set up database (PostgreSQL)
[ ] Create basic user model
[ ] Implement signup flow
[ ] Set up Stripe billing integration
[ ] Deploy to staging
[ ] Create admin/user roles

Goal by end of day: User can sign up and log in.
```

### Day 9: Core Feature 2 — Primary Functionality

```
FULL DAY (8 hours):
[ ] Build the #1 most important feature
[ ] This is the feature that delivers the core value
[ ] It doesn't need to be perfect — it needs to work
[ ] No edge cases, no error handling, no polish
[ ] Get the happy path working

Goal by end of day: User can complete the primary action.
```

### Day 10: Core Feature 3 — Secondary Functionality

```
FULL DAY (8 hours):
[ ] Build feature #2 (second most important)
[ ] Build feature #3 (third most important)
[ ] Connect features to each other
[ ] Basic data flow end-to-end

Goal by end of day: User can complete primary workflow.
```

### Day 11: Payment & Billing Integration

```
MORNING (4 hours):
[ ] Set up Stripe products and prices
[ ] Implement checkout flow
[ ] Implement subscription management
[ ] Test payment flow end-to-end (use test mode)
[ ] Set up webhooks (successful payment, failed payment)

AFTERNOON (4 hours):
[ ] Test upgrade/downgrade flow
[ ] Test free trial management
[ ] Test cancellation flow
[ ] Set up dunning (failed payment retry logic)
[ ] Create invoices/receipt flow

Goal by end of day: User can pay for the product.
```

### Day 12: Onboarding & Polish

```
MORNING (4 hours):
[ ] Build welcome email (basic)
[ ] Create in-app onboarding (tooltips or checklist)
[ ] Build post-signup redirect to first action

AFTERNOON (4 hours):
[ ] Fix the 5 most critical bugs
[ ] Improve the 5 worst UX issues
[ ] Add loading states and error messages
[ ] Mobile responsiveness check
[ ] Performance check (load time < 2 seconds)

Goal by end of day: New user can sign up and get value.
```

### Day 13: Landing Page & Public Site

```
MORNING (4 hours):
[ ] Finalize landing page copy
[ ] Add product screenshots
[ ] Write "How It Works" section
[ ] Add pricing section
[ ] Add FAQ section
[ ] Add proper CTA buttons

AFTERNOON (4 hours):
[ ] Add analytics tracking (all pages)
[ ] Add UTM tracking for campaigns
[ ] Set up Google Search Console
[ ] Submit sitemap to Google
[ ] Social media preview images (OG tags)
[ ] Set up email capture (for non-converting visitors)

Goal by end of day: Public site is ready for launch.
```

### Day 14: Week 2 Review & Launch Prep

```
MORNING (4 hours):
[ ] Full end-to-end test:
    - Visitor finds site
    - Signs up
    - Completes onboarding
    - Upgrades to paid
    - Receives confirmation email
    - Cancels subscription

[ ] Bug bash — fix anything broken
[ ] Performance optimization
[ ] Security basics (HTTPS, rate limiting, etc.)

AFTERNOON (4 hours):
[ ] Prepare launch assets (screenshots, GIFs)
[ ] Write launch post draft
[ ] Compile list of early launch targets:
    - Personal network: 20+ people
    - IndieHackers
    - Relevant communities
    - Social media followers
[ ] Prepare "first 10 customers" outreach list

EVENING (2 hours):
[ ] Deploy to production
[ ] Final test on production
[ ] Set up monitoring (errors, uptime)
[ ] Prepare support templates
[ ] Get good sleep (tomorrow is launch day)
```

## Section 4: Week 3 — Launch (Days 15-21)

### Day 15: Soft Launch

```
MORNING (4 hours):
[ ] Send personal emails to 20 people:
    "Hey [Name], I built something you might find
    useful. [Product] helps [outcome]. Would love
    for you to try it (free): [link]"

[ ] Post on personal social media:
    "Just launched [Product]! [Value prop]. Would
    love your feedback: [link]"

[ ] Share in 3-5 communities where allowed:
    - IndieHackers "Show HN" equivalent
    - Niche Slack/Discord communities
    - Relevant subreddits (if allowed)

AFTERNOON (4 hours):
[ ] Monitor signups and engagement
[ ] Respond to every user who signs up (personally)
[ ] Collect feedback from first users
[ ] Fix critical issues immediately
[ ] Follow up with anyone who showed interest

Goal: 10-50 signups, 1-5 active users.
```

### Day 16: First Feedback Loop

```
MORNING (2 hours):
[ ] Review signups from yesterday
[ ] Reach out to every user who signed up:
    "How's it going? Any questions or feedback?"

AFTERNOON (4 hours):
[ ] Conduct 5+ user feedback calls (15 min each)
[ ] Note the top 3 issues/requests
[ ] Fix the most critical 3 issues immediately

EVENING (2 hours):
[ ] Write first "product update" to users
[ ] Continue outreach for more signups
[ ] Apply feedback to product

Goal: Understand what's working and what's broken.
```

### Day 17: Marketing Push

```
MORNING (3 hours):
[ ] Write and publish first blog post:
    - "How to [solve problem] with [product]"
    - 1000+ words, SEO-optimized
    - Include screenshots and examples

AFTERNOON (3 hours):
[ ] Share blog post on:
    - LinkedIn (long-form post)
    - Twitter (thread or tweet)
    - IndieHackers
    - Relevant communities
    - Your email list

EVENING (2 hours):
[ ] Continue outreach for signups
[ ] Engage with comments and feedback
[ ] Track blog post performance

Goal: 500+ views on blog post, 5+ new signups.
```

### Day 18: Convert First Customer

```
MORNING (4 hours):
[ ] Identify 5 most engaged trial users
[ ] Reach out personally:
    "Hey [Name], I noticed you've been using [Product]
    actively. How's it going? Anything I can help with?"

[ ] Offer personalized onboarding call
[ ] Proactively address their biggest pain point

AFTERNOON (4 hours):
[ ] Follow up with all trial users approaching end
[ ] Offer extended trial if needed
[ ] Handle objections directly
[ ] Ask for the sale: "Would you like to upgrade?"

EVENING (1 hour):
[ ] Remove barriers to payment
[ ] Celebrate if you got your first customer!
[ ] Fix any issues that blocked conversion
```

### Day 19: First Customer Success

```
MORNING (3 hours):
[ ] If you have paying customers:
    - Send them a personal thank you
    - Ask for feedback
    - Ask for a testimonial
    - Ask for a referral

[ ] If you don't have paying customers:
    - Analyze where users drop off
    - Fix the #1 conversion blocker
    - Offer more help/reach out again

AFTERNOON (3 hours):
[ ] Continue outreach and marketing
[ ] Write another piece of content
[ ] Engage in communities

EVENING (2 hours):
[ ] Review first customer experience
[ ] Improve onboarding based on learnings
[ ] Plan for scaling outreach
```

### Day 20: Build Momentum

```
MORNING (3 hours):
[ ] Double down on what's working:
    - Which outreach channel got the most signups?
    - Which messaging resonated?
    - Which feature convinced people to pay?

[ ] Cut what's not working:
    - Pause channels with 0 results
    - Pause features nobody uses
    - Stop perfecting unproven things

AFTERNOON (3 hours):
[ ] Expand to 2-3 new communities/channels
[ ] Create a "share your experience" moment
[ ] Ask satisfied users to share on social media

EVENING (2 hours):
[ ] Track all metrics
[ ] Update landing page based on learnings
[ ] Prepare for Week 4 push
```

### Day 21: Week 3 Review

```
MORNING (2 hours):
[ ] Measure results:
    - Total signups: ___
    - Active users: ___
    - Paying customers: ___
    - MRR: $___
    - Churn rate: ___%

[ ] Reflect:
    - What's working? (double down)
    - What's not working? (cut)
    - What did we learn? (document)

AFTERNOON (2 hours):
[ ] Plan Week 4:
    - Goal: [X] paying customers
    - Key initiatives:
      1. [Initiative 1]
      2. [Initiative 2]
      3. [Initiative 3]

[ ] Take a break (rest is important)

EVENING (1 hour):
[ ] Prepare for growth push
[ ] Get good sleep
```

## Section 5: Week 4 — Iterate & Grow (Days 22-30)

### Day 22-23: Conversion Optimization

```
[ ] Analyze conversion funnel:
    Visitor -> Signup: ___%
    Signup -> Active: ___%
    Active -> Paid: ___%

[ ] Identify biggest drop-off point
[ ] A/B test the fix:
    - Test headline variations
    - Test CTA button text
    - Test pricing presentation
    - Test onboarding flow

[ ] Implement winning variation
[ ] Continue outreach and marketing
```

### Day 24-25: Content & SEO Push

```
[ ] Write 2 more blog posts:
    - "X ways to [solve problem]"
    - "[Case study] how [user] achieved [result]"

[ ] Repurpose into social media content:
    - 5 tweets from each article
    - 2 LinkedIn posts
    - 1 Reddit post (relevant subreddit)

[ ] Build backlinks:
    - Comment on industry blogs (with link to your content)
    - Guest post outreach (1-2 target publications)
    - List your tool on SaaS directories
```

### Day 26-27: Feedback-Driven Improvements

```
[ ] Collect feedback from all users
[ ] Identify top 3 feature requests
[ ] Build the most-requested small feature
[ ] Fix the top 3 bugs/complaints
[ ] Improve the #1 usability issue

[ ] Send "we shipped your feedback" email:
    "You asked, we built! Here's what's new..."
```

### Day 28-29: Referral & Viral Loop

```
[ ] Implement referral program (basic):
    "Refer a friend, get 1 month free"

[ ] Ask 3 satisfied customers for testimonials
[ ] Ask 3 satisfied customers for referrals
[ ] Create "share" moment in the product

[ ] Write "week 1 results" post on IndieHackers
[ ] Share learnings on Twitter/LinkedIn
```
