# Number Series — Topic Guide

> Section 1 · Aptitude · Number Series · _Estimated reading time: 30 min for the full topic_

## What the pre-test asks in this topic

The DSE pre-test's aptitude section regularly includes **1–2 number-series questions**. Three
formats appear:

1. **Next number** — "3, 6, 12, 24, ?" → 48.
2. **Missing middle term** — "1, 4, 9, ?, 36" → 25.
3. **Wrong term** — "2, 6, 12, 20, 30, 44, 56" → 44.

The arithmetic is trivial. The mark is for recognising which pattern family the series uses, and for
not spending four minutes on it.

## Learning objectives

After this topic you should be able to:

- Build a **difference row** (and a ratio row) in under 20 seconds and read the family off it.
- Recognise the eight pattern families used in these papers: arithmetic, geometric,
  difference-of-difference, square/cube, Fibonacci-like, alternating operations, prime-based and
  digit-based.
- Recognise squares, cubes, `n(n+1)` and powers of 2 and 3 **instantly** from a table.
- Split a series into odd and even positions when a single rule does not fit.
- Extend a series by **one step** and match to an option — without stretching the rule to fit.
- Solve a **wrong-term** question by finding the rule first and then recomputing the correct value.
- Recognise digit-based rules through constant digit sums.
- Use the constant second difference to extend a non-arithmetic series.
- Finish a series question in **70 seconds** or less, and mark-and-move when it will not crack.

## Quick 3-step solving procedure

1. **Write the difference row** (15 s). If it is not constant, write the ratio row (15 s). The first
   constant row you find tells you the family.
2. **Name the family and extend one step** (20 s). Test in order: arithmetic → geometric →
   difference-of-difference → squares/cubes → Fibonacci → alternating → primes → digit-based.
3. **Match to an option** (20 s). If your value is not an option, the family is wrong — do not extend
   further. For a wrong-term question, recompute the correct value and pick the printed term. At 70
   seconds, mark the closest option and move.

## Key takeaways

- ✅ **Differences first, always.** One row of differences beats ten seconds of staring.
- ✅ **Extend one step only.** A miss means the family is wrong, not that you need more steps.
- ✅ **Family test order is fixed.** Arithmetic → geometric → second difference → squares/cubes →
  Fibonacci → alternating → primes → digit-based.
- ✅ **Squares give odd-step differences; n(n+1) gives even-step differences.** Instant recognition.
- ✅ **Interleaved series:** split by position before trying more families.
- ✅ **Wrong term = the rule decides.** Recompute what the term should be.
- ✅ **70 seconds, then move.** One series is 1 mark; the next three questions are worth 3.
- ⚠️ **Meera's warning:** she starts testing random patterns and loses 3 minutes. The difference row
  would have cracked the first one in 15 seconds.
