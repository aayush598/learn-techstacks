# Question Bank — Part A: Foundations (Q1–Q24)

## In one sentence

Twenty-four foundation questions across every archetype, with the full five-option answer key and a
one-line reason for each answer.

## Why the pre-test tests this

- This is the **diagnostic**. Do it untimed first, then timed.
- The first ten questions are data sufficiency — the archetype that decides the pace of the whole
  section. The remaining fourteen are the arithmetic you must never lose points on.
- Every answer below has a reason. If your reason differs from the one printed, you solved a
  different problem — find the misread before you move on.

## Worked example

### A1 — Data sufficiency

**Q1.** What is the number of boys in a class of 40 students?

- **Statement I:** The ratio of boys to girls is 3 : 5.
- **Statement II:** The number of girls is 20 more than the number of boys.

A. Both statements together are sufficient; neither alone is.
B. Statement I alone is sufficient; Statement II alone is not.
C. Statement II alone is sufficient; Statement I alone is not.
D. Either statement alone is sufficient.
E. Even both statements together are not sufficient.

**Answer: E.** I alone gives 15 boys (40 ÷ 8 × 3) and II alone gives 10 boys (2b + 20 = 40) — each
is sufficient on its own but they disagree, so the set is inconsistent → E.

---

**Q2.** What is the value of x?

- **Statement I:** x + 5 = 12
- **Statement II:** 2x = 14

A. Both statements together are sufficient; neither alone is.
B. Statement I alone is sufficient; Statement II alone is not.
C. Statement II alone is sufficient; Statement I alone is not.
D. Either statement alone is sufficient.
E. Even both statements together are not sufficient.

**Answer: D.** Both statements fix x = 7 and agree, so either one alone is enough → D.

---

**Q3.** What is the value of y?

- **Statement I:** y = 3x
- **Statement II:** x = 4

A. Both statements together are sufficient; neither alone is.
B. Statement I alone is sufficient; Statement II alone is not.
C. Statement II alone is sufficient; Statement I alone is not.
D. Either statement alone is sufficient.
E. Even both statements together are not sufficient.

**Answer: A.** Neither statement fixes y alone; together they give y = 3 × 4 = 12 → A.

---

**Q4.** What is the total number of books on a shelf?

- **Statement I:** 60% of the books are novels.
- **Statement II:** There are 25 non-novel books.

A. Both statements together are sufficient; neither alone is.
B. Statement I alone is sufficient; Statement II alone is not.
C. Statement II alone is sufficient; Statement I alone is not.
D. Either statement alone is sufficient.
E. Even both statements together are not sufficient.

**Answer: C.** I alone gives only a proportion; II alone fixes the total (25 ÷ 0.40 = 62.5) → C.

---

**Q5.** What is the profit percent?

- **Statement I:** The cost price is ₹200 and the selling price is ₹250.
- **Statement II:** The profit is ₹50.

A. Both statements together are sufficient; neither alone is.
B. Statement I alone is sufficient; Statement II alone is not.
C. Statement II alone is sufficient; Statement I alone is not.
D. Either statement alone is sufficient.
E. Even both statements together are not sufficient.

**Answer: B.** I alone gives 50/200 = 25%; II alone gives ₹50 with no base, so it is insufficient → B.

---

**Q6.** How many days will 24 workers take to finish the job?

- **Statement I:** 12 workers complete the job in 6 days.
- **Statement II:** The total job is 144 units of work.

A. Both statements together are sufficient; neither alone is.
B. Statement I alone is sufficient; Statement II alone is not.
C. Statement II alone is sufficient; Statement I alone is not.
D. Either statement alone is sufficient.
E. Even both statements together are not sufficient.

**Answer: B.** I alone gives 12 × 6 = 72 worker-days, so 24 workers take 3 days; II alone has no rate → B.

---

**Q7.** How many students are absent from a class?

- **Statement I:** The number absent is one-fourth of the number present.
- **Statement II:** The class has 80 students in all.

A. Both statements together are sufficient; neither alone is.
B. Statement I alone is sufficient; Statement II alone is not.
C. Statement II alone is sufficient; Statement I alone is not.
D. Either statement alone is sufficient.
E. Even both statements together are not sufficient.

**Answer: A.** I gives a ratio with no total, II gives a total with no ratio; together absent = 16 → A.

---

**Q8.** How old is Meera?

- **Statement I:** Karthik's age is twice Meera's age.
- **Statement II:** Meera's age is 10 years less than Karthik's age.

A. Both statements together are sufficient; neither alone is.
B. Statement I alone is sufficient; Statement II alone is not.
C. Statement II alone is sufficient; Statement I alone is not.
D. Either statement alone is sufficient.
E. Even both statements together are not sufficient.

**Answer: A.** One relation each is not enough (Meera = 10, Karthik = 20 fits I; Meera = 25, Karthik
= 35 fits II); together they give Meera = 10 → A.

---

**Q9.** How many items in the batch are defective?

- **Statement I:** Defective items are 10% of the batch.
- **Statement II:** The batch contains 240 items.

A. Both statements together are sufficient; neither alone is.
B. Statement I alone is sufficient; Statement II alone is not.
C. Statement II alone is sufficient; Statement I alone is not.
D. Either statement alone is sufficient.
E. Even both statements together are not sufficient.

**Answer: A.** I alone is a percentage with no base and II alone is a total with no ratio; together
defective = 24 → A.

---

**Q10.** What is the speed of the train?

- **Statement I:** The train covers 300 km in 5 hours.
- **Statement II:** The train takes 15 hours to cover 900 km.

A. Both statements together are sufficient; neither alone is.
B. Statement I alone is sufficient; Statement II alone is not.
C. Statement II alone is sufficient; Statement I alone is not.
D. Either statement alone is sufficient.
E. Even both statements together are not sufficient.

**Answer: D.** I alone gives 60 km/h and II alone gives 60 km/h — they agree → D.

### A2 — Percentages, profit/loss and discount

**Q11.** What is 25% of 480?

A. 100
B. 120
C. 140
D. 160

**Answer: B.** 25% = 1/4, and 480 ÷ 4 = 120.

---

**Q12.** A student scores 80% of the maximum marks and fails by 20 marks. The passing mark is 40% of
the maximum. What is the maximum mark?

A. 40
B. 45
C. 50
D. 60

**Answer: C.** The gap 80% − 40% = 40% equals 20 marks, so the maximum is 20 ÷ 0.40 = 50.

---

**Q13.** The price of a bike is increased from ₹8,000 to ₹9,200. What is the percentage increase?

A. 12%
B. 15%
C. 17.5%
D. 20%

**Answer: B.** Increase = ₹1,200 on ₹8,000 → 1200/8000 = 15%.

---

**Q14.** A shopkeeper buys an article for ₹480 and sells it for ₹600. What is the profit percent?

A. 20%
B. 25%
C. 30%
D. 20.83%

**Answer: B.** Profit = ₹120 on a cost of ₹480 → 120/480 = 25%.

---

**Q15.** A ₹2,400 television is sold at a 15% discount, and 10% GST is then added. What is the final
price paid?

A. ₹2,204
B. ₹2,244
C. ₹2,340
D. ₹2,160

**Answer: B.** ₹2,400 × 0.85 = ₹2,040, then × 1.10 = ₹2,244.

---

**Q16.** An article is sold for ₹1,200 at a 20% profit. What is the cost price?

A. ₹960
B. ₹1,000
C. ₹1,024
D. ₹1,100

**Answer: B.** Cost = 1200 ÷ 1.20 = ₹1,000.

### A3 — Ratio, speed, work, series and inequalities

**Q17.** In a class the ratio of boys to girls is 5 : 3 and there are 320 students. How many are boys?

A. 120
B. 160
C. 200
D. 240

**Answer: C.** 8 parts of 40 → boys = 5 × 40 = 200.

---

**Q18.** The ratio of milk to water is 5 : 3. After 30 litres of water is added, the ratio becomes 1 : 1.
How much milk is there?

A. 45 litres
B. 60 litres
C. 75 litres
D. 90 litres

**Answer: C.** Milk = water + 30 and water = 3/5 milk → (2/5)milk = 30 → milk = 75.

---

**Q19.** A car covers 240 km in 3 hours. What is its speed?

A. 60 km/h
B. 72 km/h
C. 80 km/h
D. 90 km/h

**Answer: C.** 240 ÷ 3 = 80 km/h.

---

**Q20.** A car moves at 36 km/h. How far does it travel in 2 minutes?

A. 600 m
B. 720 m
C. 1,200 m
D. 2,000 m

**Answer: C.** 36 km/h = 10 m/s, and 2 minutes = 120 s → 1,200 m.

---

**Q21.** A can finish a job in 10 days and B in 15 days. Working together, how long do they take?

A. 5 days
B. 6 days
C. 7.5 days
D. 12.5 days

**Answer: B.** (10 × 15)/(10 + 15) = 150/25 = 6 days.

---

**Q22.** A boat's speed in still water is 9 km/h and the stream flows at 3 km/h. How long does the
boat take to cover 48 km upstream?

A. 4 hours
B. 6 hours
C. 8 hours
D. 12 hours

**Answer: C.** Upstream speed = 9 − 3 = 6 km/h → 48 ÷ 6 = 8 hours.

---

**Q23.** Find the next number: 2, 6, 12, 20, ?

A. 26
B. 28
C. 30
D. 32

**Answer: C.** The differences are 4, 6, 8, 10 → the next difference is 12 → 20 + 12 = 30.

---

**Q24.** If x > 5 and x < 9, which of the following is definitely true?

A. x > 7
B. x < 9
C. x = 7
D. x > 8

**Answer: B.** Only "x < 9" must hold; x could be 6, 7 or 8, so A, C and D are not definite.

## Quick rules

- DS: I alone → II alone → together → compare. Mark and move.
- Percentage of a number: multiply. Percent change: divide by the **base**.
- Successive percentages multiply, they do not add.
- Ratio: divide by the GCD; parts of a total.
- Speed: total distance ÷ total time; convert units once.
- Time and work: add rates, `T = ab/(a+b)`.
- Series: difference row first.
- Inequalities: look for the statement that must be true in **every** case.

## Common traps

| Trap | Where it bites | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Averaging the two speeds | Q2-type speed questions | `2xy/(x+y)` |
| Adding successive percentages | Q32-type questions | Multiply by (1 + p) each time |
| Percent of the selling price | Q13, Q16-type questions | Percent change uses the original base |
| Pole vs platform | Q44-type train questions | Length + platform |
| Adding times instead of rates | Q21-type questions | Add rates |
| DS: stopping after the together-test | Q3, Q7, Q8, Q9 | Both alone can still be sufficient |
