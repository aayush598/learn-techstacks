# Solved Mixture and Alligation Set

> Section 1 · Aptitude · Mixtures & Alligation · _Estimated reading time: 12 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Alligation shortcuts](../02-techniques/01-alligation-shortcuts.md)

## In one sentence

Ten questions covering every mixture shape the pre-test uses: ratios by alligation, add/remove by
constant solute, replacement in two steps, and weighted averages.

## Why the pre-test tests this

- One or two mixture questions sit in the 10-question aptitude section.
- Each question below is one of the five shapes the exam actually asks. Recognise the shape, apply
  the matching method.

## Core concept

| # | Question | Shape | Method | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 L milk + water for 2 : 3 | Parts | Parts method | 12 L |
| 2 | 36 L acid:water 1:2, add water for 1:3 | Add solvent | Constant solute | 12 L |

## Worked example

### Q1. How many litres of water must be mixed with 8 litres of milk so that the mixture contains milk
and water in the ratio 2 : 3?

A. 8 litres
B. 10 litres
C. 12 litres
D. 16 litres

**Solution**

| Step | Working |
|---|---|
| Milk = 2 parts = 8 litres | 1 part = `8 ÷ 2` = 4 litres |
| Water = 3 parts | `3 × 4` = 12 litres |

**Answer: C (12 litres).**

**Why the distractors fail**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 8 litres | Made the ratio 1 : 1 (8 : 8) |
| 10 litres | Split the difference between 8 and 12 |
| 16 litres | Doubled the milk instead of using 3 parts |

**Alligation check.** Milk = 100%, water = 0%, target milk = `2/5` = 40%.
`Q(water) : Q(milk) = (100 − 40) : (40 − 0) = 60 : 40 = 3 : 2` → milk : water = 2 : 3 ✅

---

### Q2. A vessel contains 36 litres of a mixture of acid and water in the ratio 1 : 2. How much water
must be added so that the ratio becomes 1 : 3?

A. 6 litres
B. 12 litres
C. 18 litres
D. 24 litres

**Solution — the acid does not change**

| Step | Working |
|---|---|
| Acid in 36 litres at 1 : 2 | `36 × 1/3` = 12 litres |
| Water in 36 litres | `36 × 2/3` = 24 litres |
| New ratio 1 : 3 with 12 litres of acid | 1 part = 12 → water = `3 × 12` = 36 litres |
| Water to add | `36 − 24` = **12 litres** |

**Answer: B (12 litres).**

**Why the distractors fail**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 6 litres | Half of the answer — the added amount vs the new total confused |
| 18 litres | Added to 36 L water instead of the 24 L actually present |
| 24 litres | The amount of water already in the vessel |

---

### Q3. A vessel contains 60 litres of a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 3 : 1. 15 litres of the
mixture is replaced by 15 litres of milk. What is the ratio of milk and water in the vessel now?

A. 3 : 1
B. 13 : 3
C. 4 : 1
D. 5 : 1

**Solution — two steps**

| Step | Milk | Water |
|---|---|---|
| Start (60 L at 3 : 1) | 45 | 15 |
| Remove 15 L of mixture (`×3/4`, `×1/4`) | `−11.25` | `−3.75` |
| Left | 33.75 | 11.25 |
| Add 15 L of milk | `+15` | — |
| **New** | **48.75** | **11.25** |

Ratio: `48.75 : 11.25` → divide by 3.75 → **13 : 3**

**Answer: B (13 : 3).**

**Why the distractors fail**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 3 : 1 | You assumed the ratio stayed the same |
| 4 : 1 | You added 15 litres of milk without removing the 11.25 litres of milk that went out with the mixture (45 + 15 = 60, water 15 − 15 = 0 → 60:0, or with water untouched 60:15 = 4:1) — the classic one-step error |
| 5 : 1 | Removed too much water |

**Check:** 48.75 + 11.25 = 60 litres ✅ (volume restored).

---

### Q4. A container holds 50 litres of a mixture of acid and water containing 20% acid. 10 litres of
water is removed. What is the concentration of acid in the remaining mixture?

A. 12.5%
B. 20%
C. 22%
D. 25%

**Solution — the acid is unchanged**

| Step | Working |
|---|---|
| Acid in 50 litres at 20% | `50 × 0.2` = 10 litres |
| Remove 10 litres of **pure water** | Acid still 10 litres, total = `50 − 10` = 40 litres |
| New concentration | `10/40 × 100` = **25%** |

**Answer: D (25%).**

**Why the distractors fail**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 12.5% | You halved the concentration — you removed 10 L of acid instead of water |
| 20% | You believed the concentration never changes |
| 22% | You reduced the volume but not the percentage correctly |

---

### Q5. 50 litres of a mixture contains 10% milk. How much water must be added so that the mixture
contains 5% milk?

A. 25 litres
B. 50 litres
C. 75 litres
D. 100 litres

**Solution**

| Step | Working |
|---|---|
| Milk in 50 litres at 10% | `50 × 0.1` = 5 litres |
| For 5% milk, total must be | `5 ÷ 0.05` = 100 litres |
| Water to add | `100 − 50` = **50 litres** |

**Answer: B (50 litres).**

**Why the distractors fail**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 25 litres | Halved the concentration by halving the total, not doubling it |
| 75 litres | Added 50 to the milk amount instead of to the total |
| 100 litres | The new total volume, not the amount added |

**Second method.** Total goes from 50 to 100 to halve 10% to 5% → add 50. Same answer.

### Q6. In what ratio must a 20% salt solution and a 50% salt solution be mixed to obtain a 30%
solution?

A. 1 : 2
B. 2 : 1
C. 3 : 1
D. 1 : 3

**Solution — alligation**

```
        20%          30%          50%
          \___________/  \___________/
            10                    20
```

`Q(20%) : Q(50%) = (50 − 30) : (30 − 20) = 20 : 10 = 2 : 1`

**Answer: B (2 : 1).**

**Why the distractors fail**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 1 : 2 | The ratio reversed — the weaker solution takes the larger difference |
| 3 : 1 | Used the difference of the two ends (50 − 20 = 30) against the mean |
| 1 : 3 | The ratio reversed with a wrong difference |

**Check.** 2 parts of 20% + 1 part of 50% → `(40 + 50)/3 = 30%` ✅

---

### Q7. A shopkeeper mixes 30 kg of tea worth ₹120 per kg with 20 kg of tea worth ₹90 per kg. What is
the price per kg of the mixture?

A. ₹105
B. ₹108
C. ₹110
D. ₹112

**Solution — alligation medial**

```
(30 × 120 + 20 × 90) / (30 + 20) = (3600 + 1800) / 50 = 5400 / 50 = ₹108
```

**Answer: B (₹108).**

**Why the distractors fail**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| ₹105 | Simple average of ₹120 and ₹90 — ignores the unequal quantities |
| ₹110 | Simple average of ₹120 and ₹90 rounded up, or a wrong weighting |
| ₹112 | Weighted toward the dearer tea too strongly |

**Speed check.** The mix must sit closer to ₹120 than to ₹90 because more kg of the dearer tea is
used. ₹108 ✅ (₹105 would be the midpoint).

### Q8. Two mixtures of milk and water contain milk and water in the ratios 1 : 4 and 2 : 3
respectively. In what ratio should they be mixed so that the resulting mixture contains milk and
water in the ratio 1 : 2?

A. 1 : 1
B. 1 : 2
C. 2 : 1
D. 3 : 2

**Solution — convert to fractions, then alligate**

| Mixture | Milk : water | Milk fraction |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1 : 4 | `1/5` = 20% |
| B | 2 : 3 | `2/5` = 40% |
| Target | 1 : 2 | `1/3` = 33⅓% |

```
A : B = (2/5 − 1/3) : (1/3 − 1/5)
    = (6/15 − 5/15) : (5/15 − 3/15)
    = (1/15) : (2/15) = 1 : 2
```

**Answer: B (1 : 2).**

**Why the distractors fail**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 1 : 1 | `(0.2 + 0.4)/2 = 30%` milk, but the target is 33⅓% |
| 2 : 1 | The ratio reversed |
| 3 : 2 | A guess from the ratios rather than the percentages |

**Check.** 1 unit of A (20% milk) + 2 units of B (40% milk) → milk `0.2 + 0.8 = 1.0`, total 3 →
`1/3` = 1 : 2 ✅

---

### Q9. A vessel contains 60 litres of a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 3 : 2. 12 litres of the
mixture is drawn out and the vessel is filled with milk. What is the ratio of milk and water in the
vessel now?

A. 3 : 2
B. 17 : 8
C. 4 : 1
D. 5 : 2

**Solution — two steps**

| Step | Milk | Water |
|---|---|---|
| Start (60 L at 3 : 2) | 36 | 24 |
| Remove 12 L of mixture (`×3/5`, `×2/5`) | `−7.2` | `−4.8` |
| Left | 28.8 | 19.2 |
| Add 12 L of milk | `+12` | — |
| **New** | **40.8** | **19.2** |

Ratio: `40.8 : 19.2` → `×2.4` → `102 : 48` → `÷6` → **17 : 8**

**Answer: B (17 : 8).**

**Why the distractors fail**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 3 : 2 | The original ratio, unchanged |
| 4 : 1 | The one-step error: added 12 L of milk without removing the 7.2 L of milk that left with the drawn-out mixture (36 + 12 = 48 and 24 − 12 = 12 → 4 : 1) |
| 5 : 2 | Arithmetic slip |

**Check:** 40.8 + 19.2 = 60 litres ✅

---

### Q10. Three solutions of salt contain 10%, 20% and 40% salt. They are mixed in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3.
What is the concentration of the resulting mixture?

A. 25%
B. 28.33%
C. 30%
D. 27.5%

**Solution — weighted average**

```
(10 × 1 + 20 × 2 + 40 × 3) / (1 + 2 + 3)
= (10 + 40 + 120) / 6
= 170 / 6
= 28.33%
```

**Answer: B (28.33%).**

**Why the distractors fail**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 25% | Simple average of 10, 20, 40 — ignores the ratios |
| 30% | Simple average of 20 and 40 |
| 27.5% | Simple average of 10, 20, 40 plus rounding in the wrong direction |

**Check.** The result must lie between 10% and 40%, and closer to 30% because the 40% solution has the
largest share. 28.33% ✅

## Quick rules

- Identify the shape: ratio → alligation; add/remove → constant solute; replacement → two steps;
  given quantities → weighted average.
- Alligation: `Q_cheap : Q_dear = (C_dear − C_mean) : (C_mean − C_cheap)`.
- Convert ratios to percentages before alligating.
- Solute stays constant when pure solvent is added or removed.
- Removing part of the mixture keeps the concentration constant.
- Replacement: remove (r × each part), then add. Verify the volume is restored.
- Check the answer lies between the inputs.
- 70 seconds per question.

## Common traps

| Trap | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Alligation reversed | Mirror ratio | Cheaper first |
| Alligation on ratios | Nonsense | Convert to % |
| Solute changed when water added | Wrong % | Solute constant |
| One-step replacement | 4 : 1 instead of 17 : 8 | Remove, then add |
| Volume not restored | Wrong total | Refill to original |
| Simple average used with unequal quantities | ₹105 | Weighted average |

- [ ] I converted ratios to percentages before alligating.
- [ ] I held the solute constant where required.
- [ ] I did replacement in two steps and verified the volume.
