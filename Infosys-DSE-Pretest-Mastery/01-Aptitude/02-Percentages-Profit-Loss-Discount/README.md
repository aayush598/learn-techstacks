# Percentages, Profit/Loss & Discount — Topic Guide

> Section 1 · Aptitude · Percentages, Profit/Loss & Discount · _Estimated reading time: 30 min for the full topic_

## What the pre-test asks in this topic

Roughly **2–3 of the 10 aptitude questions** are percentage questions. In the DSE pre-test they show
up in four shapes:

1. **Percentage change** — "the price fell from ₹48,000 to ₹42,000, what is the % decrease?"
2. **Profit and loss** — "bought at ₹1,360 at a profit of ₹160, what is the profit percent?"
3. **Discount and marked price** — "marked at ₹3,000, discounts of 20% and 10% applied in sequence."
4. **Interest** — "compound vs simple for 2 years" and "difference between CI and SI."

All four reduce to `part = base × rate`. The exam tests whether you picked the right base and whether
you multiplied the changes.

## Learning objectives

After this topic you should be able to:

- Convert any common fraction (1/2 … 1/20, eighths, sixteenths) to a percentage instantly, and back.
- Apply the percentage change formula with the **old** value as the base, every time.
- Compute net effect of two successive changes with `a + b + ab/100` or with multipliers, and apply
  the `x²/100` symmetric-loss rule.
- Compute `x% of y% of N` in one line as `N × x × y / 10000`.
- Convert between cost price, marked price, selling price, profit percent and discount percent in
  both directions.
- Compute two successive discounts as `d1 + d2 − d1·d2/100` instead of adding them.
- State the difference between compound and simple interest for 2 years as `P(r/100)²`.
- Distinguish a **percentage point** change from a **percentage change**.

## Quick 3-step solving procedure

1. **Name the base and the rate.** Write the base in words: "old price ₹48,000", "cost price ₹250".
   Most percentage mistakes are base mistakes.
2. **Convert the rate into a multiplier** (or a fraction) and apply it once — `× 1.2`, `× 0.8`,
   `× 0.72` for two discounts.
3. **Check the direction.** Did the value go up or down? If the answer's sign contradicts the story,
   the base is wrong. Then mark and move.

## Key takeaways

- ✅ **One formula:** `part = base × rate`. Identify the base in the first 5 seconds.
- ✅ **Successive changes multiply.** `+20%` then `−20%` is a **4% loss**, not zero.
- ✅ **Symmetric rule:** rise of x% then fall of x% always loses `x²/100 %`.
- ✅ **Profit % is on cost price.** Solving backwards: `CP = SP / (1 + p/100)`.
- ✅ **Discounts chain by multiplication** — `20% + 10%` is 28% effective.
- ✅ **`x% of y% of N = N × x × y / 10000`.**
- ✅ **Two-year CI − SI = `P(r/100)²`** — one line, no formula recall needed.
- ⚠️ **Meera's warning:** she reads "profit of ₹160 on ₹1,360" and divides by 1,360. Always convert to
  cost price first.
