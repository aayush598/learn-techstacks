# Percentages, Profit/Loss and Discount — 10 Solved Questions

> Section 1 · Aptitude · Percentages, Profit/Loss & Discount · _Estimated reading time: 14 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Percentage speed tricks](../02-techniques/01-percentage-speed-tricks.md)

## In one sentence

Ten percentage questions, ten different ways to lose a mark — every one of them solved with the base
identified **before** any arithmetic.

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Percentage, profit/loss and discount questions together make up roughly 2–3 of the 10 aptitude
  questions. At 90 seconds each, that is 4–5 minutes of the 15-minute section.
- Each question below is built around one specific error: wrong base, added percentages, profit on
  selling price, added discounts, reverse percentage, and so on.
- If you can solve all 10 in 12 minutes, the percentage archetype is closed for you.

## Core concept

Before you touch any question, answer three questions in your head:

1. **What is the base?** The old value, the cost price, the marked price, the total — whichever the
   question is anchored to.
2. **Is there one change or two?** Two changes multiply. One change is a single multiplier.
3. **What exactly is the question asking for?** A number, or a percentage of a particular base?

Get all three right and the arithmetic is usually one multiplication.

## Worked example

### Q1 — Answer **B** (percentage decrease; base = old price)

**The price of a laptop fell from ₹48,000 to ₹42,000. What is the percentage decrease?**

A. 10%
B. 12.5%
C. 14.29%
D. 12%

**Solution**

- Decrease = 48,000 − 42,000 = ₹6,000.
- Base = **old price** = ₹48,000.
- % decrease = 6,000 / 48,000 × 100 = 12.5%.

**Answer: B (12.5%).**

**Distractors**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 10% | A rounded guess from 48 → 42 "looks like 12%" |
| 14.29% | Dividing by the **new** price: 6,000/42,000. Percentage change always uses the old value |
| 12% | The raw change (₹6,000) misread as a percent, or 6/50 done sloppily |

---

### Q2 — Answer **C** (markup then discount; multiply, do not subtract)

**A shopkeeper marks an item 25% above the cost price and then offers a 10% discount. What is his
profit percent?**

A. 15%
B. 20%
C. 12.5%
D. 10%

**Solution**

```
SP = CP × 1.25 × 0.90 = CP × 1.125
```

SP is 112.5% of CP → **12.5% profit**.

**Answer: C (12.5%).**

**Distractors**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 15% | The naive "25 − 10" answer. The two percentages have different bases |
| 20% | Someone applied the 10% discount to the cost price instead of the marked price |
| 10% | Read the discount as the profit, ignoring the markup |

---

### Q3 — Answer **B** (two successive discounts)

**A jacket is marked at ₹3,000. During a sale, discounts of 20% and 10% are applied one after the
other. What is the selling price?**

A. ₹2,100
B. ₹2,160
C. ₹2,400
D. ₹2,250

**Solution**

```
SP = 3000 × 0.80 × 0.90 = 3000 × 0.72 = ₹2,160
```

**Answer: B (₹2,160).**

**Distractors**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| ₹2,100 | The "30% off" answer (3,000 − 30%). Successive discounts multiply, not add |
| ₹2,400 | Applied only the 20% discount and stopped |
| ₹2,250 | Applied only the 10% discount |

---

### Q4 — Answer **C** (profit percent is on cost price)

**An article is sold for ₹1,360 at a profit of ₹160. What is the profit percent?**

A. 11.76%
B. 10%
C. 13.33%
D. 12%

**Solution**

- CP = SP − profit = 1,360 − 160 = ₹1,200.
- Profit % = 160 / 1,200 × 100 = 13.33%.

**Answer: C (13.33%).**

**Distractors**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 11.76% | 160/1,360 — profit percentage computed on the **selling price** |
| 10% | A round-number guess |
| 12% | 160/1,333 — from assuming CP = 1,333 |

---

### Q5 — Answer **A** (three-step: buy, mark, discount)

**A shopkeeper buys an article for ₹250 and marks it at a 40% profit on cost. During the sale he
gives a further 10% discount. What is his profit percent?**

A. 26%
B. 30%
C. 36%
D. 34%

**Solution**

```
MP = 250 × 1.4 = ₹350
SP = 350 × 0.9 = ₹315
Profit = 315 − 250 = ₹65
Profit % = 65 / 250 × 100 = 26%
```

**Answer: A (26%).**

**Distractors**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 30% | 40 − 10. Different bases again |
| 36% | Applied the 10% discount to the cost price: 250 × 1.4 × 0.9 = 315 → then divided 315/250 wrongly? Actually 315/250 = 126%, so 36% is a misread of that |
| 34% | Forgot the discount entirely and then halved something |

---

### Q6 — Answer **A** (CI − SI shortcut for 2 years)

**Meera deposits ₹5,000 at 8% per annum compound interest for 2 years. By how much does the compound
interest exceed the simple interest?**

A. ₹32
B. ₹64
C. ₹400
D. ₹832

**Solution**

```
CI − SI (2 years) = P × (r/100)² = 5000 × 0.0064 = ₹32
```

Check: SI amount = 5,000 × 1.16 = 5,800. CI amount = 5,000 × 1.08² = 5,000 × 1.1664 = 5,832.
Difference = 5,832 − 5,800 = ₹32. ✅

**Answer: A (₹32).**

**Distractors**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| ₹64 | Doubled the shortcut by mistake |
| ₹400 | 5,000 × 8% = one year's simple interest |
| ₹832 | The total compound **interest** (5,832 − 5,000), not the difference |

---

### Q7 — Answer **B** (percentage of a percentage)

**What is 25% of 40% of 8,000?**

A. 800
B. 320
C. 2,000
D. 1,000

**Solution**

```
8000 × 0.25 = 2000   →   2000 × 0.40 = 800
```

Or `8,000 × 25 × 40 / 10,000 = 800`.

**Answer: B (800).**

**Distractors**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 320 | Did 8,000 × 0.25 × 0.40 = 8,000 × 0.04 = 320 — dropped a factor of 10 (used 0.025) |
| 2,000 | Stopped after the first percentage (25% of 8,000) |
| 1,000 | Used 25% of 40% of 8,000 with the wrong order: 40% of 25% = 2000, then halved |

---

### Q8 — Answer **A** (reverse percentage)

**Ananya's monthly stipend is 25% more than Rahul's. Rahul's stipend is what percent less than
Ananya's?**

A. 20%
B. 25%
C. 12.5%
D. 16.67%

**Solution**

Let Rahul's stipend = 100. Ananya's = 125. The difference is 25, and Rahul is short by
`25/125` of Ananya's amount = **20%**.

**Answer: A (20%).**

**Distractors**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 25% | Assumes the increase and the decrease are symmetric. They are not |
| 12.5% | Half of 25% — a mental shortcut gone wrong |
| 16.67% | 25/150 — divided by the sum instead of the larger amount |

---

### Q9 — Answer **C** (percentage change inside a ratio)

**In a school of 900 students, the ratio of boys to girls changes from 5 : 4 to 4 : 5. What is the
percentage change in the number of girls?**

A. 25% decrease
B. No change
C. 25% increase
D. 20% increase

**Solution**

- Before: 5 + 4 = 9 parts = 900 → 1 part = 100 → girls = 4 × 100 = **400**.
- After: 4 + 5 = 9 parts = 900 → 1 part = 100 → girls = 5 × 100 = **500**.
- Change = 100 / 400 × 100 = **25% increase**.

**Answer: C (25% increase).**

**Distractors**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| 25% decrease | Direction reversed — the girls' count went up |
| No change | The total stayed 900 and the ratio flipped, so the counts did change |
| 20% increase | 100/500 — dividing by the **new** count |

---

### Q10 — Answer **D** (discount then profit, solved backwards)

**A jacket is marked at ₹2,500 and sold during a sale at a 20% discount. The shopkeeper still makes a
profit of 25% on the sale. What was the cost price?**

A. ₹2,500
B. ₹1,750
C. ₹2,000
D. ₹1,600

**Solution**

```
SP = 2500 × 0.80 = ₹2,000
SP = 1.25 × CP   →   CP = 2000 / 1.25 = ₹1,600
```

**Answer: D (₹1,600).**

**Distractors**

| Option | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| ₹2,500 | Assumed the marked price equals the cost price |
| ₹1,750 | 2,000 − 250, subtracting the discount amount from SP |
| ₹2,000 | Reported the selling price instead of the cost price |

## Quick rules

- Identify the base first: old value, CP, MP, or total.
- Two changes multiply; `a + b + ab/100` is the arithmetic form.
- Profit and loss percentages use **cost price**.
- Successive discounts multiply: `20% + 10%` = 28% effective.
- `x% of y% of N = N × x × y / 10,000`.
- Two-year CI − SI = `P(r/100)²`; two-year CI amount = `P(1 + r/100)²`.
- Reverse percentage: `x% more` ⇒ `x/(100+x)` % less.
- In ratio-based questions, recompute both parts before computing the change.

## Common traps

| Trap | Seen in | Silently gives |
|---|---|---|
| Dividing by the new value for % change | Q1 | 14.29% |
| Adding a markup and a discount | Q2, Q5 | 15%, 30% |
| Adding two discounts | Q3 | ₹2,100 |
| Profit % on selling price | Q4 | 11.76% |
| Stopping after the first of two percentages | Q7 | 2,000 |
| Assuming rise and fall are symmetric | Q8 | 25% |
| Dividing the change by the new count | Q9 | 20% |
| Reporting SP when CP is asked | Q10 | ₹2,000 |
