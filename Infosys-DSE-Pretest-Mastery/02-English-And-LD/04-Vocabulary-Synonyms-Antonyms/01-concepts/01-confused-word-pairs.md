# Confused Word Pairs — The 26 That Get Tested

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Vocabulary, Synonyms & Antonyms ·  _Estimated reading time: 9 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Vocabulary topic README](../README.md)

## In one sentence
**Two words that sound similar but differ in meaning are the single most reliable source of vocabulary marks in Indian pre-tests — learn 26 pairs and you will own every item of this type that appears.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- "Choose the word that is most similar in meaning / most nearly similar / opposite in meaning to X" items almost
  always use a **confused pair** as one of the options.
- The pairs below are drawn from the standard pools these papers draw from. The count is finite.
- Each pair has a **distinguishing test** — a one-line rule that settles it without re-learning the definition.

## Core concept

### The 26 pairs

| # | Pair | The distinction | Distinguishing test |
|---|------|----------------|--------------------|
| 1 | **adapt / adopt / adept** | adapt = change to fit; adopt = take on (a policy, a child); adept = skilled | "She **adapted** to the new tool." / "The team **adopted** Agile." / "He is **adept** at SQL." |
| 2 | **affect / effect** | affect = verb (to influence); effect = noun (a result) | Swap test: if a noun slot fits, it is "effect" |
| 3 | **principle / principal** | principle = a rule or belief; principal = chief, or head of a school | "a guiding **principle**" / "the **principal** said" |
| 4 | **for / four** | for = preposition; four = the number | Read it aloud |
| 5 | **capital / capitol** | capital = city seat of government, or money; capitol = the building where a legislature meets | "the **capital** city" / "the US **Capitol**" |
| 6 | **ensure / insure / assure** | ensure = make sure it happens; insure = cover financially; assure = reassure a person | "**Ensure** the build passes." / "**Insure** the cargo." / "**Assure** the client." |
| 7 | **than / then** | than = comparison; then = time or sequence | "better **than**" / "and **then**" |
| 8 | **stationary / stationery** | stationary = not moving; stationery = writing materials | "The bus is **stationary**." / "Order **stationery**." |
| 9 | **site / sight** | site = a location; sight = vision, or something seen | "the construction **site**" / "a pleasant **sight**" |
| 10 | **eminent / imminent** | eminent = famous and respected; imminent = about to happen | "an **eminent** engineer" / "an **imminent** deadline" |
| 11 | **complement / compliment** | complement = completes; compliment = praise | "It **complements** the dress." / "She **complimented** me." |
| 12 | **anecdote / antonym** | anecdote = a short personal story; antonym = a word of opposite meaning | Anecdotes are stories |
| 13 | **chronic / chronological** | chronic = long-lasting; chronological = in time order | "**chronic** pain" / "**chronological** order" |
| 14 | **counsel / council** | counsel = advice (also a lawyer); council = a body that advises | "Give me **counsel**." / "The **council** met." |
| 15 | **discreet / discrete** | discreet = tactful; discrete = separate, distinct | "**discreet** in public" / "**discrete** parts" |
| 16 | **exaggerate / overstate** | exaggerate = make bigger than it is; overstate = say more than the facts support | Both mean "inflate", but exaggerate is for size/importance and overstate for facts |
| 17 | **fictitious / factual** | fictitious = invented; factual = true | Fiction is invented |
| 18 | **flaunt / flout** | flaunt = display proudly; flout = openly disregard a rule | "flaunt a designer bag" / "flout the dress code" |
| 19 | **notable / notation** | notable = worth remarking; notation = symbols or a system of symbols | "a **notable** achievement" / "musical **notation**" |
| 20 | **precede / proceed** | precede = come before; proceed = carry on | "A **precedes** B." / "Let us **proceed**." |
| 21 | **reluctant / reticent** | reluctant = unwilling; reticent = reserved about speaking | "**reluctant** to agree" / "**reticent** about the news" |
| 22 | **sensual / sensuous** | sensual = of the body/pleasure; sensuous = appealing to the senses | Both relate to the senses; sensual is bodily |
| 23 | **sceptical / cynical** | sceptical = doubting the truth; cynical = distrustful of motives | "**sceptical** of the claim" / "**cynical** about the industry" |
| 24 | **substantial / substantive** | substantial = large in amount; substantive = dealing with the substance of the matter | "a **substantial** amount" / "a **substantive** discussion" |
| 25 | **susceptible / vulnerable** | susceptible = prone to (a disease, an influence); vulnerable = open to attack | "**susceptible** to infection" / "**vulnerable** to attack" |
| 26 | **verdant / verdigris** | verdant = green with healthy growth; verdigris = the green-blue patina on copper or brass | "**verdant** hills" / "**verdigris** on the statue" |

### The 6 that get tested most

Based on frequency in reported papers, these six appear far more often than the rest:

| Rank | Pair | The trap in the options |
|------|------|------------------------|
| 1 | affect / effect | The noun/verb swap |
| 2 | adapt / adopt / adept | Three similar words, one option each |
| 3 | ensure / insure / assure | Three similar words, one option each |
| 4 | principle / principal | Used as a noun and a verb in the same item |
| 5 | since / for | Covered in error correction too |
| 6 | stationery / stationary | The classic "which word fits the sentence" item |

### How the pairs appear in questions

**Type A — "Choose the word most similar in meaning to X."**

> **Q.** Choose the word most similar in meaning to **ADEPT**.
> A. Adapt · B. Adopt · C. Skillful · D. Adjust

**Answer: C.** "Adept" means *skilled*. Note the trap: A and B are both *adapt* and *adopt*, which look like "adept"
because of the spelling. This is the single most common item shape for this pair.

**Type B — "Fill in the blank with the most appropriate word."**

> **Q.** The manager asked me to **assure** the client that the issue would be resolved.
> A. insure · B. ensure · C. assure · D. reassure

**Answer: C.** "Assure" a **person**. "Ensure" a **thing**. "Insure" covers **financial loss**.

**Type C — "Choose the word that is most nearly opposite in meaning to X."**

> **Q.** Choose the word most nearly opposite in meaning to **SCEPTICAL**.
> A. Credulous · B. Cynical · C. Doubtful · D. Uncertain

**Answer: A.** "Credulous" means *too ready to believe* — the true opposite. B "cynical" is a *near*-opposite (distrust
of motives, not doubting of facts), and C "doubtful" is a **synonym**, and D is a **milder** version of the same
idea. This item contains all three trap types at once.

## Worked example

> **Q.** Choose the word most nearly similar in meaning to **FRUGAL**.
>
> A. Thrifty
> B. Miserly
> C. Wealthy
> D. Lavish

**Answer: A — Thrifty.**

| Option | Test | Verdict |
|--------|------|---------|
| A | Thrifty = careful with money, avoiding waste. Exactly the meaning of "frugal". | ✅ Correct |
| B | "Miserly" = **hoarding** money and refusing to spend or share. It is a *negative*, close to the opposite of the positive "frugal", which implies sensible saving. | ❌ |
| C | "Wealthy" = having a lot of money. It describes **having**, not **saving**. | ❌ |
| D | "Lavish" = generous, spending freely. The **opposite** of frugal. | ❌ |

**The trap:** B. In Indian exam vocabulary, "frugal" is often paired with "miserly" as a near-synonym — the exam
wants you to notice that "miserly" carries disapproval while "frugal" does not. The distinguishing test:
**can the word be used approvingly?** "a frugal lifestyle" ✅ · "a miserly nature" ❌ (always negative).

**General rule for synonym items:** when two options are near-identical, ask which one is **neutral/positive** and
which carries a **negative judgement**. The neutral one matches a neutral word in the stem.

## Quick rules

- `adapt` (fit), `adopt` (take on), `adept` (skilled) — learn all three together.
- `affect` = verb, `effect` = noun. `its` = possessive, `it's` = it is.
- `ensure` = thing, `assure` = person, `insure` = money.
- `principle` = rule, `principal` = chief.
- `stationary` = still, `stationery` = paper.
- `eminent` = famous, `imminent` = coming soon.
- `precede` = go before, `proceed` = carry on.
- `flaunt` = display, `flout` = break a rule.
- `reluctant` = unwilling, `reticent` = reserved.
- `susceptible` = prone to, `vulnerable` = open to attack.
- When two options are near-synonyms, check for a **negative judgement** in one of them.
- The sentence's context decides. Never answer from the dictionary definition alone.

## Common traps

- **Answering from the abstract definition** instead of the sentence's context.
- **Treating a near-opposite as a synonym** ("miserly" for "frugal", "cynical" for "sceptical").
- **Confusing the spelling-similar words** — the question *wants* you to hesitate between adapt/adopt/adept.
- **Ignoring the register** — a word that is correct in a textbook but too informal for the sentence.
- **Forgetting the polysemous sense**: "effective" can mean "producing the intended result" or "in effect". Read
  the sentence.
