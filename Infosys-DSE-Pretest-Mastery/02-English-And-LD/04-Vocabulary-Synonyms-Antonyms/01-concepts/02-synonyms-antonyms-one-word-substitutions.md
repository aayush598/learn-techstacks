# Synonyms, Antonyms & One-Word Substitutions

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Vocabulary, Synonyms & Antonyms ·  _Estimated reading time: 8 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Confused word pairs](01-confused-word-pairs.md)

## In one sentence
**The vocabulary marks come from four item shapes — synonym, antonym, one-word substitution, and idiom — and each has a fixed set of high-frequency words you can learn in one sitting.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Beyond the confused pairs, the papers use a **stable core vocabulary list**: words like *endeavour, prudent,
  diligent, magnanimous, obstinate* and their antonyms.
- One-word substitution items ask for the word that fits the meaning *in the sentence*, so the sentence carries
  most of the work.
- Idioms appear occasionally and are always answered by the **literal** meaning, not the figurative one.

## Core concept

### The four item shapes

| Shape | Stem wording | What it asks | Where the points are |
|-------|--------------|--------------|---------------------|
| **Synonym** | "most nearly similar in meaning to X" / "the closest meaning" | Which word means the same? | Word class, sense, register |
| **Antonym** | "most nearly opposite in meaning to X" | Which word means the reverse? | Beware milder opposites and "opposite of the opposite" |
| **One-word substitution** | "Choose the word that best replaces the underlined phrase" | Which single word carries the same meaning here? | Context, not definition |
| **Idiom** | "What does the expression X mean?" | The everyday meaning | Ignore the figurative reading |

### One-word substitutions — the pattern

The test gives a **multi-word phrase** and asks for one word. Work in this order:

1. **Pin the meaning in the sentence.** Read the full sentence, not the phrase alone.
2. **Identify the part of speech** required.
3. **Generate 3–4 candidate words** before looking at the options.
4. **Match**, then check the register.

| Phrase | One-word candidates |
|--------|---------------------|
| "in a very good manner" | well, excellently, admirably |
| "with great care" | carefully, cautiously, meticulously |
| "in an uncertain way" | vaguely, doubtfully, ambiguously |
| "because of a sudden impulse" | impulsively, spontaneously |
| "showing no mercy" | mercilessly, cruelly, ruthlessly |
| "in a way that cannot be corrected" | irrevocably, permanently |
| "a place of complete safety" | haven, sanctuary, refuge |
| "the state of being free from doubt" | certainty, assurance, conviction |
| "to speak in a pompous way" | bombastically, grandiloquently |
| "to make something less severe" | mitigate, alleviate, lessen |
| "to make a quick decision" | decide hastily, snap |
| "a strong feeling of dislike" | aversion, antipathy, hostility |
| "a person who is difficult to please" | fastidious, exacting, demanding |
| "to put off to a later time" | postpone, defer, procrastinate |

## Worked example

> **Q.** Choose the word that best replaces the underlined phrase:
> "The manager handled the *situation with great calm* throughout the difficult client call."
>
> A. composedly
> B. silently
> C. cautiously
> D. slowly

**Answer: A — composedly.**

| Step | Finding |
|------|---------|
| Pin the meaning | "with great calm" = in a composed, self-controlled way, *especially under pressure*. |
| Part of speech | The blank needs an **adverb** describing how the action was performed. |
| Candidates generated | calmly, composedly, serenely, coolly, quietly |
| Match | "Composedly" = in a calm, self-controlled manner ✅. "Silently" = without sound ❌ (wrong meaning). "Cautiously" = carefully, to avoid risk ❌ (different nuance). "Slowly" = at a low speed ❌ (wrong meaning). |
| Register | "Composedly" is the right register for a workplace description. |

**The trap:** "cautiously" is the most attractive distractor because calm and cautious feel similar. The
distinguishing test: **calm is about your emotional state; cautious is about your care regarding risk.** The
sentence describes the manager's composure under pressure, not his caution about risk.

## Quick rules

- One-word substitution: **sentence first, part of speech second, candidates third, options last.**
- Generate your own candidates before reading the options — you will recognise the answer faster.
- Idiom: answer the **literal** meaning. Always.
- Antonym items: check for a **milder** opposite and for a **synonym offered as an antonym**.
- Synonym items: check for a **negative judgement** in one of the near-identical options.
- If two options fit, pick the **more common** word.

## Common traps

- **Answering from a dictionary definition** rather than the sentence's sense.
- **Treating a polysemous word's other sense** as the answer ("effective", "issue", "present", "sound").
- **The milder-opposite trap:** "to decrease" offered as the opposite of "to annihilate".
- **The opposite-of-the-opposite trap:** "synonym" offered as an antonym of "sluggish" (i.e. "not sluggish" =
  "fast" is right; "active" is wrong).
- **Register traps:** "terminate", "liquidate", "eliminate" for a simple workplace sentence.

## Checklist

- [ ] I read the full sentence before judging the word.
- [ ] I noted the part of speech of the blank.
- [ ] I generated my own candidate words before reading the options.
- [ ] I checked for a negative judgement, a milder opposite, or a register mismatch.
- [ ] I moved on at 40 seconds.
