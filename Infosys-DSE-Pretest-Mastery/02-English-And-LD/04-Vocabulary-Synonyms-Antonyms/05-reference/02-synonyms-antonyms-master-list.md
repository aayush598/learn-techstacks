## In one sentence
**A one-page thematic word list — the words that appear in Indian pre-test synonym and antonym items, grouped so that you can learn them in themed chunks instead of as isolated definitions.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- The papers draw from a **stable vocabulary pool**. The words on this page are the pool.
- Themed learning is faster than alphabetical learning: you remember *one example of "stubborn"* and you have
  six.
- Read this page twice, once now and once the night before the test. Do not memorise it word by word.

## Worked example

> **Q.** Choose the word most nearly opposite in meaning to **DILIGENT** in the sentence: "She was diligent in
> preparing the release documentation, checking every configuration twice."
>
> A. Thorough
> B. Negligent
> C. Careless
> D. Slow

**Answer: B — Negligent.**

| Option | Analysis |
|--------|----------|
| A | "Thorough" is a **synonym** of diligent ❌ |
| B | "Negligent" = careless, not doing the work properly — the true opposite ✅ |
| C | "Careless" is close, but it is a **milder** opposite; "negligent" carries the sense of *failing in duty*, which is the direct antonym of "diligent" |
| D | "Slow" is a different axis entirely — speed, not care ❌ |

**The rule this item tests:** among several near-opposites, pick the one that is the **direct** antonym and
matches the **same axis** (diligence, not speed). Also note that option A is a synonym — the trap that gets
candidates first.

## Quick rules

- Learn themed groups, not isolated words.
- Every antonym question: check whether the "opposite" is on the **same axis** (speed vs care vs strength).
- Watch for a **synonym** hiding among the antonym options — it is the most common trap.
- If a word appears in both a synonym list and an antonym list here, note the nuance.
- Workplace register: prefer the plain, professional word in your own writing; in the exam, prefer the option
  that fits the sentence.

## Common traps

- **Selecting a synonym because it is a stronger-sounding word** (A in the worked example).
- **Confusing the axis**: "slow" for "diligent", "cold" for "unfriendly", "heavy" for "wet".
- **Over-learning rare words** that appear once and distract you from the 30 that appear often.
