# Confused Pairs — Full Table

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Vocabulary, Synonyms & Antonyms ·  _Estimated reading time: 6 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Confused word pairs](../01-concepts/01-confused-word-pairs.md)

## In one sentence
**One printable table of every confused pair, with the one-line distinguishing test that settles it — the page to carry into the exam room.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Confused pairs account for the majority of "choose the most similar / opposite meaning" items.
- The **distinguishing test**, not the definition, is what survives under time pressure.
- Learn the pair with its test, never the pair alone.

## Worked example

> **Q.** Choose the word most nearly similar in meaning to **PREVAIL**.
>
> A. Precede
> B. Proceed
> C. Triumph
> D. Persuade

**Answer: C — Triumph.** "Prevail" = to be widespread, to win out, to be the rule ✅ ("the view prevailed"; "the
team prevailed"). "Precede" = come before ❌ (spelling twin). "Proceed" = carry on ❌ (spelling twin). "Persuade" =
convince by argument ❌.

**Memory hook in action:** "**pre**cede = **pre**vious; **pro**ceed = **pro**gress; **pre**vail = **pre**valent /
to win." Three "pre-" words, three different meanings.

## Quick rules

- Learn the pair **with its test**, never alone.
- The spelling twin is never the answer in a "most similar meaning" item.
- "The principal is a person; a principle is a rule."
- "Stationery is what you write with; stationary is what does not move."
- "Flaunt is showing off; flout is breaking a rule."
- "Precede is before; proceed is forward."
- "Eminent is famous; imminent is coming."
- If a pair is not in this table, use the substitution test from the technique file.

## Common traps

- Learning the definition without the test, and forgetting it under pressure.
- Confusing the pairs that differ by one letter — precede/proceed, eminent/imminent.
- Choosing a spelling twin because the option list is ordered alphabetically by the target's look-alikes.
