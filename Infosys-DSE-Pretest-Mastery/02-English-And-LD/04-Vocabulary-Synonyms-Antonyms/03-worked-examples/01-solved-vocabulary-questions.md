# Solved Vocabulary Questions

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Vocabulary, Synonyms & Antonyms ·  _Estimated reading time: 9 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Vocabulary elimination technique](../02-techniques/01-vocab-elimination-and-idioms.md)

## In one sentence
**Eight solved vocabulary items — synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitutions, and an idiom — each with the exact trap that kills the wrong options.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Each item below is built around one named trap. Learn the trap names, and the next eight items in a mock will
  feel familiar.
- The reasoning is short on purpose: the technique is the exam-honest part, the vocabulary list is the
  memorisation part.

## Core concept

Moves used in every solution: **sentence → part of speech → sense in context → tie-break**.

## Worked example

### Q1. Synonym with a judgement trap

> **Q.** Choose the word most nearly similar in meaning to **FRUGAL**.
>
> A. Thrifty
> B. Miserly
> C. Wealthy
> D. Lavish

**Answer: A — Thrifty.** "Frugal" = careful with money, approvingly. "Miserly" = hoarding, always negative ❌.
"Wealthy" = having money, not saving it ❌. "Lavish" = the opposite ❌.

**Trap: judgement.** Two options were near-synonyms; the negative one was wrong.

---

### Q2. Antonym with a milder-opposite trap

> **Q.** Choose the word most nearly opposite in meaning to **SCEPTICAL**.
>
> A. Credulous
> B. Cynical
> C. Doubtful
> D. Uncertain

**Answer: A — Credulous.** "Doubtful" is a **synonym** ❌. "Uncertain" is a **milder** version ❌. "Cynical" is a
*near*-opposite (distrust of motives) and can even sit close to the target ❌. "Credulous" = too ready to believe ✅.

**Trap: three traps in one item** — synonym, milder version, near-opposite.

---

### Q3. The adapt / adopt / adept item

> **Q.** Choose the word most nearly similar in meaning to **ADEPT**.
>
> A. Adapt
> B. Adopt
> C. Skillful
> D. Adjust

**Answer: C — Skillful.** "Adept" = skilled ✅. "Adapt" = change to fit, "Adopt" = take on, "Adjust" = alter —
all three are verbs of *change*, and all three were offered to trap you on the spelling ❌.

**Trap: spelling-similarity.** The three wrong options share the letters of the target.

---

### Q4. ensure / insure / assure

> **Q.** Choose the word most nearly similar in meaning to **ASSURE**.
>
> A. Insure
> B. Ensure
> C. Reassure
> D. Guarantee

**Answer: C — Reassure.** "Assure" = tell a person confidently ✅, so "reassure" is the closest ✅. "Ensure" = make
sure a **thing** happens ❌. "Insure" = cover financially ❌. "Guarantee" = a promise about a **thing** ❌.

**Trap: same root, different object.** Ensure → thing; assure → person; insure → money.

---

### Q5. One-word substitution with a near-synonym trap

> **Q.** Choose the word that best replaces the underlined phrase:
> "The manager handled the *situation with great calm* throughout the difficult client call."
>
> A. composedly
> B. silently
> C. cautiously
> D. slowly

**Answer: A — composedly.** "Composedly" = in a calm, self-controlled manner ✅. "Silently" = without sound ❌.
"Cautiously" = carefully, to avoid risk ❌ (calm is about your state; cautious is about risk). "Slowly" = at a low
speed ❌.

**Trap: near-synonym with a shifted nuance.**

---

### Q6. Register trap

> **Q.** Choose the word most nearly similar in meaning to **CANCEL** in the sentence: "Please cancel the
> booking for the 4 p.m. slot."
>
> A. Revoke
> B. Annul
> C. Abrogate
> D. Withdraw

**Answer: D — Withdraw.** "Revoke" = cancel an authorisation or a right ❌. "Annul" = declare a document invalid
❌. "Abrogate" = abolish a law, right, or treaty ❌. "Withdraw" = take back a booking or a reservation ✅.

**Trap: register.** All four are real words meaning "undo", but only "withdraw" fits a hotel booking. The
sentence decided the answer.

---

### Q7. Idiom — literal meaning

> **Q.** What does the expression "to beat about the bush" mean?
>
> A. To search in the bushes for a plant
> B. To avoid coming to the main point
> C. To work very hard in the garden
> D. To criticise someone indirectly

**Answer: B.** The exam always asks for the **literal, everyday** meaning of the idiom, and "avoid the main point"
is what the expression *means* in use. Option A is the literal-word trap, C is a meaning-invented trap, and D is a
plausible-sounding near-meaning that is not what the idiom means.

**Trap: the literal-word distractor.**

---

### Q8. Polysemy — the sense test

> **Q.** Choose the word most nearly similar in meaning to **CRITICAL** in the sentence: "The critical path was
> recalculated after the scope change."
>
> A. Crucial
> B. Analytical
> C. Faulty
> D. Severe

**Answer: A — Crucial.** In this sentence "critical" means *decisive/essential*, not "faulty" and not "severely
negative" ❌❌. "Analytical" refers to the critical approach ❌.

**Trap: polysemy.** "Critical" has at least three live senses; the sentence fixes which one is in play.

## Quick rules

- The sentence picks the answer, not the dictionary.
- Two near-synonyms in the options → look for the negative judgement.
- Antonym options that are synonyms of the target → eliminate immediately.
- Ensure/assure/insure: thing / person / money.
- Idioms: literal everyday meaning.
- Polysemous words: read the sentence for the sense.

## Common traps

- Answering from the abstract definition (Q2, Q6).
- Falling for the spelling-similar distractors (Q3).
- Missing a negative judgement (Q1).
- Picking the figurative reading of an idiom (Q7).
