# Vocabulary Traps

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Vocabulary, Synonyms & Antonyms ·  _Estimated reading time: 6 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Vocabulary elimination technique](../02-techniques/01-vocab-elimination-and-idioms.md)

## In one sentence
**The vocabulary options are not random — each wrong option is built with a specific purpose, and if you can name the purpose you will stop choosing it.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- A vocabulary item with four random distractors would be unanswerable. So the distractors are **designed**:
  one is a synonym, one is a milder version, one is a register mismatch or a different part of speech.
- Naming the purpose makes you suspicious of the right kind of option, which is much faster than evaluating all
  four on merit.

## Core concept

### The 7 distractor purposes

| # | Purpose | What it looks like | Example (target: FRUGAL) |
|---|---------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| 1 | **True synonym, wrong register** | Correct meaning, too formal/informal | "Parsimonious" (too formal for a casual sentence) |
| 2 | **Near-synonym with a negative judgement** | Same core meaning, disapproval attached | "Miserly" |
| 3 | **Milder version of the target** | A weaker form offered as the opposite | Target = "annihilate", option = "lessen" |
| 4 | **Synonym offered as an antonym** | Same meaning, listed among the antonyms | Target = "sceptical", option = "doubtful" |
| 5 | **Opposite of the opposite** | Means the same as the target | Target = "sluggish", option = "slothful" |
| 6 | **Wrong part of speech** | A verb in an adjective slot | Target = "transparent", option = "concealed" |
| 7 | **Spelling-similar decoy** | Shares the target's letters | Target = "ADEPT", options = "adapt", "adopt" |

### The one-line counters

| Purpose | Counter-question |
|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | "Would I use this word in this sentence with this person?" |
| 2 | "Can this word ever be used approvingly?" |
| 3 | "Is this the FULL opposite, or just a weaker version?" |
| 4 | "Is this option actually a synonym?" |
| 5 | "Does this option mean the same as the target?" |
| 6 | "Does it fit grammatically?" |
| 7 | "Never pick the spelling twin. Read the definitions." |

### The 4 questions to ask of an option you like

```
1. Is it the same part of speech as the target?
2. Does it survive substitution into the sentence?
3. If it is a near-synonym of the target, does it carry a judgement the target does not?
4. If the item is an antonym item, is it a SYNONYM, a MILDER version, or the opposite of the opposite?
```

### The idiom traps

| Trap | Example | Rule |
|------|---------|------|
| The literal-word reading | "to spill the beans" → "to waste the beans" | Idioms mean the figurative thing; the option giving the *figurative* everyday meaning is right |
| A meaning-invented option | "to beat about the bush" → "to search inefficiently" | Pick the option that matches a meaning you have actually heard used |
| A literal, correct-but-not-the-meaning option | "to hit the nail on the head" → "to injure someone's head" | The everyday meaning of the idiom, not the words |
| A near-meaning option | "to burn the midnight oil" → "to waste fuel" | The everyday meaning is "work late" |

### The one-word substitution traps

| Trap | Example | Rule |
|------|---------|------|
| The same word as the phrase | "with great care" → "carefully" ✅ vs "care" | Must be a **different** word |
| A word that changes the part of speech | "in a very good manner" → "well" (adverb) vs "good" (adjective) | Check the slot |
| A word that changes the meaning | "to make less severe" → "mitigate" ✅ vs "reduce" (of a number) | Read the whole sentence |
| A longer, more technical word | "a place of complete safety" → "sanctuary" ✅ vs "asepticism" | Prefer the natural word |
| A word that breaks the register | "to cancel" → "rescind" for a client email | Register decides |

## Worked example

> **Q.** Choose the word most nearly opposite in meaning to **CANDID** in the sentence: "The performance review
> was a candid discussion of the team's strengths and weaknesses."
>
> A. Evasive
> B. Frank
> C. Blunt
> D. Reserved

**Answer: A — Evasive.**

| Option | Purpose | Verdict |
|--------|---------|---------|
| A | True opposite | ✅ "Evasive" = refusing to speak openly — the opposite of candid. |
| B | **Synonym of the target** | "Frank" = candid ❌ — it means the same thing. |
| C | **Synonym of the target** | "Blunt" = frank and direct ❌ |
| D | **Milder version / different meaning** | "Reserved" = slow to show emotion or talk — a personality trait, not a refusal to be open ❌ |

**Three of the four options are synonyms or near-synonyms of the target.** Only one is genuinely opposite. That is
the most common construction of a well-made antonym item: the distractors cluster near the target, not near the
answer.

**The counter-question that saved this item:** *"Is this option a synonym of the target?"* Apply it before anything
else on any antonym question.

## Quick rules

- On an antonym item, first eliminate every option that is a **synonym** of the target.
- Then eliminate **milder versions**.
- Then eliminate **opposite-of-the-opposite**.
- What survives is the answer.
- On a synonym item, eliminate options with a different part of speech, then check the judgement.
- On a one-word substitution, the phrase must be replaced by a **different** word that fits the **whole
  sentence**.
- On an idiom item, choose the everyday meaning of the expression.
- Never pick the spelling twin of the target.

## Common traps

- **Choosing a synonym because it "sounds like a strong answer"** — on an antonym item that is the classic miss.
- **Accepting a milder opposite** because it is at least in the right direction.
- **Confusing "candid" (the target) with "frank" (a synonym offered as the answer).**
- **Choosing a technical word for a plain sentence.**
- **Over-reading the stem.** If the item says "most nearly similar", the options are near-synonyms by design and
  the *closest* one wins.

## Checklist

- [ ] I eliminated every synonym of the target on antonym items.
- [ ] I checked for a milder version.
- [ ] I checked the part of speech.
- [ ] I checked the judgement/register.
- [ ] I did not pick the spelling twin.
