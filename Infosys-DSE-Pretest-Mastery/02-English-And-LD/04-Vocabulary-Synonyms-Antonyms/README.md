# Vocabulary, Synonyms & Antonyms

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Vocabulary, Synonyms & Antonyms ·  _Estimated reading time: 6 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Section 2 overview](../README.md)

## In one sentence
**Vocabulary in the pre-test is recall, not reasoning — you either know the word or you eliminate by word-class and sentence sense in 20 seconds, and either way you should be spending under 40 seconds on the item.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- 1–3 items per batch, and they come from a **small, stable pool**: confused word pairs, high-frequency
  synonyms/antonyms, one-word substitutions, and a few common idioms.
- This is the cheapest sub-type in Section 2 to prepare: it is pure memorisation, and the memorisation list is
  finite.
- The trap is not knowing the word — it is knowing **one sense** of a polysemous word and picking an option that
  is technically a synonym but wrong in this context.

## Learning objectives

After this folder you should be able to:

- Distinguish the **26 confused word pairs** that appear most often in Indian pre-tests (adapt/adopt/adept,
  affect/effect, principle/principal, capital/capitol, ensure/insure/assure, than/then, stationary/stationery,
  site/sight, eminent/imminent, and 17 more).
- Apply the **4-step elimination technique** to any synonym/antonym question using word class and sentence sense.
- Handle **one-word substitutions** and the most common **idioms** used in these papers.
- Recognise **antonym traps**: options that are a synonym, a milder version, or the *opposite* of the opposite.
- Recognise **register traps**: a word that is technically right but wrong for the sentence's register.

## Quick procedure — the 40-second vocabulary drill

```
1. Read the sentence, find the target word, and note its PART OF SPEECH (15s).
2. Rule out options with the wrong part of speech. (5s)
3. Test the surviving options in the sentence — not in the abstract. (15s)
4. If nothing survives: pick the one that fits the sentence's sense; if two fit, pick the
   more common word. Mark and move at 40s. (5s)
```

## The 4 item shapes and their one-line strategy

| Shape | Stem wording | Strategy | Target time |
|-------|--------------|----------|-------------|
| **Synonym** | "most nearly similar in meaning to" | Find the shared core meaning, reject the option with a different core | 40s |
| **Antonym** | "most nearly opposite in meaning to" | Reject the synonyms first — three options are usually synonyms of the target | 40s |
| **Confused pair** | "choose the word that best completes the sentence" | Use the distinguishing test: ensure / assure / insure | 30s |
| **Idiom / one-word substitution** | "choose the meaning of the idiom" | Use the literal meaning, not the story behind it | 30s |

## Key takeaways

- 🔥 **The sentence is the test.** A word can be a true synonym and still be wrong in context.
- 🔥 **Word class first.** If the blank needs a noun and the option is an adjective, it is out — in 2 seconds.
- `adapt` = change to fit; `adopt` = take on; `adept` = skilled.
- `affect` = verb; `effect` = noun. `principle` = rule; `principal` = chief. `ensure` = make sure; `insure` =
  insure financially; `assure` = reassure a person.
- `stationary` = not moving; `stationery` = writing supplies. `site` = location; `sight` = vision.
- `eminent` = famous; `imminent` = about to happen. `susceptible` = prone to; `vulnerable` = defenseless.
- The classic antonym trap is a **milder opposite**: "big/small" pairs offered for "extreme" questions.
- A **register trap** is a word that is correct but too informal, technical, or archaic for the sentence.
- Idiom questions are testing the **literal meaning trap**: pick the option with the everyday meaning, not the
  figurative one.
