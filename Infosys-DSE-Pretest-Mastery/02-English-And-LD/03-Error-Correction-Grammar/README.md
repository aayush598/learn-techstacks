# Error Correction (Grammar & Usage)

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Error Correction ·  _Estimated reading time: 6 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Section 2 overview](../README.md)

## In one sentence
**The error-correction item gives you one sentence with four underlined segments and asks you to pick the segment with the error — so you are not looking for the worst writing, you are looking for the one segment that breaks a specific rule.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- 2–4 items per batch, in the standard "identify the part of the sentence containing an error" format with
  four options that are **segments of the same sentence**.
- Every other English item in the paper is recall or reading. This one is **detection**, and it is the fastest
  one to score: 45 seconds per item is realistic once you own the scan.
- The trap is emotional: the wrong segment often *sounds* wrong to a weak-English reader (Meera's blind spot),
  while the correct segments often sound slightly formal or unusual.

## Learning objectives

After this folder you should be able to:

- Run the **8-step scan** on any error-correction sentence in under 45 seconds.
- Recognise the **14 high-frequency error families**: subject-verb agreement, articles, prepositions, tense
  consistency, pronoun agreement, misplaced modifiers, comma splices, double negatives, since/for, who/which/that,
  fewer/less, than/then, affect/effect, redundant modifiers, infinitive vs gerund.
- Use the **verb-first heuristic**: locate the finite verb, then test agreement and tense before anything else.
- Recognise the **four-segment format** and how the distractors are built (one error, three defensible segments).
- Distinguish a **real grammar error** from a **stylistic oddity** — style is not an error.
- Apply the **6 elimination tests** to any segment you are unsure about.

## Quick procedure — the 8-step scan in one line

```
MEANING → VERB → AGREEMENT → TENSE → PREPOSITION → ARTICLE → MODIFIER → CONNECTOR
```

Read the sentence once for meaning, find the finite verb, then walk the checklist in order. **Never** read the
options first.

## Time budget

| Step | Seconds | Note |
|------|---------|------|
| Read the sentence once, complete | 10 | Do not stop at the options |
| Locate the finite verb | 5 | The verb is the anchor |
| Agreement + tense tests | 15 | Covers most items |
| Article / preposition / modifier / connector | 10 | Only if still undecided |
| Decide and move | 5 | Hard stop at 45 seconds |

## Key takeaways

- 🔥 **Exactly one segment is wrong.** If two look wrong, you have mis-read where the segments begin and end.
- 🔥 **Find the verb first.** Most errors in this format are agreement or tense errors, and both are invisible
  until you locate the verb.
- A **plural subject takes a plural verb**; an **indefinite pronoun (each, every, someone) takes a singular verb**.
- **Articles** (a / an / the) are the most commonly overlooked family — "a" before a vowel *sound*, not a vowel letter.
- **Comma splice**: two independent clauses joined by only a comma is an error. Use a semicolon, a conjunction, or a full stop.
- **Double negatives** are wrong in standard formal English, except after "not only".
- **Fewer** counts countable nouns; **less** counts uncountable nouns. **Than** is comparison; **then** is time/sequence.
- **Affect** is usually the verb; **effect** is usually the noun. **Its** is possessive; **it's** is "it is".
- **Misplaced modifiers** attach to the wrong noun. Long modifiers go after the noun; short ones before it.
- **Who** for people, **which** for things, **that** for both (restrictive).
- "Used to" + infinitive; "enjoy", "mind", "avoid", "finish", "suggest" + gerund.
- If a segment is merely **stylistically odd**, it is not the error. The error always breaks a rule.
