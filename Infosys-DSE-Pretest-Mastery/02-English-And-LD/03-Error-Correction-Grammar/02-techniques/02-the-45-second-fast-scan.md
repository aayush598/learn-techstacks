# The 45-Second Fast Scan

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Error Correction ·  _Estimated reading time: 7 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [The 8-step scan](01-the-eight-step-scan.md)

## In one sentence
**When the 8-step scan is not enough time, the fast scan is four checks in 30 seconds — verb, subject, tense, article/preposition — and it resolves roughly three quarters of the items on its own.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Section 2 gives you 15 questions in 22 minutes. At 90 seconds each, error correction is easy **only if** you
  do not spend 90 seconds on it.
- The fast scan exists for the moment when you are behind schedule and need to bank the error-correction marks
  without burning three minutes.
- Knowing which step to skip is worth more than knowing one more rule.

## Core concept

### The fast scan — 4 checks, 30 seconds

```
CHECK 1 (6s)  Find the finite verb of the MAIN clause.
CHECK 2 (8s)  Name the subject of that clause. Does the verb agree?
CHECK 3 (6s)  Is the tense consistent with the time markers?
CHECK 4 (10s) Scan for: a/an by sound · a missing article · a wrong preposition
                 · "the team of X are" · "each of X were" · a comma splice.
              → If one chunk lights up, that is your answer.
```

If Check 4 finds nothing, **go back to the full 8-step scan** — but you have already saved the 10 seconds that
the first three checks cost you, because they are the same checks.

### The decision tree

```
        +-----------------------------+
        | Find the finite verb (6s)    |
        +--------------+--------------+
                       ↓
        +-----------------------------+
        | Name its subject (8s)        |
        | Does the verb match?         |
        +--+----------------------+----+
           | NO                   | YES
           ↓                     ↓
    +-------------+      +--------------------+
    | Check the   |      | Check tense (6s)   |
    | other clause|      +------+-------+-----+
    | of the same |             | NO    | YES
    | sentence    |             ↓       ↓
    | (coordination|     +----------+  +------------+
    |  agreement) |     | Tense    |  | Fast scan 4|
    +------+------+     | error    |  | (10s)      |
           |            +----------+  +-----+------+
           ↓                               ↓
      THAT IS THE ANSWER            Found something? → answer.
                                   Nothing? → full 8-step scan (15s more)
```

### The 12 highest-yield quick checks

Print this list. In the order below, they cover most items.

| # | Check | The wrong form | The right form |
|---|-------|----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Main-clause agreement | "the team of X **are**" | "the team of X **is**" |
| 2 | Indefinite-pronoun subject | "**each** of the X **were**" | "**was**" |
| 3 | List/quality head noun | "the list of items **are**" | "**is**" |
| 4 | Past + present in one sentence | "Yesterday I **am** working" | "**was**" |
| 5 | Article before a vowel sound | "**a** hour", "**a** university" | "**an** hour", "**a** university" |
| 6 | Missing article | "She is **engineer**" | "She is **an** engineer" |
| 7 | Preposition collocation | "discuss **about**", "depends **of**" | "discuss", "depends **on**" |
| 8 | No article before "production" | "deployed to **the** production" | "deployed **to production**" |
| 9 | Comma splice | "It failed, we fixed it." | "It failed**;** we fixed it." |
| 10 | Double negative | "did **not** get **no** reply" | "did not get **any** reply" |
| 11 | since vs for | "**for** since 2020", "**since** two years" | "**since** 2020", "**for** two years" |
| 12 | Fewer vs less | "**less** errors" | "**fewer** errors" |

### When to skip the full scan

| Situation | What to do |
|-----------|-----------|
| You are behind schedule | Run checks 1–4 only. Bank the obvious items |
| The sentence is long (30+ words) | Slow down. Long sentences hide clause boundaries — that is usually where the error is |
| The sentence has a number/date/percentage | Check the **verb that agrees with the number**: "The number of modules **is**", "A **number of** modules **are**" |
| You see a quoted phrase | Usually a distractor boundary; do not let a quotation slow you down |
| Two chunks look wrong | Stop, re-read the **options**, and check where each chunk begins and ends |
| You found the error but want to be sure | Confirm by repairing: does your fix make the whole sentence correct? |

### The 5-second sanity rule

If the whole sentence reads naturally to you and you cannot name a rule being broken, **pick the chunk with the
verb** and move on. Do not force an error into a sentence that does not have one — but note that the item
guarantees one error, so if nothing is visible, you have probably mis-segmented the options.

## Worked example

> **Q.** Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
>
> "**Each** of the four modules **has** **a different set of** **dependencies**, **and the** **integration testing take** **more time than we anticipated**."
>
> A. Each of the four modules has
> B. a different set of
> C. dependencies, and the
> D. integration testing take more time

**Fast scan run:**

| Check | Finding |
|-------|---------|
| 1 Verb (6s) | "**has**" and "**take**" — two finite verbs, two clauses |
| 2 Subject + agreement (8s) | "Each of the four modules **has**" — the subject is "each", singular, and the verb is singular ✅. "**the integration testing take**" — the subject is "testing" (a single, uncountable process), so the verb must be "**takes**" ❌ |
| 3 Tense (6s) | Both verbs are present simple, both describing current facts ✅ |
| 4 Fast checks (10s) | Articles: "a different set of dependencies" ✅ (indefinite first mention), "the integration testing" ✅ (specific). Preposition: "set **of** dependencies" ✅, "more time **than** we anticipated" ✅ (than = comparison). Comma: ", **and**" joining two independent clauses ✅ |

**Answer: D** — "the integration testing **take**" must be "the integration testing **takes**".

**Why the other three are right:**
- A: "Each … has" — indefinite pronoun + singular verb ✅
- B: "a different set of" — indefinite article for a first mention, and "of" links "set" to "dependencies" ✅
- C: "dependencies, and the" — the comma + "and" correctly coordinates two independent clauses, and "the" introduces the second clause's subject ✅

**Note the speed:** the fast scan found the error at check 2, in about 20 seconds. The full scan would have
reached the same place, 25 seconds later.

**Second example — the fast scan finds nothing:**

> "**The number of** **open defects has** **increased** **after the** **latest release**, **and the** **testing team are** **reviewing the** **test cases again**."
>
> A. The number of open defects has increased
> B. after the latest release
> C. and the testing team are
> D. reviewing the test cases again

Check 2 fires immediately: "**The number of** open defects **has**" ✅ (a single number → singular), but "the
testing team **are**" — in Indian English a unified team takes a **singular** verb, so it should be "**is**".
**Answer: C.** Note the trap pair inside the same sentence: "the number of … has" (correct) vs "the team are"
(wrong). One sentence, two similar-looking agreement items, only one of them wrong.

## Quick rules

- Checks 1–4 (verb, subject, tense, article/preposition) resolve most items.
- If the fast scan is clean, go to the full scan — do not guess.
- "The number of X **is**" vs "A number of X **are**" — memorise this pair.
- In "each of X", the verb agrees with **each**, never with X.
- Re-read the **options** if you find two candidate errors.
- 45 seconds is the budget. At 60 seconds, flag it and move on.

## Common traps

- **Running the full scan on a short, easy sentence.** Wasted seconds.
- **Forgetting the item guarantees one error** and forcing a wrong conclusion.
- **Treating "the number of" as "a number of".**
- **Stopping after the fast scan** when it finds nothing. The fast scan is a filter, not an answer.

## Checklist

- [ ] I found the verb before looking at the options.
- [ ] I named the subject of the verb's clause.
- [ ] I ran the 12 quick checks.
- [ ] If nothing fired, I ran the remaining steps instead of guessing.
- [ ] I moved on within 60 seconds.
