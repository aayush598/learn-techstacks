# The 8-Step Scan

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Error Correction ·  _Estimated reading time: 8 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Error taxonomy](../01-concepts/01-error-taxonomy.md)

## In one sentence
**A fixed 8-step scan takes any error-correction sentence from unread to identified in 45 seconds — read once for meaning, find the verb, then test agreement, tense, preposition, articles, modifiers, and connectors in that order.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- The order matters. Agreement and tense errors are the most frequent and the fastest to spot **once you know
  where the verb is**. Articles and modifiers are slower and rarer, so they come last.
- Reading the options first is the single biggest time waster in this sub-type. The scan fixes the reading order:
  **sentence first, always.**
- A fixed procedure also protects you from the panic re-read. Meera's error-correction losses come from re-reading
  the same sentence four times and changing her mind.

## Core concept

### The 8 steps with a time budget

| Step | Action | Time | What it catches | How to do it |
|------|--------|------|-----------------|--------------|
| **1** | **Read for meaning** | 6s | Sets context; you must be able to paraphrase the sentence | Read the whole sentence once, ignoring the underlines. Say what it means to yourself |
| **2** | **Find the finite verb** | 5s | Locates 80% of errors | Circle it mentally. Every clause has one. If there are two clauses, find both |
| **3** | **Check agreement** | 8s | Subject-verb, number, person, head-noun traps | For each verb, name its subject. Does the verb match? Watch "each/every/everyone", "the team of X", "the list of X" |
| **4** | **Check tense consistency** | 6s | Tense shifts, reported speech | Do all verbs sit in the same time frame? Check for "yesterday/last/ago" (past) and "every day/usually" (present) |
| **5** | **Check prepositions** | 6s | "discuss about", "depends of", "married with" | For each verb/noun + preposition pair, ask if it is the standard collocation |
| **6** | **Check articles** | 5s | Missing article, wrong article, "a" vs "an" | Does a singular countable noun need an article? Is "a"/"an" chosen by **sound**? |
| **7** | **Check modifiers** | 5s | Misplaced modifiers, dangling participles | Does the phrase immediately after a noun describe that noun? |
| **8** | **Check connectors** | 4s | Comma splices, double negatives | Two independent clauses joined by only a comma? Two negatives in a row? |

**Total: 45 seconds.** Then match the answer to the option and stop.

### Why this order

```
Verb found (step 2)
   ↓
Agreement (3) → the most frequent family, and the verb is already in front of you
   ↓
Tense (4) → second most frequent, same clause
   ↓
Prepositions (5) → needs the verb or the noun it attaches to
   ↓
Articles (6) → needs the noun phrase
   ↓
Modifiers (7) → needs the noun phrase + the rest of the clause
   ↓
Connectors (8) → needs the whole sentence
```

Steps 5–8 need you to have already located the verb and its clause. Doing them earlier wastes time.

### Step 3 in detail — the head-noun test

The subject of a sentence can be many words long. Find the **head noun** — the noun that the verb agrees with.

| Subject phrase | Head noun | Verb |
|----------------|-----------|------|
| The **team** of developers | team (singular) | has ✅ |
| The **list** of items | list (singular) | is ✅ |
| The **quality** of the deliverables | quality (singular) | was ✅ |
| **Each** of the trainees | each (singular) | was ✅ |
| **Everyone** on the team | everyone (singular) | is ✅ |
| **The managers** and the **engineers** | managers + engineers (plural) | are ✅ |
| **Either** of the two options | either (singular) | is ✅ |
| **Neither** of the answers | neither (singular) | is ✅ |

⚠️ The exam loves "the team of developers **are**" and "each of the trainees **were**". Both are wrong in Indian
English.

### Step 4 in detail — the time-frame map

| Time marker in the sentence | Verb tense required |
|------------------------------|---------------------|
| yesterday, last week, in 2019, two days ago | past |
| now, today, currently, at the moment | present |
| every day, usually, generally, on Fridays | present (habitual) |
| tomorrow, next month | future/present (Indian English often uses present) |
| since 2019, for two years | present perfect |
| by the time…, until…, as soon as… | present for the time clause |

**Reported speech back-shift:** "He said he **is** tired" → "He said he **was** tired." This is the most common
legitimate tense shift, and a candidate can waste 30 seconds arguing about it. Memorise: **reported speech
shifts back**; everything else in one sentence should stay put.

### Step 6 in detail — the article decision tree

```
Is the noun countable and singular?
 |- No (plural / uncountable) → does it need "the"? (specific ⇒ yes; general ⇒ no)
 +- Yes → is it the FIRST mention of a specific one?
      |- Yes, one of many → "a" / "an"
      +- No, already known / unique → "the"

"a" vs "an": use the SOUND of the first letter.
  an hour (silent h) · a university (yu- sound) · an MBA (em- sound) · a one-rupee coin
```

### Step 7 in detail — the modifier proximity test

```
"the file [that I sent to Rahul yesterday] by email"
   +-- modifier --+
   → does it describe "the file"? YES → correct, but awkward.
   → the fix: "the file that I sent to Rahul yesterday by email" is fine;
     "the file I sent to Rahul yesterday by email" is also fine.

"the report [prepared by the team] for the client"  → modifies "report" ✅
"the report [that I gave to Rahul] for the client"  → modifies "the client"? ❌ ambiguous
   → fix: "the report for the client that I gave to Rahul"
```

**The dangling-participle error:** a phrase that must modify the subject but is attached to the object.
- "Walking into the room, **the projector failed**." ❌ — the projector was not walking.
- "Walking into the room, **I saw the projector fail**." ✅

### Step 8 in detail — the comma-splice test

Ask: **is the second clause independent?** (subject + finite verb, and it could stand alone)

```
"The build failed, we rolled back the release."
  "we rolled back the release" is independent → comma splice ❌
  → "The build failed; we rolled back the release." ✅
  → "The build failed, and we rolled back the release." ✅
  → "The build failed. We rolled back the release." ✅
  → "Because the build failed, we rolled back the release." ✅
```

**The four legitimate comma joins:**
1. Before a coordinating conjunction joining two independent clauses: "…, **and** …"
2. Between two independent clauses with a semicolon.
3. Before an adverbial clause: "**After the build failed**, …"
4. Between items in a list, or after a short introductory phrase.

## Worked example

> **Q.** Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
>
> "**Although the code was reviewed by the team lead**, **the module was deployed to the production** **before the testing was completed**."
>
> A. Although the code was reviewed by the team lead
> B. the module was deployed to the production
> C. before the testing
> D. was completed

**Answer: B**

| Step | Finding |
|------|---------|
| 1 Meaning | The code was reviewed, but it went to production before testing finished — the reviewer missed something. |
| 2 Verb | "was reviewed", "was deployed", "was completed" — three finite verbs. |
| 3 Agreement | "the code **was** reviewed" ✅ (uncountable singular) · "the module **was** deployed" ✅ · "the testing **was** completed" ✅ |
| 4 Tense | All three verbs are past simple in one reported narrative ✅ consistent. |
| 5 Preposition | "deployed **to** the production" ❌ — the standard usage is "deployed **to** production" (no article) or "deployed **in** production". "the production" is wrong. |
| 6 Articles | "the code", "the module", "the testing" ✅ |
| 7 Modifiers | "Although the code was reviewed by the team lead" correctly modifies the main clause ✅ |
| 8 Connectors | "Although …, …" is a subordinate clause joined to a main clause — a **correct** comma use ✅ |

**Answer: B** — "the module was deployed to **the** production" should be "deployed **to production**" (or "deployed
in production"). The article "the" before "production" is the error.

**Why the other three are right:**
- A: "Although" + a subordinate clause + a main clause is the textbook comma pattern.
- C: "before the testing" — correct preposition and article.
- D: "was completed" — correct past passive, agreeing with "the testing".

**The trap:** options A and C both look "long and clause-heavy" and feel like the place a sentence breaks. They
do not. The error is one article word.

## Quick rules

- Read the sentence once, for meaning, before you look at a single option.
- Circle the finite verb mentally, then test agreement and tense.
- Match the clause to its own subject — never across clauses.
- "The team of X", "the list of X", "each of X" all take a **singular** verb.
- Reported speech back-shifts; nothing else in one sentence shifts tense.
- "a"/"an" is decided by **sound**.
- Two independent clauses need more than a comma between them.
- "deployed to production" — no article before "production" in the standard usage.
- If your fix does not repair the whole sentence, it is not the error.

## Common traps

- **Re-reading instead of scanning.** The scan is a fixed order; re-reading breaks it.
- **Judging the option before the sentence.** You lose the clause context, which is where the error lives.
- **Stopping at the first suspicious chunk.** Find the violation, then confirm the other three are clean.
- **Assuming a long chunk is the error.** Chunk length is uncorrelated with error.
- **Forgetting "although/because/if/when"** — the presence of a subordinating conjunction makes a comma correct
  even between two clauses.

## Checklist

- [ ] I read the full sentence before the options.
- [ ] I found every finite verb.
- [ ] I checked agreement and tense first.
- [ ] I checked articles by sound, not by spelling.
- [ ] I confirmed the other three chunks are clean and that my fix repairs the sentence.
