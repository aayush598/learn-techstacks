# Solved Error Corrections — Part 1

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Error Correction ·  _Estimated reading time: 10 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [The 8-step scan](../02-techniques/01-the-eight-step-scan.md)

## In one sentence
**Five fully solved four-option items — one from each of the five most frequent error families — with the scan applied step by step and every rejected chunk justified.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- These five families are the highest-yield: agreement, articles, prepositions, tense, and modifiers.
- The analysis shows the **order of the scan** in action, which is what you need under the 90-second cap.
- Every rejected chunk gets a reason, so you learn why it was *not* the error.

## Core concept

The scan order used in every solution below:

```
MEANING → VERB → AGREEMENT → TENSE → PREPOSITION → ARTICLE → MODIFIER → CONNECTOR
```

## Worked example

### Q1. Subject-verb agreement

> **Q.** Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
>
> "**Each of the four modules** **are tested** **before they** **are deployed**, **and the** **test results are** **attached to the** **release notes**."
>
> A. Each of the four modules are tested
> B. before they are deployed
> C. and the test results are
> D. attached to the release notes

**Answer: A**

| Step | Finding |
|------|---------|
| Meaning | Each module is tested first, then deployed; the results go into the release notes. |
| Verb | "are tested", "are deployed", "are attached" |
| Agreement | "Each of the four modules **are tested**" — the subject is "**each**", an indefinite pronoun → singular verb required ❌. "they are deployed" ✅ (plural "they"). "the test results **are** attached" ✅ (plural subject). |
| Tense | All passive present simple ✅ |
| Others | "before they are deployed" ✅ · "and the test results are" ✅ (coordinating "and" joining two independent clauses) · "attached to the release notes" ✅ |

**Fix:** "Each of the four modules **is tested** before they are deployed…"

**Why not the others:** B is a correct time clause with a correct plural verb. C is a correct plural subject with
a plural verb — it is the *distractor that looks like* the answer because it also involves "are". D is a correct
passive participle phrase.

---

### Q2. Article error

> **Q.** Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
>
> "The team was given **a** task of compiling the **historical** **data**, **which** **took** **a hour** **of** **manual** **verification**."
>
> A. a task of compiling the historical data
> B. which took
> C. a hour of
> D. manual verification

**Answer: C**

| Step | Finding |
|------|---------|
| Meaning | The team compiled historical data; the job took an hour of checking by hand. |
| Verb | "was given", "took" |
| Agreement | "The team **was** given" ✅ · "which **took**" ✅ |
| Tense | Mixed but justified: past passive for the team, past simple for the relative clause ✅ |
| Preposition | "a task **of** compiling" ✅ · "**an** hour **of** manual verification" ✅ |
| Article | "**a hour**" ❌ — "hour" begins with a **vowel sound** (the h is silent), so it needs "**an**". |

**Fix:** "…took **an** hour of manual verification."

**Why not the others:** A is correct — "a task of compiling the historical data" is a perfectly good noun phrase,
and "the historical data" correctly takes "the" because it is specific. B is a correct relative clause with an
agreeing verb. D is a plain noun phrase; there is nothing wrong with a modifier sitting after "of".

**The lesson:** the error is one letter. The distractors are all *longer and more complicated* than the error —
length is not a signal.

---

### Q3. Preposition error

> **Q.** Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
>
> "The team **discussed about** **the requirement** **and then** **agreed** **on the** **revised** **scope**."
>
> A. discussed about
> B. the requirement
> C. and then agreed
> D. on the revised scope

**Answer: A**

| Step | Finding |
|------|---------|
| Meaning | The team talked about the requirement and then settled on a revised scope. |
| Verb | "discussed", "agreed" |
| Agreement | "The team **discussed**" ✅ · "they/they agreed" — "the team agreed" ✅ |
| Tense | Both past simple ✅ |
| Preposition | "**discussed about**" ❌ — "discuss" is transitive; it takes no preposition ("discuss the requirement") or takes "with" ("discuss it with the client"). |
| Article | "the requirement" ✅ (specific) · "the revised scope" ✅ |
| Modifier | "revised" correctly modifies "scope" ✅ |
| Connector | ", and then" — a coordinating conjunction joining two independent clauses ✅ |

**Fix:** "The team **discussed** the requirement and then agreed on the revised scope."

**Why not the others:** B is a correct noun phrase with a definite article. C is a valid connector sequence
("and then agreed"). D is a correct prepositional phrase — "agree **on**" is the standard collocation, and "the
revised scope" is correct.

---

### Q4. Tense consistency

> **Q.** Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
>
> "**Yesterday** the team **was given** **the** **requirement**, **but** **they are still** **waiting** **for the** **clarification from the** **business analyst**."
>
> A. Yesterday the team was given
> B. but they are still waiting
> C. for the clarification from the
> D. business analyst

**Answer: B**

| Step | Finding |
|------|---------|
| Meaning | The team got the requirement yesterday but is still waiting for the analyst's clarification. |
| Verb | "was given", "are still waiting" |
| Agreement | "the team **was** given" ✅ · "**they are** still waiting" ✅ (plural "they") |
| Tense | "Yesterday" sets a past frame for the first clause. "are still waiting" is present continuous — a tense mismatch in the coordinated clause ❌ |
| Others | "for the clarification from the business analyst" ✅ — "the clarification" is specific, "from the business analyst" is a correct prepositional chain |

**Fix:** "…but **they were still waiting** for the clarification…" (or recast the second clause as
"…but **they have still not received** the clarification…").

**Why not the others:** A is correct — "yesterday" + past passive + singular "team" (unified team in Indian
English). C is a correct prepositional phrase with a definite article. D is a plain noun phrase with a correct
article.

**Note:** the distractor B *looks* defensible because present continuous is normal in modern English. The
sentence's own time anchor ("Yesterday") makes it the error here.

---

### Q5. Misplaced modifier

> **Q.** Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
>
> "The manager **sent** **the file to the developer** **that was signed by the client** **yesterday**."
>
> A. sent the file to the developer
> B. that was signed by the client
> C. yesterday
> D. by the client

**Answer: A**

**The scan:**

| Step | Finding |
|------|---------|
| Meaning | The manager sent the file to the developer yesterday. The file had been signed by the client. |
| Verb | "sent", "was signed" |
| Agreement | "the manager **sent**" ✅ · "**that** was signed" — "that" refers to "the developer" in the printed word order, and "the developer" is singular, so "was signed" agrees ✅ — **but that is the problem, not the solution** |
| Tense | Past simple for both verbs, consistent ✅ |
| Preposition | "signed **by** the client" ✅ |
| Article | "the file", "the developer", "the client" — all specific, all ✅ |
| Modifier | "that was signed by the client" sits immediately after "**the developer**", so it attaches to the developer ❌ — the sentence says the *developer* was signed by the client. |

**Fix:** "The manager sent **the file that was signed by the client** to the developer yesterday."

**Why not the others:** B is a relative clause that is grammatically correct in itself — it is only wrong *because
of where it sits*, which is exactly why you cannot judge chunks in isolation. C is a correctly placed time
adverbial at the end of the clause. D is the preposition in "signed by the client", which is correct.

## Quick rules

- In Q1 the answer was a **plural verb after a singular pronoun** ("each").
- In Q2 the answer was a **single letter** ("a" → "an").
- In Q3 the answer was a **single extra word** ("about").
- In Q4 the answer was a **verb form**.
- In Q5 the answer was a **word order**.
- The error is never the longest chunk. In all five items it was among the shorter or plainer ones.

## Common traps

- **Q2:** picking the long "a task of compiling the historical data" because it "sounds" heavy. Grammatically it
  is fine.
- **Q3:** picking "on the revised scope" because it *contains* a preposition. The preposition there is correct.
- **Q4:** accepting present continuous inside a "yesterday" frame.
- **Q5:** judging each chunk in isolation. Chunk B is grammatical; it is the *placement* that is wrong.

## Checklist

- [ ] I named the head of every subject before judging a verb.
- [ ] I checked "a"/"an" by sound.
- [ ] I checked the time anchor of the whole sentence against every verb.
- [ ] I checked whether a modifier attaches to the noun immediately before it.
- [ ] I confirmed my fix repairs the entire sentence.
