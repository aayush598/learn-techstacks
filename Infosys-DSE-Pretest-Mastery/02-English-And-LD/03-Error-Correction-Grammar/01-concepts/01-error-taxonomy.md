# Error Taxonomy — The 16 Families

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Error Correction ·  _Estimated reading time: 10 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Error correction topic README](../README.md)

## In one sentence
**Every error-correction question in this format breaks one of 16 rules — and the rules are ranked by how often they appear, so you can spend your time where the marks are.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- An item is built by taking a correct sentence and corrupting **one segment**. The corruption is almost always
  from a small, high-frequency family: agreement, articles, prepositions, tense.
- Knowing the families turns a vague "does this sound wrong?" judgement into a checklist of binary tests.
- The ranking below is the reason to learn some families deeply and others only lightly.

## Core concept

### The 16 families, ranked by frequency in reported items

| Rank | Family | The rule | Wrong → Right | Mini example |
|------|--------|---------|--------------|--------------|
| 1 | **Subject-verb agreement** | The verb must match the subject in number and person | "The list of items **are**" → "**is**" | Each of the modules **require** → **requires** |
| 2 | **Articles** | `a` = one of many, not specified; `an` = vowel *sound*; `the` = specific/already known | "**a** hour" → "**an** hour" | "**the** data is" ✅ (specific) vs "**a** data" ❌ |
| 3 | **Prepositions** | Fixed collocations: *discuss about* ❌, *discussed* ✅; *dependent on* ✅; *reach at* ❌ | "discuss **about**" → "discuss" | "prior **to**" ✅ vs "prior of" ❌ |
| 4 | **Tense consistency** | The sentence must stay in one time frame unless a shift is justified | "He said he **is** tired" → "**was**" | "Yesterday I **am** working" → "**was**" |
| 5 | **Pronoun agreement / antecedent** | A pronoun must match a real, singular, clearly identified antecedent | "The team **they**" → "**it**" | "Each student **submit** their own" → "**submits** … **their own**" (fine) but "The company **they**" → "**it**" |
| 6 | **Misplaced modifier** | A modifier must attach to the noun it describes | "The file **that I sent to Rahul yesterday** by email" | "**the file that I sent** to Rahul" ✅ |
| 7 | **Comma splice** | Two independent clauses cannot be joined by a comma alone | "The build failed, we rolled back." → "… failed; we rolled back." |
| 8 | **Double negative** | Standard formal English avoids two negatives | "I did **not** receive **no** reply." → "… did not receive **any** reply." |
| 9 | **since vs for** | `since` = a point in time; `for` = a period of time | "**for** two years" ✅ period; "**since** 2024" ✅ point |
| 10 | **who / which / that** | `who` = people, `which` = things, `that` = both (restrictive) | "the engineer **which**" → "**who**" |
| 11 | **fewer vs less** | `fewer` = countable, `less` = uncountable | "**less** errors" → "**fewer** errors" |
| 12 | **than vs then** | `than` = comparison, `then` = time/sequence | "**more** [than]" vs "**and then**" |
| 13 | **affect vs effect** | `affect` = verb, `effect` = noun | "It will **affect** the release." ✅ / "the **effect** on release" ✅ |
| 14 | **Redundant modifiers** | Don't say the same thing twice | "**absolutely essential**", "**free gift**", "**final conclusion**", "**past history**", "**future plans**", "**completely finished**" |
| 15 | **Infinitive vs gerund** | Some verbs take only an infinitive, some only a gerund | "I enjoy **to work**" → "**working**" | "I **suggest** doing" ✅ / "**suggest to do**" ❌ |
| 16 | **Miscellaneous high-frequency** | `its` vs `it's`, `their` vs `there`/`they're`, `your` vs `you're`, `then`/`than`, `loose`/`lose` | "**its** important" → "**it's** important" |

### The details that decide real items

**Subject-verb agreement — the four traps**

| Trap | Example |
|------|---------|
| Plural noun + intervening singular phrase | "The list of **items are** wrong." → "The list of items **is** wrong." |
| Indefinite pronoun + plural verb | "**Each** of the modules **require**…" → "**requires**" |
| Uncountable subject + plural verb | "The **information** **are** confidential." → "**is**" |
| Compound subject with a preposition | "The quality **of the** deliverables **are**…" → "**is**" — the head noun is "quality" |

**Articles — the four traps**

| Trap | Rule |
|------|------|
| `a` before a vowel letter but a consonant sound | "a hour" ❌ → "an hour" ✅ (silent h) |
| `an` before a consonant letter but a vowel sound | "an university" ❌ → "a university" ✅ |
| Missing article before a singular countable noun | "She is **engineer**." → "She is **an** engineer." |
| `the` for something already mentioned or unique | "the sun", "the manager I mentioned" ✅ |

**Prepositions — the collocations that get tested**

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right |
|---------|---------|
| discuss **about** | discuss / discuss it **with** you |
| emphasize **on** | emphasize |
| depend **of** | depend **on** |
| consists **of** | consists **of** ✅ / consists **in** |
| prior **to** | prior **to** ✅ |
| reason **for** | reason **for** ✅ |
| responsible **for** | responsible **for** ✅ |
| according **to** me | according **to** me ✅ |
| different **than** | different **from** |
| married **with** | married **to** |
| reach **at** | reach |
| provide **a** feedback | provide **feedback** (no article) |
| a **discussion** about / a **research** on | a discussion / research (uncountable, no article) |

**Tense consistency — the 4 shifts that are usually legitimate**

1. Reported speech back-shifts: "He said he **is** coming." → "He said he **was** coming."
2. A conditional: "If he **comes**, we **start**."
3. A timeless truth: "The sun **rises** in the east."
4. A time-clause contrast: "Although he **was** tired, he **continues** to work." (rare — treat as suspect)

Everything else in a single sentence is usually an error.

**Pronoun agreement**

| Situation | Rule |
|-----------|------|
| "The team **are**" (British) vs "the team **is**" (Indian English) | Both exist; Indian English uses "is" for a single team. Do not treat "the team are" as the error |
| A plural noun phrase with "of" | "The team of testers **are** ready" — head is "team" → "is" in Indian usage |
| "Everyone / each / somebody" | Singular verb: "Everyone **is** here." |
| "Company / organisation / team" as a single entity | Singular: "The company **has** announced…" |
| A possessive pronoun | "the company's **own** policy" vs "the company **itself**" |

**Misplaced modifiers — the three shapes**

| Shape | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Noun phrase modifier drifting | "The file **that I sent to Rahul yesterday** by email" → the modifier attaches to "file", so it must sit next to "file" |
| Adverb placed between verb and object | "She **carefully** reviewed the file" ✅ / "She reviewed **carefully** the file" ❌ |
| Prepositional phrase with a distant antecedent | "I sent the file **to Rahul for the meeting**" — ambiguous; fix with "for the meeting, I sent the file to Rahul" |

**Comma splice — the fix options**

| Broken | Fixed |
|--------|-------|
| "The build failed, we rolled back." | "The build failed**;** we rolled back." |
| "The build failed, we rolled back." | "The build failed**,** **and** we rolled back." |
| "The build failed, we rolled back." | "The build failed**. W**e rolled back." |
| "The build failed, we rolled back." | "**Because** the build failed, we rolled back." |

**Double negatives**

| Wrong (informal/standard-ESE) | Right |
|-------------------------------|-------|
| "I don't have no idea." | "I have no idea." / "I don't have any idea." |
| "He never said nothing." | "He said nothing." |
| "The report isn't not approved." | "The report isn't approved." |

**Exception:** "not only" is a legitimate correlative: "He is not only talented **but** also hardworking."

**since vs for**

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| `since` + a point | "since **Monday**", "since **2019**", "since **two hours ago**" ✅ |
| `for` + a period | "for **two hours**", "for **three years**", "for **a long time**" ✅ |
| `since` + a duration in informal use | "It's since **two hours**" — avoid; use "for" |

**who / which / that**

| Word | Use for | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| `who` | people | "the engineer **who** wrote it" |
| `which` | things | "the module **which** failed" |
| `that` | people or things, restrictive only | "the module **that** failed" ✅; a comma before "that" ❌ |

**fewer vs less**

| Countable | Uncountable |
|-----------|-------------|
| fewer errors, fewer people, fewer items | less time, less water, less information |

**than vs then**

| `than` | `then` |
|-------|--------|
| comparison: "better **than**", "more **than**" | sequence/time: "**then** we tested", "at that point **then**" |

**affect vs effect**

| Affect (verb) | Effect (noun) |
|---------------|---------------|
| "The delay **will affect** the release." | "The **effect** of the delay was…" |
| "Affects**:** the schedule." | "**Effect**s: schedule, cost." |
| "He was **affected** by the change." | "The change had a strong **effect**." |

**Redundant modifiers**

| Redundant ❌ | Correct ✅ |
|--------------|-----------|
| absolutely essential | essential |
| completely finished | finished |
| free gift | gift |
| final conclusion | conclusion |
| past history | history |
| future plans | plans |
| end result | result |
| new innovation | innovation |
| each and every | each / every |
| first and foremost | foremost |
| unexpected surprise | surprise |
| repeat again | repeat |
| unite together | unite |
| close proximity | proximity |

**Infinitive vs gerund**

| Verb + gerund only | Verb + infinitive only |
|--------------------|----------------------|
| enjoy, mind, avoid, finish, suggest, consider, imagine, risk, keep, miss, deny, admit, recommend | want, hope, decide, plan, intend, agree, offer, refuse, promise, learn, expect, manage, arrange, claim |

Also: `used to + infinitive`, `be used to + gerund`.

## Worked example

> **Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.**
>
> "The team of developers **has** agreed to release the module **on Friday**, **and the client have been informed** about the **change in the** **scope of the work**."
>
> A. The team of developers has
> B. and the client have been informed
> C. change in the
> D. scope of the work

**Answer: B**

| Segment | Verdict | Reason |
|---------|---------|--------|
| A | ✅ Correct | "The team of developers **has**" — the head noun of the subject is "team" (singular), and Indian English takes a singular verb with a unified team. |
| B | ❌ **Error** | The subject of this clause is "the client" — singular — so the verb must be "**has been informed**", not "have been informed". |
| C | ✅ Correct | "change in the" — the preposition and article are right: "change **in** the scope". |
| D | ✅ Correct | "scope of the work" — correct preposition chain; "of" links the second noun to the first. |

**Why this item is hard:** three of the four segments are perfectly correct in isolation, and the error is only
visible if you check the **whole clause** the segment belongs to. That is the standard construction of a good
error-correction item.

## Quick rules

- Find the finite verb before judging anything.
- The head noun of the subject controls the verb.
- The verb inside a relative clause belongs to that clause; judge it against its own subject.
- Article choice depends on **sound**, not spelling.
- Two independent clauses need more than a comma between them.
- "Its" (possessive) vs "it's" (it is); "their" (possessive/pronoun) vs "there" (place) vs "they're" (they are).
- "Each", "every", "everyone", "somebody" take **singular** verbs.
- One error only — if you have two candidates, you have mis-read a segment boundary.

## Common traps

- **Judging a segment in isolation.** A segment can only be judged in the context of the clause it sits in.
- **Treating "the team are" as an error.** In Indian English the verb is singular. The exam follows Indian usage.
- **Treating British/American vocabulary differences as errors** (travelled/traveled, organise/organize) — these
  are not grammar errors.
- **Flagging a long or unusual phrase.** Long ≠ wrong. Error-correction distractors are often long, formal,
  and perfectly grammatical.
- **Missing the error inside a relative clause** because the relative pronoun "hides" the subject.

## Checklist

- [ ] I located the finite verb of each clause before judging any segment.
- [ ] I checked subject-verb agreement for the head noun of every subject.
- [ ] I checked tense consistency across coordinated clauses.
- [ ] I did not flag a segment that was merely long or formal.
- [ ] I confirmed that exactly one segment is wrong before committing.
