> Section 2 · English & L&D · Error Correction ·  _Estimated reading time: 3 min_

## In one sentence
**Two files: the 16-family error taxonomy with rules and examples, and the anatomy of the four-segment item format.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Knowing the families turns "does this sound wrong?" into a checklist of binary tests.
- Knowing the format stops you reading the options before the sentence.

## Files

| File | Contents |
|------|----------|
| [`01-error-taxonomy.md`](01-error-taxonomy.md) | 16 families ranked by frequency, each with rule + wrong form + right form + example; agreement, article, preposition, tense, pronoun, modifier, comma, negative, since/for, who/which/that, fewer/less, than/then, affect/effect, redundancy, gerund/infinitive, its/it's |
| [`02-the-four-segment-format.md`](02-the-four-segment-format.md) | Item anatomy, how distractors are built, the verb-carrying-chunk heuristic, the 6 elimination tests, judging a chunk in context |

## Quick rules

- Exactly one chunk is wrong; if two look wrong, re-read the options.
- Test the verb-bearing chunks first.
- Correcting your chosen chunk must repair the whole sentence.

## Common traps

- Judging a chunk in isolation.
- Treating style as an error.
