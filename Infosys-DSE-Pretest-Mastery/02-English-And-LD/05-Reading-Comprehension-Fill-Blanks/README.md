# Reading Comprehension & Fill Blanks

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Reading Comprehension & Fill Blanks ·  _Estimated reading time: 6 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Section 2 overview](../README.md)

## In one sentence
**Reading comprehension in this section is one short passage of 100–150 words with 1–3 questions attached — and the whole thing is solved by mapping each question to a specific line of the passage, never by reading the options first.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Section 2's fastest sub-type **if** you are disciplined: 4–5 minutes for the whole passage, including all its
  questions.
- The passages are short and about **workplace, technology, or general knowledge** — never literature. You do not
  need background knowledge, and using any is a liability.
- The questions are almost always of 5 fixed types: main idea, detail, inference, vocabulary-in-context, and
  "why does the author say this". Learn the types and the answers become mechanical.

## Learning objectives

After this folder you should be able to:

- Run the **passage-mapping method**: label each paragraph with its function before reading the questions.
- Apply the **cloze deletion technique** for fill-in-the-blank items: delete the blank, then predict the missing
  word from the grammar and the topic.
- Recognise the **5 question types** and know what kind of evidence each one requires.
- Answer **inference** questions correctly — including the "too strong" trap.
- Handle **"why does the author say X"** questions with the writer's-intent method.
- Respect the **90-second rule per question**, and the 4–5 minute budget for the whole passage.

## Quick procedure — the passage in 4 phases

```
PHASE 1 (60s)  Read the passage once. Label each paragraph P1, P2, P3 and note the
               topic in 3 words. Do NOT read the options yet.
PHASE 2 (60s)  For each question, find the line(s) that contain the answer.
               Underline them. Then read the options.
PHASE 3 (45s)  Eliminate options that are not supported by the underlined lines.
PHASE 4 (30s)  For the survivor, check it against the question type's rule.
```

## The 5 question types at a glance

| Type | Stem wording | Evidence | Trap |
|------|--------------|----------|------|
| Main idea | "mainly about", "best title" | The whole passage, including the P2 twist | A true fact from one paragraph |
| Detail | "according to the passage" | One explicit line | An added claim |
| Inference | "it can be inferred" | Exactly one step of reasoning | Universals; outside knowledge |
| Vocabulary | "the word X means" | The sentence around the word | The dictionary sense |
| Intent | "why does the author mention" | The sentence's job in the paragraph | Restating the content |

## Key takeaways

- 🔥 **Read the passage first, options second.** Reading the options first turns the passage into a search for
  justification, which is slower and biases you.
- 🔥 **Every answer has a line.** If you cannot point to the line, you are inferring — and inference questions
  have rules; other questions do not.
- The **main idea** is the answer that the whole passage supports, not one paragraph's point.
- **Inference** questions need exactly one step of reasoning from the passage — no more.
- **"Too strong"** options ("always", "only", "never", "proves") are usually wrong.
- **"Why does the author…"** asks for the writer's purpose, not the fact.
- For **fill blanks**, predict the missing word from grammar and topic *before* you read the options.
- **Outside knowledge is a liability.** If the passage says it, use the passage.
