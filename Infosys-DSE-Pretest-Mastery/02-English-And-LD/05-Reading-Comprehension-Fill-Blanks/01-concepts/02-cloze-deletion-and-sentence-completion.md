# Cloze Deletion & Sentence Completion

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Reading Comprehension & Fill Blanks ·  _Estimated reading time: 8 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [The passage-mapping method](01-passage-mapping-method.md)

## In one sentence
**A fill-in-the-blank item is a grammar puzzle with a topic clue — predict the missing word from the grammar and the surrounding topic first, and only then read the options.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- The pre-test uses two variants: **sentence completion** (one blank, 4 word options) and **paragraph completion**
  (the last sentence of a paragraph, 4 options).
- Both reward the same move: read the sentence with the blank **deleted**, see what the grammar demands, and
  only then look at the options.
- Reading the options first produces the classic error: choosing the option that reads most smoothly in
  isolation, rather than the one that fits the **slot**.

## Core concept

### The 3-layer prediction

Before looking at any option, predict the missing word on three layers:

```
LAYER 1 — GRAMMAR (10s)
   What part of speech must the blank be? What number? What tense? What gender/number
   for the verb? Is the blank a subject or an object?

LAYER 2 — TOPIC (10s)
   What noun/verb/idea belongs in the world of this passage? (delivery? training?
   customer? deadline?)

LAYER 3 — COLLOCATION (10s)
   Which words habitually sit next to each other in English?
   "raise" + "concern" ✅ | "raise" + "question" ✅ | "raise" + "matter" ⚠️
   "meet" + "deadline" ✅ | "meet" + "satisfaction" ❌
```

Then read the options and pick the one that satisfies all three layers.

### Layer 1 — the grammar demands

| The blank is… | Look for | Example |
|---------------|----------|---------|
| the subject of the sentence | a noun / pronoun | "_____ the current backlog grew." → "The size of" |
| the object of a verb | a noun | "We reviewed _____." → "the estimates" |
| after an adjective | a noun | "a _____ review" → "thorough" |
| after a verb | a verb in the right form | "the team decided _____ the release" → "to postpone" |
| after "to" | an infinitive | "aims to _____ delivery" → "improve" |
| after a modal | a bare infinitive | "can _____ the risk" → "mitigate" |
| after a preposition | a noun | "responsible for _____" → "the schedule" |
| a plural noun | plural options only | "three _____" → "options" |

### Layer 3 — high-frequency collocations

| Verb | Natural partners |
|------|-----------------|
| raise | a concern, an issue, a question, a risk, a point |
| meet | a deadline, a target, a requirement, a need |
| address | an issue, a concern, a risk, a problem |
| make | a decision, an effort, progress, an assumption |
| take | a decision, responsibility, action, a risk |
| draw | a conclusion, an inference, a distinction |
| draw on | experience, research, data |
| carry out | a review, an analysis, a survey |
| set out | to explain, aims, goals |
| bridge | a gap, the divide |
| shed | light on |
| bear | in mind, responsibility, a resemblance |
| place | emphasis on, reliance on |
| come up with | an idea, a solution, a plan |

### Paragraph completion — the 4 checks

For the final sentence of a paragraph:

1. **Grammar** — does the option fit the blank grammatically (tense, person, number)?
2. **Reference** — does it correctly refer to the entities in the paragraph?
3. **Topic** — does it continue the paragraph's subject rather than jumping to a new one?
4. **Tone/register** — does it match the passage's level (formal passage → formal option)?

**The trap:** the option that is most *interesting* or most *specific* is often a topic-jump. Paragraph completion
wants the **boring, consistent** option.

### The 3 types of fill-in-the-blank items in this paper

| Type | Shape | What decides it |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| **Grammar cloze** | A sentence with one blank and 4 word forms | Part of speech + verb form |
| **Lexical cloze** | A sentence with one blank and 4 content words | Topic + collocation |
| **Summary cloze** | A paragraph with a missing final sentence | Reference + topic + tone |

### The elimination order

```
1. Delete the blank mentally and read the sentence for meaning.        (10s)
2. Cross out options with the wrong part of speech or number.          (10s)
3. Cross out options whose topic does not belong.                     (10s)
4. Cross out options with unnatural collocation.                     (10s)
5. Read the survivor in place. If it fits and reads naturally, take it. (10s)
```

## Worked example

> **Q.** Choose the word that best completes the sentence:
> "The team decided ______ the release of the payment module by two days in order to allow more time for
> regression testing."
>
> A. postponing
> B. to postpone
> C. postpone
> D. postponed

**Answer: B — to postpone.**

**Layer 1 — grammar:** "decided" is followed by a bare infinitive: `decide to + verb`. Options A (gerund) and D
(past participle) are both eliminated. Between B and C: "decided **postpone**" without "to" is wrong in standard
English ❌. B survives.

**Layer 2 — topic:** the module release is being moved later — "postpone" fits; the other verbs in the options
family would be "delaying/delayed" (a different sense).

**Layer 3 — collocation:** "decide to postpone" ✅ is the natural collocation. "Decide postponing" ❌.

**Confirmed by reading it in place:** "The team decided to postpone the release of the payment module by two
days…" — reads naturally and matches the reason clause "in order to allow more time for regression testing" ✅.

**A second worked example (lexical cloze):**

> **Q.** Choose the word that best completes the sentence:
> "The client's request for a change to the agreed scope could not be accommodated without ______ the delivery
> date by three days."
>
> A. advancing
> B. extending
> C. postponing
> D. delaying

**Answer: C — postponing.**

**Layer 1 — grammar:** "without" is followed by a **gerund**, so all four options survive this test
("advancing", "extending", "postponing", "delaying" are all gerund forms). This item cannot be solved on grammar
alone — which is exactly the design of a lexical cloze.

Layers 2 and 3 decide it:
- "advancing the delivery date" — wrong meaning ❌ (advancing means moving **earlier**).
- "extending the delivery date" — sounds plausible, but "extend a date" is not idiomatic; you **postpone** or
  **delay** a date ❌.
- "postponing the delivery date" — the standard collocation ✅.
- "delaying the delivery date" — "delay" is usually used with an event or a task ("delay the release"), not with
  "the delivery date" ❌.

**Confirmed in place:** "…could not be accommodated without postponing the delivery date by three days" — natural,
and it matches the intent implied by "in order to allow more time".

## Quick rules

- Delete the blank, then predict. Never read options first.
- Grammar kills most options; topic and collocation finish the job.
- For paragraph completion, the **boring and consistent** option is usually right.
- "decide/agree/hope/plan/manage + **to** + verb" ✅
- "without / by / of + **-ing**" ✅
- Check the **tense** of the surrounding sentence — if the paragraph is in the past, the option should be past.
- If two options survive all three layers, read the whole paragraph again and pick the one with better reference
  and tone.

## Common traps

- **Choosing the option that reads well on its own.** It must read well **in the sentence**.
- **Ignoring the verb form after "decided", "agreed", "suggested".**
- **Choosing a topic-jump in paragraph completion** because it is more specific.
- **Failing to notice the tense** of the passage.
- **Confusing "extend" and "postpone"** — both are "to make later", but only one collocates with "a date".

- [ ] I checked the part of speech and the verb form.
- [ ] I checked the topic and the collocation.
- [ ] I read the survivor in place and confirmed it fits.
