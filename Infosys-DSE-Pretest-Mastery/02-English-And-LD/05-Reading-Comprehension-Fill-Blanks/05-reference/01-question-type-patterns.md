# Question Type Patterns

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Reading Comprehension & Fill Blanks ·  _Estimated reading time: 5 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Reading traps](../04-pitfalls/01-reading-traps.md)

## In one sentence
**One page: every question type you will meet, the exact stem wording that signals it, what evidence it needs, and the signature of its wrong option.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Comprehension marks are lost almost entirely because the wrong evidence standard is applied.
- Identifying the type from the **stem wording alone** takes 5 seconds and removes most of that loss.

## Core concept

### The master table

| Type | Stem wording | Evidence required | Wrong-answer signature | Time |
|------|--------------|-------------------|-------------------------|------|
| **Main idea** | "mainly about", "central idea", "best title", "best describes", "summarises" | The whole passage, including the P2 twist | True but covers one paragraph only | 60s |
| **Title** | "The most suitable title" | Topic + turn of the passage | Too broad ("Life in organisations") or too narrow (one paragraph) | 45s |
| **Detail** | "According to the passage", "the author says", "which of the following is stated" | One explicit line | Adds a claim; or changes the quantifier | 45s |
| **Inference** | "It can be inferred", "the author implies", "one may conclude" | Exactly one step of reasoning | Universals; two-step reasoning; outside knowledge | 60s |
| **Vocabulary in context** | "the word X means", "X refers to", "the phrase is closest in meaning to" | The sentence containing the word | The dictionary sense rather than the contextual sense | 45s |
| **Writer's intent** | "why does the author mention", "in order to", "the purpose of" | The sentence's job in the paragraph | Restates the content instead of the purpose | 60s |
| **Tone / attitude** | "the tone of the passage", "the author's attitude towards X" | Adjectives in the passage's own language | Extremes ("highly optimistic" for a neutral passage) | 45s |
| **Structure** | "how is the passage organised", "what is the main function of paragraph 2" | Paragraph role (context / contrast / conclusion) | Swapping the roles of P2 and P3 | 45s |
| **Reference** | "the pronoun X refers to", "the phrase X stands for" | The nearest matching antecedent, matched in number and logic | The nearest noun by position rather than by meaning | 40s |
| **Fill blank** | "choose the word that best completes" | Grammar → topic → collocation | Reads well in isolation, wrong in the slot | 60s |

### Paragraph-role quick reference

| Role | Signal phrases | Typical question |
|------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Context / definition** | "In recent years", "X is defined as", "At many organisations" | Main idea, "what is X" |
| **Development / contrast** | "However", "In practice", "Despite", "What matters is" | Inference, intent, the key detail |
| **Illustration / example** | "For example", "Consider", "A recent survey" | Why the author mentions it |
| **Conclusion / recommendation** | "Therefore", "Organisations should", "The practical consequence" | Main idea, recommendation, tone |
| **Caveat / qualification** | "This does not mean", "Not all", "provided that" | Trap questions — the caveat is the answer |

### Stem-wording → action

```
"mainly about / central idea"     → cover P1 + P2, run the 3-test
"according to the passage"         → find the line, do not infer
"it can be inferred"               → one step, reject universals
"why does the author"              → purpose, not content
"the word X most probably means"   → sense used in this sentence
"the most suitable title"          → topic + turn, not one paragraph
"the passage is mainly concerned"  → same as main idea
"which of the following is NOT"    → find the one the passage contradicts
```

### The "NOT" question

The reverse items are easier, and often safer marks:

1. Find the passage line for each option.
2. Three options will be supported; one will be contradicted or unmentioned.
3. **Unmentioned is enough** — you do not need a contradiction, only the absence of support.

Common trap: picking the option that is "slightly off" instead of the one the passage never mentions. Prefer the
option that is **clearly absent**.

### Tone — the intensity ladder

```
neutral <----------------------------------> extreme
unconcerned   measured   mildly critical   strongly critical   hostile
```

The passage's own adjectives set the level. If the passage says "a serious concern", the tone is **serious**,
not **hostile** and not **casual**. When two tone options are both plausible, choose the **less extreme** one.

### A 30-second self-check for every answer

- [ ] I can point to the evidence line.
- [ ] The answer type matches the question type.
- [ ] No universal quantifier unless the passage is explicit.
- [ ] No claim that the passage does not make.
- [ ] On inference items, exactly one step.

## Worked example

> **Q.** "The author mentions the discovery of sleep cycles in order to:" — identify the type and the evidence.

**Type:** writer's intent ("in order to"). **Not** a detail question, even though the fact itself is in the
passage.

**Evidence:** the sentence "That account has been undermined by the discovery of sleep cycles, during which the
brain is measurably active and memory is consolidated."

**Job of the sentence in the paragraph:** it is the *contrast* — it undermines P1's traditional account. Its
purpose is to replace a passive view of sleep with an active one.

**Trap:** an option that says "to show that sleep length matters more than quality" ❌ (a new claim), or one
that restates "the brain is active during sleep" ❌ (content, not purpose).

**Correct shape:** "to challenge the traditional view that sleep is a passive state" ✅.

## Quick rules

- Identify the type from the stem wording before you read the options.
- Main idea covers the twist; detail needs an explicit line; inference needs one step.
- "NOT" items: the answer is the option the passage never mentions.
- For tone, pick the less extreme option when two are plausible.
- Fill blanks: grammar → topic → collocation.

## Common traps

- Applying a detail-question standard to an inference question (or vice versa).
- Choosing an extreme tone option.
- Treating "NOT" items as if the answer must be contradicted rather than merely absent.
- Ignoring the caveat paragraph when the question is about a generalisation.

## Checklist

- [ ] I identified the question type from the stem wording.
- [ ] I know what evidence that type requires.
- [ ] I can state the wrong-answer signature for that type.
- [ ] I ran the 30-second self-check.
