# Solved Etiquette Questions

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Workplace Professionalism & Etiquette ·  _Estimated reading time: 10 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [The etiquette decision check](../02-techniques/02-the-etiquette-decision-check.md)

## In one sentence
**Seven fully solved workplace-conduct items — email, meeting, punctuality, confidentiality, code of conduct, phone conduct, and a follow-up — with a reason for every rejected option.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- These seven cover the conduct items that appear in almost every batch.
- The distractors are deliberately *close*: each wrong option is only wrong in one specific way. Learning to name
  that one way is the whole skill.

## Core concept

Labels used below: **RULE** (breaks a written rule), **LAZY** (avoids effort/confrontation), **DISRESPECT**,
**SLOPPY** (mechanical flaw), **DISRUPTIVE**, **RIGHT**.

## Worked example

### Q1. The email with no ask

> **Q.** You need your team lead to approve a revised plan by 4 p.m. today. Which email is the most professional?
>
> A. "Dear Sir, please find attached the client's request for a change. It will take 3 extra days. Please
>    advise. Thanks & Regards, Ananya."
> B. "Dear Sir, the client has asked for an extra feature that will take 3 more days. I have attached their mail.
>    Please advise. Regards, Ananya."
> C. "The client has asked for an extra feature that will take 3 more days. I have attached their mail. I need
>    your decision on whether we absorb the delay or renegotiate the date by 3 days, so that I can reply to the
>    client by 5 p.m. today."
> D. "Hi sir, the client wants some more work done, sorry for the delay this is causing us, let me know what
>    to do, thanks."

**Answer: C** — it names the decision, the two options, and the deadline. A and B never state the ask; D is
informal and vague.

---

### Q2. The meeting versus the task

> **Q.** You have an important review meeting at 3 p.m. The task in front of you needs 3 hours to finish and
> will be late otherwise. What should you do?
>
> A. Attend the meeting as scheduled; before it starts, hand over the task to a colleague with a clear status
>    note, or split the task and complete the remaining part after the meeting if the dependency allows.
> B. Skip the meeting because the task is more urgent and tell the team after the meeting what happened.
> C. Attend the meeting but keep checking your phone for the task's status during the discussion.
> D. Ask your team lead to cancel the meeting, since the task is more important.

**Answer: A**

| Option | Label | Failure |
|--------|-------|---------|
| A | ✅ RIGHT | Protects both the meeting and the task, and hands over with a written brief. |
| B | DISRUPTIVE / LAZY | Skipping a scheduled review damages the team and hides a real dependency risk. "More urgent" is not your call alone. |
| C | DISRESPECT / DISRUPTIVE | Multi-tasking in a review meeting; also, an absent task owner cannot be monitored by phone. |
| D | LAZY | Escalates a personal scheduling conflict as if it were a project decision, and asks a manager to cancel a meeting that others depend on. |

---

### Q3. Arriving late to a client meeting

> **Q.** You will be 15 minutes late for a client meeting because of an unavoidable delay in transit. What is
> the most appropriate behaviour?
>
> A. Send a brief message to the client and the organiser now stating that you are running 15 minutes late due
>    to an unavoidable delay, apologise, and give a firm revised arrival time.
> B. Hurry to the office and join the meeting as soon as you reach the desk, without mentioning the delay.
> C. Attend the meeting, apologise at the start for the delay, and explain in detail why it happened.
> D. Ask a colleague to join in your place and inform the client that you are unavailable.

**Answer: A**

| Option | Label | Failure |
|--------|-------|---------|
| A | ✅ RIGHT | Early, brief, factual, with a firm new ETA. The client can plan around it. |
| B | DISRESPECT | The client waits without knowing why. Apparent dishonesty is worse than the delay. |
| C | DISRESPECT | Apologising at the start is too late — the information arrives when it cannot be acted on. Also a long explanation turns a delay into a monologue. |
| D | LAZY | The colleague does not have the context for the meeting; the client is not left with a decision-maker. |

**Key distinction:** A communicates **before** the impact; C communicates **after** it.

### Q4. The confidentiality request

> **Q.** A colleague asks you to send them a copy of the client dataset you are working on, so that they can use
> it for a personal portfolio project. What should you do?
>
> A. Send it, since the data is only used internally and your colleague is a trusted person.
> B. Decline politely, explain that the client's data is confidential and that sharing it outside the authorised
>    team would breach the code of conduct, and offer to help them build the portfolio project on a public or
>    synthetic dataset.
> C. Send a small sample of the data so that the risk is low.
> D. Ask the client whether you may share the data with your colleague.

**Answer: B**

| Option | Label | Failure |
|--------|-------|---------|
| A | RULE | A breach of client confidentiality and the code of conduct. "Internal" is not the test — the authorisation is. |
| B | ✅ RIGHT | Refuses, cites the reason, offers a compliant alternative. |
| C | RULE | A partial breach is still a breach. Data minimisation does not authorise an unauthorised copy. |
| D | RULE / LAZY | The client does not authorise your colleague. Asking the client does not make the sharing routine — and the default in any organisation is that client data is not shared without a formal, documented approval. |

---

### Q5. The mandatory training

> **Q.** A mandatory "Information Security" session is scheduled. You have already read the policy and believe
> the session will be repetitive. What should you do?
>
> A. Skip it — the content repeats what you already know.
> B. Attend it; if you have a genuine conflict, inform your manager in advance and request a rescheduled slot or
>    the online module if the policy offers one.
> C. Attend but leave once the content repeats.
> D. Ask a colleague who attended to summarise it and mark your attendance on that basis.

**Answer: B** — attendance is the control. A, C, and D all leave a compliance gap or falsify a record.

---

### Q6. The phone in the meeting

> **Q.** You are in a project review with 8 people. Your phone rings and you can see it is a personal call. What
> is the most appropriate action?
>
> A. Answer it quietly and step out of the room for a moment.
> B. Decline the call, keep the phone silent and face down on the table, and take the call afterwards from a
>    private area if it is genuinely urgent.
> C. Answer it in the meeting because it might be important.
> D. Ask the meeting owner to pause so you can take the call.

**Answer: B**

| Option | Label | Failure |
|--------|-------|---------|
| A | DISRUPTIVE | Leaving mid-meeting for a personal call in an 8-person review is a visible disruption, and "quietly" does not work in a small room. |
| B | ✅ RIGHT | Protects the meeting and the privacy of the caller. |
| C | DISRESPECT / DISRUPTIVE | Answering in the meeting is the worst version — it is audible to everyone and shows no consideration. |
| D | DISRUPTIVE | Interrupting the chair to make the meeting serve your personal call inverts the priority. |

---

### Q7. After the meeting

> **Q.** You attended a client requirements meeting with 6 stakeholders. Two decisions were taken and three
> action items were assigned. What is the most appropriate next step?
>
> A. Nothing — the meeting is over; the minutes will be circulated by the project manager.
> B. Send a written summary within 24 hours listing the decisions, the actions, the owners, and the due dates,
>    and ask the recipients to correct anything inaccurate.
> C. Update only your own task tracker with your own action items.
> D. Send a short message to the client thanking them for their time.

**Answer: B**

| Option | Label | Failure |
|--------|-------|---------|
| A | LAZY | Assuming someone else will document it. If nobody does, the actions are lost — and professional behaviour does not depend on who else is paying attention. |
| B | ✅ RIGHT | Closes the loop in writing with owners and dates, and invites correction. |
| C | LAZY | Personal task tracking is not a shared record. The other owners and the client have no visibility. |
| D | DISRUPTIVE (as a substitute for a summary) | A thank-you note is polite, but it does not record the actions. It is the incomplete version of the professional answer. |

**Pattern:** the professional option always **creates a record and a closure**, not just a feeling.

## Quick rules

- The professional option in conduct items contains **two things**: an action *and* a communication.
- Never leave a client without an acknowledgement, even on leave.
- Never answer a policy question by doing your own thing.
- Timing beats volume: communicate **before** the impact when you can.
- After any meeting with actions, write the actions down with owners and dates.
- A thank-you note is not a substitute for a summary.
- Decline + explain + offer an alternative is the professional formula for a refused request.

## Common traps

- **The polite-but-useless option** (D in Q3, D in Q7): it looks professional and does nothing operational.
- **The efficient-but-non-compliant option** (A in Q5).
- **The helpful-but-breaching option** (A in Q4).
- **The over-communicating option**: broadcasting a client's request into a team group.
- **Forgetting the 24-hour follow-up.** Many candidates assume the project manager will write the minutes.

## Checklist

- [ ] I checked the timing of the communication — before or after the impact?
- [ ] My chosen option protects the client.
- [ ] My chosen option does not break a written policy.
- [ ] My chosen option treats every person with dignity.
- [ ] My chosen option creates a record or a next step.
