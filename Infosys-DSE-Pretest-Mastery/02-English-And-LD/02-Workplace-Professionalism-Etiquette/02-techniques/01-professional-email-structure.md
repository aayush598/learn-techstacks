# Professional Email Structure

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Workplace Professionalism & Etiquette ·  _Estimated reading time: 7 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Email & written communication etiquette](../01-concepts/01-email-and-written-communication-etiquette.md)

## In one sentence
**Write the email in five blocks — subject, greeting, context, action, close — and you will never leave out the part the exam is checking: the ask.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- "Write a professional email to X about Y" items ask for two things at once: **form** (professional structure)
  and **content** (the right ask, the right recipient, the right timing).
- Options in these items typically differ on **what you would include**. The one that includes a clear ask, a
  deadline, and a professional tone is the answer.
- Even in a multiple-choice item, writing the structure in your head first prevents you from being seduced by an
  option that is short but incomplete.

## Core concept

### The 5-block template

```
SUBJECT:  <Topic> — <What you need>
GREET:    Dear <Name/Role>,
CONTEXT:  <1–2 lines: what, when, impact>
ACTION:   <What you need, from whom, by when>
CLOSE:    Thank you. <Next step you will take.>
NAME
```

### The 15-point pre-send checklist

| # | Check | Fail example |
|---|-------|--------------|
| 1 | Subject is specific and under ~10 words | `URGENT!!` |
| 2 | Greeting matches the formality of the reader | `Hey Ananya` to a client |
| 3 | The point is in the first two lines | Point buried in paragraph 4 |
| 4 | Facts are separated from the ask | Blended into a wall of text |
| 5 | There is exactly one primary ask | Three unrelated asks |
| 6 | The ask has a deadline | "Requesting your approval" (when?) |
| 7 | Attachments are attached | "Please find attached" with nothing attached |
| 8 | File names are clear and versioned | `final_final_v3.docx` |
| 9 | No typos in names, dates, and figures | `Dear Ramash` |
| 10 | No CAPS used for emphasis | `PLEASE APPROVE` |
| 11 | No sarcasm, no passive aggression | "As per my last mail (twice)" |
| 12 | No blame directed at a named person | "Rahul's delay has caused…" |
| 13 | Recipients and CC are deliberate | Reply-all by accident |
| 14 | It contains nothing personal | Gossip, job-search, complaints |
| 15 | Sent within the response window | Replied 6 days later with no apology |

### Block-by-block rules with examples

**1. Subject**

```
Good: Approval needed — reconciliation module release plan (by 4 p.m. today)
Good: Status: UAT sign-off delayed to 18 March
Bad:  Please help
Bad:  FW: FW: RE: RE: query
```

**2. Greeting**

```
To a senior / client:  Dear Sir/Madam,  |  Dear Ms. Iyer,
To a peer:             Hi Ananya,  |  Hello Ananya,
Never:                 Hey / Hi sir / Dear sir/madam/team
```

**3. Context — inverted pyramid**

Put the conclusion first, the detail after.

```
Good: The reconciliation module will miss Friday's release date by two days. The cause is
      the unresolved mapping for the legacy currency table. Details below.
```

**4. Action — the part candidates forget**

```
Good: Requesting your approval for Option 2 — release the two minor fixes on Friday and
      move the calculation fix to the 28 March release. Please confirm by 4 p.m. today
      so that we can notify the client within the day.
```

**5. Close**

```
Good: Thank you for your guidance. I will confirm the release plan to the team by 6 p.m.
      regardless of the decision, so that the client is informed in time.
```

### 4 ready templates

**Template 1 — Request for approval**

```
Subject: Approval needed — <project/item> <decision> (by <time>)

Dear <Name>,

<One-line status: what is the situation and why it matters.>
<Two lines of detail: what was attempted, what the options are, what each costs.>

Requesting your approval for <Option X> because <reason>. Please confirm by <time> so
that <next step> can proceed.

Thank you for your guidance.

Regards,
<Name>
<Role> | <Team>
```

**Template 2 — Apology / owning an error**

```
Subject: Correction to <item> — impact and recovery plan

Dear <Name>,

I am writing to inform you that <specific thing> was incorrect/missed by me. The impact is
<impact on the client, the team, or the date>.
<What I have already done and what I propose to do next.>

I will <verification step> and confirm by <time>. I have also updated <tracker/doc> so the
record is accurate.

Regards,
<Name>
```

**Template 3 — Scheduling / rescheduling**

```
Subject: Reschedule request — <meeting name> on <date>

Dear <Name>,

I am writing to request that the <meeting> currently scheduled for <original time> be moved
to <proposed options>, because <short factual reason>.
This would let me <benefit> and still meet <commitment>.

Kindly let me know which slot works for you. I will update the invite once confirmed.

Regards,
<Name>
```

**Template 4 — Follow-up after a meeting**

```
Subject: Summary and actions — <meeting name>, <date>

Dear <all attendees>,

Thank you for the discussion. Below is a summary of the decisions and the agreed actions.

1. <Decision> — Owner: <name> — Due: <date>
2. <Decision> — Owner: <name> — Due: <date>
3. <Decision> — Owner: <name> — Due: <date>

Open questions: <question> (owner: <name>).
Please correct anything I have summarised inaccurately.

Regards,
<Name>
```

## Worked example

> **Q.** You must inform your manager that a client has requested a change that will add 3 days to the
> delivery. Which email is the most professional?
>
> A. "Dear Sir, please find attached the client's request for a change. It will take 3 extra days. Please
>    advise. Thanks & Regards, Ananya."
> B. "Dear Sir, the client has asked for an extra feature that will take 3 more days. I have attached their
>    mail. Please advise. Regards, Ananya."
> C. "The client has asked for an extra feature that will take 3 more days. I have attached their mail. I need
>    your decision on whether we absorb the delay or renegotiate the date by 3 days, so that I can reply to the
>    client by 5 p.m. today."
> D. "Hi sir, the client wants some more work done, sorry for the delay this is causing us, let me know what
>    to do, thanks."

**Answer: C**

| Option | Failure |
|--------|---------|
| A | Correct-ish skeleton but no decision requested and no deadline — "please advise" hands the problem back. It also says "please find attached" with nothing attached. |
| B | Facts only, still **no ask and no deadline**. The manager has to work out what is being requested from him. |
| C | ✅ Professional structure compressed: the status, the impact, the evidence, the **two options**, and the **explicit ask with a deadline**. |
| D | Casual register to a manager, apologises for the client's request as if it were her own failure, and does not state the ask. |

**What made C the answer:** it names the **decision needed** and the **time by which it is needed**. That is the
difference between informing and communicating.

## Quick rules

- One email, one primary ask, one deadline.
- Conclusion first, evidence after.
- Attach the evidence and name it.
- Match formality to the most senior reader.
- No caps, no blame, no passive aggression, no personal content.
- Follow up in writing after any meeting that produced actions.
- Never send a mail that says "please advise" without saying what advice you need.

## Common traps

- **"Please find attached" with no attachment** — the most common hard error in these options.
- **The ask buried in the last line.** Busy readers stop at line three.
- **Three asks in one mail.** Split them; mark the priority.
- **Over-long context.** If the mail is more than ~150 words, you have probably put the background before the point.
- **Tone that is too casual** to a senior or a client.

## Checklist

- [ ] Subject: specific, ~10 words, names the ask.
- [ ] Greeting matches the reader's seniority.
- [ ] First two lines carry the point.
- [ ] Exactly one primary ask with a deadline.
- [ ] Attachments attached, named, referenced.
- [ ] No caps, no blame, no personal content, deliberate CC list.
