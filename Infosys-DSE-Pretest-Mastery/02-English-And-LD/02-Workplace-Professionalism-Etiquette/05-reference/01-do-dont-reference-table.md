# Do / Don't Reference Table

## In one sentence
**One page, twelve contexts, do-versus-don't — the fastest way to settle any conduct question you have not seen before.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Etiquette items are situational, so you cannot memorise them all. You can memorise the **norms**, and the norms
  resolve almost every item.
- This page is built to be printed. Read it the night before the test; do not read it while the clock is running.
- Karthik's retrospective note: "I did not need to know the etiquette rules for every situation. I needed the ten
  norms underneath them."

## Core concept

### The master do / don't table

| Context | ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **Email subject** | Name the topic and the ask | "URGENT", "Help", "FYI" |
| **Email body** | Put the point in the first 2 lines; one ask with a deadline | Bury the ask; three asks at once |
| **Email tone** | Match formality to the most senior reader | Casual register to a senior or client |
| **Email recipients** | Deliberate To/Cc; CC only for awareness | Reply-all; CC to pressure someone |
| **Attachments** | Attach, name clearly, reference in the body | "Please find attached" with nothing attached |
| **Replies** | Acknowledge within 2 working days; give a revert time | Silent for a week; "as per my last mail" |
| **Meetings** | Arrive 5–10 minutes early; agenda read; phone silent | Late; phone on the table; interrupting |
| **Meetings** | Contribute with facts; disagree with data, not attitude | Dominating; silent on your own topic; public arguments |
| **Meetings** | Disagree privately after the meeting if not on the agenda | Campaigning against a decision to peers |
| **Meetings — follow-up** | Written summary with actions, owners, dates, within 24 h | Assuming someone else will do it |
| **Calls / video** | Silent device; quiet location; camera on; mute when not speaking | Answering in a meeting; eating or walking on a call |
| **Client calls** | Clarify requirements; agree the next step in writing | Guessing; promising timelines you cannot keep |
| **Punctuality** | Arrive early; if late, inform with a firm ETA | Turning up late; explaining at the door instead of before |
| **Dress — office day** | Follow the written dress code | Following the casual habit of your team |
| **Dress — client day** | Business formal, ID badge visible | Office casual on a client day |
| **Grooming** | Clean, tidy, badge above the waist | Slippers on the work floor; strong fragrance |
| **Confidentiality — client data** | Authorised team, authorised channel, authorised device only | Personal email, personal laptop, personal drive, WhatsApp |
| **Confidentiality — colleague** | Do not discuss personal or performance issues | Gossip, even kindly, even in the washroom |
| **Confidentiality — request** | Decline, explain the rule, offer a compliant alternative | "Just this once", "only a small sample" |
| **Policy — training** | Attend; request a reschedule or the online route in advance | Skip it, leave early, or sign for someone else |
| **Policy — ID badge** | Wear it visibly; never lend it | Badges on the desk; lending to a visitor |
| **Policy — company machines** | Work only on company systems for company work | Personal mail, personal browsing, side projects, pirated software |
| **Policy — social media** | Keep client, project, and company-identifying content out of posts | Posting dashboards, screenshots, client names |
| **Policy — reporting** | Report errors and breaches promptly through the channel | Concealing; silently fixing; posting about it online |
| **Office hours** | Be reachable in your declared hours; block focus time | Expecting instant replies at 11 p.m. |
| **Personal work** | Use personal devices and personal time | Company assets for personal errands |
| **Conflict with a senior** | Rationale in private, then commit to the decision | Public argument; ignoring and doing it your way |
| **Grievances** | Use the formal HR / ethics channel | Team chat, a friend's personal email, complaining to colleagues |
| **Mistakes** | Own it, state impact, propose recovery, verify | Explaining why it was not your fault |

### The 10 norms under every row

1. **Be on time** — arrive 5–10 minutes early.
2. **Respect the hierarchy** — escalate through the line of reporting.
3. **Protect the client** — client impact outranks personal convenience.
4. **Protect confidentiality** — data, information, and colleague privacy.
5. **Follow the written rule** — even when it is inconvenient or you already know the content.
6. **Communicate early** — before the impact, not after.
7. **One ask, one owner, one deadline** — no silent requests.
8. **Record it** — written, so it survives forwarding and memory.
9. **No public correction** — private, factual, specific feedback.
10. **Own your errors** — impact, recovery, prevention.

### Quick facts worth memorising

| Fact | Value |
|------|-------|
| Arrival buffer | 5–10 minutes early |
| Acknowledgement window for normal work items | 1 working day |
| Maximum silent period on any work item | 2 working days |
| Meeting summary deadline | 24 hours |
| Response to a client emergency | Immediately, and by phone as well as mail |
| IDs | Badge visible at all times on the work floor |
| Company data on personal devices | Never |
| Mandatory training | Attended, or rescheduled through the process in advance |

## Worked example

> **Q.** Which behaviour best demonstrates professionalism in an office setting?
>
> A. Arriving 10 minutes before a meeting so that you can review your notes on the way in.
> B. Answering a colleague's question while walking in the corridor, since it saves both of you time.
> C. Closing your laptop only after the office shutdown announcement ends.
> D. Forwarding every interesting article to the team group so that everyone stays informed.

**Answer: A**

| Option | Why it fails |
|--------|--------------|
| A | ✅ Prepared, punctual, and considerate of the meeting's start. |
| B | A corridor conversation is neither private nor focused; if it involves client or project matters, it is a confidentiality risk as well as a distraction. |
| C | Padding the last few minutes. The announcement marks the end of the working day, not the end of your effort. |
| D | Information overload for everyone and an unowned use of a shared channel. "Informed" is not the goal; **relevant** is. |

## Quick rules

- Ten norms, not twenty situations. The norms decide the situations.
- "Everyone does it" is a red flag, not a justification.
- Professional = **on time, informed, documented, respectful, compliant**.
- When two options are both compliant, pick the one that **communicates**.
- Arrival buffer 5–10 min · acknowledgement ≤ 1 working day · summary ≤ 24 h.

## Common traps

- Choosing the option that shows **effort** over the option that shows **process**.
- Choosing an option that solves a **personal** problem rather than a **work** problem.
- Forgetting that the professional option often requires a **small extra step** (a summary, a confirmation, a
  written note).
