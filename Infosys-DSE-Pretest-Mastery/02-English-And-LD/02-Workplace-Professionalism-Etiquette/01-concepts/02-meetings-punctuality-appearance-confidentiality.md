# Meetings, Punctuality, Appearance & Confidentiality

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Workplace Professionalism & Etiquette ·  _Estimated reading time: 8 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Email & written communication etiquette](01-email-and-written-communication-etiquette.md)

## In one sentence
**Meeting etiquette, punctuality, grooming, and confidentiality are four separate test topics that share one rule — professionalism is what you owe the work when no one is watching.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- "You have an important client meeting at 3 p.m. and your current task will take 3 hours" is a recurring
  punctuality-and-prioritisation item. So is "the meeting starts at 3, you arrive at 3:15".
- Confidentiality items test whether you know that **client data and colleague information are both protected**,
  and that the protection cuts both ways.
- Grooming and dress-code items look trivial but appear regularly in Indian pre-tests, usually in the form of
  "a new joiner arrives on day one in jeans and a T-shirt" or "a client visits and a developer comes to work in
  casual clothes".

## Core concept

### A. Meeting conduct

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Punctuality | Arrive **5–10 minutes early**. Joining a call 2 minutes late is normal; joining a client meeting 10 minutes late is not |
| Preparation | Read the agenda / invite; bring the data you are expected to present |
| Devices | Silent, face down. Take notes on paper unless notes are expected to be shared |
| Contribution | Speak when you have something relevant; do not dominate; do not stay silent on a topic you own |
| Disagreement | Raise it in the meeting if it is on the agenda, with facts; otherwise take it after with the owner |
| Interruptions | Do not interrupt. If you must, say "May I add something here?" |
| Phone calls | Take them outside. Never scroll during someone's presentation |
| Feedback | About people, always privately. About work, in the meeting |
| Minutes | Send a written summary within 24 hours if you convened or took the notes |
| Ending | Leave when the agenda is done. Do not linger in the room |
| Recording | Never record a meeting without the explicit consent of the participants |

**The 3-question meeting test:** Is the behaviour **relevant**, **respectful**, and **done at the right level**?
If any is no, it is a distractor.

### B. Punctuality and prioritisation

| Scenario | Professional answer |
|----------|--------------------|
| Meeting starts 3 p.m.; your current task needs 3 hours | Reschedule or hand over the task with the status and a clear brief; attend the meeting. If the task is higher priority than the meeting, confirm with your manager in advance — do not decide unilaterally |
| You will be 15 minutes late to a client meeting | Inform the organiser as early as possible, with a reason and a firm new ETA. Do not just turn up late |
| The meeting has no agenda | Request one in advance; if none arrives, prepare your own status note |
| Someone is dominating the discussion | Contribute factually when you have something; raise it with the chair afterwards if needed |
| A decision is made that you think is wrong | Note the concern, follow the decision, take it up with the decision owner after the meeting with a rationale |

⚠️ The trap in this archetype is "skip the meeting to finish the task". The professional answer almost always
**keeps the meeting** (it was scheduled by someone with more context) and **delegates or rescopes the task**.

### C. Dress code and grooming

| Context | Expectation |
|---------|-------------|
| Regular office day | Company-prescribed business casual; neat, ironed, closed shoes at the work floor |
| Client visit or demo | Full business formal |
| Remote / WFH | Neat top, plain or virtual background, camera on unless asked off |
| Grooming | Clean, trimmed, hygienic; no strong fragrance; ID badge visible above the waist |
| ❌ Never | Slippers at the desk on the work floor, dirty shoes, casual wear during a client visit, sagging clothing, visible tattoos if the policy prohibits them |

**The exam's framing:** the question asks which behaviour is *appropriate for a new joiner on day one*. The
answer is "follow the company's dress code and the client-visit convention". The distractor is "dress as you
like; it does not matter" or "wear the most casual thing you own because the office is relaxed".

### D. Phone and video-call etiquette

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Ring on silent | Always, in every meeting and every floor area |
| Camera on | For internal meetings with a professional expectation; off only for personal reasons with notice |
| Mute when not speaking | Prevents background noise; also expected in large calls |
| Join from a quiet place | Not a corridor, not a cafeteria, not a vehicle |
| Video on | Dress appropriately; check lighting and background before joining |
| No multi-tasking | Do not eat, walk, or read other documents during a call |
| Calls in public areas | Take them in a designated area; do not discuss client or project details where others can hear |
| Ring-back etiquette | If a colleague's phone rings in a meeting, the owner should apologise briefly and step out |

### E. Confidentiality — the four tiers

| Tier | Examples | Rule |
|------|----------|------|
| **1. Client confidential** | Client data, contracts, code, credentials, source data, pricing | Never discuss outside the authorised team. Never on personal devices, personal email, or social media. Never in public areas |
| **2. Company confidential** | Internal processes, deployment plans, salary structures, training material | Not for external disclosure; sharing internally only on a need-to-know basis |
| **3. Personal confidential** | Medical leave reasons, personal issues, a colleague's performance | Not shared without consent, even in a friendly way |
| **4. Public** | Published product features, public announcements, tech talks | No restriction, but still communicate professionally |

**The five-question confidentiality test:**
1. Am I authorised to know this? 2. Am I authorised to share it? 3. With whom? 4. In which channel?
5. Would the client be comfortable if they knew I shared it this way?

⚠️ **Confidentiality cuts both ways.** Two classic items:
- *You saw a colleague's confidential data — what do you do?* → Do not read further, do not forward, do not
  discuss; inform the data owner or your lead.
- *A colleague asks you to share client data with them for a personal project — what do you do?* → Politely
  decline, explain it breaches the client's confidentiality and the code of conduct, and offer to help them
  through the proper route (a sanitised dataset via the data owner).

### F. Follow-up and office hours

| Practice | Professional standard |
|----------|----------------------|
| After a meeting | Send a written summary with actions, owners, and dates — within 24 hours |
| After an interview / training | Thank-you mail within 24 hours |
| Outside office hours | Do not expect responses; do not send non-urgent mail that demands an instant reply |
| Availability | Be reachable during your declared working hours; block focus time in a shared calendar |
| Working during breaks | If the office is open, treat it as work time |
| Leaving early | Inform your manager; do not slip away |

## Worked example

> **Q.** You are a new joiner. On your first day, you learn that a client will visit the office next week and
> that the dress code for client days is business formal. Your teammates wear T-shirts and jeans daily. What is
> the most appropriate response?
>
> A. Continue wearing T-shirts and jeans since that is what your team does, and change only on the client day.
> B. Read the company dress code and follow it, and for the client day follow the business-formal
>    convention, regardless of what teammates wear day to day.
> C. Ask your team lead to clarify what you should wear each day until you are sure.
> D. Wear the most conservative possible outfit every day so that you never risk being wrongly dressed.

**Answer: B**

| Option | Failure |
|--------|---------|
| A | Follows the visible practice rather than the stated policy. A new joiner's appearance is judged individually; the team does not set your standard. |
| B | ✅ Reads the rule, follows the rule, and applies the stricter convention for the client-facing day. |
| C | Reasonable as a courtesy, but it asks someone else to do the work of reading the policy. A professional reads the policy themselves and then confirms anything genuinely unclear. |
| D | Over-correction. Wearing formal every day when the norm is business casual looks out of place and can read as not understanding the organisation. |

**The exam's pattern here:** the right answer is the one that says **follow the stated rule and adjust for the
client context**. Neither "ignore the rule because of the team" nor "over-apply it" is right.

## Quick rules

- Arrive **5–10 minutes early** to anything.
- Never skip a scheduled meeting to finish a task; delegate or rescope the task instead.
- Confine client data to authorised people, authorised channels, and authorised devices.
- Confidentiality protects **colleagues' information** too — no gossip about anyone's personal or performance
  issues.
- Follow the **written** dress code, and go one step more formal for client-facing days.
- No personal work, no personal browsing, no personal calls on company systems during work hours.
- Record nothing without consent; always write down what was agreed.
- Silence on a mistake is a breach; silence on a policy question is a judgement call — ask your lead.

## Common traps

- **"Whatever the team does"** as a justification for ignoring a written rule.
- **Over-applying formality.** Business casual every day is not safer than business formal every day; read the
  policy.
- **Being "nice" about confidentiality** — telling a colleague "just this once" about client data.
- **Late arrival framed as a personal issue** — the professional action is an advance apology with a firm ETA.
- **Leaving a meeting immediately when it ends** without any transition. A quick "thanks, good discussion" or
  a summary mail is what closes the loop.

## Checklist

- [ ] I know the expected arrival buffer (5–10 min early).
- [ ] I know that client data leaves the authorised team, channel, and device — always.
- [ ] I know the rule for client-day dress versus everyday dress.
- [ ] I would send a meeting summary with actions within 24 hours.
- [ ] I know that personal work on company machines is not permitted.
