# Email & Written Communication Etiquette

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Workplace Professionalism & Etiquette ·  _Estimated reading time: 8 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Workplace professionalism topic README](../README.md)

## In one sentence
**A professional email is short, has a meaningful subject, names the action needed, and is written for the most senior person who will read it — everything else is detail.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- "You have to send an email to your manager about X" items are among the most common in the etiquette sub-type,
  and the options usually differ on **tone, timing, or channel**, not on the facts.
- The exam is testing whether you understand that **written communication at work is a permanent, forwarded,
  recorded artifact** — you would not say it out loud.
- The same principles cover WhatsApp/chat etiquette, which many Indian pre-tests use as a modern variant.

## Core concept

### The 5-part structure of a professional email

| Part | Rule | Example |
|------|------|---------|
| **Subject** | Specific, 6–10 words, action-oriented where possible. Never "urgent!!" or "help" | `Delay in payment reconciliation module — revised plan for Friday release` |
| **Greeting** | "Dear Sir/Madam," or the person's name with "Hi/Ananya," depending on the relationship | `Dear Ramesh Sir,` |
| **Context** | 1–2 lines. What, when, why. Most relevant information first (inverted pyramid) | `The reconciliation module is 60% complete as of today, against a Friday release commitment.` |
| **Action** | What you need, from whom, by when. Make the ask unmissable | `Requesting your approval for option 2 (release the two minor fixes on Friday, move the calculation fix to the next release) by 4 p.m. today.` |
| **Close** | Thank-you + next step + contact | `Thank you for your guidance. I will confirm the release plan by 6 p.m. regardless of the decision.` |

### Subject-line rules

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|--------|--------|
| Name the topic and the ask | "URGENT", "help", "please see below" |
| Use a colon or dash for structure: `Status: XYZ` | Typing in all caps |
| Keep it under ~10 words | A one-paragraph subject |
| Put the decision you need in it | "Question" with no topic |

### Tone rules — the "email register"

| Situation | Register |
|-----------|---------|
| To your manager / a senior / a client | Formal, complete sentences, no slang, no emojis |
| To a peer in a project group | Semi-formal, brief, clear |
| To a chat group | Short, lowercase is acceptable, no caps for emphasis |
| To anyone | No sarcasm, no passive-aggressive phrasing, no "as per my last mail" |

⚠️ **"as per my last mail" is a red flag.** It reads as an accusation. Professional alternatives:
- "Could you please confirm the status of the below request?"
- "Following up on my previous note about the reconciliation module — the revised plan was due today."

### Reply-timing norms

| Priority | Expected response |
|----------|-------------------|
| Production outage / client escalation | Immediately — and a phone call beats an email |
| Blocking request | Same working day |
| Normal project request | Within 1 working day |
| FYI / non-urgent | Within 2 working days |
| Out of hours | Next working day, unless it is an emergency |

**Never** leave an email unanswered for more than 2 working days. If you cannot answer, acknowledge and give a
time: `I will revert by Thursday 5 p.m.`

### CC etiquette

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| CC a person who **needs to act or to know**, not to show you copied someone | Blame-by-CC is a real workplace offence |
| Never CC your manager to escalate a peer issue you could have raised directly | Process violation |
| Do not CC the whole distribution list for a one-to-one matter | Noise; it hides the ask |
| Use "To" for the person who must act; "Cc" for awareness | Clarity of ownership |

### Attachments and file naming

- Attach before you forget, and **name the file clearly**: `PaymentReconciliation_ReleasePlan_v2_2026-03-14.docx`.
- Reference the attachment in the body: `Please find the release plan attached (v2, 14 March).`
- Re-check the attachment before you press send. "Attachment missing in mail" is the most embarrassing
  professional failure in the list.

### The 6 email sins

1. Sending to the wrong recipient / all-reply-all by accident.
2. Blaming a named colleague in writing.
3. Using capital letters for emphasis.
4. Sending without reading the recipients list.
5. Forwarding a chain with 40 replies and no summary.
6. Discussing personal matters, grievances, or job searching on a work channel.

### Chat / messaging etiquette (the modern variant)

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Response expectation | Same working day for work chats; do not expect instant replies out of hours |
| Channels | Use the official channel for official work; keep personal discussions out of work groups |
| Emojis | Fine with peers on chat, never in an email to a senior or a client |
| Voice notes | Avoid for anything needing a record or an action |
| Official records | Anything that needs a trail goes in mail, not in a chat |

## Worked example

> **Q.** You need your manager's approval by 4 p.m. today for a revised release plan. What is the most appropriate
> way to send this information?
>
> A. Send a chat message to your manager: "Hi sir, please approve the new plan when you get time. Thanks."
> B. Send an email with the subject "Plan change — approval needed today", a two-line summary of the delay, the
>    two options with their impact, and a clear request for approval by 4 p.m.
> C. Send the same request to your manager, the delivery manager, and the whole project distribution list so
>    that someone can approve it.
> D. Wait until tomorrow's stand-up and raise the plan change there.

**Answer: B**

| Option | Failure |
|--------|---------|
| A | Chat is not a record, the subject is missing, the ask is buried, and "when you get time" ignores the deadline. Low formality for a decision request. |
| B | ✅ Professional email: descriptive subject, context first, options with impact, explicit ask with a deadline. |
| C | Broadcast escalation. It bypasses your manager's authority to decide, and CCing a distribution list to force a decision is a process violation. |
| D | Loses a full day on a request with a 4 p.m. deadline today. |

**Why B over C:** both reach the right people; only one respects the decision path and the recipient's time.

## Quick rules

- Subject says what the mail is about; the body says what you need; the close says what happens next.
- Lead with the most important information. Busy seniors read the first three lines.
- Match the formality to the **most senior reader**.
- Acknowledge within 2 working days even if you cannot resolve.
- Never blame in writing; never CC to pressure; never use caps for emphasis.
- One mail, one ask. If you need two things, order them and mark which is the priority.

## Common traps

- **Writing a mail with no ask.** The "professional" option that only informs is a trap — informing without
  requesting an action wastes the reader's time.
- **Over-formality that reads as cold.** "With due respect I shall acknowledge receipt of your communication"
  is grammatically fine and professionally wrong in an Indian corporate context. Keep it civil and plain.
- **Chat-for-anything.** Using a messaging app for a formal decision request is a classic wrong option.
- **The reply-all.** Sending to the whole group because you are unsure who needs to know.
- **Attachment neglect.** Mentioning "the document" without naming or attaching it.

## Checklist

- [ ] My subject line is specific and under ~10 words.
- [ ] The first line of the body states the point.
- [ ] There is exactly one clear ask, with a deadline.
- [ ] Recipients and CC list are deliberate; no blame-by-CC.
- [ ] Attachments are attached, named, and referenced in the body.
- [ ] No caps, no "as per my last mail", no personal content.
