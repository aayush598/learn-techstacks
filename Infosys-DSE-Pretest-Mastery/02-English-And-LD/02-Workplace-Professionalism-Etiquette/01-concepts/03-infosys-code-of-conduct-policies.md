# Infosys-Style Code of Conduct & Policies

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Workplace Professionalism & Etiquette ·  _Estimated reading time: 7 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Meetings, punctuality, appearance & confidentiality](02-meetings-punctuality-appearance-confidentiality.md)

## In one sentence
**Company-policy items in the pre-test are always about one thing — follow the written rule, use the official channel, and never put personal work on company resources.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Policy-based items (training attendance, ID badge, personal work on company machines, alcohol, conduct with
  seniors) appear in most batches. They are **easy marks if you know the convention** and near-zero marks if
  you guess.
- The distractors are built from what a new joiner might actually do: skip a mandatory session because the
  content "wasn't useful", lend an ID badge to a friend, "quickly" check personal mail on the office laptop.
- The correct option is always the **rule-compliant** one, even when it feels inconvenient. That is the whole
  point of the item.

## Core concept

### The 10 policy rules that get tested

| # | Rule | The professional behaviour | The wrong option sounds like |
|---|------|---------------------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Mandatory training** | Attend all sessions assigned to you; inform your manager in advance if you cannot | "Skip it if the content repeats what I already know" |
| 2 | **ID badge** | Wear it visibly at all times in the office, including during lunch | "Give it to a colleague visiting from another team" |
| 3 | **Personal work on company machines** | Do not use company laptops, desktops, internet, or systems for personal work | "Quickly check personal mail during a break" |
| 4 | **Office hours** | Work within the declared hours; if you need to work late, follow the approval process | "Stay late unannounced and leave the next morning late" |
| 5 | **Confidentiality of client data** | Use only authorised systems, devices, and locations | "Download the client dataset to a personal laptop to work from home" |
| 6 | **Intellectual property** | Work done in company time on company systems belongs to the company; declare side projects | "Write a personal app during office hours on the office laptop" |
| 7 | **Alcohol / substances** | Not on campus, not during work hours, not at client sites | "One drink at the office party after hours is fine" |
| 8 | **Respectful conduct** | No discrimination, no harassment, no bullying, no abuse of authority | "My senior is aggressive with the team; I will keep quiet to avoid trouble" |
| 9 | **Social media** | Do not post client, project, or office-related content that identifies the company or a client | "Post a photo of the client dashboard with the client name visible" |
| 10 | **Leave and attendance** | Apply for leave through the process; do not simply stay away | "Inform only my friend; the team will find out" |

### Honesty and reporting

| Situation | Professional behaviour |
|-----------|----------------------|
| You made an error | Report it promptly to your manager, with impact and a recovery plan |
| You spot a policy breach | Report through the right channel — your manager, the ethics helpline, or the compliance team. Not to social media, not to the client's site |
| You are asked to do something against policy | Decline, state the policy, offer a compliant alternative, escalate if pressure continues |
| A colleague is being treated badly | Report through the formal channel (HR / ethics). Supporting the colleague informally is not enough |
| You made a mistake that reached the client | Report immediately; do not try to quietly fix it first |

### Conduct with seniors, peers, and juniors

| With | Expected behaviour |
|------|-------------------|
| **Senior** | Professional respect; disagree with facts and a rationale, not with attitude; never argue in front of a client; never refuse a legitimate instruction without discussion |
| **Peer** | Peer support, share credit, help without being asked where possible, no competitive sabotage |
| **Junior / new joiner** | Be patient; share knowledge; correct in private, never in front of the team; never make them look foolish |

### Conflict with a senior — the graded options

| Option | Grade | Why |
|--------|-------|-----|
| Follow the instruction and support the decision | ✅ Acceptable | If the instruction is legitimate, this is the correct professional response |
| Raise the concern privately with a rationale, then follow the decision | ✅ Best | It is the "disagree and commit" pattern |
| Publicly argue or refuse to execute | ❌ Wrong | Unprofessional; damages the team and the client |
| Ignore and do it your own way | ❌ Wrong | Insubordination; hides the risk |
| Report to HR immediately | ❌ Wrong (usually) | Over-escalation for a technical disagreement |

### Professional etiquette on client sites

| Do | Don't |
|----|------|
| Follow the client's dress code and security rules | Wear your casual office clothes |
| Keep your ID badge visible | Share your badge with a colleague |
| Stay within the approved area | Explore other floors "for a quick look" |
| Use only authorised networks and devices | Use personal hotspots for client data |
| Speak to the client only about what is in your scope | Field questions you cannot answer; promise timelines |

### Why these rules are marked the way they are

The exam's logic is: **an organisation's policies exist to protect clients, data, and the delivery. A candidate
who treats policy as optional is a delivery risk.** That is why the correct option is nearly always the one
that looks slightly inconvenient.

## Worked example

> **Q.** A mandatory "Information Security" training session is scheduled for 11 a.m. You have already read the
> policy document and believe the session will be a repetition of what you know. What should you do?
>
> A. Skip the session because the content repeats what you already know, and send your manager a note saying you
>    read the material.
> B. Attend the session as scheduled; if a genuine conflict exists, inform your manager in advance and request a
>    rescheduled slot, or complete the assigned module online if the policy offers that route.
> C. Attend the session but leave once the content repeats, since the attendance register is what matters.
> D. Ask a colleague who attended to summarise the session and mark your attendance on the basis of that summary.

**Answer: B**

| Option | Rule broken | Why it is wrong |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| A | Training attendance | Attendance is the control, not knowledge. Skipping it leaves a compliance gap and a record that you skipped mandatory training. |
| B | ✅ None | Follows the rule, uses the legitimate alternatives (request a slot, or the online route if the policy allows), and communicates. |
| C | Training attendance | Attending in part but not completing the session is still a non-compliance, and leaving early is a record. |
| D | Training attendance | An attendance register signed off by a proxy is falsified compliance — worse than skipping. |

**Generalisation:** for every policy item, the answer that **follows the rule and uses the sanctioned alternative
route** is the correct one. The answer that **substitutes your own judgement for the rule** is the trap.

## Quick rules

- **Never** skip mandatory training, ever, even when it is redundant.
- **Never** lend your ID badge, and never leave it off the work floor.
- **Never** do personal work on company systems — mail, browsing, downloads, side projects.
- **Never** move client data to personal devices, personal drives, or personal email.
- **Never** post client, project, or company-identifying content on social media.
- Report errors, breaches, and unethical requests **promptly, through the channel**.
- If a policy creates a genuine conflict, **use the sanctioned alternative** (reschedule, online module,
  escalation) — that is what makes option B the correct one.
- A policy question never rewards the "efficient" option; it rewards the **compliant** option.

## Common traps

- **Efficiency framing.** "It wastes time", "the content is the same", "I already read it" — the exam uses
  efficiency to lure you into non-compliance.
- **Bending a rule for a good reason.** "The client asked for it", "my manager said it was fine" — a rule
  bent for a good reason is still bent.
- **Faking compliance.** Signing an attendance register for someone else, or marking a module done without
  doing it, is worse than the violation it hides.
- **Silent non-compliance.** Doing nothing about a breach you observed is treated as participation once you
  know about it.
- **The "everyone does it" argument.** If a practice is common and also a policy violation, the correct answer
  is still compliance. This is exactly the trap in the dress-code item.

## Checklist

- [ ] I know the rule for training attendance, ID badge, and personal work on company machines.
- [ ] I would report a policy breach through the official channel, not to a social network or a client site.
- [ ] If a policy conflicts with a task, I would use the sanctioned alternative route.
- [ ] I never post client or project information on social media.
- [ ] I would refuse, cite the policy, and escalate if asked to do something non-compliant.
