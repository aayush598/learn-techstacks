# Answer Template Bank

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Behavioural & Scenario-Based ·  _Estimated reading time: 6 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Writing the ideal answer in 3 sentences](../02-techniques/02-writing-the-ideal-answer-in-3-sentences.md)

## In one sentence
**A phrase bank of the exact wording the professional option uses — steal the phrasing, and you cannot accidentally pick the passive, aggressive, or over-escalating option.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Recognising a phrase is faster than reasoning from scratch. Under the 90-second cap, pattern recognition wins.
- The phrase bank also works in reverse: it tells you **which phrases are never in a correct option** — the
  distractor vocabulary.
- It is the reference to use in the last 20 minutes before a mock test.

## Core concept

### Phrases that signal the professional option

| Function | Phrases |
|----------|---------|
| Ownership | "take ownership of", "ensure this is resolved", "I will drive this to closure", "I will make sure this does not recur" |
| Early communication | "inform immediately", "share the details as soon as possible", "flag it early", "raise it before it becomes a risk" |
| Right level | "inform my team lead", "escalate it appropriately through the delivery manager", "discuss it with the module owner" |
| Evidence | "with the impact analysis", "along with the test evidence", "documented in writing", "logged in the defect tracker" |
| Planning | "propose two options", "with a revised date and a rollback plan", "agree a recovery plan", "suggest a workaround for the demo" |
| Closure | "confirm the fix is in place", "follow up after the review", "track it to closure", "verify before the release" |
| Boundary-setting (ethics) | "while staying within the policy", "refuse to bypass the approval", "offer a compliant alternative" |

### Phrases that signal a distractor

| Phrase | Why it is a distractor |
|--------|-------------------------|
| "I would stay silent", "I would not mention it", "I would ignore" | Passive archetype |
| "I would inform the CEO", "I would go directly to the client", "without informing my manager" | Over-escalation archetype |
| "I would blame", "I would complain loudly", "I would argue", "I would refuse to work" | Aggressive archetype |
| "I would handle it appropriately", "I would take appropriate steps", "I would ensure things are sorted" | Filler — no mechanism, no recipient |
| "I would be a team player", "I would maintain a positive attitude" | Character, not process |
| "It is not my fault", "the requirement was unrealistic" | Deflection, no action |
| "I would inform the whole team", "I would send it to all stakeholders" | Broadcasting; no recipient logic |

### The 3-sentence skeleton with variants

```
S1  <Trigger> has occurred, and <my role / impact> is <specific fact>.
S2  I would <OWN> and <COMMUNICATE EARLY> to <RECIPIENT>, <EVIDENCE>, and <PROPOSE>.
S3  This is the right step because <business impact / principle>.
```

**Variant A — missed deadline**

> I would list what is complete and what is not, tell my team lead immediately that the module will not be
> finished by the committed date, and propose two options — cut the scope for this release or move the date by
> two days.
> This is the right step because a late, small scope is recoverable while a missed commitment discovered at the
> deadline is not.

**Variant B — angry customer**

> I would let the client finish speaking, acknowledge the impact of the mismatch on their reporting, state
> clearly what I can and cannot confirm yet, and commit to calling back with the root-cause analysis by a
> specific time; I would inform my delivery manager straight afterwards.
> This is the right step because the client's trust depends on receiving accurate information quickly, not on an
> immediate explanation I cannot yet support.

**Variant C — credit taken**

> I would first talk to my teammate privately, share the evidence of my contribution from the repository and the
> task tracker, and ask them to clarify the attribution with the team; if the situation does not change, I would
> put the facts in writing to my manager.
> This is the right step because a direct, factual conversation fixes a credit problem without creating a team
> conflict, while a formal complaint as the first step escalates a small issue unnecessarily.

**Variant D — my own mistake**

> I would inform the affected team immediately that I missed the submission, state the impact on the client's
> onboarding timeline, submit the form right away, and confirm with them that the client file is complete.
> This is the right step because early disclosure gives the team the option to re-sequence the work, whereas late
> disclosure turns my mistake into a client-facing incident.

**Variant E — disagreement with a manager**

> I would prepare a short note with the profiling data and the specific risk, request a 15-minute review with
> my manager to understand any constraint I may have missed, and — if the decision stands — support it fully
> while recording the risk in the project log.
> This is the right step because the decision is my manager's to make, and a prepared, evidence-based objection
> is far more likely to change it than a complaint made to others.

**Variant F — motivation**

> I want to work on enterprise-scale delivery problems where my Java and DBMS foundation is directly useful,
> and I am looking for a structured environment in which to build that depth rather than fragments of it.
> I would bring the discipline of building things properly — testing, documentation, and clear communication —
> and I want to learn how those systems are actually run at scale in industry.

### Words that should never appear in your written answer

| ❌ Never write | ✅ Write instead |
|---------------|----------------|
| "he stole", "she is dishonest", "they are lazy" | "the attribution was inaccurate", "the requirement was missed" |
| "it's not my fault", "it was not possible" | "the submission was missed; here is the recovery" |
| "obviously", "clearly", "of course" | remove them |
| "I would definitely, without a doubt" | "I would first…" |
| "as per my personal opinion" | "based on the profiling data" |

## Worked example

> **Q (written).** Your team lead asks you to share your completed module with a colleague who will present it
> to the client as his own work. What do you do?

**Written answer (3 sentences, 61 words):**

> I would check the contribution and acknowledgement records for the module, then share the module with my
> colleague while making sure the git history, the tracker entries, and the release notes all reflect my
> authorship.
> If my lead intended to present it as the colleague's work, I would ask him directly and in writing to confirm
> the attribution, and offer to walk through the module's technical details with the colleague so that he is
> able to answer questions on it honestly.
> Ownership of authorship is a matter of record, not of presentation, and keeping the record accurate protects
> both the client and me when the module is extended later.

**Breakdown:**

| Sentence | Function | Framework steps hit |
|----------|----------|--------------------|
| S1 | Situation + immediate safe action (records) | Own, Document |
| S2 | The ask, done respectfully and in writing | Communicate, Right person, Document |
| S3 | The reason, stated as a business fact | Reason |

**Why it works:** no blame named, no accusation of the lead, no escalation threat, and the collaborator is
handled with dignity — the same "professional" shape that the MCQ options reward.

## Quick rules

- The professional option contains at least **two** phrases from the "professional" table.
- A distractor contains at least **one** phrase from the "distractor" table.
- "Take appropriate steps" is never enough. Replace it with a role + an action.
- In a written answer, the **first sentence must contain a concrete fact**, not an attitude.
- Use the variant skeletons; they are already inside the 90-second budget.

## Common traps

- **Copying a variant so literally that it does not fit the stem.** The skeletons are structural, not textual.
- **Using "ensure" with nothing after it.** "I would ensure the team is informed" — informed of what, by when,
  and by whom?
- **Adding a fourth sentence.** Three is the target; a fourth usually adds an apology or a justification you
  do not need.
- **Writing in passive voice.** "It was informed" hides who acts. Use "I would inform…".

## Checklist

- [ ] My answer names a role and an action.
- [ ] My answer contains no blame or deflection words.
- [ ] My answer is exactly three sentences.
- [ ] My reasoning sentence mentions a business impact.
- [ ] I have not used "take appropriate steps" or "handle it professionally".
