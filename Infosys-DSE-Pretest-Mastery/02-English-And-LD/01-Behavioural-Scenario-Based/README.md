# Behavioural & Scenario-Based Questions

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Behavioural & Scenario-Based ·  _Estimated reading time: 6 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Section 2 overview](../README.md)

## In one sentence
**Every behavioural option you will ever meet is one of four archetypes — passive, aggressive, over-escalating, or professional — and the professional one is always the calmest, most specific, most process-shaped option on the sheet.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Section 2's biggest single sub-type. Recent batches put **5–8 behavioural items** in the 15 questions.
- The stems are recycled heavily: missed deadline, colleague takes credit, angry customer, you disagree with your
  manager, you spot a bug before release, you are overloaded, a teammate's mistake, a conflict in the team.
- There is **no knowledge to memorise**. Either you can recognise the professional response pattern, or you lose
  1/4 of the section.
- Wrong picks here are the classic trap of a fresh graduate: you pick the *aggressive* option because it feels
  decisive, or the *over-escalating* option because it feels proactive.

## Learning objectives

After this folder you should be able to:

- Name the **four option archetypes** and label any option in under 10 seconds.
- Apply the **5-step Infosys-professional response framework** (own → communicate early → right person → document → close the loop) to any scenario stem.
- Tell a **best**-behaviour question from a **least**-behaviour question and flip your elimination accordingly.
- Recognise the 12 **scenario archetypes** and recall the professional answer shape for each in one breath.
- Answer a **motivational** question ("Why Infosys?", "Why this stream?") with an answer that is specific, mutual, and non-transactional.
- Write the **ideal answer in three sentences** if a written form appears.
- Kill the two classic traps: **silence-as-strategy** and **escalation-as-proactivity**.

## Quick procedure — the 90-second scenario drill

```
1. Read the stem only (10s). Note the trigger: deadline / credit / conflict / angry / bug / overload.
2. Note the judgement word if present: BEST / LEAST / MOST APPROPRIATE (5s).
3. Label each option silently: PASSIVE / AGGRESSIVE / OVER-ESCALATING / PROFESSIONAL (20s).
4. If exactly one PROFESSIONAL → pick it. Stop. (60s used.)
5. If none is clearly professional → re-read options for the one with a concrete, proportionate action (20s).
6. At 70s, if still unsure, pick the option with ownership language ("I will take ownership / inform / propose"),
   flag it, move on.
```

## Key takeaways

- 🔥 The correct option is **the calmest one**, not the most decisive-sounding one.
- The professional option names a **specific person and a specific action** ("inform my delivery manager and
  propose a fix plan"), never a vague attitude ("handle it sensitively").
- Over-escalation is **not** virtue. Escalating past your line of reporting is a process failure, and the test
  knows it.
- Silence is **never** the answer when the trigger affects delivery or a client.
- Blaming a named colleague is **never** the answer, even when the colleague is clearly at fault.
- Ownership language ("I will take ownership of…") plus communication language ("inform", "discuss", "escalate
  appropriately") is the strongest positive signal on the sheet.
- A motivational answer must be **specific to Infosys and mutual** — what you give, what you get, what you want to learn.
- If a question is written rather than multiple-choice, the answer has the same structure: **situation → action → reasoning**.
