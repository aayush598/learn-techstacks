# Writing the Ideal Answer in Three Sentences

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Behavioural & Scenario-Based ·  _Estimated reading time: 6 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [The Infosys-professional response framework](../01-concepts/02-infosys-professional-response-framework.md)

## In one sentence
**If a behavioural question ever asks for your answer in your own words, three sentences is the winning length: what happened, what I would do, and why that is the right call.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- Some batches and some interview-linked formats ask you to **describe** your response rather than pick one.
  The written version is scored with the same rubric: ownership, communication, correct escalation, evidence, follow-up.
- Three sentences is not a style preference. It maps exactly onto the three things you must demonstrate:
  **situation → action → reasoning**. Longer answers add adjectives, not marks.
- It also protects the 90-second rule: a written answer you can structure in 30 seconds is an answer you can
  actually finish inside the time limit.

## Core concept

### The 3-sentence template

```
S1 (situation)  : Acknowledge the trigger and your role. 1 clause. No adjectives.
S2 (action)     : The professional chain — OWN + COMMUNICATE + (PROPOSE / DOCUMENT).
S3 (reasoning)  : The business reason. Impact on the client / delivery / team + the principle applied.
```

### Filled example

> **Situation:** A critical defect is found in a module I own, two days before the release.

```
S1  I would first quantify the defect's impact on the client-facing functionality and confirm the
    reproduction with the test evidence.
S2  I would take ownership of it, inform my team lead and the delivery manager immediately with the impact
    analysis and a proposed fix plan, and log it in the defect tracker.
S3  Early, factual communication at the right level gives the delivery manager the options to decide about the
    release date, whereas silence would remove those options entirely and turn my omission into a client issue.
```

Word count: 78. Time to write: 30 seconds. It contains all five framework steps.

### The sentence-2 phrase slots

Fill these in and sentence 2 is always correct.

| Slot | Phrases to use |
|------|----------------|
| Ownership | "take ownership of", "ensure this is resolved", "drive it to closure" |
| Recipient (Level 2) | "my team lead", "my manager", "the module owner" |
| Recipient (Level 3) | "the delivery manager", "the project manager", "the client relationship manager" |
| Evidence | "with the impact analysis", "along with the test evidence", "documented in the defect tracker" |
| Plan | "proposing two options — …", "with a revised date and a rollback plan", "agreeing a recovery plan" |
| Follow-up | "and confirm the fix before the release", "and track it to closure" |

### The sentence-3 reasoning slots

The reason must point at **business impact**, not at your feelings.

| Weak reasoning ❌ | Strong reasoning ✅ |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| "Because I am a responsible and dedicated employee." | "Because early, factual information lets the delivery manager protect the release date and the client relationship." |
| "Because it is the right thing to do." | "Because the cost of a late disclosure is borne by the client, while the cost of an early disclosure is borne by a five-minute conversation." |
| "Because my manager would want to know." | "Because the decision about the release date is not mine to make; the information needed to make it is." |
| "To avoid being blamed." | "To keep the decision with the person accountable for the delivery, and to keep the record accurate." |

### How this maps to the MCQ options

Even when the test gives you four options, mentally write your 3-sentence answer **before** reading them. Then
pick the option that matches. This is the fastest way to stop being seduced by the aggressive option.

```
Write S2 mentally  →  "inform my team lead + impact + plan"
   ↓
Option that matches → correct
Option with ownership but no recipient → incomplete
Option with a recipient but no plan → incomplete
Option with blame / threat / bypass → eliminated
```

### Verbal (interview) version

If a pre-test or an interview asks the question aloud, use the same three beats but shorter:

1. "First I would **understand the impact** — X."
2. "Then I would **inform my manager with the facts and a proposed plan**, and document it."
3. "That keeps the decision with the accountable person and gives them options early, instead of a surprise later."

🔥 **Do not** open with "I would definitely…". Do not open with "It depends". Open with the first action.

## Worked example

> **Q (written form).** A colleague has taken credit for a module you built. Your manager is unaware. Describe
> how you would respond.

```
S1  A teammate has presented work that I contributed to as their own, and my manager is not aware of it.
S2  I would first approach the teammate privately and factually, share the evidence of my contribution, and
    ask them to correct the attribution with the team; I would also document my own contribution in the
    repository, the task tracker, and the release notes.
S3  A private, evidence-based correction first protects the working relationship and fixes the actual problem,
    while a formal complaint to my manager straight away would escalate a credit dispute into a team conflict.
```

**Why this beats a one-line answer:** it shows the escalation ladder (private first, manager only if it
continues), shows evidence-based framing, and shows the business reason. It contains **zero** blame language —
it describes the situation, not the character's motives.

**Answer quality check:**

| Criterion | Present? |
|-----------|----------|
| Names the trigger | ✅ |
| Owns the outcome | ✅ (implicitly: "I would…") |
| Names a recipient | ✅ teammate first, manager conditionally |
| Includes a plan / action | ✅ correct the attribution + document contribution |
| Explains the business reason | ✅ protects relationship, avoids escalation |
| Uses blame words ("he stole", "dishonest") | ❌ absent — correct |

## Quick rules

- Three sentences. Situation → action → reason. Never more than four.
- Reason with **impact**, never with your character.
- Name the person you talk to, in order: the closest one first, then escalate only if needed.
- Include evidence. "With the impact analysis" beats "with an apology" (an apology is for *your* mistake, not
  someone else's).
- **Private first** for colleague issues, **public-safe** for delivery issues.
- Never write "I would inform HR" as your first move.
- If the trigger is your own mistake, sentence 2 becomes: own it, report it with the impact, propose the recovery.

## Common traps

- **The apology essay.** Three sentences of "I would be very sorry and would apologise" scores zero because nothing
  is fixed. Apologise *and* propose.
- **Running out of the 90 seconds mid-sentence.** Structure first, write second. Three bullets, then sentences.
- **The passive-aggressive line.** "I would politely inform the team that I had in fact done the work" is a
  complaint in disguise. Say it in the private conversation, not in a public channel.
- **The escalation jump in written form.** "I would escalate this to the HR head" reads as an escalation-happy
  candidate.
- **Generic reasoning.** "To maintain professionalism" is the same as no reasoning. Name the concrete risk avoided.

## Checklist

- [ ] My answer has exactly three sentences: situation, action, reason.
- [ ] Sentence 2 names **who** I tell and **what I propose**.
- [ ] Sentence 3 points at a business impact, not at my character.
- [ ] I used zero blame words about a third party.
- [ ] I could have written it in under 30 seconds.
