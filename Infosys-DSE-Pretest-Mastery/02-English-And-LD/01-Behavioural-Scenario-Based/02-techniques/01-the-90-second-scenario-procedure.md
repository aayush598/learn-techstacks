# The 90-Second Scenario Procedure

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Behavioural & Scenario-Based ·  _Estimated reading time: 7 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [The four-option answer scheme](../01-concepts/01-the-four-option-answer-scheme.md)

## In one sentence
**Every scenario question can be answered in six mechanical steps inside 90 seconds — the procedure below never asks you to think about anything the option set does not already tell you.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- A behavioural question is not a knowledge question. It is a **pattern-recognition** question wearing a paragraph
  of costume. Time spent re-reading the scenario is wasted.
- Meera's failure mode in Section 2 is not ignorance; it is **re-reading the stem four times**. The procedure
  removes re-reading.
- The procedure is also the answer to "I read all four options and they all sound plausible". They don't.
  They are archetypes, and archetypes are predictable.

## Core concept

### The 6-step timed procedure

```
+- T+00s -- STEP 1: TRIGGER (read the stem only, stop at the first sentence)
|           Name the archetype in your head:
|           deadline | credit | conflict | angry client | bug | overload |
|           credit-stealing | disagreement | policy | unclear requirement
|
|- T+10s -- STEP 2: JUDGEMENT WORD
|           best / least / most appropriate / not recommended?
|           If "least", SWITCH your elimination direction now.
|
|- T+15s -- STEP 3: FIRST PASS OVER OPTIONS — label, do not evaluate
|           P = passive | A = aggressive | O = over-escalating | F = professional
|           Read only the first 8 words of each option.
|
|- T+45s -- STEP 4: THE COUNT TEST
|           Exactly one F? → answer it.
|           Zero F? → go to Step 5.
|           Two F? → the shorter one is passive → the longer one wins.
|
|- T+60s -- STEP 5: THE RECIPIENT + PLAN TEST
|           For each remaining option ask:
|             (a) Does it name a specific person or role?
|             (b) Does it include an action or a plan, not just an attitude?
|             (c) Does it keep me inside the process (no bypass, no blame)?
|           Any option passing all three is the answer.
|
+- T+70s -- STEP 6: THE 70-SECOND RULE
            Still unsure? Pick the option with ownership language
            ("take ownership", "inform", "propose", "document").
            Flag it. Move on. Never spend the full 90.
```

### Step 3 in detail — the 8-word label pass

You do not need to read the options fully to label them. The archetype is almost always visible in the opening
clause.

| Opening clause you see | Label |
|------------------------|-------|
| "Stay quiet", "ignore", "wait for", "hope", "leave it to", "not mention" | **P** (passive) |
| "Blame", "tell him off", "argue", "complain", "confront", "refuse", "insist that" | **A** (aggressive) |
| "Inform the CEO", "go directly to", "bypass", "without informing my manager", "inform the client directly", "escalate to HR immediately" | **O** (over-escalating) |
| "Inform my manager and …", "take ownership and …", "discuss with … and propose …", "escalate appropriately", "share the impact and …" | **F** (professional) |

### The escalation-ladder check (10 seconds, worth 1 mark)

Before you commit, ask: **is the option skipping a level?**

| The option says | Is that a skip? | Verdict |
|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| "Inform my team lead / project manager" | No — that is Level 2/3 | ✅ Usually correct |
| "Inform the delivery manager" | No — Level 3 | ✅ Correct when client or deadline impact exists |
| "Inform the client directly" | Yes, unless it is a client-visible emergency and you own the account | ⚠️ Usually wrong |
| "Inform HR / the CEO / the client's IT head" | Yes | ❌ Almost always wrong |
| "Go over my manager's head" | Yes, by definition | ❌ Always wrong |

### The three-question quality test for the remaining options

If two options still look alive, ask these in order:

1. **"Does it protect the delivery or the client?"** — the professional option does. The aggressive option does not.
2. **"Would a trainer sign this off as professional behaviour at Infosys?"** — if it would embarrass you in a
   mock interview, it is a distractor.
3. **"Does it produce a next state?"** — professional options move the situation forward (planned, documented,
   tracked). Passive and over-escalating options usually only express a reaction.

## Worked example

> **Q.** Ananya's team has 6 hours to release a payment module. Testing reveals 3 defects, 2 of which are minor
> and 1 of which affects a calculation. Her manager is on leave. The client has a go-live event on Friday.
>
> A. Fix all three defects in the next 6 hours with the team, and report the release as on track on Thursday.
> B. Inform the delivery manager that 3 defects exist, with the impact of the calculation defect, and propose
>    releasing the two minor fixes on Friday while moving the calculation fix to the next release.
> C. Escalate to the project sponsor and inform the client that Friday's go-live is at risk.
> D. Wait for the manager to return from leave before telling anyone, and ask the team to stay late.

**Step 1 — trigger:** release risk + manager unavailable → archetype "bug / deadline".
**Step 2 — judgement word:** none present, so "best".
**Step 3 — labels:** A = passive (reports a false status; "report on track" while hiding a defect is worse than
silence), B = professional, C = over-escalating, D = passive (delays information).

**Step 4 — count test:** exactly one professional → **B**.

**Step 5 — ladder check on B:** informs the delivery manager (Level 3) — appropriate, because the trigger is
client-facing release risk and the line manager is absent. Not a skip. ✅

**Answer: B.**

**Why not A:** "Report the release as on track" while a calculation defect is open is **false reporting**. Even
well-meant, it destroys trust the moment the defect appears in production. This is the trap that looks most
like "teamwork".

**Why not C:** Two skips at once — straight to the project sponsor, and straight to the client. The delivery
manager exists precisely to route this. The answer to "go-live might slip" is not "tell the client it might slip";
it is "tell the delivery manager so they can manage the go-live".

**Why not D:** A manager on leave is not a reason to lose 6 hours of information. The correct move is to go one
level up, not to freeze.

**Score check:** you should reach B in about 45 seconds if you had labelled the options in one pass.

## Quick rules

- **Step 1 and Step 3 are where the time goes. Everything else is fast.**
- Label, do not evaluate, on the first pass over the options.
- Exactly one professional option → take it, no further reading.
- Two professional options → the **longer, more specific** one wins.
- Zero professional options → read the options fully for the recipient + plan test.
- The 70-second rule is absolute. Flag and move.
- **Never** pick an option that requires you to misreport status, hide a defect, or blame a person.
- If your instinct says "that answer is too aggressive to be the distractor, so it must be right" — no. Aggressive
  options are the most attractive distractors.

## Common traps

- **Reading the options before the stem.** You then spend 20 seconds reconstructing the scenario you just read.
- **Arguing with the content of the scenario** ("but I would never do that"). The question is which option the
  exam wants.
- **Spending 90 seconds on one behavioural item.** Section 2 has 15 questions; 90 seconds on one behavioural
  question and 40 seconds on the other 14 is a bad trade.
- **Trusting length as proof.** Length is a tie-breaker, not a rule.
- **Forgetting to re-check the judgement word** when the question says "least appropriate".
- **Panic-flipping at 80 seconds.** If you flip an answer at 80 seconds, you have probably not been reading the
  archetype, only reacting to the wording.

## Checklist

- [ ] I read the stem first and named the archetype before touching the options.
- [ ] I checked for a judgement word and flipped direction if needed.
- [ ] I labelled all four options before evaluating any of them.
- [ ] I applied the escalation-ladder check to my chosen option.
- [ ] I marked and moved on at 70 seconds if still unsure.
