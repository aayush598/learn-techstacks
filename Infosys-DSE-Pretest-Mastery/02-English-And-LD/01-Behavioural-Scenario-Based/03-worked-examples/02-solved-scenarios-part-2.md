# Solved Scenarios — Part 2

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Behavioural & Scenario-Based ·  _Estimated reading time: 10 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Situational judgement & motivation](../01-concepts/03-situational-judgement-and-motivation.md)

## In one sentence
**Five more solved items — team conflict, disagreement with a manager, a teammate's mistake, your own mistake, and two motivational questions — plus one "least appropriate" item to show the scoring flip.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- "Least appropriate" items and motivational items are the two formats candidates most often lose marks on,
  for opposite reasons: one because they score the options backwards, the other because they answer
  enthusiastically instead of specifically.
- "Disagreement with the manager" and "your own mistake" appear in nearly every reported batch.

## Core concept

Labels used below: **P** passive, **A** aggressive, **O** over-escalating, **F** professional.

## Worked example

### Q1. Team conflict (least appropriate)

> **Q.** Two members of your team, Ananya and Meera, have had repeated arguments over who owns a shared module.
> The argument is now visible in daily stand-ups and the module is slipping. **Which of the following is the
> LEAST appropriate response?**
>
> A. Meet with both of them separately, focus on the module and the deadlines, and agree a clear ownership
>    split that you confirm with the team lead.
> B. Raise it in the next team meeting, describe the ownership ambiguity objectively, and ask for a decision.
> C. Ignore the interpersonal part and just take over the module yourself so the work moves.
> D. Send both of them a note saying the arguments are disruptive and ask them to behave professionally.

**Answer: D**

**Reasoning (scoring backwards):**

1. The judgement word is **LEAST**. Eliminate the professional option first → **A is out**.
2. Is the passive option the worst? No. C takes over and unblocks the delivery — it is lazy, not harmful. **C is out.**
3. B raises it objectively in a meeting and asks for a decision — defensible, if not ideal. **B is out.**
4. D is the only option that attacks the **people** rather than the **work**. It creates defensiveness, invites
   escalation, and solves nothing.

**Why D is the least appropriate:** it labels two colleagues as unprofessional without a specific incident
being examined, delivers no solution, and hands both of them a grievance to carry. It is the aggressive archetype
aimed at a conflict instead of at the conflict.

**Note:** A — the correct *professional* response — is not the answer here. This is exactly the trap the "least
appropriate" version is designed to set.

### Q2. You disagree with your manager

> **Q.** Your manager has chosen a design that you believe will not meet the non-functional requirement for
> response time. You have the profiling data to support your view. The manager has already shared the decision
> with the client.
>
> A. Accept the decision without comment, since it has already been communicated externally.
> B. Prepare a short written note with the profiling data and the specific risk, request a 15-minute review with
>    your manager, and — if the decision stands — support it while recording the risk in the project log.
> C. Send the profiling data to the client so that they can make an informed decision.
> D. Discuss it with other team members to build support for your position before raising it with your manager.

**Answer: B**

| Option | Label | Failure |
|--------|-------|---------|
| A | P | Silent non-compliance. You have data and a duty to share it; "already communicated" is irrelevant to whether the client is at risk. |
| B | **F** | Evidence + formal review + disagree-and-commit. ✅ |
| C | O | Goes over your manager to the client. The client is not the right level, and this is a technical dispute about a requirement you are accountable for. |
| D | A | Campaigning. It builds politics instead of a case, damages your working relationship, and makes your manager defensive. |

**The key sentence in B:** *"if the decision stands, support it while recording the risk."* Professional options
often contain a conditional second clause — that is the **disagree-and-commit** behaviour the exam wants.

### Q3. A teammate's mistake

> **Q.** A teammate's faulty configuration change causes a staging environment outage for 2 hours. Your team lead
> is travelling. The client demo is at 4 p.m.
>
> A. Restore the previous configuration yourself, message the team lead with a summary of what happened, the
>    cause, and the preventive action you recommend, and check the environment again before the demo.
> B. Tell the client that the outage was caused by a configuration error by a specific team member.
> C. Do not restore anything until the teammate returns, so the root cause is not disturbed.
> D. Write a long email to the whole project explaining in detail how the outage happened.

**Answer: A**

| Option | Label | Failure |
|--------|-------|---------|
| A | **F** | Contain, inform, document, prevent, verify. ✅ |
| B | A | Blames a named colleague to a client. Blame to the client damages the team and does not restore the environment. |
| C | P | Lets the demo fail while waiting. "Preserving the root cause" is a post-hoc justification for inaction. |
| D | O/A | A long public email about someone else's failure is a process violation; it does not address the demo. |

**Bonus signal in A:** "check the environment again before the demo" — that is the **close the loop** step.

### Q4. Your own mistake

> **Q.** You forgot to submit a compliance form before the deadline. It affects a client's onboarding file. The
> form can still be submitted, but it will be processed late.
>
> A. Submit it late quietly and hope the delay does not affect the client's onboarding.
> B. Inform the responsible team immediately that you missed the submission, state the impact on the client's
>    onboarding timeline, submit the form right away, and set up a check with them to confirm the client file is
>    complete.
> C. Tell your manager that the deadline was unrealistic because you were also assigned other work.
> D. Wait until the client complains, then explain the situation.

**Answer: B**

| Option | Label | Failure |
|--------|-------|---------|
| A | P | Hidden delay. The client discovers it, and a processing delay becomes a trust issue. |
| B | **F** | Own → inform early → impact → immediate action → verify. ✅ |
| C | A | Shifts responsibility to your workload. Even if true, it is not the action that fixes the client's file. |
| D | P | Reactive and much worse. Waiting for the complaint removes the entire value of early disclosure. |

**Note the structure of B:** it is the only option with **four** verbs of action. Length here is evidence, not
decoration.

### Q5. Motivational — "Why do you want to work at Infosys?"

> **Q.** Why do you want to work at Infosys?
>
> A. Because Infosys is one of the largest IT companies and offers a good salary and brand name.
> B. Because I could not get a job elsewhere, so it is the best option available to me.
> C. Because of the scale of the work and the structured learning — I want to build strong foundations in
>    enterprise delivery on large global projects, and I would contribute my Java and DBMS skills while
>    learning how those systems are actually run in industry.
> D. Because my friend is already working here and told me it is a good place to start a career.

**Answer: C**

| Option | Component check | Verdict |
|--------|-----------------|---------|
| A | Transactional, no fit, no contribution | ❌ |
| B | Desperation; no professional motivation | ❌ |
| C | Specific (enterprise delivery on global projects) + mutual (contributes Java and DBMS skills) + Infosys-relevant (structured learning at scale) + forward-looking | ✅ |
| D | Referral, not a reason | ❌ |

**What C does that the others do not:** it names a *domain of work*, a *learning direction*, and a
*contribution*. It could plausibly be written by any of the other candidates in your batch, which is exactly
what an examiner wants.

### Q6. Motivational — "Why did you choose this stream?"

> **Q.** You have been allotted the Data Engineering stream. Why?
>
> A. Because it was the stream that was left open when I finished filling the form.
> B. Because I want to work on data — I enjoy SQL and DBMS, I want to turn that into pipeline and ETL work on
>    large datasets, and I want to learn how data platforms are engineered for real enterprise use rather than
>    only using them.
> C. Because data science and AI are the future and everyone says so.
> D. Because my friend took it and said the trainers are good.

**Answer: B**

| Option | Failure |
|--------|---------|
| A | No agency; sounds like a default, not a choice. |
| B | **Names the actual work (pipelines, ETL, large datasets), the genuine skill (SQL/DBMS), and a learning direction.** ✅ |
| C | Trend appeal, not personal fit. An examiner wants to know what *you* would do, not what the market says. |
| D | Again, a referral. |

**Comparison trap:** C sounds more "enthusiastic" than B, but enthusiasm without specifics is empty. The
exam rewards **fit evidence**.

## Quick rules

- In a "least appropriate" question, the answer is the option that **attacks a person** or **creates a new
  problem**, not the option that does nothing.
- Disagreement with a manager: **evidence → review → commit**. Three beats, in that order.
- Your own mistake: own it, report the impact, fix it, verify. Never "it wasn't my fault".
- A teammate's mistake: contain and prevent. Never name the person to the client.
- Motivational answers: **specific work + mutual contribution + learning direction + forward-looking**.
- "Everyone says data is the future" is not a reason. "I enjoy SQL and I want to build pipelines" is.

## Common traps

- **Answering a "least appropriate" item with the professional option** because it is the best answer you have
  seen. It is still the wrong pick for that question.
- **Confusing "professional but passive" with "professional".** Taking over a module yourself to avoid conflict
  (Q1 option C) is not the same as fixing the ownership problem.
- **Over-explaining in a written motivation answer.** Three sentences, as in the technique file.
- **Writing a motivation answer that could be pasted into any company's form.** If it could be sent to TCS, it is
  not specific enough.
- **Naming a personal grudge.** If the scenario includes a friend or a rival, that is a hint that the correct
  option must stay professional about the person.

## Checklist

- [ ] I circled **best** or **least** before answering.
- [ ] For "least", I eliminated the professional option first.
- [ ] For disagreement, my answer contains evidence → review → commit.
- [ ] For my own mistake, my answer starts with ownership and states the impact.
- [ ] My motivational answer names the specific work and what I contribute.
