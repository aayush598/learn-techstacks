# The Infosys-Professional Response Framework

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Behavioural & Scenario-Based ·  _Estimated reading time: 8 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [The four-option answer scheme](01-the-four-option-answer-scheme.md)

## In one sentence
**The professional answer to any Infosys-style scenario always does the same five things — take ownership, communicate early, tell the right person at the right level, document it, and close the loop.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- A new hire at a delivery firm has almost no authority. The test checks whether you know that the correct move
  is **not** to fix things alone and **not** to shout, but to *route the problem to the person who can act on it*.
- The same five steps appear behind every correct option in every batch: ownership, early communication,
  correct escalation level, evidence, follow-up.
- Learn the framework once and you can answer **any** scenario stem, including ones you have never seen.

## Core concept

### The 5-step framework

| Step | Name | What you do | Signals that prove it |
|------|------|-----------|----------------------|
| 1 | **OWN** | Take responsibility for the outcome, not just your part of it | "I will take ownership", "I will ensure this is resolved" |
| 2 | **COMMUNICATE EARLY** | Share the facts as soon as you have them — the earlier the better, because early information gives the team options | "inform immediately", "share the details early", "flag it before it becomes a risk" |
| 3 | **RIGHT PERSON, RIGHT LEVEL** | Route it through your line of reporting first; escalate further only when impact demands it | "inform my manager / the delivery manager / the client relationship manager" |
| 4 | **DOCUMENT** | Put it in writing so there is a record and the reasoning survives | "share the analysis in writing", "log it in the tracker", "attach the evidence" |
| 5 | **CLOSE THE LOOP** | Confirm the resolution and check that the action was completed | "follow up after the review", "confirm that the fix is in place" |

⚠️ Steps 2–5 in one option is the typical shape of a correct answer. Steps 1 and 3 are the two that kill the
distractors.

### The escalation ladder

```
Level 0  Do nothing, hope it goes away          → NEVER
Level 1  Fix it silently, mention it later       → passive trap
Level 2  Inform your immediate owner/team lead   → usually CORRECT
Level 3  Inform the delivery / project manager   → correct when client
                                                   impact or a deadline
                                                   is at stake
Level 4  Client relationship manager / account    → correct only for a
                                                   client-visible issue,
                                                   and normally via Level 3
Level 5  HR, top management, the client's IT head → over-escalation trap
                                                   unless there is a legal,
                                                   safety, or ethical
                                                   emergency
```

**The exam tests Level 3 discipline.** Most fresh graduates reach for Level 5 because it feels like ownership.

### The 12 scenario archetypes and the professional answer shape

| # | Archetype | Trigger words | Professional answer shape |
|---|-----------|---------------|---------------------------|
| 1 | **Missed deadline** | "delay", "behind schedule", "not going to finish" | Assess remaining work → inform the manager early with options (scope cut / deadline shift / resource help) → document the plan → track daily |
| 2 | **Colleague takes credit** | "he took my work", "the manager praised him" | Approach the colleague privately first, factually, without accusation → if unresolved, inform the manager with evidence of your contribution, framed as project visibility, not complaint |
| 3 | **Disagree with your manager** | "I think this is the wrong approach" | Prepare a written rationale with data → request a review meeting → if the decision stands, support it and log the risk |
| 4 | **Angry customer** | "the client is upset", "escalating complaint" | Stay calm, listen fully, do not argue or promise what you cannot deliver → acknowledge, restate the requirement, commit to a response time → follow up in writing |
| 5 | **Bug found before release** | "critical defect", "found a problem in testing" | Immediately quantify the impact → inform the module owner and delivery manager → propose fix/roll-back/release options → document in the defect tracker → verify after the fix |
| 6 | **You are overloaded** | "too many tasks", "unable to manage" | Do not silently drop work → list tasks with priority and effort → tell the manager with a proposed priority order → renegotiate scope or ask for help |
| 7 | **Team conflict** | "argument with a teammate", "friction in the team" | Have a direct, private, factual conversation first → if unresolved, involve the team lead as a facilitator, not as a judge → focus on the task, not the person |
| 8 | **A teammate's mistake** | "he made a mistake", "the code broke" | Do not blame publicly → check the facts, then help contain the impact → report the risk through the right channel → support the fix |
| 9 | **Mistake you made** | "I made an error", "I forgot to submit" | Own it immediately, state the impact, propose the recovery, and tell the person affected before they find out |
| 10 | **Unclear requirement** | "the requirement is ambiguous" | Do not guess silently → ask the clarification question in writing → record the agreed interpretation → flag the risk if the deadline is close |
| 11 | **Ethics / policy conflict** | "asked to do something against policy" | Refuse the specific act, state the policy, offer a compliant alternative, and escalate to the responsible authority if pressure continues |
| 12 | **New/unknown technology** | "I have never used this" | Acknowledge the gap, ask for the reference material, propose a small scoped task, pair with someone experienced, and set a check-in point |

### The escalation triggers table

| Situation | Correct first recipient | Timing |
|-----------|------------------------|--------|
| Task at risk of missing your own commitment | Your team lead | Same day |
| Sprint/release slipping, client visible | Delivery / project manager | Within hours |
| Client complaint | Client relationship manager (via your lead) | Same day |
| Personal HR issue (leave, medical, conduct) | HR / your manager | As soon as possible |
| Risk to a client due to your error | Your manager, then the CRM | Immediately |
| Security or data issue | Security team / management | Immediately, no delay |

## Worked example

> **Q.** During a client demo, the client asks a question about a feature that Ananya knows was not completed.
> The account manager answers confidently with an estimate. The client looks unconvinced.
>
> A. Say nothing. The account manager is senior and the client will move on.
> B. After the demo, inform the account manager in writing that the feature is incomplete, share the current
>    status, and propose a revised date and a demo workaround.
> C. Interrupt the account manager during the demo and correct him in front of the client.
> D. Tell the client directly that the account manager overstated the feature's readiness.

**Step 1 — trigger:** risk of a false commitment + reputational exposure.
**Step 2 — framework on each option:**

| Option | OWN | EARLY | RIGHT LEVEL | DOCUMENT | CLOSE | Verdict |
|--------|-----|------|-------------|----------|-------|---------|
| A | ❌ | ❌ | — | ❌ | ❌ | Passive — lets a wrong commitment stand |
| B | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | **Professional** |
| C | partial | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | Right instinct, wrong moment — creates a scene and damages a colleague |
| D | partial | ✅ | ❌ | partial | ❌ | Over-escalating + aggressive — bypasses the account manager and damages him publicly |

**Answer: B.**

**Why not A:** Silence here is not neutrality; it is complicity. If the client later finds the feature missing, the
credibility cost is far higher than a short written correction now.

**Why not C:** Being right is not the same as being effective. Correcting a senior person in front of a client is a
communication failure even when the facts are correct. The professional instinct is: fix it, but not like this.

**Why not D:** The client is not the account manager's judge. This bypasses the CRM, damages a colleague, and hands
the client an internal conflict.

## Quick rules

- **Own the outcome** even when the cause is somebody else's. "It is not my fault" is not in the correct option.
- **Early > complete.** A partial truth told now beats a perfect truth told after the deadline.
- **Level 2 or 3 is your default first move.** Escalate further only when the impact demands it.
- **Put it in writing.** Verbal-only escalation has no audit trail and no protection.
- **Always propose.** "I will inform" without "and here is what I propose" is half an answer.
- **Never a public correction** of a senior colleague or a client-facing colleague.
- **Never a threat.** Resignation, refusal, and "I will complain" are not professional options.
- When the scenario involves **ethics or policy**, the correct option refuses the act, cites the policy, and offers
  an alternative.

## Common traps

- **Helping is not owning.** "I will help my teammate fix it" is good. "I will take ownership of the outcome and
  keep the team informed" is what the option needs.
- **Informing without proposing** reads as dumping a problem on the manager. Add the plan.
- **Escalating to protect yourself.** Blowing up the chain to avoid a difficult conversation fails every scenario
  where the trigger is small.
- **Ignoring hierarchy because "it's a team".** Infosys is a large, layered organisation. The exam wants you to
  respect that.
- **Assuming silence protects you.** In every scenario where the trigger touches a client or a deadline, silence is
  the worst option on the sheet.
- **Answering the emotion instead of the problem.** "I would remain calm and empathetic" is filler. Filler is what
  the distractor is made of.

## Checklist

- [ ] I identified the trigger archetype before reading the options.
- [ ] I found the option with **ownership + a named recipient + a proposed action**.
- [ ] The recipient I chose is at the right level for the impact (Level 2–3 by default).
- [ ] The option includes written documentation or a follow-up.
- [ ] I did not pick an option with blame, a threat, a public correction, or a skipped hierarchy.
