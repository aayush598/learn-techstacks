# The Four-Option Answer Scheme

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Behavioural & Scenario-Based ·  _Estimated reading time: 7 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [Behavioural topic README](../README.md)

## In one sentence
**Infosys-style behavioural MCQs are built from exactly four option archetypes — the passive option, the aggressive option, the over-escalating option, and the correct professional option — so if you can name the archetype in 10 seconds you can eliminate three answers in 30 seconds.**

## Why the pre-test tests this

- These questions are not testing "what would a good person do". They are testing **whether you understand
  workplace process at a delivery company**: hierarchy, escalation, ownership, documentation, and communication.
- The four options are *constructed*, not sampled. The distractors exist to punish three specific mistakes:
  **avoidance**, **emotional reactivity**, and **process jumping**.
- The same four-archetype set appears in the questions that ask for the *least* appropriate behaviour — only the
  direction of the scoring changes.

## Core concept

### The four archetypes

| # | Archetype | What it sounds like | Why it is wrong | Signal words |
|---|-----------|---------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| 1 | **Passive** | "Stay quiet", "ignore", "hope it resolves", "say nothing", "wait and see", "avoid the issue" | Creates hidden risk. The problem grows and you are blamed for the delay. | ignore, avoid, silence, leave it, wait, hope, pretend |
| 2 | **Aggressive** | "Blame X", "argue", "complain loudly", "refuse to work", "confront", "escalate emotionally" | Destroys the team and fixes nothing. There is a process for every one of these. | blame, complain, argue, confront, shout, refuse, insist, accuse |
| 3 | **Over-escalating** | "Inform the CEO", "bypass my manager", "escalate to the client directly", "involve HR immediately", "threaten to resign" | Skips the line of reporting. You are measured on process, not on loudness. | CEO, HR directly, directly inform the client, without informing, immediately escalate, resign |
| 4 | **Professional** | "Inform my manager / the owner of the module, explain the impact, propose a plan, document it" | — (this is the answer) | inform, escalate appropriately, take ownership, propose, document, discuss, schedule a review |

### The strongest positive signals on the sheet

A correct option almost always contains **at least two** of these:

1. **Named recipient** — "my delivery manager", "the module owner", "the client relationship manager", not "someone".
2. **Specific action** — "share the impact analysis", "propose two options", "put it in writing".
3. **Ownership** — "I will take ownership", "I will ensure it is tracked".
4. **Proportionality** — timing matches severity ("immediately" for a client-facing outage, "at the next review" for a
   style preference).
5. **Fix orientation** — "how do we recover", "what is the plan", not only "what went wrong".
6. **Evidence** — "with the test results", "in the defect tracker", "documented in the mail".

### The strongest negative signals

Kill the option immediately if it contains any of these:

| Signal | Why it is wrong | Alternative you should look for |
|--------|-----------------|--------------------------------|
| Names a person as the cause | Blame is never the professional answer | Owner + impact + plan |
| "Immediately" + skipping your own manager | Process jumping | Inform your manager first, then let them route it |
| "Because I was right" | Earning a point, not solving one | Evidence-based disagreement |
| "My manager will not understand" | Pre-judging | Prepare a written rationale |
| "Personal work on the company machine" | Policy violation | Use personal time and devices |
| Ending the relationship | Not a resolution strategy | Formal escalation as the last step |

### Why "professional" is usually the *longest* option

Writers build the correct option as the only one that includes a **complete chain**: action → recipient → reasoning →
follow-up. It runs 20–30 words. The distractors are 6–12 words.

⚠️ Length alone is not a rule — a distractor can be long. But **if exactly one option has a second clause, that
option is the intended answer 8 times out of 10.** Use it as a tie-breaker only.

## Worked example

> **Q.** Two days before the delivery deadline, Rahul finds that the module he owns has a defect that will fail
> client acceptance testing. His team lead has already left for the day. Rahul can either stay and fix it, or
> leave for his train.
>
> A. Stay late, fix the defect, and tell no one since the deadline will still be met.
> B. Message his team lead with the defect details, the impact, and a proposed fix plan, and copy the
>    delivery manager for visibility.
> C. Wait until the next morning so his team lead can decide what to do.
> D. Tell the client relationship manager that the defect is fatal and the release will have to be called off.

**Step 1 — trigger:** deadline + risk. Not a personal conflict; it is a delivery risk.
**Step 2 — archetype each option:**

| Option | Archetype | Note |
|--------|-----------|------|
| A | Passive | Silence. Risk stays hidden; if the fix fails, the whole team finds out on Monday. |
| B | Professional | Named recipient, impact, plan, and visibility. |
| C | Passive / under-communicating | Delay is the risk here, not the other way round. |
| D | Over-escalating | Jumps past the line of reporting, escalates a 2-day-old defect as fatal, and makes a release decision that is not his to make. |

**Answer: B.**

**Why not A:** "Fix it and say nothing" is the single most common trap in this section. It looks responsible and
it is the opposite — it removes the information the team needs to plan. If the fix fails, the discovery cost is
paid twice.

**Why not C:** The trigger is time-critical. "Wait for tomorrow" removes the 2 hours that are the entire point.

**Why not D:** Escalating to the CRM before informing the line of reporting, and using "fatal" when the defect has
a known fix, is textbook over-escalation. The professional version of D is: inform the team lead, ask for a
decision on the release plan, and escalate through the delivery manager if the impact changes.

## Quick rules

- Label the archetypes **before** you read the options carefully — it makes the options legible.
- Exactly one option is professional. If you see two, one of them is missing a recipient or a plan.
- **Silence, blame, and skipping the hierarchy** are the three failures the exam wants you to detect.
- "Immediately" is not automatically correct; **proportionate timing** is. Escalate immediately only when the
  trigger is already hurting a client or a delivery.
- Never pick an option whose subject is a third party ("Rahul should…", "The team should…"). The stem asks
  what **you** would do.
- If the option mentions "appropriate escalation" or "as per the process", it is almost always the answer.
- If the option ends with a *threat* ("I will refuse…", "I will complain to HR unless…"), it is a distractor.

## Common traps

- **The trigger changes the answer.** "Angry customer" and "a colleague's untidy code" need different
  recipients, but the *shape* of the professional answer is identical: act → inform the right person → propose.
- **Over-escalation reads as confidence.** "I will inform the client's IT head directly" sounds bold. It is a
  process violation and the exam marks it wrong every time.
- **The aggressive option feels true.** You *are* annoyed. The test is not about whether you feel it; it is about
  what a delivery firm wants recorded.
- **The passive option looks calm.** "Let it go" is calm. It is also the answer that ends careers when the
  problem reaches a client.
- **Choosing the option that fixes the symptom fastest** rather than the one that keeps the process intact.
  Fixing without informing is still wrong.

## Checklist

- [ ] I can label any given option as passive / aggressive / over-escalating / professional in under 10 seconds.
- [ ] Before choosing, I confirm the option names **a specific recipient and a specific action**.
- [ ] I checked the stem for **best vs least appropriate** and adjusted my elimination direction.
- [ ] I did not pick an option whose subject is a third party.
- [ ] I can state the 5-step professional framework from memory: own → communicate early → right person → document → close the loop.
