# Error Correction & Vocabulary — Question Bank

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Error Correction & Vocabulary ·  _20 questions · ~30 min_

**How to use this bank:** 12 error-correction items in the four-segment format (find the single incorrect part),
then 8 vocabulary items. Answer everything on paper with a 90-second cap each. Answers + one-line reasoning at
the bottom.

**For the error items:** read the sentence once, complete. Test the two verb-bearing segments first (agreement
and tense), then the other two (articles, prepositions, modifiers).

# Answer key

| Q | Answer | Why (one line) |
|---|--------|----------------|
| 1 | D | "The client" is singular, so it takes "has been informed" — not "have been informed". |
| 2 | C | The fixed phrase is "due to"; "due of" is not used with a noun. |
| 3 | B | "Hour" begins with a vowel sound, so the article is "an hour", not "a hour". |
| 4 | A | The plural subject "results" takes "were", not "was". |
| 5 | D | In reported speech the tense shifts back: "said that he **would** complete", not "will". |
| 6 | A | An introductory subordinate clause takes a comma: "Although the test environment was unavailable, the team completed…". |
| 7 | C | The verb is "depend **on**", not "depend of". |
| 8 | A | "Each" is singular and takes a singular verb: "Each of the trainees **was** given". |
| 9 | C | "Equipment" is uncountable: "new equipment for the lab", never "equipments". |
| 10 | B | You **assure** a person; you **ensure** a thing. The client is the person → "assured the client". |
| 11 | D | The plural possessive of "client" is "clients'" before the noun: "the clients' complaints for the month". |
| 12 | B | "Discuss" takes the object directly: "discussed the new requirements", not "discussed about". |
| 13 | C | Candid means truthful and direct — "frank" is the match; "reluctant" and "secretive" are near-opposites. |
| 14 | A | Meticulous means showing great attention to detail — "thorough" is the match; "hurried" is the opposite. |
| 15 | D | Abate means to become less intense — "lessen" is the match; "intensify" is the opposite. |
| 16 | B | Pragmatic means dealing with things practically — "practical" is the match; "idealistic" and "theoretical" are contrasts. |
| 17 | A | The opposite of frank is evasive; "frank" repeats the target word and is therefore wrong. |
| 18 | D | Exacerbate means to make a problem worse, so the opposite is "improve"; the other three are synonyms of the target. |
| 19 | C | The idiom means to face an unpleasant situation with courage and determination. |
| 20 | B | Writing materials are "stationery" (uncountable); "stationary" means not moving. |

## What each error item was testing

| Q | Error family | Segment position |
|---|-------------|------------------|
| 1 | Subject-verb agreement | D |
| 2 | Preposition (fixed phrase) | C |
| 3 | Article by sound | B |
| 4 | Subject-verb agreement (plural head) | A |
| 5 | Reported speech tense | D |
| 6 | Comma after introductory clause | A |
| 7 | Preposition collocation | C |
| 8 | "Each" = singular | A |
| 9 | Uncountable noun | C |
| 10 | assure vs ensure | B |
| 11 | Plural possessive | D |
| 12 | Verb + preposition | B |

**Balance check:** 3 items per segment position. If you are consistently wrong at one position, that is a specific,
fixable habit — not a general weakness.
