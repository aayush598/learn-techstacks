# Behavioural & Scenario-Based — Question Bank

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Behavioural & Scenario-Based ·  _24 questions · ~40 min_

**How to use this bank:** answer all 24 questions on paper with a 90-second cap each, and pick the option you
would actually say out loud. Score yourself honestly. Answers + one-line reasoning at the bottom.

**Scoring bands**

| Score | Meaning | Action |
|-------|---------|--------|
| 20–24 | Paper-ready | Move to the mock tests |
| 15–19 | Framework is there, phrasing is not | Re-read the 5-step response framework and the archetype playbook |
| 10–14 | Guessing | Work the topic README, then redo this bank untimed |
| 0–9 | Start here | [Behavioural topic README](../01-Behavioural-Scenario-Based/README.md) first |

**The 90-second rule applies here.** Read the situation, name the archetype of each option in one word, and
choose. Do not read the explanation of a question you have not yet attempted.

## Q1. Friday, 6:40 p.m. A client calls and asks for a status report by 7:00 p.m. You started the report at
6:15 p.m. and it needs another 90 minutes to be complete and accurate. What do you do?

A. Tell the client you cannot finish by 7:00 p.m., that the report requires more time to be accurate, and that you
will send it by 7:45 p.m. with a short interim note now.
B. Say yes to the client to keep the relationship smooth, then send an incomplete report at 7:00 p.m. and a
corrected version later.
C. Tell the client the report cannot be done by 7:00 p.m. and that they should raise it with your manager because
the request is unreasonable.
D. Say the report is already sent and continue with your other work until the client complains.

**Tests:** time pressure + honesty under pressure.

## Q2. A teammate on your team consistently misses the deadlines you jointly depend on. You have raised it
informally twice with no result. What is the best next step?

A. Continue to cover their share silently and mention it to your manager in a general team discussion, without
naming them.

B. Tell the manager privately that the teammate is not pulling their weight, and that you have had to complete
their work for the last three sprints.

C. Send an email to the whole team listing the tasks that were late and asking the owner to reply.

D. Speak to the teammate directly, mention the specific deadlines missed and the impact on your work, and agree
a written plan with checkpoints; tell your manager if it does not improve.

**Tests:** escalation ladder + documentation.

---

## Q3. A senior colleague's technical approach to a module you have reviewed differs from the one you would
have chosen. The module is due the next day. What do you do?

A. Implement your own approach quietly, because you are confident it is better, and mention the difference in the
sprint retrospective.
B. Implement the senior's approach exactly, and raise your alternative in the retrospective.
C. Put both approaches in writing, state the specific risk of each in one line, and ask for a 10-minute decision
before you start coding.
D. Ask the team lead to decide without explaining your reasoning, to avoid appearing argumentative.

**Tests:** disagreeing upward without over-escalating.

## Q4. A colleague from another team asks you to email a spreadsheet containing named client employee details so
they can work on it from home. What do you do?

A. Send the file, since the colleague works on the same client and is only asking to save time.
B. Send the file with the file name changed so it is not obvious what it contains.
C. Ask which client data is actually needed, offer to provide only the fields required, and point them to the
approved secure sharing process for the rest.
D. Refuse the request and copy the client's account manager on the refusal.

**Tests:** confidentiality + proportionate response.

## Q5. You configured a production environment incorrectly and it caused a 40-minute outage for one client.
Nobody else knows. What do you do?

A. Stay quiet, fix the configuration, and check the monitoring more often so it does not happen again.
B. Tell your team lead immediately, with what happened, what you fixed, and what you are changing to prevent a
repeat; log it in the incident record.
C. Write a detailed self-criticism in the team channel so everyone learns from it.
D. Wait to see whether the client mentions it before telling anyone.

**Tests:** ownership + speed of escalation.

## Q6. Two colleagues are arguing loudly about a design decision near your desk. Ten people are working in the
area. What do you do?

A. Move to a private space yourself, and if the discussion does not settle within a few minutes, suggest they
continue in a meeting room with the team lead involved if they are still stuck.

B. Interrupt them and tell them to stop arguing, because it is disturbing the team.

C. Ignore it. They will resolve it themselves.

D. Join the argument and take the side you believe is correct.

**Tests:** conflict de-escalation without taking sides.

## Q7. You have been in the team for two weeks. You are given a task that uses a framework you have never
worked with, and it is due in four days. What do you do?

A. Tell your manager you cannot do the task and ask for a reassignment.

B. Tell your team lead what you have not used, ask for the smallest working version of the task with a named
reviewer for the unfamiliar parts, and state what you will complete each day.

C. Start coding immediately from memory, and ask questions in the code review when the problems surface.

D. Look for a tutorial, learn the framework fully in one night, and deliver the whole task solo.

**Tests:** asking for help early + incremental delivery.

## Q8. Three weeks into a development cycle, the client changes a requirement that will add approximately ten
person-days of work. What do you do?

A. Absorb the change quietly and work longer hours to keep the original date.

B. Tell the client the change cannot be done and that the original scope is final.

C. Escalate directly to your project manager's manager, since the client is being unreasonable.

D. Raise a written impact note the same day: what changes, the effort it adds, and two date/scope options with
your recommendation, then book a call with the client.

**Tests:** structured impact communication + option framing.

## Q9. In a project report, your manager has attributed a module that you designed and built to another team
member. What do you do?

A. Say nothing. Attribution is not worth raising a conflict over.
B. Reply to the report thread, correcting the attribution, and copy your manager and the other team member.
C. Send your manager a short note with the specific evidence (ticket IDs, commits, review comments) and ask them
to correct the attribution in the next version of the report.
D. Send the whole team a message listing everything you contributed.

**Tests:** assertive communication + evidence-based.

## Q10. A colleague who is new to the project asks you a question while you are working on a task that is on
your critical path, due in two hours. What do you do?

A. Tell them you are busy and they should figure it out themselves.

B. Give them the answer in one sentence, point them to the relevant document or file, and offer 15 minutes at the
end of your task.

C. Answer fully right away, however long it takes, because they asked.

D. Ask your manager to reassign the new colleague to someone else.

**Tests:** helpfulness with a boundary.

## Q11. Your manager assigns you a new task on the day you are finishing another task you promised to deliver by
end of day. What do you do?

A. Take the new task and miss the deadline on the original one, then explain at the end of the day.

B. Decline the new task outright, since you are busy.

C. Tell your manager the original task is due today and ask which of the two should take priority, offering your
recommendation.

D. Do both tasks badly and report both as complete.

**Tests:** prioritisation with your manager, not around them.

## Q12. You are asked to approve a vendor invoice for a service whose deliverables you cannot verify. The
vendor is known to the client and the amount is within budget. What do you do?

A. Approve it, since the vendor is approved and the amount is inside budget.
B. Approve it but add a note saying you could not verify the deliverables.
C. Ask the project owner for the deliverables record or evidence of completion, and approve once the link between
invoice items and accepted work is established; escalate if it cannot be established.
D. Reject the invoice and email the vendor directly telling them your organisation does not work with them
anymore.

**Tests:** proportional escalation + evidence before approval.

## Q13. You are invited to a one-hour meeting with 12 invitees and no agenda. What do you do?

A. Ask the organiser, before the meeting, for the agenda and the decisions expected, and say which of those
decisions need you; if there is no agenda, attend only the relevant part.

B. Decline the invitation and send your comments by email instead.

C. Attend, listen, and let the meeting run as scheduled.

D. Invite two more colleagues, in case a wider view is useful.

**Tests:** meeting discipline + polite scoping.

## Q14. During a code review you notice a defect in a module that was deployed to production last month. It
affects an error path only, and no customer has reported it. What do you do?

A. Fix it in the next release cycle along with other queued changes, since it is low impact.
B. Raise a defect ticket with severity, reproduction steps, and the affected version, notify the release owner,
and agree a fix window — do not close it silently.
C. Mention it in a general team meeting as an example of why reviews matter.
D. Test it once more; if it still fails, wait for the next release train to pick it up.

**Tests:** defect discipline + transparency without alarm.

## Q15. A milestone you were responsible for has slipped by two days and the client has complained to your
manager. What do you do?

A. Send a note to the client explaining the technical reasons for the delay.

B. Ask the client for a week, and make the date work later by reducing testing.

C. Tell the client directly that the delay was caused by another team and give them the name of that team's
lead.

D. Meet your manager first, give them the facts and your recovery plan, then let them decide what the client
hears and when.

**Tests:** escalation order + no blame-shifting.

## Q16. A colleague asks you to tell the client that a defect they caused will be fixed in the next release,
when in fact you do not know when it will be fixed. What do you do?

A. Say you will mention the release target only if the release manager confirms it in writing.

B. Agree to say it, since the colleague is accountable for the defect.

C. Tell the client the exact date you believe it will be fixed, so they have a concrete plan.

D. Repeat it after the colleague, so you are not the source of the statement.

**Tests:** honesty + not becoming a second channel for an unverified claim.

## Q17. It is Saturday evening. Your team lead sends a message asking you to come in and fix a demo issue that
could have been fixed by a one-line config change during the working day. What do you do?

A. Go in immediately, without checking whether a remote fix is possible.

B. Ignore the message until Monday.

C. Reply that weekend work needs to be approved by your project manager first.

D. Reply that you are available remotely, ask whether the one-line config change can be applied and verified
without anyone travelling in, and offer to do it tonight if a physical presence is genuinely required.

**Tests:** proportionate response + practical alternatives.

## Q18. A new colleague asks you a question every ten minutes through the day, and it takes you roughly 20
minutes to answer each one. You are behind on your own work. What do you do?

A. Answer every question as it comes, because they are new and need the support.

B. Tell them to stop asking you questions and to read the documentation.

C. Ask your manager to reassign their mentoring to someone else.

D. Set aside a fixed 30-minute slot each day for their questions, share the key documentation links up front, and
tell them the slot so they can batch their questions.

**Tests:** mentoring structure + boundary + batching.

## Q19. While reviewing a module, you find that a developer has hardcoded a database password in the source
file, which is committed to the repository. What do you do?

A. Raise it in the sprint review as a code-quality issue to be fixed later.
B. Raise it immediately to the security/tech lead with the file location and impact, recommend rotating the
credential, and remove it from the code and the repository history through the standard process.
C. Fix it yourself, replace the password with another one, and do not mention it to anyone.
D. Ask the developer privately to fix it before the next release, and leave it to them.

**Tests:** severity-appropriate escalation + credential rotation.

## Q20. Your manager asks you to present analysis to the client in which one finding has to be described
favourably, even though the data does not support that description. What do you do?

A. Say to your manager before the meeting that you are not comfortable with a description the data does not
support, propose the most favourable statement the evidence does allow, and offer to present that instead.

B. Present it favourably and add a private caveat to your own notes afterwards.

C. Present it favourably, since the framing is your manager's call.

D. Present the data neutrally without any framing, and let the client draw their own conclusion.

**Tests:** integrity + negotiating the alternative rather than refusing outright.

## Q21. A peer on your team is missing their targets for a second month. You are not their manager. What do you
do?

A. Tell their manager, so the manager can act on it.

B. Nothing — you do not manage them, so it is not your place.

C. Speak to the peer first, in private, about what is getting in the way, and offer one concrete way you can help
within your own capacity.

D. Send them a copy of your own work as an example to follow.

**Tests:** peer feedback, boundaries of role, support offered.

## Q22. Two team leads disagree about which team owns a task that both teams will be measured on. You are
assigned to the task. What do you do?

A. Pick the team you think has the better argument and start work.

B. Split the task arbitrarily and hope both are satisfied.

C. Ask the project manager to decide, and wait for the decision before doing anything.

D. Ask both leads to state their position in writing, and in the meantime start on the parts of the task that are
common to both views, so the project does not wait.

**Tests:** unblocking while a decision is pending, and keeping the decision with the right level.

## Q23. Two weeks before a release, you realise the requirements document says one thing and the client
actually needs something else. The requirement was confirmed in writing by the client two months ago. What do
you do?

A. Build what the client actually needs, since that is what they want.

B. Raise the gap in writing the same day with the evidence of both, state the effort difference for each option,
and get the client to confirm the change against the change-control process.

C. Build what the signed document says, since that is what was agreed.

D. Delay the release until the client happens to notice the difference.

**Tests:** change control + evidence.

## Q24. You are asked to update a piece of technical documentation that is owned by another team. The content is
out of date and wrong. What do you do?

A. Do not touch it. Send a note to the owning team listing the specific errors and offering to draft the
corrections for their review.

B. Update it directly, since a wrong document is worse than no document.

C. Delete the document, since inaccurate documentation is a risk.

D. Add a note at the top saying "this document may be out of date" and leave the rest.

**Tests:** ownership boundaries + offering help rather than acting unilaterally.

## Pattern analysis — what the 24 questions are made of

| Archetype of the distractor | How many times it appears | What it looks like |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| **Passive / avoidant** | 15 | Ignore it, absorb it, do nothing, wait for someone else |
| **Aggressive / accusatory** | 13 | Blame publicly, escalate over the head, refuse, argue |
| **Over-escalating** | 14 | Straight to the top, cc the client, formal complaint for a first incident |
| **Professional** (the answer) | 24 | Own it early, name the fact, offer options, document, close the loop |

**Note the pattern:** the same behaviour can be right or wrong depending on the **level** and the **timing**.
Escalation is the correct answer only when the normal, direct route has been tried and failed, or when the risk
is severe (Q19 credential exposure, Q5 a client-visible outage).
