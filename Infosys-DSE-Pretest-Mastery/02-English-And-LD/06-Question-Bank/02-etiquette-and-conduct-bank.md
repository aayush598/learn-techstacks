# Etiquette, Conduct & Professionalism — Question Bank

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Etiquette, Conduct & Professionalism ·  _10 questions · ~15 min_

**How to use this bank:** answer all 10 questions on paper with a 90-second cap each. These items test workplace
norms, not opinions — one option matches the expected professional standard and the other three each break a
specific rule. Answers + one-line reasoning at the bottom.

---

## Q1. You receive an email from a client addressed to your manager, with your name in the body. What is the
correct professional response?

A. Act on the parts addressed to you, acknowledge the rest, and keep your manager copied on the thread so
nothing is dropped.

B. Leave it for your manager to handle, since it is addressed to them.

C. Forward it to your team channel so everyone knows the client is waiting.

D. Reply directly to the client without copying your manager.

**Tests:** professional email routing and information flow.

---

## Q2. You are 20 minutes late to a client meeting because of traffic, and no one else from your team is
present. What do you do?

A. Send a message when you arrive explaining the traffic, then start the meeting and apologise briefly at the
start.

B. Say nothing, because you were not late by much.

C. Apologise once the meeting is over and avoid raising it again.

D. Call the client as soon as you know you will be late, give a revised arrival time, and send a short
apology and a brief agenda so the wait is productive.

**Tests:** punctuality, proactive communication, recovery behaviour.

---

## Q3. Which of the following is the most professional way to disagree with a senior colleague in a meeting with
the client present?

A. Say nothing in the meeting and raise your objection in the internal retrospective.
B. Say directly that the senior's approach is incorrect and that it would not work technically.
C. Acknowledge the approach's merit, then state the specific constraint you are concerned about and the
alternative you suggest, and ask for the decision to be made.
D. Suggest a break so you can speak to the senior privately before continuing.

**Tests:** face-saving disagreement and appropriate hierarchy.

---

## Q4. A colleague asks you to keep a minor workplace conflict between you out of the project review. What is the
correct response?

A. Agree to keep it out of the review, since it is a personal matter.
B. Agree, but tell your manager about it in private so you are covered.
C. Explain that you are happy not to raise it personally in a public review, but that any decision affecting the
work will be documented and shared through the normal channel.
D. Refuse and tell the colleague that you do not keep secrets about work matters.

**Tests:** discretion versus record, and honest boundaries.

---

## Q5. While working in a client's office, you notice an unattended laptop displaying a confidential dashboard.
What do you do?

A. Close the laptop, report it to the site contact, and do not use the information you saw.

B. Ignore it — it is not your equipment and not your project.

C. Photograph the screen in case it is useful for your report.

D. Use the opportunity to open any documents you need for your task while you are there.

**Tests:** confidentiality and integrity of information handling.

---

## Q6. You are introduced to a new client contact and they extend a handshake. What is the expected professional
response?

A. Shake firmly and briefly, make eye contact, and use their name once you know it.
B. Wait to see whether they bow or shake hands, and mirror them exactly.
C. Use a single hand and keep your other hand in your pocket.
D. Nod politely instead of shaking, since handshakes can be avoided in professional settings.

**Tests:** basic professional courtesy and adapting appropriately.

---

## Q7. Which practice is the clearest breach of professional email etiquette?

A. Replying "Noted, thanks" to acknowledge a colleague's instruction.

B. Replying to a 12-person thread with all 12 copied, to make sure nobody is left out.

C. Using British spellings in an email to a client in another country.

D. Using "Dear Sir / Dear Madam" as the salutation for a person whose name you know.

**Tests:** recognising a genuine etiquette breach versus a harmless habit.

---

## Q8. You need information from a team in another location during your working hours. What is the most
professional request?

A. Send a message at 9:00 p.m. so they see it first when they start work.
B. Send the request in the shared channel with the specific question, the deadline, and the context of why it is
needed.
C. Call their personal mobile number to ask, since it is quicker.
D. Send a long email describing your whole task and ask them to reply with whatever they think is relevant.

**Tests:** communication channel selection, respect for working hours, and question discipline.

---

## Q9. A colleague's work contains an error that affects a shared deliverable, and you notice it the day before
submission. What is the professional response?

A. Fix the error silently and let the deliverable go out correct.
B. Tell the colleague immediately, show them the specific correction, and offer to apply it if they prefer to
make the change themselves.
C. Submit the deliverable as it is and raise the error in the next sprint review.
D. Tell the project manager that the colleague's work was wrong.

**Tests:** peer-to-peer correction and protection of the deliverable.

---

## Q10. Which of the following is the most appropriate use of a colleague's contact details you were given for a
specific project?

A. Add them to a company-wide announcement list so they receive future updates.

B. Keep using them for messages about other projects where you need help, since you already have the number.

C. Forward the contact details to another team member who "will definitely need it".

D. Use them only for the purpose they were given, and put other questions through the official channel.

**Tests:** purpose limitation and data minimisation in professional contact handling.

---

# Answer key

| Q | Answer | Why (one line) |
|---|--------|----------------|
| 1 | A | Acts on what is addressed to you, keeps the thread visible to the manager, and loses nothing in the handover. |
| 2 | D | Communicates the delay before it becomes a wait, and gives the client something useful to do — not an apology alone. |
| 3 | C | Preserves the colleague's standing, states a specific constraint and an alternative, and still leaves the decision explicit. |
| 4 | C | Keeps the personal matter out of a public forum while refusing to let it disappear from the project record. |
| 5 | A | Protects the information and reports it to the owner, without exploiting what you saw. |
| 6 | A | A clear, conventional greeting with eye contact and the person's name is the safe default in Indian corporate settings. |
| 7 | D | Addressing a named person as "Dear Sir/Madam" is a recognised breach of modern professional email etiquette. |
| 8 | B | Uses a shared, documented channel with a specific question, a deadline, and the reason — respecting the other team's working hours. |
| 9 | B | Corrects the issue at the source and credits the colleague, so the deliverable is safe and the relationship survives. |
| 10 | D | Purpose limitation is the standard: contact details given for one task are not a general-purpose resource. |

## Quick reference — the 10 etiquette rules these questions test

| Rule | Where it shows up |
|------|-------------------|
| Respond to the person who owns the action; keep the interested party visible | Q1 |
| Never make someone wait for you without telling them | Q2 |
| Disagree with evidence, and protect the other person's standing | Q3 |
| Discretion is fine; an undocumented decision is not | Q4 |
| Information you stumble on is not information you may use | Q5 |
| Adapt to the other person's custom, but keep the professional default | Q6 |
| Use the person's actual name in the salutation | Q7 |
| Respect other people's working hours | Q8 |
| Report errors to the owner, not around them | Q9 |
| Use data only for the purpose it was given for | Q10 |

**Scoring:** 8–10 solid · 6–7 review the etiquette topic · 0–5 read the topic README first.
