# Reading Comprehension & Fill Blanks — Question Bank

> Section 2 · English & L&D · Reading Comprehension & Fill Blanks ·  _10 questions · ~20 min_

**How to use this bank:** three passages, ten questions, 90 seconds per question. Read each passage once, label the
paragraphs in three words, then work the questions. Answers + one-line reasoning at the bottom.

**Passage lengths match the pre-test:** 100–150 words, three paragraphs, on a workplace, general-knowledge, or
business-process topic.

---

## Passage 1 — Read the passage and answer Q1–Q4.

> "When a project misses its deadline, the instinct is to explain why the delay was unavoidable. Experienced
> delivery managers do something different: they separate the cause of the delay from the decision about what to
> do next. The cause is often uninteresting — a dependency was late, a requirement was unclear, a test
> environment was unavailable. The decision is where the value is. A team that reports a delay with two options
> and a recommendation has converted a problem into a decision, and a decision can be made quickly. A team that
> reports only the cause has handed its manager a problem and asked someone else to think. The habit that
> separates the two is simple: every status report ends with a question and a recommendation, never with a
> reason alone."

## Passage 2 — Read the passage and answer Q5–Q7.

> "Sleep has traditionally been explained as a passive state in which the body simply repairs itself. That
> account has been undermined by the discovery of sleep cycles, during which the brain is measurably active and
> memory is consolidated. The practical consequence for organisations is easy to miss. A company that schedules
> training at 3 p.m. and considers that the employee's choice is making a decision about cognitive performance
> rather than about convenience. Most people do not know that their own alertness varies across the day, and
> they cannot be expected to argue for better scheduling. It falls to the organisation to set the default
> sensibly, because the default is what most people will accept."

## Passage 3 — Read the passage and answer Q8–Q10.

> "For a long time, project status meetings were treated as a formality: a short agenda, a series of
> colour-coded slides, and a closing decision to schedule the next one. This format survived because it served a
> purpose nobody had bothered to examine — it distributed information to everyone at once. Distributed
> information, however, is not the same as shared understanding. Two teams can read the same dashboard and
> disagree about what it means, and the disagreement only surfaces weeks later, when it is expensive. Reports from
> delivery organisations that replaced slide decks with short written summaries of decisions and risks suggest a
> different outcome: fewer surprises, and issues raised earlier, when they are still cheap to fix. The change
> was not a new tool. It was the decision to treat a status update as a communication rather than as a
> broadcast."

---

# Answer key

| Q | Answer | Why (one line) |
|---|--------|----------------|
| 1 | A | The whole passage is about how to report a delay; B, C and D each cover only one idea or none. |
| 2 | A | "Has handed its manager a problem and asked someone else to think" states it directly. |
| 3 | D | One step from "separate the cause from the decision" and "ends with a question and a recommendation". |
| 4 | D | The clause sets up the contrast with "asked someone else to think" — it is a purpose clause, not a claim about managers. |
| 5 | A | The sentence's job is to undermine the traditional account ("has been undermined by"). |
| 6 | B | Stated in the sentence immediately before the quoted phrase. |
| 7 | C | "Is making a decision about cognitive performance rather than about convenience" — one step, no absolutes. |
| 8 | C | Reference, topic and tone all hold: it continues the entities already in the passage without adding a claim. |
| 9 | B | "The disagreement only surfaces weeks later, when it is expensive" is stated directly. |
| 10 | B | It contrasts the tool with the decision to "treat a status update as a communication rather than as a broadcast". |
