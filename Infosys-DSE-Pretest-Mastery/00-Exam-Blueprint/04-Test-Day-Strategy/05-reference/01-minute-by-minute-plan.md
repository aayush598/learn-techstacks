# Minute-By-Minute Plan

> Section 00 · Exam Blueprint · Test Day Strategy · _Estimated reading time: 13 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [../03-worked-examples/01-minute-by-minute-mock-run.md](../03-worked-examples/01-minute-by-minute-mock-run.md)

## In one sentence
**Print this plan: six time windows, each with a goal and a checkpoint, ending with a flagged-only review.**

## Why the pre-test tests this
- **Executable script:** it removes in-test indecision about what to do next.
- **Checkpoints:** it catches a pace problem while there is still time to fix it.
- **Flag-driven ending:** it puts your last 15 minutes where the most convertible marks are.
- **Printability:** one page you can keep beside you.

## Core concept

### The plan
| Window | Goal | Checkpoint |
|--------|------|------------|
| 0:00-0:02 | Read instructions; note the policy | Negative marking recorded |
| 0:02-0:20 | Bank cheap marks across sections | ~12-14 done |
| 0:20-0:40 | Mid-section work; flag at 70s | ~25-27 done |
| 0:40-0:60 | Continue; protect the largest pool | ~38-40 done |
| 0:60-0:75 | Tighten; convert easy flags | ~50 done |
| 0:75-0:90 | Review flagged only; no blanks | 60 answered |

### Checkpoint actions
| If... | Do |
|-------|-----|
| Behind pace | Skip expensive archetypes; do cheap marks |
| On pace | Hold the 90-second rule |
| Ahead | Bank the margin; still flag at 70s |
| Panicking | 3-breath reset → re-project → cheap marks |

### The final-15 rule
- Open flags only.
- Convert return-priority first.
- Change a guess only for a clear error.
- Confirm no blanks (per the live policy).

### Why the windows are shaped this way
- The first 20 minutes harvest the cheapest marks while you are fresh.
- The middle 40 minutes carry the bulk of the paper at steady pace.
- The last 15 minutes are spent converting flags, which is where the margin over 39 usually comes from.

## Worked example
**Applying the plan to Rahul's run.** At 0:60 his checkpoint shows 40 done (on target). He therefore spends 0:60-0:75 tightening — converting two easy flags — rather than starting new hard questions. At 0:75 he has 52 done and moves to the flagged-only review, finishing with all 60 answered and three flags converted. The plan's windows told him exactly what each block of time was for.

| Window | Rahul's actual | Target | Action taken |
|--------|----------------|--------|--------------|
| 0:20 | 13 | 12-14 | On pace |
| 0:40 | 26 | 25-27 | On pace |
| 0:60 | 40 | 38-40 | On pace |
| 0:75 | 52 | ~50 | Flag conversion |
| 0:90 | 60 | 60 | Done |

## Quick rules
- **Six windows, each with a checkpoint.**
- **Check at every window boundary.**
- **The 70-second flag rule applies throughout.**
- **The final 15 minutes are flags only.**
- **No blanks if there is no penalty.**
- **Panic → reset, not rush.**

## Common traps
- **Ignoring checkpoints.**
- **Using the final 15 minutes on already-answered questions.**
- **Leaving blanks.**
- **Not tracking pace against the target counts.**

## Checklist
- [ ] You have printed or memorised the six windows.
- [ ] You know each checkpoint's target count.
- [ ] You know the behind/on/ahead actions.
- [ ] You know the final-15 rule.
- [ ] You leave no blanks per the live policy.
- [ ] You use the reset when panicking.
