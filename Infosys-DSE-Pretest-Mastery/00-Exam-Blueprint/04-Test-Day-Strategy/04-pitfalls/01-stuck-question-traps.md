# Stuck-Question Traps

> Section 00 · Exam Blueprint · Test Day Strategy · _Estimated reading time: 14 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [../02-techniques/02-what-to-do-when-stuck.md](../02-techniques/02-what-to-do-when-stuck.md)

## In one sentence
**A stuck question is only fatal if you let it consume the next question's time.**

## Why the pre-test tests this
- **Universality:** everyone gets stuck 5-10 times in 60 questions.
- **Cost:** a single 150-second stall can flip a 40 to a 37.
- **Avoidability:** the unstick procedure neutralises the trap.
- **Learned behaviour:** it must be practiced, not just understood.

## Core concept

### The trap table
| Trap | Behaviour | Cost | Fix |
|------|-----------|------|-----|
| Staring | Eyes on the question, no progress | 30-90s | Re-read the stem, eliminate |
| Restart | New approach without flagging | 60s+ | Do not restart; flag |
| Sunk-cost completion | "I've spent too long to quit" | 60s+ | Flag anyway; sunk time is gone |
| Perfectionism | Must solve fully | 2-3 questions | Guess, flag, move |
| Option-lock | Re-reading the options for a hint | 30s | Go back to the stem |
| No flag | Forget the question exists | 1 mark | Always flag uncertain questions |

### The cascade, quantified
Suppose you stall 90 seconds over budget on one question. You then rush the next three and get two wrong. Net effect:
- Time lost: 90 seconds.
- Marks lost: 2.

**The stall costs more than itself because it damages the accuracy of the questions that follow.**

### Section-specific stuck patterns
| Section | Stuck pattern | Fix |
|---------|---------------|-----|
| Aptitude | Can't see the series rule | Parity check, then flag |
| English | Ambiguous grammar | Pick the most professional option |
| DBMS | Multi-join trace | Trace only needed columns |
| Java | Nested scoping | Trace two lines, then flag |

## Worked example
**Meera on a number series at minute 40.**

She stares for 60 seconds. The correct move is to flag at 70 seconds. Instead she restarts with a second hypothesis (differences) and spends to 140 seconds, eventually getting it.

**Cost:** 70 seconds over budget — roughly one lost Java output question. Net: likely **−1 despite "solving" it.**

| Action | Time | Marks |
|--------|------|-------|
| Flag at 70s, guess, move | 70s | 0 or 1 |
| Stall to 140s and solve | 140s | 1, but −1 elsewhere |
| **Net difference** | | **−1 or −2** |

## Quick rules
- **Stuck = flag, not fight.**
- **Never restart without flagging.**
- **Sunk cost is gone; move on.**
- **Re-read the stem, not the options.**
- **One stall cascades; the fix stops it.**
- **Do a cheap question immediately after a stall.**

## Common traps
- **Believing more time equals more accuracy.**
- **Restarting mid-question.**
- **Forgetting to flag.**
- **Rushing the next three questions after a stall.**

## Checklist
- [ ] You flag the moment you are stuck.
- [ ] You never restart without flagging.
- [ ] You ignore sunk cost.
- [ ] You consciously slow down the question after a stall.
- [ ] You practice the unstick procedure.
- [ ] You do a cheap question to restore rhythm.
