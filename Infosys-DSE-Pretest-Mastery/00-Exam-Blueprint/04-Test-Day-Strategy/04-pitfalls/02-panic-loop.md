# The Panic Loop

> Section 00 · Exam Blueprint · Test Day Strategy · _Estimated reading time: 14 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [../01-concepts/02-test-day-mindset.md](../01-concepts/02-test-day-mindset.md)

## In one sentence
**The panic loop is behind → rush → errors → more behind → more panic, and the only break is a 10-second reset followed by cheap marks.**

## Why the pre-test tests this
- **Most preventable failure:** it is the documented risk for candidates like Meera.
- **Compounding:** panic degrades every subsequent decision, so it costs more than the trigger.
- **Fixable:** the loop breaks the moment you stop and re-project.
- **Testable:** it shows up in mocks as a time-related score drop after minute 50.

## Core concept

### The loop
```
See time/score shortfall
      ↓
Rush blindly  →  errors  →  further shortfall
      ↓                              ↑
   Panic  ←──────────────────────────┘
```

### The break sequence
| Step | Action | Effect |
|------|--------|--------|
| 1 | Stop for ~10s, three slow breaths | Lowers arousal |
| 2 | Re-project your net score | Replaces vague dread with a number |
| 3 | Switch to cheap-mark mode | Guarantees progress |
| 4 | Do 3 cheap questions | Restores confidence and marks |
| 5 | Resume the normal 90-second rule | Breaks the loop |

### The cheap-mark set (panic kit)
1. DBMS theory (ACID, normalization, keys).
2. Behavioural / etiquette English.
3. Data sufficiency, ranking, inequalities.

### Why re-projecting works
"Behind" is vague and terrifying. "Net 32 with 6 cheap marks available" is specific and solvable. Converting fear into arithmetic is the whole point of the reset.

## Worked example
**Meera at minute 55.**

She projects a net score of 32 — below the gate. Instead of rushing, she runs the break sequence:
1. Three slow breaths.
2. Re-projects: net 32, six cheap marks available.
3. Lists them: 2 DBMS theory, 3 behavioural, 1 data-sufficiency.
4. Completes them in seven minutes → net ≈ 38.
5. Resumes Java with 28 minutes left, picks up 3 more → **41**.

| State | Before reset | After reset |
|-------|--------------|-------------|
| Net score | 32 | 41 |
| Emotional state | Panicking | Neutral |
| Method | Rushing | 90-second rule |

The loop was broken by a 10-second reset and a list of six cheap marks.

## Quick rules
- **Notice the loop early.**
- **10-second reset.**
- **Re-project with numbers.**
- **Cheap marks break the loop.**
- **Resume the normal rule.**
- **A panic kit is prepared in advance.**

## Common traps
- **Rushing to "catch up".**
- **Skipping the reset** because it feels like losing time.
- **Wallowing in the score** instead of re-projecting.
- **Staying in panic for the final 30 minutes.**

## Checklist
- [ ] You can name the loop.
- [ ] You know the break sequence.
- [ ] You have a prepared panic kit of cheap marks.
- [ ] You re-project instead of panicking.
- [ ] You resume the 90-second rule after the reset.
- [ ] You have practiced the reset in a mock.
