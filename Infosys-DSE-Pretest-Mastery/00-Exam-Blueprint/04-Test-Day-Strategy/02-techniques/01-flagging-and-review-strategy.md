# Flagging And Review Strategy

> Section 00 · Exam Blueprint · Test Day Strategy · _Estimated reading time: 16 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [../01-concepts/01-the-ninety-second-rule-procedure.md](../01-concepts/01-the-ninety-second-rule-procedure.md)

## In one sentence
**Flag anything genuinely uncertain at 70 seconds, then spend the final 15 minutes only on flagged questions — never on questions you already answered.**

## Why the pre-test tests this
- **Deferred judgment:** a flag lets you move now and decide later, without losing the question.
- **Time protection:** it stops hard questions from owning your clock.
- **Focus:** the final sweep should target uncertain questions, not random ones.
- **Recovery:** flags are exactly where you convert margin in the last 15 minutes.

## Core concept

### What to flag
| Marker | When | Example |
|--------|------|---------|
| Confident | Marked fast and sure | Percentages, behavioural, ACID |
| Uncertain | At 70s, best-guess made | Nested Java output, long DI |
| Return-priority | High chance with more thought | Inner-class scoping, subtle SQL |

If the platform offers only a single flag, use it for **return-priority** questions.

### The review procedure (final 15 minutes)
1. Open the flagged list.
2. Sort mentally by likely gain per minute.
3. Re-attempt the return-priority items first.
4. Confirm uncertain best-guesses; change only if you find a clear error.
5. Never re-read confident answers unless you genuinely doubt them.

### The flag budget
| Phase | Flags per ~20 questions | If you exceed |
|-------|-------------------------|---------------|
| First third | 1-3 | You may be over-flagging |
| Middle third | 2-4 | Normal |
| Last third | 2-4 | Normal |
| Total | 5-10 | More than 12 means a knowledge gap, not a pacing one |

### Why flags convert to marks
A flag is a question you could probably solve with more time but chose to defer. In the final 15 minutes, you have exactly that time — for the questions that need it. The conversion rate for return-priority flags is often 50% or higher.

## Worked example
**Ananya finishes 60 questions with 8 flags (2 return-priority, 6 uncertain) and 12 minutes left.**

- **Minutes 72-78:** she does the two return-priority items (an inner-class output and a 3-row join trace) → converts both.
- **Minutes 78-85:** she reviews the six uncertain items; changes one where she now sees the parity in a series.
- **Minutes 85-90:** she confirms everything is marked; no unmarked questions remain.

**Net:** +3 marks from flags, confirming a total above 40. Without the flag system, those three marks would likely have been lost to rushed first-pass answers.

### Conversion rate benchmark
| Flag type | Expected conversion |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Return-priority | 50-70% |
| Uncertain | 10-20% |
| Over-flagged trivia | ~0% (this is why 5-10 is the target) |

## Quick rules
- **Flag at 70 seconds.**
- **The final 15 minutes are for flagged questions only.**
- **Prioritise return-priority flags first.**
- **Change an uncertain answer only for a clear error.**
- **Never re-read confident answers.**
- **Keep your total flags in the 5-10 range.**

## Common traps
- **Flagging everything** so the review is unfocused.
- **Reviewing answered questions "just to be safe"** and wasting the surplus.
- **Spending review time on questions you cannot resolve.**
- **Not reviewing at all** and leaving flag conversions on the table.

## Checklist
- [ ] You flag at 70 seconds.
- [ ] You keep total flags to 5-10.
- [ ] You prioritise return-priority flags.
- [ ] You review only flagged questions.
- [ ] You leave no blank questions (per the live policy).
- [ ] You change uncertain answers only for a clear error.
