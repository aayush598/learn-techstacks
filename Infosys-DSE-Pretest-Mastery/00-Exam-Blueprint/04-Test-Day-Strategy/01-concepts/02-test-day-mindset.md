# Test-Day Mindset

> Section 00 · Exam Blueprint · Test Day Strategy · _Estimated reading time: 15 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [01-the-ninety-second-rule-procedure.md](01-the-ninety-second-rule-procedure.md)

## In one sentence
**Treat the pre-test as a procedure you execute, not a test you feel — emotions are data, not instructions.**

## Why the pre-test tests this
- **Panic is the top failure mode** for candidates like Meera.
- **A neutral state improves speed** because attention stays on the question, not the clock.
- **Procedure beats mood** when the clock is running and decisions must be automatic.
- **Confidence is built in practice and spent on test day** — you cannot manufacture it mid-test.

## Core concept

### The mindset rules
| Situation | Natural reaction | Correct rule |
|-----------|------------------|--------------|
| You hit a hard question | Frustration | Flag and move — it is one of 60 |
| You realise you are behind | Panic | Switch to cheap-mark mode |
| You blank on an easy one | Alarm | Move; return in review |
| You are ahead | Relief, relaxing | Bank it, do not relax — protect margin |
| You find a section hard | Dread | Jump sections if the order allows |

### The 3-breath reset
1. Notice the spike (racing thoughts, shallow breathing).
2. Take three slow breaths (about 10 seconds).
3. Restate the rule: "eliminate, decide, move."

This costs 10 seconds and prevents a 10-minute panic spiral.

### Pre-test readiness signals
- You have done at least one full 90-minute mock.
- You have run timed 90-second drills.
- You know your per-section targets.
- You know your plan for the live guessing policy.

### Why "behind" is not "failing"
Being behind the pace line at a checkpoint is common and recoverable. The pace line is `0.667 questions per minute`; being two questions under it needs only a single avoided stall to fix. Panic, by contrast, degrades every subsequent decision. The reset converts a scare into a plan.

## Worked example
**Meera mid-test at minute 50.**

She projects a net score of 33, behind the gate. Her heart rate rises and she starts rushing.

Her recovery:
1. **Reset:** she applies the 3-breath reset (10 seconds).
2. **Re-project:** she lists six cheap marks still available — 3 behavioural English, 2 DBMS theory, 1 data-sufficiency.
3. **Execute:** she completes those six in about seven minutes → net ≈ 39.
4. **Resume:** with 28 minutes left she returns to the normal 90-second rule on Java.

**Outcome:** she finishes at net 41. The reset cost her 10 seconds and saved the attempt.

## Quick rules
- **Emotions are data, not instructions.**
- **Apply the 3-breath reset when arousal spikes.**
- **Feeling behind → switch to cheap-mark mode.**
- **Feeling ahead → bank it, do not relax.**
- **Procedure beats mood.**
- **A ready candidate has done a full mock.**

## Common traps
- **Letting a hard question dictate the next 10 minutes.**
- **Confusing "behind" with "failing".**
- **Relaxing when ahead and losing the margin.**
- **Ignoring the reset** because it "feels like wasting time".

## Checklist
- [ ] You know the 3-breath reset.
- [ ] You know the cheap-mark switch trigger.
- [ ] You have practiced staying neutral under time pressure.
- [ ] You will not relax when ahead.
- [ ] You have completed a full 90-minute mock.
- [ ] You know your live-policy guessing plan.
