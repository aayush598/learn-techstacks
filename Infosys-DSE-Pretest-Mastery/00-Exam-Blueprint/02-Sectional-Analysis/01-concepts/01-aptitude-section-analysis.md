# Aptitude Section Analysis (10 Q)

> Section 00 · Exam Blueprint · Sectional Analysis · _Estimated reading time: 17 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [../README.md](../README.md)

## In one sentence
**The 10-question Aptitude section is the smallest pool with the widest difficulty spread — pick the cheap archetypes and abandon the expensive ones without guilt.**

## Why the pre-test tests this
- **Cheap points:** data sufficiency, percentages, and ranking are fast and reliable; long data interpretation and tricky number series are not.
- **Weight:** Aptitude is only about 16.7% of the paper, so over-investing here directly steals time from the larger Java section.
- **Archetype stability:** the seven recurring topics below surface across batches, even when the exact counts shift.
- **Speed support:** it is where Ananya (average aptitude) must avoid stalling, because a single long DI set can cost two other questions.

## Core concept

### The seven recurring topics and their cost
| Topic | Typical difficulty | Cost per mark | Priority |
|-------|-------------------|---------------|----------|
| Data sufficiency | Medium | Low-medium | High (structured) |
| Percentages | Easy-medium | Low | High |
| Data interpretation | Medium-hard | High (long) | Take the shortest set only |
| Ranking | Easy | Low | High |
| Inequality-based reasoning (`a > b < n ≥ i < p`) | Easy-medium | Low | High |
| Number series | Easy-hard | Medium-high | Do obvious ones, skip obscure |
| Mixtures & alligations | Medium | Medium | Do formula-driven ones |

### Difficulty distribution (reported feel)
| Band | Share of section | Notes |
|------|------------------|-------|
| Easy | ~4-5 Q | Percentages, ranking, simple data sufficiency |
| Medium | ~3-4 Q | Alligations, inequalities, easy series |
| Hard | ~1-3 Q | Long DI, obscure series |

### Where the marks are cheapest
1. **Data sufficiency:** a practiced structure. You evaluate two statements against a question stem. No long computation; high accuracy once you know the four standard outcomes.
2. **Ranking and inequalities:** pure symbol logic. The qualifying inequality `a > b < n ≥ i < p` type needs no arithmetic at all.
3. **Percentages:** formula plus a shortcut. One or two lines.

### Where the marks are most expensive
- **Data interpretation:** often needs three or more operations per sub-question with a shared base (percentage of a percentage).
- **Obscure number series:** if the rule does not surface in 30 seconds, it rarely surfaces at all.

### How the weight maps to score
10 questions = 10 marks = 16.7% of the paper. A realistic target is **5-7 correct**. Pushing beyond 7 rarely justifies the time cost, because the extra questions are usually the hard DI and series items that also endanger Java.

### Data sufficiency in one table
| Statement combination | Verdict |
|-----------------------|---------|
| Only statement 1 sufficient | Answer depends on 1 alone |
| Only statement 2 sufficient | Answer depends on 2 alone |
| Both together sufficient, neither alone | Answer depends on both |
| Neither, even together | Insufficient data |

## Worked example
**Ananya sees this mix:** 2 data sufficiency, 2 percentages, 1 DI, 1 ranking, 2 inequalities, 1 series, 1 mixtures.

Her plan:
1. Do data sufficiency (2), percentages (2), ranking (1), inequalities (2), easy series (1), mixtures (1) → 9 questions, target ~10 minutes.
2. Attempt the DI set **only** if she can see the path within 70 seconds; otherwise flag it.
3. Skip the obscure series if no pattern appears in 30 seconds.

**Realistic outcome:** 6-7 correct. If she had attacked the DI set first, she could have lost 3 minutes on one mark — the equivalent of two easy questions.

### Distractor logic example
**Q:** What is 30% of 40?
A. 10  B. 12  C. 14  D. 15
**Answer: B (12).** A is 25%, C is 35%, D is 37.5% — each is a plausible mis-multiply. Knowing the distractor pattern (adjacent percentages) lets you sanity-check fast.

## Quick rules
- **Do structural archetypes first:** data sufficiency, ranking, inequalities.
- **DI is a time sink:** take it only if the data set is small.
- **Number series:** if no pattern in 30 seconds, flag it.
- **Alligations:** formula first, do not derive.
- **Cap ambition:** 6-7 correct is a good outcome for this section.
- **Read the live count:** 10 is reported, not guaranteed.

## Common traps
- **Deriving a long DI set** and spending two to three times a normal question's time.
- **Forcing an obscure number series** when the rule is not visible.
- **Skipping easy data sufficiency** because it "looks wordy" — it is cheap once you know the structure.
- **Over-investing in the smallest section** and letting it steal from Java.

## Checklist
- [ ] You can name all seven Aptitude archetypes.
- [ ] You know which four or five are cheap.
- [ ] You will cap DI time at 70 seconds before deciding.
- [ ] Your Aptitude target is 5-7 correct.
- [ ] You will flag rather than force an obscure series.
- [ ] You know the four data-sufficiency outcomes.
