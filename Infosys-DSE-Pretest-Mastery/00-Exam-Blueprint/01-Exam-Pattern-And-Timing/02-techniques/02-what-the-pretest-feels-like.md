# What The Pre-Test Feels Like, Minute By Minute

> Section 00 · Exam Blueprint · Exam Pattern And Timing · _Estimated reading time: 16 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [../01-concepts/02-timing-by-section.md](../01-concepts/02-timing-by-section.md)

## In one sentence
**The pre-test feels like a controlled sprint:** two calm minutes of reading, then relentless 90-second chunks, a predictable concentration dip around minute 45-60, and a final 15-minute tightening phase.

## Why the pre-test tests this
- **Realism reduces panic.** Knowing the emotional shape of the test helps candidates like Meera, who panics under time pressure, stay neutral.
- **Fatigue is real.** Concentration dips mid-test; a plan for the dip prevents a mid-paper collapse.
- **Momentum compounds.** Early wins build confidence; early stalls create a panic cascade.
- **Finishing matters.** The final sweep is where leftover easy questions get answered.

## Core concept

### The feel map
| Window | What it feels like | What you should be doing |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------------|
| 0-2 min | Settled, reading | Read instructions; note negative marking and section order |
| 2-15 min | Confident sprint | Land easy questions across sections (if the order allows) |
| 15-30 min | Rhythm | Predictable pace; flag unknowns, do not stall |
| 30-45 min | Steady | Hold the pace; do not check the clock anxiously |
| 45-60 min | First dip | Stamina drops; rely on the rule, not on mood |
| 60-75 min | Resolve | Protect the largest section if not yet covered |
| 75-90 min | Tightening and sweep | Review flagged; confirm every easy question is answered |

### The mental model: three phases
| Phase | Minutes | Goal | Dominant activity |
|-------|---------|------|-------------------|
| Harvest | 0-30 | Bank cheap marks | Fast, confident questions |
| Grind | 30-65 | Cover the rest | Steady application of the rule |
| Sweep | 65-90 | Recover and confirm | Flag review and no-blank check |

### Handling the dip
| Symptom | Countermeasure |
|---------|----------------|
| Panic spike | Three slow breaths (~10s), then "eliminate, decide, move" |
| Blanking out | Move to the next known question; return only with surplus |
| Physical fatigue | Reset posture; relax the shoulders; micro-reset between sections if allowed |
| Time anxiety | Check the clock once per 15 minutes, never continuously |

### Why checking the clock constantly backfires
Every glance costs attention and raises arousal. A single check every 15 minutes gives you the same information with none of the drip-feed anxiety.

## Worked example
**Meera's mid-test recovery.** At minute 50 Meera notices she has completed only 33 questions — behind her target of ~40 at that point (the pace line is 0.667 questions per minute). Her instinct is to panic and start guessing.

Her correct response:
1. She stops for three slow breaths (10 seconds).
2. She re-projects: she estimates net ~33 and lists six cheap marks still available (2 DBMS theory, 3 behavioural English, 1 data-sufficiency).
3. She completes those six in seven minutes → projected net ~39.
4. With 28 minutes left she resumes the normal 90-second rule on Java.

**Outcome:** she finishes at net 41. The panic was managed by procedure, not willpower. Her earlier mock, run without a plan for the dip, finished at 37 from the same knowledge.

## Quick rules
- **Expect the dip at 45-60 minutes.** It is normal; lean on the rule.
- **Check the clock once per 15 minutes.**
- **Early wins build the momentum that carries you through the dip.**
- **The final 15 minutes are for tightening and sweeping**, not new derivations.
- **Never let panic change your method** — only the *pace* adjusts, never the procedure.

## Common traps
- **Mood-driven pacing:** sprinting when confident and freezing when scared.
- **Time-checking loops:** glancing at the clock after every question destroys rhythm.
- **Early over-derivation:** spending the calm early minutes on long DI sets steals from the whole paper.
- **Ignoring the dip:** treating fatigue as failure instead of as a scheduled event.

## Checklist
- [ ] You know the seven-window feel map.
- [ ] You have a personal panic countermeasure (e.g., three breaths).
- [ ] You will check the clock only every 15 minutes.
- [ ] You have a plan for the final 15 minutes.
- [ ] You will move on immediately when you blank.
- [ ] You expect the dip and will not let it change your method.
