# Simulated 90-Minute Timeline (Ananya's Run)

> Section 00 · Exam Blueprint · Exam Pattern And Timing · _Estimated reading time: 22 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [../02-techniques/01-ninety-second-rule.md](../02-techniques/01-ninety-second-rule.md)

## In one sentence
**A realistic 90-minute walkthrough shows that discipline at the 70-second mark — not raw speed — is what carries a candidate past 39/60.**

## Why the pre-test tests this
- **Concrete rehearsal:** a timeline you can visualise sticks better than abstract advice about "managing time".
- **Decision points:** the log shows exactly where most candidates lose points, and what the correct move was.
- **Ananya archetype:** average aptitude, first Infosys attempt — a representative candidate, not a prodigy.
- **Score tie-in:** it connects pacing decisions directly to the pass threshold.

## Core concept

### The setup
Ananya sits a 60-question, 90-minute pre-test. The reported section **order** on the day (illustrative) is Java → Aptitude → English & L&D → DBMS/SQL. There is no negative marking stated in the instructions. She applies the 90-second rule throughout.

### Ananya's profile
| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Aptitude | Average — reliable on percentages, ranking, DS; slow on long DI |
| English & L&D | Solid judgment, okay grammar |
| DBMS/SQL | Knows joins, ACID, normalization basics |
| Java | Recognises common output patterns; slower on inner classes |
| Weakness | Tends to linger on questions she feels she "almost" has |

### Minute log
| Minute | Q done | Event | Decision |
|--------|--------|-------|----------|
| 0-2 | 0 | Reads instructions | Notes: no negative marking stated; order is Java first |
| 2-10 | 8 | Java output-prediction easy ones | 8 recognised → marked fast (avg 60s) |
| 10-18 | 16 | Java OOP/collections | 2 flagged at 70s (inner-class scoping) |
| 18-28 | 25 | Java exceptions + remaining | Java complete (25), one earlier flag settled |
| 28-42 | 33 | Aptitude | Percentages/ranking/series fast; 1 DI skipped; 1 inequality flagged |
| 42-58 | 46 | English & L&D | Behavioural + error correction; moved fast, avg 60s |
| 58-72 | 56 | DBMS/SQL | Joins traced carefully (~100s each, within budget); ACID/theory fast |
| 72-82 | 60 | Final questions + dive back | Completed remaining; revisited flags with surplus |
| 82-90 | 60 | Final sweep | Converted 2 flags via elimination; confirmed all answered |

### Score reasoning (illustrative)
| Section | Attempted | Confident | Estimated correct |
|---------|-----------|-----------|--------------------|
| Java | 25 | 15 | 15 |
| Aptitude | 10 | 6 | 6 |
| English & L&D | 15 | 12 | 12 |
| DBMS / SQL | 10 | 8 | 8 |
| **Total** | **60** | **41** | **41 → clears 39** |

The two-point margin (41 vs 39) came almost entirely from two habits: **not over-running Java early** and **finishing English fast**.

### The decision points that mattered
1. **Minutes 10-18:** two flagged Java questions. She did **not** stall. She saved roughly 40 seconds on each — 80 seconds total, about one question's worth of time.
2. **Minutes 28-42:** one DI question. She skipped it early instead of deriving for three minutes. She recovered it late via elimination — turning a time sink into a fast guess.
3. **Minutes 72-82:** her surplus time went to revisiting flags, not to re-reading already-answered questions.

## Worked example
**Contrast with Rahul's run (same paper, no rule).**

Rahul stalls 110 seconds on a Java inheritance question at minute 15, then 95 seconds on a number series in Aptitude. By minute 30 he is five questions behind Ananya. At the end he guesses several Java output questions he could have solved with a slower trace, because he is out of time. His estimated net: **36 → fails the threshold.**

| Candidate | Java | Aptitude | English | DBMS | Total |
|-----------|------|----------|---------|------|-------|
| Ananya (rule) | 15 | 6 | 12 | 8 | 41 ✅ |
| Rahul (no rule) | 12 | 5 | 12 | 7 | 36 ❌ |

The only material difference is the 70-second discipline. Both know roughly the same material.

## Quick rules
- **Java first is fine if you are confident — but never over-run it.**
- **Flag early, revisit late.**
- **English is the fastest section:** budget it tight and bank the time.
- **DI and number series are time sinks:** skip early if unclear.
- **The margin (41 vs 39) is won or lost in the final 15 minutes.**
- **Read the live instructions before the first question.**

## Common traps
- **Treating this timeline as rigid.** Adjust for your real section order and counts.
- **Over-running Java because it comes first.**
- **Skipping a DI question and forgetting it.** Always revisit flagged questions.
- **Confusing "answered" with "correct".** Verify your elimination, do not just count attempts.

## Checklist
- [ ] You can narrate your own 90-minute plan out loud.
- [ ] You will flag, not stall, at 70 seconds.
- [ ] You will budget English tightly and bank its time.
- [ ] You will revisit every flagged question in the final 15 minutes.
- [ ] You aim for a margin above 39, not exactly 39.
- [ ] You will read the live instructions before starting.
