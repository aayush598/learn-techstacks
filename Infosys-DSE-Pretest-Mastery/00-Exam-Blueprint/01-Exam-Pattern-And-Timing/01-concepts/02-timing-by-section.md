# Timing By Section

> Section 00 · Exam Blueprint · Exam Pattern And Timing · _Estimated reading time: 18 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [01-full-pattern-breakdown.md](01-full-pattern-breakdown.md)

## In one sentence
**Plan a time budget for each section now, then flex it on test day** — the budgets are guardrails, not shackles, and their job is to stop one section from silently consuming another's time.

## Why the pre-test tests this
- **Asymmetric return:** some sections give points faster per minute than others. English & L&D and DBMS theory are often quicker marks than long Aptitude data-interpretation sets.
- **Java load:** 25 questions is more than twice any other section's count, so it needs deliberate protection.
- **English & L&D is often the fastest section:** mostly judgment and error-correction, not deep computation.
- **DBMS/SQL is predictable:** joins, ACID, and normalization recur, so its time needs can be estimated.

## Core concept

### Reported budgets (planning baselines)
| Section | Q | Suggested minutes | Seconds/Q | Rationale |
|---------|---|-------------------|-----------|-----------|
| Aptitude | 10 | 14-16 | 84-96 | Mix of DI/series/inequalities; take the easy ones first |
| English & L&D | 15 | 20-22 | 80-88 | Scenario + error correction; scan the stem fast |
| DBMS / SQL | 10 | 17-19 | 102-114 | Joins + theory + small queries; methodical but bounded |
| Java problem-solving | 25 | 33-36 | 79-86 | Largest; output prediction varies; do recognised patterns first |
| **Total** | **60** | **90** | **90 avg** | — |

### Checkpoints
| Time elapsed | Target questions done | If behind | If ahead |
|--------------|----------------------|-----------|----------|
| 15 min | ~10 | Do not chase; tighten by flagging harder questions | Bank the surplus |
| 30 min | ~20 | Skip the most expensive remaining archetype | Attempt one more medium question |
| 45 min | ~30 | Enter cheap-mark mode if net projection < 39 | Hold pace, protect margin |
| 60 min | ~40 | Prioritise flagged conversions over new questions | Start reviewing flagged |
| 75 min | ~50 | Convert easy flags only; no new derivations | Convert remaining flags |
| 90 min | 60 | Stop; no blanks per live policy | Stop; no blanks per live policy |

### Per-section timing tips
| Section | Tip |
|---------|-----|
| Aptitude | Do data sufficiency, percentages, and ranking first; skip long DI if unclear after 70s |
| English & L&D | Read the scenario once, fast; pick the most professional answer; do not re-read |
| DBMS / SQL | For joins/output, trace small tables step by step; for theory, eliminate obviously wrong options |
| Java | Do compile/execute identification and recognised output patterns first; trace with a variable table; flag nested inner-class puzzles |

### The flex rule
Budgets flex in one direction only: **you may borrow from review time, never from a per-question cap.** If English runs long, shorten the final review sweep — do not let any single question run past 90 seconds to "make up" the debt.

## Worked example
**Rahul's plan.** Rahul is strong in Java and weak in English. He sets: Aptitude 15 min, English 21 min, DBMS 18 min, Java 36 min. Walk it:

1. Suppose English runs 25 minutes (4 over). His Java budget of 36 minutes is protected because he refuses to stall on any English question past 70s.
2. He borrows the 4 minutes from his planned final review (which was ~5 min), leaving only 1 minute of review.
3. He targets converting one Java flag during that minute.

**Numbers:** if he had *instead* let English run to 30 minutes to "finish properly", he would have lost 9 minutes from Java — roughly `9 × 60 / 84 ≈ 6.4 Java questions` of time. Discipline at the 70-second mark beats "finishing properly".

## Quick rules
- **Write four budget lines before the test:** one per section.
- **Check at 15/30/45/60/75 minutes** — use question counts, not just the clock.
- **Trade review time, never the per-question cap.**
- **Protect the largest section (Java).**
- **Bank English time; it is the fastest section.**
- **Flex budgets by solvability, not by anxiety.**

## Common traps
- **Front-loading the hardest section** and never recovering the time.
- **Perfectionism:** solving fully instead of moving on when the clock is against you.
- **Ignoring checkpoints** and noticing the problem at minute 80.
- **Budget rigidity:** refusing to flex when the live section order differs.
- **Borrowing from the per-question cap** to "catch up" — this is how a cascade starts.

## Checklist
- [ ] You have written a minute budget for all four sections.
- [ ] You know the 15/30/45/60/75 checkpoints.
- [ ] You know the flex rule (trade review, not the cap).
- [ ] You will track questions-left at every checkpoint.
- [ ] You have identified your largest section and protected it.
- [ ] You will bank English & L&D time rather than relax in it.
