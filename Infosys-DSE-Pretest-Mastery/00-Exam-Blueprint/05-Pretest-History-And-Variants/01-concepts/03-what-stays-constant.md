# What Stays Constant

> Section 00 · Exam Blueprint · Pretest History And Variants · _Estimated reading time: 14 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [02-the-two-variants.md](02-the-two-variants.md)

## In one sentence
**Counts, order, and negative marking vary — but the 90-second pressure, the core topics, and the stream-selection gate are the constants you can rely on.**

## Why the pre-test tests this
- **Stability under variance:** knowing the constants prevents over-reaction when the format changes.
- **Study triage:** the constant topics deserve the study hours.
- **Rule design:** because the mechanics vary, your rules must be robust to variation.
- **Honesty:** it separates what candidates report from what is actually fixed.

## Core concept

### Constants vs variables
| Constant | Variable |
|----------|----------|
| 90-second-per-question pressure (in a 60×90 format) | Exact question count |
| Core topics: aptitude, English/L&D, SQL, Java fundamentals | Section order |
| Stream selection gated by clearing | Negative marking |
| Batch-to-batch instruction changes | Section presence (across variants) |
| Fast, objective, output-prediction style | Exact marks per question |

### The guaranteed core
1. **Aptitude archetypes:** data sufficiency, percentages, DI, ranking, inequalities, series, mixtures.
2. **English/L&D judgment** with the "intended professional answer" rule.
3. **SQL up to JOINs** plus normalization and ACID.
4. **Java:** OOPs, interfaces and inner classes, packages and access modifiers, collections, exceptions, output prediction, compile/execute identification.

### Rule robustness
| If the live test... | Your rule still works because... |
|---------------------|----------------------------------|
| Changes counts | You work by 90-second chunks |
| Changes order | You attack by solvability |
| Adds negative marking | You match the live policy |
| Drops a section | You protect the largest remaining section |

### Why constants matter more than variables
Variables change the *packaging* of the test. Constants determine whether your knowledge applies. If you prepare the constants, any packaging is survivable. If you memorise the packaging, one change breaks you.

## Worked example
**Meera prepares the constant core.**

On test day the live test has a slightly different order — Java first. Because she planned by 90-second chunks and by solvability rather than by section order, she simply attacks Java first, where she is strong. She loses no time.

Her friend, who planned rigidly by section order, spends roughly two minutes mentally reorganising when the order changes. At 90 seconds per question, those two minutes are worth about 1.3 questions.

**The constant (solvability-based attack) protected her from the variable (order).**

## Quick rules
- **90-second pressure is constant.**
- **Core topics are constant.**
- **The gate (clear → stream selection) is constant.**
- **Counts, order, and penalties vary.**
- **Build rules robust to variance.**
- **Prepare constants; adapt to variables.**

## Common traps
- **Treating counts as constants.**
- **Building rigid, order-dependent plans.**
- **Ignoring the guaranteed core.**
- **Assuming one batch's mechanics apply to yours.**

## Checklist
- [ ] You know the constants.
- [ ] You know the variables.
- [ ] Your rules work despite variance.
- [ ] You study the guaranteed core.
- [ ] You read the live instructions.
- [ ] You attack by solvability, not by expected order.
