# Stream Decision Support

> Section 00 · Exam Blueprint · Marks Threshold And Stream Choice · _Estimated reading time: 14 min_
> **Prerequisite:** [../01-concepts/04-stream-choice-gating.md](../01-concepts/04-stream-choice-gating.md)

## In one sentence
**Match your strongest pre-test signal to the stream that weights it most, and keep a backup for seat variability.**

## Why the pre-test tests this
- **Post-gate decision:** choosing well affects your entire training experience.
- **Strength evidence:** your section scores are the clearest, most objective signal you have.
- **Seat uncertainty:** first choice is not guaranteed, so you should rank all five streams.
- **Karthik's retrospective:** "choose the stream that fits you, not the trendiest one."

## Core concept

### Signal → stream matrix
| Your strongest signal | Primary stream | Backup stream |
|-----------------------|----------------|---------------|
| Java output / OOP | Java + Angular | Microsoft Azure + .NET |
| DBMS/SQL joins | Data Engineering | Data Science & Agentic AI |
| Aptitude / logic | Data Science & Agentic AI | Data Engineering |
| English/L&D + UI interest | UI Stack | Data Engineering |
| Broad balance | Java + Angular | Data Engineering |

### The ranking procedure
1. List the five streams.
2. Score each 1-5 on: your strength fit, your interest, your career direction.
3. Break ties by your best section.
4. Keep your ranking ready before the form opens.

### Karthik's retrospective
"I cleared with 44. I ranked UI Stack first because it matched my frontend interest and my English score was my best non-Java signal. Two friends ranked purely by 'highest paying' and ended up in streams they did not enjoy. Rank by fit."

## Worked example
**Ananya clears with 40:** Java 14, English 11, DBMS 8, Aptitude 7.

- Her best signal is effectively a tie between Java (14, largest raw) and English (11, near-top of its section).
- Her interest is data.

Her ranking:
1. **Data Science & Agentic AI** — uses logic and data; her Aptitude and DBMS both support it.
2. **Data Engineering** — she is solid in DBMS.
3. **Java + Angular** — her Java is fine, but she is not passionate about backend.
4. **Microsoft Azure + .NET** — Java OOP transfers.
5. **UI Stack** — possible but not her direction.

Because she ranked all five, she does not depend on any single stream being available.

### Tie-breaking rules
| Tie situation | Break it by... |
|---------------|----------------|
| Two streams equal on all scores | Your best section |
| Strength vs interest conflict | Interest, if your strength is "good enough" for the stream |
| Career direction unclear | Your best section's stream |

## Quick rules
- **Rank all five streams.**
- **Primary = strongest signal plus interest.**
- **Keep a backup for seat variability.**
- **Tie-break by your best section.**
- **Fit beats trend.**

## Common traps
- **Ranking only one stream** and being stranded if it is full.
- **Choosing by prestige alone.**
- **Ignoring your weakest-signal streams entirely** despite seat variability.
- **Deciding after the form opens** instead of before.

## Checklist
- [ ] You have ranked all five streams.
- [ ] Your primary matches your strongest signal.
- [ ] You have a backup stream.
- [ ] You know your tie-break rule.
- [ ] You will read the live seat rules.
- [ ] You decided by fit, not by trend.
