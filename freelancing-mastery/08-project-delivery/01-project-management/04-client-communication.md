# Client Communication

## Professional Standards That Command Premium Rates

How you communicate defines how clients perceive your value. Every email, every message, every call is a signal about your professionalism. Great communication is what separates $50/hr freelancers from $200/hr freelancers.

This guide covers professional communication standards, handling difficult clients, escalation management, and the communication systems that build long-term relationships.

## The Communication Premium

### Why Communication Determines Your Rate

| Skill Level | Communication Quality | Typical Rate |
|-------------|----------------------|--------------|

| 1. Respond | Reply within 24 hours | "They're reliable" |
| 2. Update | Send weekly progress reports | "They're organized" |

| 5. Lead | Guide decisions, educate, mentor | "They're essential" |

**Target:** Level 4-5 with every client. Anything less is leaving money on the table.

## Professional Communication Standards

### Email Standards

**Subject Line Format:**
```
[Project Name] — [Topic] — [Action Needed/Status/Update]
```

Examples:
```
Acme Dashboard — Weekly Status — Week 4
Acme Dashboard — Design Approval Needed by Friday
Acme Dashboard — Project Complete 🎉
```

**Email Structure:**
```
Subject: [Clear, actionable subject line]

Hi [Name],

[Context — 1 sentence on why this email exists]
[Action — what you need from them, or what they need to know]
[Details — 2-3 bullet points max if needed]
[Deadline — if action required]

Best,
[Your name]

—
[Optional: link to project dashboard / contact info]
```

**Response Time Guidelines:**
- Email: Within 4 hours during business hours
- Slack/IM: Within 1 hour during business hours
- Urgent: Within 15 minutes
- After hours: Respond next business day (unless emergency)

**The 24-Hour Rule:**
If an email requires more than 24 hours to resolve, send a holding response within 4 hours:
"Got your email. I'm looking into this and will have a detailed response by [date]."

### Writing Style Guidelines

**Do:**
- Use active voice ("I will deliver the report by Friday")
- Be concise (short paragraphs, bullet points for lists)
- Use clear subject lines
- Include one clear call to action
- Proofread before sending
- Use professional greeting and sign-off
- Keep emails to 5 sentences or less when possible

**Don't:**
- Use vague language ("sometime next week")
- Use all caps or excessive punctuation
- Use emojis in formal communication (unless client uses them first)
- Make assumptions without confirmation
- Write emotional emails when frustrated
- CC people unnecessarily
- Use "just" or "sorry" excessively (undermines authority)

**Word Replacements:**

| Instead of | Use |
|------------|-----|
| "I just wanted to check in" | "I'm following up on" |
| "Sorry for the delay" | "Thank you for your patience" |
| "I think" | "I recommend" |
| "Maybe we could" | "I suggest we" |
| "That's not my fault" | "Here's what happened and how we'll fix it" |
| "I'm not sure" | "Let me look into that" |
| "That's a good question" | "Great question" |
| "No problem" | "Happy to help" |
| "I'll try" | "I will" |
| "Does that make sense?" | "How does that sound?" |

### Video Call Standards

**Before the Call:**
- Test audio, video, and screen sharing 5 minutes before
- Have a clear agenda (send in advance if possible)
- Close distracting apps and notifications
- Have a notepad/document ready for notes
- Ensure good lighting and professional background

**During the Call:**
- Start on time (if client is late, wait 5 min, then email follow-up)
- State the agenda and expected duration
- Keep camera on (builds trust and engagement)
- Take visible notes (shows you value the conversation)
- Stay on topic (parking lot for off-topic items)
- Summarize decisions and action items before ending
- End on time or early

**After the Call:**
- Send meeting notes within 4 hours
- Include: decisions made, action items with owners, next meeting date
- Update project management tool

**Meeting Notes Template:**
```
Meeting: [Project] — [Date]
Attendees: [Names]
Duration: [Time]

Decisions:
1. [Decision 1]
2. [Decision 2]

Action Items:
- [Person]: [Task] by [Date]
- [Person]: [Task] by [Date]

Next Meeting: [Date/Time]
```

## Communication by Project Phase

### Phase 1: Discovery / Planning

**Communication Goal:** Build trust, demonstrate understanding, set expectations

**Key Communications:**
- Kickoff email with project plan
- Weekly discovery updates
- Requirements confirmation document
- Decision documentation

**Frequency:** 2-3 touch points per week

**Tone:** Consultative, curious, thorough

### Phase 2: Development

**Communication Goal:** Maintain confidence, demonstrate progress, prevent surprises

**Key Communications:**
- Weekly status reports with demos
- Blocker notifications (immediate)
- Change request documentation
- Decision requests with deadlines

**Frequency:** 1-2 touch points per week

**Tone:** Confident, transparent, proactive

### Phase 3: Testing / Launch

**Communication Goal:** Build excitement, ensure readiness, manage launch logistics

**Key Communications:**
- Testing schedule and status
- Launch checklist
- Go/no-go decision documentation
- Rollback plan (if applicable)

**Frequency:** Daily during launch week

**Tone:** Energized, detailed, prepared

### Phase 4: Post-Launch / Support

**Communication Goal:** Demonstrate ongoing value, set up upsell

**Key Communications:**
- Post-launch review report
- Performance metrics
- Maintenance recommendations
- Upsell opportunities

**Frequency:** Weekly → monthly as project stabilizes

**Tone:** Proud of results, forward-looking, strategic

## Handling Difficult Client Situations

### Situation 1: Client Is Unhappy with Progress

**Don't:** Get defensive, blame them, make excuses
**Do:** Listen, acknowledge, present a solution

**Script:**
"I hear your concern. Let me understand exactly what's not meeting your expectations. [Listen]. I appreciate you sharing that. Here's what I think happened, and here's my plan to address it:
1. [Immediate fix]
2. [Process change to prevent recurrence]
3. [Timeline adjustment if needed]

Does this address your concerns?"

### Situation 2: Client Wants More Work for Free

**Don't:** Say yes to keep them happy, get angry
**Do:** Refer to scope, offer change order

**Script:**
"I understand you'd like [feature]. That's outside the current scope, but I can absolutely add it. Here are two options:
1. **Add to scope:** It will take approximately [X] hours and cost [$X]. I can start after you approve.
2. **Save for Phase 2:** We can include it in our next engagement.

Which works better for you?"

### Situation 3: Client Is Micromanaging

**Don't:** Get frustrated, ignore them, work slower
**Do:** Over-communicate proactively, build trust

**Script:**
"I want to make sure you feel confident in the progress. To give you full visibility, I'll send:
- A daily summary of what I worked on
- A weekly demo of completed features
- Immediate notification of any blockers

Would that give you the visibility you need?"

### Situation 4: Client Is Slow to Respond

**Don't:** Wait indefinitely, do work without approval
**Do:** Set deadlines, use implied approval

**Script (before deadline):**
"I need your feedback on [deliverable] to proceed with [next step]. If I don't hear from you by [date], I'll move forward with the current direction to keep the project on schedule."

**Script (after deadline):**
"Since I didn't receive feedback by the deadline, I've proceeded with [approach] to maintain the timeline. We can make adjustments in the next sprint."

### Situation 5: Client Disputes an Invoice

**Don't:** Get emotional, threaten, ignore
**Do:** Review the work done, walk through the hours, find resolution

**Script:**
"I understand you have questions about this invoice. Let me walk through what was delivered during this period:

[Detailed breakdown of work]

This was all within the agreed scope. If there's a specific line item you'd like to discuss, I'm happy to provide more detail.

If there are budget concerns, let's discuss adjusting the scope going forward."

### Situation 6: Client Wants to Cancel

**Don't:** Get angry, discount desperately, burn bridges
**Do:** Understand why, find resolution, or part professionally

**Script:**
"I'm sorry to hear that. Can I ask what's driving this decision? [Listen]. I understand. Here's what I'll do:
1. I'll deliver everything completed to date
2. I'll invoice only for work completed (per our contract)
3. I'll provide documentation for handover

I hope we can work together again in the future when the timing is better."

## Escalation Management

### The Escalation Ladder

| Level | Issue Type | Who Handles | Timeline |
|-------|-----------|-------------|----------|
| 1 | Minor concern, clarification | You | Same day |
| 2 | Scope question, process issue | You + Client | 24 hours |
| 3 | Quality concern, missed deadline | You + Client (formal) | 48 hours |
| 4 | Payment dispute, contract issue | You + Legal | 1 week |
| 5 | Termination, legal action | Lawyer | Per legal |

### Escalation Script Template

"I'd like to formally address an issue that needs resolution.

**Issue:** [Clear description]
**Impact:** [What's affected — timeline, budget, relationship]
**Attempted solutions:** [What we've tried already]
**Proposed resolution:** [What I recommend]
**Decision needed by:** [Date/time]

Please let me know if you'd like to discuss further or if the proposed resolution works."

## Client Feedback Systems

### Structured Feedback Collection

**Mid-Project Check-In (Monthly):**
1. "How would you rate our communication so far?"
2. "Is there anything you'd like more or less of?"
3. "Are you feeling confident about the project timeline?"
4. "Is the quality meeting your expectations?"
5. "What could I do better?"

**Post-Project Review:**
1. "Overall satisfaction (1-10):"
2. "Would you refer me to others? (Yes/No)"
3. "What was the best part of working together?"
4. "What could I improve?"
5. "Any additional services you'd like help with?"

### Handling Negative Feedback

**Don't:** Get defensive, argue, dismiss
**Do:** Thank them, understand, improve

**Script:**
"Thank you for this feedback. I appreciate your honesty.

Can you tell me more about [specific issue]? I want to make sure I fully understand.

[After understanding]

I hear you. Here's what I'll do differently going forward: [specific change].

Would that address your concern?"

## Boundaries and Availability

### Setting Boundaries

**What to Set Boundaries On:**
- Working hours (e.g., 9 AM - 6 PM, Mon-Fri)
- Response times (e.g., within 4 hours during business days)
- Scope limits (what's included, what's extra)
- Revision limits (e.g., 2 rounds per deliverable)
- Meeting frequency (e.g., 1 weekly status call)
- Payment terms (e.g., Net 15, late fees)

**Boundary Setting Script:**
"I want to make sure we have a great working relationship. Here's how I structure my availability:

- I'm available [hours/timezone] on [days]
- I respond to emails within [timeframe]
- Urgent issues get priority response
- I take [off days/hours] for focused work without interruptions

This ensures I give your project my full attention during work hours and maintain quality in everything I deliver."

### Enforcing Boundaries

**When a Client Crosses a Boundary:**
"I'm happy to help with this. However, to maintain quality on your project, I need to follow my standard communication schedule. I'll address this first thing [next business day]. For truly urgent issues, here's how to reach me: [emergency contact]."
