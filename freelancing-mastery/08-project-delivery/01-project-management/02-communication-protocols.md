# Communication Protocols

## The Communication Rhythm That Eliminates Client Anxiety

Poor communication is the #1 reason clients fire freelancers. It doesn't matter how good your code is — if they don't know what's happening, they'll assume the worst.

Great communication is a competitive advantage. It justifies premium rates, reduces revisions, and generates referrals.

---

## Why Communication Matters

### The Cost of Poor Communication

| Problem | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Client doesn't know progress | They micromanage |
| No regular updates | They assume you're not working |
| Surprises at milestones | Trust is broken |
| Slow responses | They feel unimportant |
| Vague status reports | They don't feel confident |
| No escalation process | Small issues become big crises |

### The ROI of Great Communication

- 50% fewer revisions (clear expectations)
- 80% less micromanagement (they trust you)
- 90% referral rate (they tell others)
- Premium pricing (professionalism commands premium)
- Long-term relationships (they stay for the communication, not just the code)

## The Weekly Status Report

### The Template

Subject: Weekly Status Report — [Project Name] — Week [X]

**TL;DR:**
[1 sentence summary — on track? ahead? behind?]

**What was completed this week:**
- [Feature/task 1] — Done
- [Feature/task 2] — In review
- [Feature/task 3] — In progress

**What's planned for next week:**
- [Feature/task 4]
- [Feature/task 5]
- [Feature/task 6]

**Blockers (if any):**
- [Blocker 1] — Need [client action] by [date]
- [Blocker 2] — Need [decision/input]

**Metrics:**
- Burn rate: [X] hours planned, [X] hours used
- Budget/spend: [$X] of [$X] total
- Timeline: On track / Ahead / Behind by [X] days

**Upcoming milestones:**
- [Milestone 1] — [date] — On track / At risk
- [Milestone 2] — [date] — On track / At risk

**Client action items:**
- [ ] Review and approve [deliverable] by [date]
- [ ] Provide [materials] by [date]
- [ ] Make decision on [topic] by [date]

### The Script for the Weekly Call

**Opening (2 min):**
"Here's where we stand. Overall, we're on track for the [milestone] deadline. This week we completed [X] and next week we're focused on [Y]."

**Demo (10-15 min):**
"Let me show you what we built this week. [Share screen and demo]. Here's what's working, and here's what I'd like your feedback on."

**Next Steps (5 min):**
"For next week, my priority is [X]. I need [Y] from you by [date] to stay on track. Any questions or concerns?"

**Closing (2 min):**
"Great. I'll send the meeting notes and we'll pick up on [next check-in date]."

## Communication Standards

### Response Time Standards

| Channel | Response Target | Maximum |
|---------|----------------|---------|
| Email | Within 4 hours | 24 hours |
| Slack/IM | Within 1 hour | 4 hours (business hours) |
| Urgent (SMS/Phone) | Within 15 min | 1 hour |
| Weekend | Next business day | Next business day |

### What to Communicate Immediately

**Always notify the client about:**
- Any delay to a milestone or deadline
- Any blocker that requires their input
- Any discovery that changes the approach
- Any cost or timeline impact
- Any security or data concern
- Any personnel change (if you sub out work)

**Proactive Communication Examples:**

"Quick heads up — I hit an unexpected issue with [dependency]. I've identified a solution that will cost [X] additional hours. Can we discuss on our next call?"

"Good news — I found a more efficient approach to [feature]. I'll save about [X] hours compared to the estimate."

"I need your input on [decision]. Here are the options with pros and cons. Can you let me know by [date]?"

### Meeting Etiquette

**Before Meeting:**
- Send agenda 24 hours in advance
- Confirm meeting with calendar invite
- Test audio/video 5 minutes before
- Have notes ready

**During Meeting:**
- Start on time
- State the agenda and timebox
- Stay on track — don't let tangents eat time
- Take notes
- End with clear action items
- End on time or early

**After Meeting:**
- Send meeting notes within 4 hours
- Include: decisions made, action items, next meeting
- Update project management tool

## Managing Client Expectations

### The Expectation Setting Framework

**At Project Start:**
1. Communication cadence
2. Response time expectations
3. Revision process
4. Change request process
5. Decision deadlines
6. What's in scope and out of scope
7. Definition of done

**Expectation Setting Script:**
"Before we kick off, let me share how I work:

1. **Communication:** I send weekly status reports every Friday with a video demo. I'm available on Slack during business hours and respond within 1 hour. Urgent issues get a phone call.

2. **Revisions:** You get 2 rounds of revisions per deliverable. Additional revisions are billed at my hourly rate.

3. **Changes:** If scope changes, we use a change request process. Small changes under 2 hours are free. Larger changes get a proposal.

4. **Decisions:** When I need a decision from you, I'll flag it with a deadline. Missed deadlines push the project timeline.

5. **Definition of done:** We'll agree on acceptance criteria for each deliverable before I start building.

Does this work for you?"

### The "No Surprises" Policy

**Rule:** No client should ever be surprised by project status. If they're surprised, that's a communication failure.

**How to implement:**
- Bad news early. Bad news often. Never let bad news be a surprise.
- Good news is nice. Bad news is critical.
- If something might cause a delay, tell them the moment you know.
- If you're unsure about something, communicate the uncertainty.

**Bad News Delivery Script:**
"I have some news that's not ideal. [Problem]. Here's the impact: [timeline/cost]. Here's my plan to address it: [solution]. I'll have this resolved by [date]. Do you have concerns about this approach?"

## Handling Difficult Clients

### The Difficult Client Types

| Type | Behavior | Strategy |
|------|----------|----------|
| The Micromanager | Wants daily updates, CC'd on everything | Over-communicate proactively |
| The Ghost | Goes silent for days/weeks, then reappears demanding progress | Set decision deadlines, use email as record |
| The Scope Creep | Constantly adds "small" requests | Change request process, flag every addition |
| The Late Payer | Drags feet on payments | Milestone-based payments, late fees |
| The Perfectionist | Never satisfied, demands endless revisions | Define "done" upfront, limit revisions |
| The Critic | Questions everything you do | Document decisions, build trust with wins |
| The Over-Sharer | Wants to be friends, calls about non-work | Maintain professional boundaries |
| The Panicker | Freaks out over every minor issue | Give regular updates to prevent anxiety |

### The Micromanager Protocol

**Why they micromanage:** They don't trust you (or anyone) to deliver without oversight.

**Solution:** Over-communicate before they ask. Answer questions they haven't asked yet.

**Daily Micromanagement Script:**
"Morning! Here's my plan for today:
1. [Task A]
2. [Task B]
3. [Task C]

Here's what I completed yesterday:
- [Completed item]

Here's what I'm working on now:
- [Current focus]

All on track. Will update at EOD."

**The Paradox:** The more you communicate, the less they'll ask. Give them control through information, and they'll stop trying to control you.

### The Ghost Client Protocol

**Why they ghost:** They're busy, they struggle with decisions, or they've lost interest.

**Strategies:**

1. **Set decision deadlines:**
"I need your feedback on [deliverable] by [date] to stay on schedule. If I don't hear from you, I'll assume approval and proceed."

2. **Use the "implied approval" clause:**
"Per our agreement, if I don't receive feedback within 3 business days, I'll consider this approved and move to the next phase."

3. **Scheduled check-ins:**
"Let's set a recurring 15-minute check-in every [day] to keep things moving."

4. **The decision document:**
"Here's a document with all decisions needed this week. Please review and respond by [date]."

### The Scope Creep Protocol

**Why they scope creep:** They see you as a resource, not a partner. They think "small" requests are part of the deal.

**The "Yes, and" Technique:**
"I can add that. Since it's outside the current scope, here's the impact:
- Timeline impact: +X days
- Cost impact: +$X
- Priority: This would push [other feature] to the next sprint

Would you like me to proceed with the change order?"

**The "Free vs. Paid" Threshold:**
Define a threshold (e.g., 2 hours or $100). Changes under the threshold are free. Above it goes through change control.

**Script for Small Request:**
"Happy to handle that. Since it'll take about [X hours], I'll include it in this week's work. For future reference, requests under 2 hours are on me; larger ones go through our change request process."

## Escalation Management

### The Escalation Framework

**Level 1: Issue Identification**
- You notice a problem
- Document the issue
- Assess the impact

**Level 2: Internal Resolution**
- Can you solve it?
- Estimate the fix
- Implement if under your authority

**Level 3: Client Notification**
- Inform the client
- State the impact
- Present the solution
- Get approval

**Level 4: Escalation**
- If issue is beyond your scope
- If client relationship is strained
- If payment or legal issues arise

### Escalation Script

"I need to raise an issue that requires your attention.

**What happened:** [Issue description]
**Impact:** [Timeline/cost/quality impact]
**My recommendation:** [Solution]
**Decision needed:** [What I need from you by when]

I'm available to discuss further if you'd like."

## Communication Tools Setup

### Recommended Stack

| Purpose | Tool | Why |
|---------|------|-----|
| Async communication | Slack or Discord | Real-time, searchable, organized |
| Video calls | Zoom or Google Meet | Reliable, recording, screen sharing |
| Status reports | Loom (video) + Email (written) | Personal touch + documentation |
| Documentation | Notion or Google Docs | Collaborative, versioned |
| Project management | Linear, Trello, or Notion | Transparent task tracking |
| Client portal | Basecamp or Notion | All-in-one for client |

### Setting Up Communication Channels

**Slack Structure for Client Projects:**

```
#general — Weekly updates, milestone announcements
#daily-standup — Daily updates (optional)
#feedback — Client feedback on deliverables
#decisions — Documented decisions
#random — Non-work chat
```

**Channel Etiquette:**
- `#general`: Only you post weekly updates
- `#feedback`: Client posts feedback, you respond
- `#decisions`: You document every decision made

### Creating a Client Hub (Notion Template)

Create a shared workspace with:

**Home Page:**
- Project overview
- Current status
- Key contacts

**Dashboard:**
- Timeline
- Budget tracker
- Milestone progress

**Documents:**
- Contract
- Scope of work
- Technical spec
- Meeting notes

**Tasks:**
- Current sprint
- Backlog
- Decision log

**Communication:**
- Weekly updates archive
- Meeting notes archive

## The Communication Audit

### Weekly Self-Assessment

1. Did the client express any confusion this week?
2. Were there any surprises for the client?
3. Did I communicate bad news immediately?
4. Did I respond to all messages within my target time?
5. Did the weekly report go out on time?

### Monthly Client Pulse Check

1. "How's the communication working for you?"
2. "Is there anything you'd like more or less of?"
3. "Are you feeling confident about the project progress?"
4. "Is there anything I could do better?"
