# Results-Based Engagements

## Overview

Results-based pricing ties your compensation directly to the outcomes you deliver — equity, revenue share, performance bonuses, or success fees. This is the highest-risk, highest-reward model. A single hit can pay more than 5 years of project work. A miss pays nothing. This model is for experienced freelancers who can accurately assess risk and have a portfolio that absorbs misses.

## When to Accept Results-Based Deals

### You SHOULD accept when:
- You have high confidence in the outcome (>80%)
- Client has strong traction (revenue, users, funding)
- Client is well-capitalized but cash-constrained
- You can control the execution
- The upside is 5x+ your normal fee
- You have a portfolio that can absorb a total loss
- The learning / case study value is enormous

### You SHOULD NOT accept when:
- You need the money to pay bills (take cash)
- The client has no track record
- The outcome depends on factors outside your control (sales, marketing)
- The upside is capped below 3x your normal fee
- The client is undercapitalized (can't pay even if successful)
- You have no leverage if things go wrong

## Results-Based Models

### 1. Equity Deals
You take company equity (stock, options, or SAFEs) instead of or in addition to cash.

**Structure:**
| Stage | Typical Equity Range | Cash Component | Vesting |
|-------|---------------------|----------------|---------|
| Idea / Pre-seed | 5-15% | $0-$10k | 4-year, 1-year cliff |
| Seed | 1-5% | $10k-$50k | 4-year, 1-year cliff |
| Series A | 0.5-2% | $50k-$100k | 3-year, 1-year cliff |
| Series B+ | <0.5% | Market-rate cash | 2-year |

**Valuation Considerations:**
- Pre-seed: Use standard SAFE with valuation cap
- Seed: 409A valuation or priced round
- Later stage: Use latest round valuation

**Protecting Yourself:**
- Insist on pro-rata rights (ability to invest in future rounds)
- Get information rights (quarterly financials)
- Include MFN clause (most favored nation)
- Ensure you're on the cap table properly (lawyer review)
- Get acceleration on change of control

**When to take equity:**
- You believe the company could be worth $10M+ in 5 years
- You're taking less than 50% cash
- You can afford to lose the entire amount
- You believe in the founder deeply

### 2. Revenue Share / Profit Share

You take a percentage of revenue or profit generated from your work.

**Structure:**
```
Revenue Share: 5-20% of incremental revenue for 12-36 months
Profit Share:  10-30% of incremental profit for 12-36 months
```

**Example: E-commerce Optimization**
- You build a system that increases conversion rate by 20%
- Client currently does $1M/year in revenue
- Incremental revenue: $200k/year
- Your share: 10% = $20k/year for 2 years = $40k total
- Plus: $10k upfront cash (to cover costs)

**Revenue Share Terms to Negotiate:**
| Term | Typical Range | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Share percentage | 5-25% | Higher if you also build the sales channel |
| Duration | 12-36 months | Longer = lower %, shorter = higher % |
| Cap | 2-5x your normal fee | Prevents infinite liability for them |
| Floor | Your costs at minimum | Never do pure rev share without covering costs |
| Attribution window | 30-90 days | Revenue within X days of your work counts |
| Audit rights | Quarterly | You can verify their numbers |
| Payment timing | Monthly/Quarterly | Net-30 or Net-60 |

### 3. Performance Bonus

You take a lower base fee + a bonus tied to specific metrics.

**Structure:**
```
Base Fee:    50-70% of your normal rate
Bonus Pool:  50-100% of base fee, tied to results
```

**Examples:**
| Engagement | Base Fee | Bonus Trigger | Bonus Amount |
|-----------|----------|---------------|------------|
| SEO optimization | $5k | 50% increase in organic traffic | $5k |
| Conversion optimization | $8k | 25% increase in conversion rate | $8k |
| App development | $20k | 10k users in first 90 days | $10k |
| Performance optimization | $3k | 40% reduction in page load time | $5k |

**Note:** Only accept bonuses tied to things you control. Never tie bonus to revenue if you're doing tech work.

### 4. Success Fee / Commission

You get paid a percentage of a specific transaction.

**Common Scenarios:**
| Scenario | Typical Success Fee | Notes |
|----------|-------------------|-------|
| Fundraising introduction | 1-5% of funds raised | Common for advisors |
| Acquisition introduction | 1-3% of deal size | Common for M&A advisors |
| Partnership brokering | 10-20% of first year deal value | Common for BD |
| Client referral | 10-20% of first project | Common for freelancers |

**Important:** Success fees are heavily regulated for certain industries (fundraising, M&A). Consult a lawyer.

### 5. Hybrid Model (Cash + Upside)

**The most practical approach.** Take enough cash to cover your costs and basic profit, then add upside.

**Example:**
```
Cash Component:    $15,000 (covers costs + $5k profit)
Equity Component:  1% vested over 2 years
OR
Revenue Share:     5% of incremental revenue for 18 months
OR
Bonus:             $10k if users exceed 50k within 6 months
```

**Why hybrid works:**
- Covers your downside (you're profitable regardless)
- Gives you upside (if they win big, you win big)
- Aligns incentives (both parties want success)
- Easier to sell to clients (they see you're invested)

## The Results-Based Deal Scorecard

Before accepting any results-based deal, score it:

| Factor | Weight | Score (1-10) | Weighted |
|--------|--------|-------------|----------|
| Founder quality (track record, grit, vision) | 20% | ___ | ___ |
| Market size (TAM, growth rate) | 15% | ___ | ___ |
| Traction (revenue, users, growth) | 20% | ___ | ___ |
| Time to liquidity (when can you cash out?) | 15% | ___ | ___ |
| Your control over outcome | 15% | ___ | ___ |
| Upside multiple vs your normal fee | 10% | ___ | ___ |
| Legal protection (rights, vesting, clawback) | 5% | ___ | ___ |

**Score:**
- 8+: Go for it
- 6-7: Proceed with caution, tighten terms
- Below 6: Pass (or take only cash)

## Structuring the Deal

### Term Sheet Essentials
Put everything in a term sheet before legal docs:

1. **Parties** — Who's who
2. **Services** — What you'll do
3. **Compensation** — Cash + upside details
4. **Vesting** — If equity, vesting schedule
5. **Cliff** — Initial period before any upside vests
6. **Anti-dilution** — Protection if they raise more money
7. **Information rights** — What you can see
8. **Exit provisions** — What happens on acquisition
9. **Non-compete / non-solicit** — Reasonable only
10. **Termination** — How either party can exit
11. **Dispute resolution** — Arbitration vs court
12. **Governing law** — Which jurisdiction

### Contract Must-Haves
- **Scope of work** (specific, measurable)
- **Definition of success** (clear, binary where possible)
- **Payment triggers** (exactly when each payment happens)
- **Audit rights** (you can verify their numbers)
- **Clawback protections** (they can't take your code and run)
- **IP escrow** (code held in escrow until paid)
- **Exit provisions** (what happens in acquisition/IPO)
- **Most favored nation** (you get any better terms offered later)

## Negotiating Results-Based Deals

### Your Leverage Points
1. **Your expertise is scarce** — They can't find someone else as good
2. **You're taking risk** — That deserves premium returns
3. **You have options** — You can take cash deals elsewhere
4. **You're aligned** — They benefit from you winning too

### What to Push For
- **Higher percentage** (especially if you're early)
- **Shorter vesting** (faster liquidity)
- **Cash floor** (minimum payment regardless)
- **Information rights** (visibility into their numbers)
- **Pro-rata rights** (ability to maintain ownership %)
- **Acceleration** (on change of control)

### What to Give On
- **Exclusivity** (you won't work for competitors)
- **Longer vesting** (if more equity)
- **Board/mtg attendance** (they want your input)
- **Right of first refusal** (on future work)
- **Non-compete** (reasonable scope only)

## Tracking Results

### Build an Outcomes Dashboard
Track everything:
- Their revenue (monthly)
- Their users (monthly)
- Metrics you impacted (specific KPI)
- Your equity value (quarterly valuation estimate)
- Cash received to date
- Projected upside

### Verification Mechanisms
- Quarterly financial statements
- Real-time dashboard access
- Third-party validation (if applicable)
- Audit right exercised annually

## The Tax Implications

Results-based income is taxed differently depending on structure:
- **Cash bonuses:** Ordinary income
- **Stock options:** Taxed on exercise (AMT risk with ISOs)
- **Equity grants:** Taxed on vesting (83(b) election available)
- **Revenue share:** Ordinary income
- **Capital gains:** Only when you sell equity (held >1 year)

**Always consult a CPA before signing.** Stock compensation can create massive tax bills without cash to pay them.

## Real Success Stories

### Case 1: The $1M Equity Payout
- Freelance developer built MVP for pre-seed startup
- Took $20k cash + 5% equity
- Startup raised Series A at $50M valuation 3 years later
- Developer's equity: $2.5M (diluted to 3% after rounds)
- Cash out: $1M partial liquidity in secondary sale

### Case 2: The Revenue Share Goldmine
- Freelance SEO specialist worked with e-commerce company
- $5k/month cash + 10% of incremental revenue
- Grew revenue from $50k/mo to $200k/mo in 18 months
- Monthly pay: $5k cash + $15k revenue share = $20k/mo
- Total over 2 years: $480k

### Case 3: The Miss
- Freelance CTO took 10% equity at pre-seed
- Rejected $15k/month cash offers to work on startup
- Startup ran out of money after 18 months
- Zero payout, zero salary for 18 months
- Total loss: $270k in opportunity cost

## When to Walk Away

Results-based deals have asymmetric risk. Walk away when:
- You can't afford to lose the time/money
- The founder has red flags (integrity, competence, commitment)
- The market is tiny or shrinking
- You have no control over the outcome
- The legal terms are unfair
- Your gut says no (trust it)

## Summary Rules

1. Never do pure results-based — always get some cash
2. 10x upside minimum for equity deals
3. 3x minimum for revenue share
4. Always have clear, measurable success metrics
5. Get legal review on every equity deal
6. Build a portfolio (don't put all eggs in one basket)
7. Verify everything (don't trust, verify)
8. Have an exit strategy (what happens if they fail/succeed)
9. Track your portfolio value monthly
10. Take wins when available (partial liquidity)
11. 80% of results-based deals will pay zero — plan for it
12. The one that hits will cover all the misses
