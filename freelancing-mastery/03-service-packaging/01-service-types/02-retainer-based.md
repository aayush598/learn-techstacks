# Retainer-Based Engagements

## Overview

Retainers are recurring monthly contracts that provide predictable income and deep client relationships. Building a base of 5-10 retainer clients at $3k-$15k+/mo each is the fastest path to $200k-$500k+/year with a manageable workload. Retainers are the #1 wealth-building vehicle in freelancing.

## Why Retainers Are Superior

| Metric | Project-Based | Retainer |
|--------|--------------|----------|
| Income predictability | Low (lumpy) | High (steady) |
| Client relationship | Transactional | Partnership |
| Sales effort | Constant (new projects) | Periodic (upsells) |
| Income ceiling | Hours-limited | Value-limited |
| Recession resilience | Low | High |
| Selling value | Harder (fixed deliverable) | Easier (ongoing value) |
| Workload smoothing | Impossible | Controllable |

## Retainer Models

### 1. Monthly Block Retainer ($2k-$8k/mo)
Client buys a block of hours each month. Unused hours may roll over (1 month max) or expire.

**Structure:**
- 20 hours/month @ $150/hr = $3,000/mo
- 40 hours/month @ $125/hr = $5,000/mo
- 80 hours/month @ $100/hr = $8,000/mo

**Pros:** Simple, familiar, easy to sell
**Cons:** Still time-based (caps upside), tracking overhead

**Best for:** Ongoing development, maintenance, small features

### 2. Fixed Scope Retainer ($3k-$15k/mo)
Client pays a flat monthly fee for a defined set of deliverables each month.

**Structure:**
| Tier | Monthly Fee | Deliverables |
|------|------------|--------------|
| Starter | $3,000/mo | 20 hours dev + 2 hours strategy + monthly report |
| Growth | $6,000/mo | 40 hours dev + 4 hours strategy + priority support |
| Scale | $12,000/mo | 80 hours dev + 8 hours strategy + on-call support |

**Pros:** Predictable delivery, easy to communicate value
**Cons:** Scope creep risk if deliverables aren't well-defined

**Best for:** Maintenance, content updates, ongoing feature development

### 3. Fractional Role Retainer ($5k-$25k+/mo)
You act as a part-time executive or lead — Fractional CTO, Fractional DevOps Lead, Fractional Head of Engineering.

**Structure:**
- Weekly strategic meetings (1-2 hours)
- Monthly strategy session (2-4 hours)
- On-call for emergencies (defined SLAs)
- Hands-on work (X hours included)
- Team management / vendor oversight
- Architecture decisions
- Code reviews

**Pricing:**
| Role | Hours/Week | Monthly Fee |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Fractional CTO (startup) | 5-10 hrs | $5k-$8k |
| Fractional CTO (growth) | 10-20 hrs | $8k-$15k |
| Fractional DevOps Lead | 10-20 hrs | $6k-$12k |
| Fractional Head of Product | 10-15 hrs | $7k-$12k |
| Fractional Security Officer | 5-10 hrs | $5k-$10k |

**Pros:** Highest rates, high perceived value, deep relationships
**Cons:** Requires senior expertise, more responsibility, harder to replace

**Best for:** Senior/expert freelancers, startups that can't afford full-time execs

### 4. Support & Maintenance Retainer ($1k-$5k/mo)
Post-launch support for projects you or others built.

**Structure:**
- Bug fixes (X hours included)
- Security updates (dependency bumps)
- Uptime monitoring
- Monthly health reports
- Emergency response (defined SLA)

**Pricing:**
| SLA Level | Response Time | Monthly Fee |
|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| Standard | Next business day | $1,000-$2,000 |
| Priority | 4 hours | $2,000-$4,000 |
| Critical | 1 hour | $4,000-$8,000 |

**Pros:** Easy upsell from project work, low cognitive load
**Cons:** Can be interrupt-driven, lower rates

### 5. Advisory Retainer ($3k-$10k/mo)
Strategic guidance without hands-on delivery. Investors, advisors, board members.

**Structure:**
- 2-4 strategy calls per month
- Email/chat access (async)
- Document review
- Intro meetings / network access

**Pros:** Highest hourly rate ($300-$1000/hr), no execution burden
**Cons:** Requires deep expertise + network, harder to find clients

## Retainer Pricing Strategy

### The Minimum Viable Retainer
Never go below $1,000/mo. Below that, the administrative overhead kills your effective rate. $2,500/mo is a better floor.

### Tiered Pricing Structure
```
Basic:   $3,000/mo  — 20 hours/mo + email support
Pro:     $6,000/mo  — 40 hours/mo + priority support + monthly strategy
Elite:   $12,000/mo — 80 hours/mo + 24/7 support + monthly strategy + quarterly review
```

**The Magic Ratio:** Each tier is roughly 2x the previous. Creates clear differentiation and upsell path.

### Annual vs Monthly
- **Monthly:** Standard, easier to sell
- **Quarterly (paid upfront):** 5-10% discount — improves your cash flow
- **Annual (paid upfront):** 15-20% discount — eliminates churn risk
- **Auto-renewal:** Default, with 30-day cancellation notice

Always prefer longer commitments. Annual retainers are worth the discount because they remove sales cycle overhead.

## Getting Retainer Clients

### 1. Start with Past Project Clients
Your best retainer clients are people you've already delivered for. After any project:
```
"Hey [Client], it was great building [Project] for you. 
I want to make sure it keeps running smoothly and continues 
to grow with your business.

I have a few spots open for monthly retainer clients. 
Would a 30-minute call to explore what ongoing support 
could look like make sense?"
```

### 2. The "Maintenance Gap" Pitch
Many clients don't think about maintenance until something breaks. Be proactive:
```
"Most people don't realize this, but software needs regular 
updates to stay secure and compatible. Dependencies go out 
of date, security patches are released, and your hosting 
needs monitoring.

My [Retainer Name] ensures your project stays secure, fast, 
and up-to-date. Want to discuss it?"
```

### 3. Fractional Role Positioning
For fractional roles, position as the affordable executive:
```
"Getting a full-time CTO costs $200k+/year + equity. 
You get me 10 hours/week for $8k/month. I bring 
[Y years] experience across [X companies] — you skip 
the learning curve and the full-time commitment."
```

### 4. The "From Project to Partner" Transition
After delivering a successful project:
1. Schedule a "quarterly review" call
2. Review metrics, successes, opportunities
3. Identify ongoing needs (features, maintenance, scaling)
4. Propose a retainer that addresses those needs

## Retainer Contracts

### Essential Clauses
- **Scope of services** — what's included, what's excluded
- **Hours/deliverables** — monthly commitment (yours and theirs)
- **Fee structure** — monthly amount, payment terms
- **Term** — month-to-month, 3-month minimum, annual
- **Notice period** — 30-60 days cancellation
- **Excess hours** — rate for overage ($X/hr, billed monthly)
- **Expenses** — what's covered, approval process
- **Emergency definition** — what constitutes an emergency vs normal request
- **IP ownership** — code produced is theirs (paid monthly)
- **Non-solicitation** — they can't hire your subcontractors

### Auto-Renewal Language
```
This Agreement shall automatically renew for successive 
one-month terms unless either party provides written notice 
of non-renewal at least 30 days prior to the end of the 
then-current term.
```

## Managing Retainer Clients

### Communicate Value Monthly
Send a monthly report showing:
- Hours worked (with breakdown)
- Deliverables completed
- Issues resolved
- Metrics improved (uptime, performance, etc.)
- Value delivered ($$$ saved, revenue enabled)

This prevents clients from wondering "what am I paying for?"

### Over-Deliver Strategically
- Use 80% of their hours consistently (leave 20% buffer for emergencies)
- Occasionally do a small thing outside scope (goodwill)
- Send relevant articles/opportunities unprompted

### The 80/20 Rule
80% of your retainer revenue will come from 20% of clients. Identify your top clients and give them disproportionate attention. Fire the bottom 20% to free capacity.

### The Retainer Review Cadence
- **Weekly:** Status update (async, 5 min)
- **Monthly:** Deliverable review + next month plan
- **Quarterly:** Business review (metrics, strategy, alignment)
- **Annually:** Contract renewal + rate adjustment

## Scaling Retainers

### From 1 to 5 Clients
- Total: $3k-$15k/mo
- Requires: Great delivery, responsive communication
- Tools: Simple tracking (Toggl/Harvest), Google Docs

### From 5 to 10 Clients
- Total: $15k-$50k/mo
- Requires: Systems, templates, some automation
- Tools: CRM, project management, time tracking
- Need: Standardized reports, fixed schedules

### From 10 to 20+ Clients
- Total: $50k-$150k+/mo
- Requires: Team, subcontractors, multiple fractional roles
- Tools: Full agency stack (HubSpot, Asana, Slack, Harvest)
- Need: Operations manager, delivery lead

## Retainer Churn Prevention

### Top Reasons Clients Cancel
1. They don't see the value (you didn't communicate it)
2. Their budget changed (unexpected, but sometimes real)
3. They hired internally (you didn't provide enough strategic value)
4. They don't need you anymore (you did your job too well)

### Churn Prevention Tactics
- Monthly value report (quantify everything)
- Quarterly business review (in-person or video)
- Build a relationship beyond the work (their goals, family, hobbies)
- Be proactive, not reactive (suggest improvements before they ask)
- Create switching costs (deep integration with their processes)
- Offer annual discount (locks them in for 12 months)

## Summary Rules

1. Minimum $2,500/mo retainer
2. 3-month minimum commitment for new retainers
3. Annual contracts get 15% discount
4. Monthly value report for every client
5. Quarterly business reviews
6. Never let a single client exceed 40% of retainer revenue
7. Raise rates 10-20% annually on renewals
8. Fire clients who are >20% over hours consistently
9. Always have a waitlist (scarcity sells)
10. Fractional roles pay 2-3x more per hour than block retainers
