# Tiered Packages (Good / Better / Best)

## Overview

Three-tier pricing is the most effective pricing structure in freelancing. By offering Good / Better / Best, you anchor perception at the top tier, make the middle tier look reasonable, and give budget-conscious clients an entry point. This structure can increase revenue by 30-60% without changing your actual service.

## The Psychology of Three Tiers

### The Anchoring Effect
When you show three prices, the client's brain compares them against each other, not against external benchmarks. The highest tier becomes the anchor, making the middle tier look like a deal — even if it's priced higher than what you'd sell as a single offer.

### The Decoy Effect
The middle tier is designed to be your "sweet spot" — the one most people will choose. The basic tier is stripped down to push people up. The premium tier is loaded with extras to make the middle look reasonable.

### The Goldilocks Principle
Too cheap = not credible. Too expensive = out of reach. Three tiers let clients choose what feels right for them, while you steer them toward your preferred option.

## The Standard Three-Tier Structure

```
Basic         Pro           Premium
$X,XXX        $X,XXX        $X,XXX
[30% of       [50% of       [20% of
 sales]        sales]        sales]
```

**Pricing ratio:** 1x / 2x / 4x (or 1x / 2x / 3x)

**Examples:**
| Basic | Pro | Premium |
|-------|-----|---------|
| $2,500 | $5,000 | $10,000 |
| $3,000 | $6,000 | $12,000 |
| $5,000 | $10,000 | $20,000 |
| $1,000 | $2,500 | $5,000 |

## How to Structure Each Tier

### Basic Tier (The Entry Point)
**Purpose:** Capture price-sensitive clients. Show low barrier to entry.

**Characteristics:**
- Just the core deliverable, no extras
- Self-service or minimal involvement
- Longer timeline
- Limited revisions (if any)
- Email-only support

**Pricing:** 1x base unit
**Revenue target:** 30% of sales

**Example — Web Dev:**
```
BASIC — $3,000
- 3-page responsive website
- Template-based design
- 1 round of revisions
- Basic SEO setup
- 5 business days delivery
- Email support only
```

### Pro Tier (The Sweet Spot)
**Purpose:** This is what you WANT people to buy. Most revenue comes from this tier.

**Characteristics:**
- Full core deliverable
- Some customization
- Faster timeline
- Reasonable revisions (2-3 rounds)
- Mixed support (email + 1 call)

**Pricing:** 2x base unit
**Revenue target:** 50-60% of sales

**Example — Web Dev:**
```
PRO — $6,000
- 5-page custom website
- Custom design (not template)
- 2 rounds of revisions
- Full SEO optimization
- Social media integration
- Analytics setup
- 3 business days delivery
- Email + 1 strategy call
```

### Premium Tier (The Anchor)
**Purpose:** Anchor the value. Make Pro look reasonable. Capture high-end clients.

**Characteristics:**
- Everything from Pro
- More pages / features
- Additional deliverables (strategy, training, etc.)
- Fastest timeline
- Unlimited revisions (within reason)
- Priority support (phone/Slack)
- Bonuses that cost you little but have high perceived value

**Pricing:** 3-4x base unit
**Revenue target:** 10-20% of sales

**Note:** Even if only 10% buy Premium, it dramatically increases perception of all tiers.

**Example — Web Dev:**
```
PREMIUM — $12,000
- 10+ page custom website
- Custom design + branding package
- Unlimited revisions
- Full SEO + content strategy
- E-commerce integration
- Blog setup with 5 blog posts
- Email marketing integration
- 2 business days delivery
- 3 months of maintenance
- Priority Slack support
- 1-hour strategy session monthly
```

## Pricing Ratios and Psychology

### The 1x / 2x / 4x Ratio
```
Basic:   $3,000  (1x)
Pro:     $6,000  (2x)
Premium: $12,000 (4x)
```

**Why it works:** The jump from Basic to Pro is 2x (seems reasonable for what you get). The jump from Pro to Premium is 2x again (seems like a lot, but the extras make it tempting for those who want the best).

### The 1x / 2.5x / 5x Ratio
```
Basic:   $2,000  (1x)
Pro:     $5,000  (2.5x)
Premium: $10,000 (5x)
```

Use when there's a bigger gap in scope between tiers.

### The 1x / 3x / 6x Ratio
```
Basic:   $1,500  (1x)
Pro:     $4,500  (3x)
Premium: $9,000  (6x)
```

Use when Basic is genuinely reduced in scope (not just stripped down).

## Feature Differentiation Strategy

### What Goes in Basic?
- Core deliverable (minimum viable version)
- No customization (templates, patterns, defaults)
- Self-service onboarding
- Standard timeline
- Minimal support

### What Goes in Pro?
- Full core deliverable
- Some customization
- Assisted onboarding
- Faster timeline
- Priority support
- 1-2 strategic bonuses

### What Goes in Premium?
- Everything in Pro
- Maximum customization
- White-glove onboarding
- Fastest timeline
- 24/7 support
- Multiple strategic bonuses
- Long-term value (maintenance, training, etc.)

### Creating Clear Differentiation
Each tier should have at least 3-4 differentiators from adjacent tiers.

**Bad differentiation:**
| Basic | Pro | Premium |
|-------|-----|---------|
| 5 pages | 7 pages | 10 pages |
| 2 revisions | 4 revisions | 6 revisions |

**Good differentiation:**
| Basic | Pro | Premium |
|-------|-----|---------|
| Template design | Custom design | Custom branding |
| 5 pages | 10 pages | Unlimited pages |
| 1 revision round | 3 revision rounds | Unlimited revisions |
| Email support | Email + Slack | Slack + Phone |
| No maintenance | 1 month maintenance | 3 months maintenance |

## Presenting the Tiers

### The Comparison Table
Display on your sales page as a horizontal comparison:

```
                    BASIC       PRO         PREMIUM
Price               $3,000      $6,000      $12,000
Pages               Up to 5     Up to 10    Unlimited
Design              Template    Custom      Custom Brand
Revisions           1 round     3 rounds    Unlimited
Timeline            5 days      3 days      2 days
Support             Email       Email+Slack Slack+Phone
SEO                 Basic       Advanced    Advanced +
Analytics           —           Included    Included +
Maintenance         —           1 month     3 months
Training            —           —           2 hours
                    [SELECT]    [SELECT]    [SELECT]
```

### Recommended vs Non-Recommended
Mark one tier as "Most Popular" or "Recommended":

```
BASIC       ★ PRO (RECOMMENDED)       PREMIUM
$3,000      $6,000                     $12,000
```

This pushes clients toward your preferred tier.

### The "None of These Work" Option
If a client says none of the tiers fit, you have two options:
1. Custom quote (typically between Basic and Pro if they want less, or above Premium if they want more)
2. Refer them to someone else (if they can't afford Basic)

## Feature Selection: How to Decide What Goes Where

### The Value Matrix
| Feature | Value to Client | Cost to You | Tier |
|---------|----------------|-------------|------|
| Core deliverable | Very high | High | All tiers |
| Custom design | High | Medium | Pro+ |
| Unlimited revisions | High | High | Premium only |
| Training session | Medium | Low | Premium only |
| Priority support | Medium | Low | Pro+ |
| Analytics setup | Medium | Low | Pro+ |
| Maintenance | High | Medium | Premium only |
| Strategy call | Very high | Low | Pro+ |

**Rule:** Low-cost, high-value features go in Premium to make it look amazing. Medium-value features go in Pro to make it compelling. Basic has the bare minimum.

### The Bonuses Strategy
Premium tier should have 3-5 "bonus" features that cost you almost nothing but have high perceived value:

| Bonus | Your Cost | Perceived Value |
|-------|-----------|----------------|
| Strategy session (1 hour) | 1 hour | $500 |
| Template pack | 0 (already created) | $300 |
| Loom walkthrough | 15 min | $200 |
| Referral to [partner] | 5 min | $500 |
| Case study inclusion | 30 min | $1,000 |
| Early access to future services | 0 | $500 |
| PDF guide | 0 (already created) | $100 |

## Sales Conversations with Tiered Pricing

### How to Present Tiers

**Option 1: The "Which Is Best for You?" Approach**
```
"We have three packages. Most clients go with Pro 
because it includes [key feature]. But let me ask 
you a few questions to see which fits best."
```

**Option 2: The Top-Down Approach**
```
"Let me start by showing you what's possible. 
Our Premium package includes [amazing things]. 
For most businesses, Pro is the sweet spot — 
it has [key features] without the extras. 
And Basic covers the essentials if you're 
getting started."
```

**Option 3: The Diagnostic Approach**
```
"Based on what you've told me about your goals, 
I think the Pro package would work well. 
Here's why: [tie features to their needs]."
```

### Handling "This Is Too Expensive"

**Response 1: Frame Against the Alternative**
```
"I understand it's an investment. But consider this:
- Hiring someone in-house: $80k+/year
- Doing nothing: losing $X/month in [problem cost]
- Our Pro package: $6,000 one-time

For less than one month of an employee's salary, 
you get a solution that works immediately."
```

**Response 2: Offer a Better Payment Structure**
```
"Would it help if we split it into payments?
$2,000 upfront, $2,000 on delivery — that's
$4,000/month for 3 months instead of $6,000 now."
```

**Response 3: Suggest Basic with an Upgrade Path**
```
"The Basic package at $3,000 would cover your 
core needs now. You can always upgrade to Pro 
or Premium later as your needs grow."
```

### Handling "I Want Custom"

```
"That's fair. Based on what you need, I'd recommend
starting with Pro ($6,000) and we can add 
[custom element] as an add-on for $X.
Would that work, or would you like me to put 
together a custom quote?"
```

## Advanced Tiered Strategies

### The "Hidden" Decoy
Create a fourth tier that's absurdly expensive to make Premium look reasonable:

```
Starter:   $3,000
Growth:    $6,000  (what you want to sell)
Premium:   $12,000
Enterprise: $25,000 (custom, published as "Contact Us")
```

### The "As Low As" Strategy
Show monthly pricing to make tiers feel affordable:
```
Basic:    $250/month ($3,000 one-time)
Pro:      $500/month ($6,000 one-time)
Premium:  $1,000/month ($12,000 one-time)
```

### The "Pay More, Get More" Upsell
At the end of delivery for Basic/Pro:
```
"FYI — a lot of Basic clients end up upgrading to Pro
for [feature]. I can do that upgrade now for the 
difference in price ($3,000). Want me to add it?"
```

## Measuring Tier Performance

### Key Metrics
| Metric | Target | Action If Off |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| Basic sales % | 30-40% | If >50%, Basic is too good (strip features) |
| Pro sales % | 40-50% | If <30%, Pro doesn't differentiate enough |
| Premium sales % | 10-20% | If <5%, Premium isn't compelling enough |
| Average order value | Target $X | Raise prices or shift feature mix |
| Upgrade rate (Basic→Pro) | 20%+ | Improve onboarding, show Pro value |

### Adjusting Tiers Based on Data

**Too many Basic sales?**
- Cut Basic features further
- Pro needs more compelling features
- Basic price might be too close to Pro

**Too many Premium sales?**
- You're underpricing Premium
- Basic might be too weak (no one chooses it)
- Premium might be offering too much for too little

**Too many Pro sales? (rare but possible)**
- Pro is priced too low
- Premium isn't differentiated enough
- Increase Pro price or add more value to Premium

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Too Little Differentiation
```
Basic: $3,000 - 5 pages
Pro: $4,000 - 7 pages
Premium: $5,000 - 10 pages
```
This is weak. The client sees 3 similar options and chooses the cheapest. Increase differentiation significantly.

### Mistake 2: Too Many Tiers
4+ tiers cause analysis paralysis. 3 is optimal. 2 is okay. 4+ hurts conversion.

### Mistake 3: Hiding Prices
"Contact for pricing" on tiered packages defeats the purpose. List prices visibly.

### Mistake 4: Making Basic Too Good
If Basic is too good, nobody upgrades. Basic should feel like it's missing things, not like a great deal.

### Mistake 5: Not Marking a Recommendation
Without a "Most Popular" badge, clients don't know which to pick. Guide them.

## Summary Rules

1. Always use three tiers: Good / Better / Best
2. Price ratio: 1x / 2x / 3-4x
3. Basic = bare minimum (30% of sales)
4. Pro = sweet spot, marked "Recommended" (50% of sales)
5. Premium = anchor with bonuses (20% of sales)
6. Differentiate by type of feature, not just quantity
7. Add low-cost, high-value bonuses to Premium
8. Show prices publicly on your site
9. Present top-down (show Premium first)
10. Track tier mix and adjust monthly
11. Raise prices when Pro becomes "too easy to sell"
12. Custom quotes go between tiers, not below Basic
