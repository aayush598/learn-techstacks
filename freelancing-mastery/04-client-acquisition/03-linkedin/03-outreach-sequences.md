# LinkedIn Outreach Sequences

## The Art of the Cold DM

LinkedIn direct messages (DMs) are the highest-converting cold outreach channel for freelancers — when done right. The key difference from email: LinkedIn is a professional social network. Your outreach must be contextually aware, personalized, and value-first.

This guide gives you complete sequences for every stage of the outreach process.

---

## 1. The Connection Request

Your connection request note is the first thing a prospect sees. This single message determines whether they accept or ignore you.

### The Formula

```
[Compliment/Commonality] + [Reason for Connecting] + [No Pitch]
```

**Never pitch in a connection request. Ever.**

### Connection Request Templates

**Template 1: Content-Based (Highest Conversion)**
```
I really enjoyed your recent post about [topic]. I've been exploring the same area and would love to connect and follow your work.
```

**Template 2: Mutual Connection**
```
I see we both know [Name]. They've spoken highly of your work in [area]. Would love to connect.
```

**Template 3: Industry Peers**
```
I've been following [Company]'s growth in [industry]. Impressed by what you're building. Would love to connect.
```

**Template 4: Direct Compliment**
```
Your work on [specific project/result] is impressive. I'd love to connect and learn more about your approach.
```

**Template 5: Value-First (for decision-makers)**
```
I've been researching [their industry/problem] and I think my [service] could help. No pitch — just want to connect and understand your needs.
```

### Connection Request Rules

- **300 characters max** (LinkedIn limits)
- **Personalize every request** (no copy-paste)
- **Mention something specific** (their post, their role, their company)
- **Zero sales pitch** in the request
- **No links** (LinkedIn may flag)

## 2. The Post-Accept Sequence

Once they accept, you have a window of opportunity. Don't pitch immediately. Build a foundation first.

### Day 1: Welcome Message

**Template (Value-First):**
```
Thanks for connecting, [Name]! I really enjoyed [specific thing about their work].

I've been working on [topic related to their work] recently. If you're interested, I'd be happy to share [valuable resource — article, guide, tool].

No pressure, just wanted to say hi!
```

**Template (Question-Based):**
```
Appreciate the connect, [Name]!

I'm curious — what's the biggest challenge you're facing with [their area] right now?

Just trying to understand the landscape better.
```

**Template (Observation):**
```
Thanks, [Name]! I noticed you've been doing interesting work with [topic]. I've been exploring similar approaches with my clients.

Would love to hear how it's going on your end.
```

### Day 3-7: Add Value

After the welcome, don't message again immediately. Instead:

- Like and comment on their posts
- Share their content with your network
- Tag them (sparingly and relevantly)
- Send a relevant resource

**Value-Add Message:**
```
Hi [Name], saw your post about [topic] — really insightful.

Thought you might find this [article/resource/tool] useful. It covers [brief description].

Hope it helps!
```

### Day 7-14: Move Toward Conversation

After establishing a connection and adding value:

**Template 1: Common Challenge**
```
Hi [Name], I've been thinking about [problem in their industry] and how it affects companies like yours.

I've developed an approach that [specific benefit]. Would you be open to a 15-minute chat to discuss?

No pitch — just curious if my thinking aligns with what you're seeing.
```

**Template 2: Direct Offer**
```
Hi [Name], based on our conversation, I think I could help with [their challenge].

I've helped similar companies achieve [result] in [timeframe].

If you're interested, I can share a case study. No pressure either way.
```

**Template 3: Event/Content Invite**
```
Hi [Name], I'm hosting a [webinar/workshop/AMA] on [topic] this [day/time].

I think you'd find it valuable given your work in [area]. Here's the link: [link]

No pressure if you can't make it — I'll share the recording.
```

## 3. The Full Outreach Sequence

### The 7-Step Sequence for Decision-Makers

**Step 1: Connection Request (Day 1)**
```
I really enjoyed your article about [topic] in [publication]. Would love to connect.
```

**Step 2: Welcome Message (Day 2)**
```
Thanks for connecting! I found your perspective on [topic] really refreshing. I've been working in this space for [time] and agree with your take on [specific point].

If you ever want to chat about [topic], I'm always happy to connect with fellow professionals in the space.
```

**Step 3: Add Value (Day 4)**
Share their post, comment thoughtfully, send a resource.

**Step 4: Insight Message (Day 7)**
```
Hi [Name], I've been analyzing [their company/industry] and noticed [insight].

I have an idea that could help with [specific challenge]. Would you be open to a brief call to discuss?
```

**Step 5: Direct Offer (Day 10)**
```
Hi [Name], following up on my last message. I've helped [similar company] achieve [specific result] with [specific approach].

I think a similar approach could work for [their company]. Happy to share more details if you're interested.
```

**Step 6: Social Proof (Day 14)**
```
Hi [Name], here's a case study showing how I helped [client] solve a similar challenge: [link]

Thought you might find this relevant to what you're working on.
```

**Step 7: Breakup (Day 21)**
```
Hi [Name], I understand you're busy. I'll stop following up for now.

If anything changes on your end, feel free to reach out. I'm always happy to chat about [topic].

Wishing you and [company] all the best.
```

### The 5-Step Sequence for Warm Leads

**Step 1: Connection Request with Comment**
```
Your recent post about [topic] was excellent. Particularly your point about [specific detail]. Would love to connect.
```

**Step 2: Engage with Their Content**
Comment on 2-3 of their posts. Be thoughtful, not generic.

**Step 3: Direct Message**
```
Hey [Name], I've been following your content on [topic] and really value your perspective.

I specialize in [service] for [target client]. Given your work in [area], I think we might be able to collaborate or I could help with [specific need].

Would you be open to a 10-minute chat to explore?
```

**Step 4: Follow Up (Day 5)**
```
Hey [Name], just following up on my message. I know you're busy — even a quick "not interested" is appreciated so I don't keep following up 😊
```

**Step 5: Final Attempt (Day 10)**
```
Last message from me on this, [Name]. If [specific problem] ever becomes a priority, I'd love to help. I'll keep sharing content on [topic] — hope you find it valuable.
```

## 4. Personalization at Scale

### What to Personalize

**Must personalize:**
- Their name (obviously)
- Their company name
- Something specific about their work (post, project, role)
- The problem they likely face

**Nice to personalize:**
- Mutual connections
- Their recent activity
- Their industry news
- Their location

### How to Research at Scale

**For 10 prospects/day:**
- Spend 5 minutes per prospect
- Review their profile (headline, about, experience)
- Read their 3 most recent posts
- Check their company's recent news
- Find one specific thing to reference

**For 30 prospects/day:**
- Spend 2 minutes per prospect
- Review their headline and about section
- Read their most recent post
- Find one thing to reference

**For 100+ prospects/day:**
- Use automation tools (see below)
- Segment by industry/role
- Use variable-based templates
- Focus on company-level personalization

### Tools for Personalization at Scale

**LinkedIn Automation (Use Carefully — Risk of Ban):**
- Expandi (safest, cloud-based)
- Dux-Soup (Good for scraping)
- Phantombuster (API-based)
- Waalaxy (European, compliant)

**Warning:** LinkedIn aggressively bans automation. Use with extreme caution. Keep volumes reasonable (< 50 actions/day) and use human-like behavior.

### The 80/20 of Personalization

80% of the value comes from 20% of the personalization:
- Get their name right
- Reference something specific
- Show you did basic research

Don't over-personalize. A well-crafted template with 2-3 personalized elements beats a fully custom message that takes 30 minutes each.

## 5. Timing and Cadence

### Best Times to Send

**Connection Requests:**
- Tuesday-Thursday, 8-10 AM (their timezone)
- Tuesday-Thursday, 5-7 PM (end of day)
- Sunday evening (planning for the week)

**DMs:**
- Tuesday-Thursday, 9-11 AM (morning check)
- Tuesday-Thursday, 2-4 PM (post-lunch dip)
- Avoid Monday mornings (busy)
- Avoid Friday afternoons (checked out)

### Follow-Up Cadence

```
Day 1: Connection request
Day 2: Welcome message (if accepted)
Day 4: Add value (engagement or resource)
Day 7: Insight message
Day 10: Direct offer
Day 14: Social proof
Day 21: Breakup message
```

### The "No Response" Protocol

| Days Since Last Message | Action |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | Wait, they might be busy |
| 4-7 | Send a value-add (not a follow-up) |
| 7-10 | Follow up with a specific offer |
| 10-14 | Send social proof / case study |
| 14-21 | Breakup message |
| 30+ | Re-engage with new content/value |

## 6. Objection Handling in DMs

### "I'm Not Interested Right Now"

```
Totally understand. I'm not going anywhere — feel free to reach out when the timing is right.

In the meantime, I'll keep sharing content on [topic] that you might find valuable.
```

### "Send Me More Information"

```
Happy to! What specifically would be most useful?

1. Case studies from similar companies
2. A framework/approach document
3. Pricing overview
4. A quick 10-minute call to discuss your specific needs

Let me know and I'll send it over.
```

### "We Already Have Someone"

```
That's great to hear! If that doesn't work out for any reason, I'd love to be a backup option.

In the meantime, I can share some insights I've gained from similar projects that might be useful.
```

### "We Don't Have Budget"

```
Understood. If budget opens up, I'd love to discuss how I can help.

For now, I'd be happy to share [free resource] that might help you get started on your own.
```

### "I'm Too Busy to Talk"

```
No problem at all. I'll follow up in [timeframe] when things might be calmer.

In the meantime, I'll send over [resource] — no response needed.
```

## 7. Converting DMs to Calls

### The Goal of Every Sequence

Every outreach sequence has one goal: get them on a discovery call.

**The Conversion Formula:**
```
Acknowledgment + Value + Low-Commitment Ask

"Hey [Name], I'm curious — what's your biggest challenge with [topic] right now?

I might have some ideas that could help. Happy to hop on a 10-minute call if you're open to it."
```

### Call Scheduling Best Practices

**Don't:**
- "Are you free to talk?" (too open-ended)
- "When works for you?" (too much work for them)
- "Let me know" (no urgency)

**Do:**
- "I have time Tuesday at 2 PM or Thursday at 10 AM — which works?"
- "Would a quick 15-minute call this week work?"
- Send your Calendly link with specific availability

### Calendar Link Etiquette

```
I'd love to continue this conversation. Here's a link to my calendar for a 15-minute call: [link]

No prep needed — just a casual chat to see if there's alignment.
```

## 8. Compliance and Platform Safety

### LinkedIn's Anti-Spam Rules

- Don't send more than 100 connection requests/week
- Don't use automation (you will get banned)
- Don't send the same message to multiple people (pattern detection)
- Don't pitch in connection requests
- Don't send links in initial messages
- Don't send more than 300 messages/week

### Safe Outreach Volumes

| Account Age | Connection Requests/Day | Messages/Day |
|---|---|---|
| New (< 30 days) | 10-20 | 10-15 |
| Established (1-6 months) | 30-50 | 20-30 |
| Mature (6+ months) | 50-80 | 30-50 |
| Power User (1+ year) | 80-100 | 50-75 |

### Account Warming

If you have a new account or haven't done outreach before:

Week 1: 5 connections/day, no messages
Week 2: 10 connections/day, 5 messages/day
Week 3: 20 connections/day, 10 messages/day
Week 4: 30 connections/day, 20 messages/day
Week 5+: Ramp to target volume

### A/B Testing

Always test one variable at a time:

- Connection request message (2-3 versions)
- Welcome message length
- Offer presentation
- Call to action
- Follow-up timing

## Final Truth

LinkedIn outreach works when it feels personal and value-first. The moment it feels like a sales pitch, you lose.

Treat every prospect as a human you want to help, not a wallet you want to access. Lead with value, be patient, and the clients will come.

The best outreach doesn't feel like outreach at all — it feels like a natural conversation between professionals who can help each other.
