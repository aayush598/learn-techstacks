# Upwork Top Rated Strategy

## Why Top Rated Status Matters

Top Rated status is not a badge — it's a business asset. It unlocks:
- Higher search ranking (appear before non-Top Rated freelancers)
- Invitation-only projects (access to higher-budget clients)
- 70 free connects per month (vs 10 for basic)
- Reduced service fee (20% → 5% after $10K with a client)
- Top Rated badge (trust signal that increases conversion)
- Access to Top Rated weekly newsletter (some job leads)

This guide covers getting Top Rated, keeping it forever, and using it to maximize income.

---

## 1. Requirements for Top Rated Status

### Minimum Requirements

- Active on Upwork for 90+ days
- Completed 13+ contracts
- 100% Job Success Score (JSS) maintained
- No recent account holds
- Meets the "Eligible Weeks" threshold (see below)

### Eligible Weeks Calculation

Upwork looks at your last 16 weeks. You need to be "eligible" for at least 13 of 16 weeks:

**Eligible means:**
- Active on platform (logged in, applying, or working)
- No account restrictions
- Compliant with terms of service
- You can have weeks with no earnings and still be eligible

### The 90-Day Rule

You can't get Top Rated before 90 days. Even if you have 20 perfect contracts, you must wait.

**Strategy:**
- Day 1: Start applying aggressively
- Day 1-90: Complete 13+ small contracts flawlessly
- Day 91: Apply for Top Rated (if eligible)
- Day 91+: Maintain momentum

### The "Rising Talent" Alternative

If you can't get Top Rated yet, aim for Rising Talent:

- Available to new freelancers with strong profiles
- Less strict requirements
- Still gives a badge and better visibility
- Can lead to more invites
- Transition to Top Rated as soon as eligible

**Getting Rising Talent:**
- Complete profile 100%
- Pass relevant skill tests
- Get a few contracts completed with good feedback
- Upwork invites you (you can't apply)

## 2. Job Success Score (JSS) Optimization

### What Affects JSS

JSS is calculated based on 8 key factors:

**1. Private Feedback (MOST IMPORTANT)**
After every project, clients rate you privately on:
- Likelihood to recommend
- Quality of work
- Communication
- Professionalism

This accounts for ~70% of your JSS calculation. Public star rating is public. Private feedback is internal.

**2. Public Star Rating**
- Less weight than private feedback but still matters
- 4.7+ is excellent
- Below 4.5 is concerning
- Below 4.0 triggers account review

**3. Contract Value**
- Higher-value contracts have MORE impact on JSS
- A $10K contract "counts more" than a $100 contract
- One bad $50 contract does less damage than one bad $5K contract
- But a bad $50 contract still hurts if you have few contracts

**4. Contract Duration**
- Longer contracts show stability
- Multiple short contracts (< $100) can hurt (looks like you can't keep clients)
- Sweet spot: 1-6 month contracts

**5. Client Recidivism**
- Repeat clients count MORE than one-off clients
- A second project with the same client is extra valuable for JSS
- Long-term relationships are rewarded

**6. Contract Outcome**
- Completed successfully = positive
- Canceled/no payment = negative
- Closed without feedback = neutral
- Disputed = very negative

**7. Client Quality**
- Feedback from high-spending clients (>$10K spent) carries more weight
- Feedback from clients with verified payment matters more
- Feedback from clients who have hired many freelancers is more reliable

**8. Time Since Events**
- Recent events (last 3 months) affect JSS more
- Old events (12+ months ago) gradually drop off
- A bad contract from 8 months ago is less damaging than one from 2 weeks ago

### The JSS Calculation Trick

Upwork states JSS is calculated on a "rolling basis" over 12-24 months. The exact formula secret, but these patterns are known:

- Contracts are grouped into 6-month windows
- The most recent 6 months have highest weight
- 6-12 months ago has medium weight
- 12-24 months ago has low weight
- Contracts older than 24 months don't count

**Strategic Implication:**
If you have a bad contract from 10 months ago, it will drop off completely in 2 months. You can accelerate JSS recovery by closing more great contracts in the meantime.

### The "Always Exceed" Policy

To maintain 100% JSS, you must always exceed expectations:

| Expectation | What You Deliver |
|---|---|
| Deliver in 2 weeks | Deliver in 10 days |
| Basic features | Polished features |
| Occasional updates | Weekly updates |
| Functional code | Clean, documented code |
| On-time delivery | Early delivery |
| OK communication | Proactive communication |

### The "Underpromise, Overdeliver" Framework

**In proposals:**
```
Timeline: 2-3 weeks (I deliver in 1.5)
Deliverables: Core features (I add polish and extras)
Communication: Weekly updates (I send two updates per week)
```

**In project management:**
- Set milestones at 60-70% of your actual capability
- Beat every milestone
- Surprise them with small extras
- Document everything they didn't ask for but would want

### Red Flag Clients to Avoid

Not all $500 projects are worth the JSS risk:

**Avoid these clients:**
- "I have an idea, need developer" — undefined scope, high risk
- "Need this done yesterday" — unrealistic expectations
- "I've had bad experiences with freelancers" — they're the common denominator
- No payment method verified — can't pay you
- History of contracts with no feedback — likely non-payers
- Client has been flagged by Upwork support
- Client's rating is below 4.0

**Screen for these green flags:**
- 4.8+ client rating
- $10K+ spent on Upwork
- 90%+ hire rate
- Detailed project descriptions
- Positive feedback for previous freelancers
- Responsive to messages

## 3. JSS Recovery Plan

If your JSS drops below 90%:

### Immediate Actions (Day 1-7)

1. Stop applying to new projects (you need to fix existing contracts first)
2. Complete all open contracts with exceptional quality
3. Ask satisfied clients for private feedback (they can update it)
4. Check your "Account Health" page for specific issues
5. Remove problematic contracts from your profile (if possible)

### Medium-Term Actions (Week 2-8)

1. Start 3-5 new contracts with low-risk clients
2. Focus on smaller fixed-price contracts ($100-500)
3. Deliver everything ahead of schedule
4. Communicate proactively
5. Ask every client to leave feedback

### Long-Term Recovery (Month 2-6)

1. Continue building contracts with excellent feedback
2. Avoid any client who seems problematic
3. Target repeat clients (they boost JSS more)
4. Let time heal — bad contracts eventually drop off
5. Monitor JSS weekly

### JSS Damage Control Scenarios

**Scenario: Client is unhappy**
```
Hi [Client], I want to make this right. Can we hop on a 5-minute call to discuss what you need changed?

I'm committed to delivering something you're happy with. If we can't get there, I'm happy to refund a portion.
```

**Scenario: Project scope creep**
```
I've delivered everything in the original scope. I'm happy to continue with the additional features — here's a new milestone for [amount].
```

**Scenario: Client won't respond**
Send project updates anyway. Document everything. Close the contract professionally after 2 weeks of no response.

## 4. Long-Term Client Strategy

### The "First Project Loss" Strategy

To lock in a long-term client relationship:

1. **First project:** Deliver exceptional value at or slightly below market rate
2. **Second project:** Maintain quality at normal rate
3. **Third project:** Offer a retainer or package deal
4. **Ongoing:** Become their go-to person

**The Lock-In Effect:**
After 3 successful projects, switching costs are high for the client:
- You know their codebase / brand / voice
- You understand their business
- They trust your judgment
- You have institutional knowledge

This lets you raise rates 10-20% per year while competitors can't undercut you.

### Converting One-Time to Ongoing

After every successful project:

```
Hi [Client], glad we got [project] launched successfully!

I have capacity to continue working on [related work] if you need it. I can offer a retainer of [X hours/month] at [Y rate] to handle:
- Ongoing maintenance and updates
- Quick fixes and improvements
- Priority support (response within 2 hours)

Would a monthly retainer work for your team?
```

### Retainer Types

**Fixed Hours (most common):**
20 hours/month at $100/hr = $2,000/month
Client pays whether they use them all or not.

**On-Demand (flexible):**
Priority access when needed, billed hourly
Client pays only for hours used
Minimum commitment: 10 hours/month

**Hybrid (best for both):**
10 hours base retainer ($1,000/month)
+ Additional hours at $90/hr (discount for extra)
Client has predictable base + flexibility

### Rate Escalation Strategy

**Every 6-12 months, increase rates for existing clients:**

```
Hi [Client], I wanted to let you know that I'm adjusting my rates for new projects starting [date].

For you, as a valued long-term client, I'm grandfathering your current rate of $[current rate]/hr until [date 3 months from now]. After that, your rate will adjust to $[new rate]/hr.

This increase reflects the increased value I'm able to deliver based on our work together, and my continued commitment to being your go-to resource.
```

### Multi-Project Discounting

Offer volume discounts to lock in long-term commitment:

```
Standard rate: $100/hr
5-10 hours/week: $90/hr (10% discount)
10-20 hours/week: $80/hr (20% discount)
20+ hours/week: $75/hr (25% discount)
Full-time (40 hrs): $70/hr (30% discount)
```

The discount is worth it because:
- Consistent income
- No acquisition cost
- Lower admin overhead
- Deep context reduces ramp-up time

## 5. Maintaining Top Rated Status

### Monthly Maintenance Checklist

- [ ] Respond to all messages within 24 hours (preferably 1 hour)
- [ ] Close contracts properly (don't leave them open)
- [ ] Request feedback after every completed project
- [ ] Check your "My Stats" page for JSS change
- [ ] Review "Account Health" for any issues
- [ ] Remove any problematic contracts
- [ ] Maintain active proposal sending (at least 5/week)

### Don't Do These

- Close contracts without client's consent
- Ask for 5-star reviews (violates TOS)
- Work outside Upwork with a client you met on Upwork (before 2 years)
- Have long periods of inactivity (2+ months)
- Get paid outside of Upwork's system
- Share contact info aggressively (Upwork scans messages)
- Accept projects you can't deliver

### The 2-Year Rule

After 2 years of continuous relationship with a client, you can take them off Upwork without penalty. This is legitimately the best way to eventually have your own client base.

## 7. The Exponential JSS Curve

Early career: JSS is volatile (few contracts, each one matters a lot)

Established: JSS stabilizes (50+ contracts, individual ones barely move it)

Institutional: JSS is cemented (100+ contracts, it only moves with consistent patterns)

### The 50-Contract Goal

Your goal in Year 1: complete 50 contracts successfully.
- 50 contracts at 99%+ satisfaction = bulletproof JSS
- No single bad contract can significantly hurt you
- You have enough data for Upwork's algorithm to trust you
- You can weather the occasional bad client

After 50 contracts, your JSS is essentially indestructible unless you start a pattern of bad work.

---

## Final Truth

Top Rated status is a tool, not a goal. The real goal is building a reputation that makes clients come to you. Top Rated accelerates that process, but if you're not delivering exceptional work consistently, Top Rated won't save you.

The best freelancers on Upwork don't chase top rated — they deliver work so good that Upwork gives it to them automatically. Focus on being undeniable, and the recognition follows.
