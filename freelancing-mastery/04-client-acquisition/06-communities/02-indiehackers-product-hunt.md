# Indie Hackers, Product Hunt, and Hacker News

## Why These Platforms Matter

Indie Hackers, Product Hunt, and Hacker News are the three platforms where ambitious builders hang out. Founders, CTOs, and startup employees — your ideal clients — are disproportionately active on these platforms.

Each platform has different norms, but the core principle is the same: build credibility by sharing knowledge and helping builders.

---

## 1. Indie Hackers

### What It Is

Indie Hackers is a community of 100K+ bootstrapped founders and indie developers. It's the best place to find founders who:
- Are building something real (not just ideas)
- Have revenue (many are profitable)
- Need technical help (most are non-technical)
- Value quality over price
- Are accessible (founders are on the platform daily)

### Profile Optimization

**Your Bio:**
```
I'm [Name], a [service] freelancer working with bootstrapped SaaS founders. I've helped 20+ founders launch and scale their products.

I specialize in: [your skills]

Currently: [current project]

Previous projects:
- [Project 1] — $X MRR
- [Project 2] — $Y MRR

Find me at: [link to portfolio/calendar]
```

**Your Photo:** Professional headshot or friendly photo

**Topics to Follow:**
- Your technology stack
- Your target industry
- "Looking for a technical co-founder"
- "Freelance and contract work"
- "Marketing and growth"

### Content Strategy on Indie Hackers

**Post Types That Build Authority:**

**1. "How I [achieved result]"**
```
How I Built a SaaS MVP in 6 Weeks as a Solo Developer

I used:
- Next.js for frontend
- Supabase for backend
- Stripe for payments
- Vercel for deployment

Key learnings:
1. Start with the payment flow (it affects everything)
2. Use a component library (don't build from scratch)
3. Launch before you think it's ready
...
```

**2. Technical Posts**
```
5 React Performance Tips Every Founder Should Know

If your app is slow, check these first:
1. Are you using useEffect correctly?
2. Are images optimized?
3. Is code splitting set up?
...
```

**3. "Ask Me Anything"**
```
I'm a full-stack developer who's built 20+ SaaS products. Ask me anything about building your MVP, choosing your tech stack, or avoiding common mistakes.
```

**4. Case Studies**
```
How I helped [Founder] launch [Product] and hit $5K MRR in 3 months

The challenge:
The approach:
The results:
The lessons:
```

### Engagement Strategy

**Comment on posts regularly:**
```
Great post! One thing I'd add about [topic] is that [insight from your experience].

I've seen this work well for [specific scenario]. Thanks for sharing!
```

**Answer questions thoroughly:**
```
Question: "Should I use Next.js or plain React for my SaaS?"
Your answer: A detailed comparison based on specific needs, not just generic advice
```

**Offer help freely:**
```
Question: "How do I handle Stripe webhooks?"
Your answer: A complete step-by-step guide, not just "hire me"
```

Leadership doesn't come from asking for it. It comes from the quality of what you share, the substance of your comments, and the generosity of your help.

### Finding Clients on Indie Hackers

**Direct Signals:**
- "Looking for developer to build MVP"
- "Need help with [specific technology]"
- "Anyone know a good freelancer for [project]?"
- "Rate my landing page" (opportunity to offer design help)

**Indirect Signals:**
- Founder sharing their product (they're building)
- "Struggling with [tech challenge]"
- "Should I build or hire?"
- "Looking for technical co-founder"

### Outreach on Indie Hackers

**After commenting helpfully:**
```
Hey [Name],

I saw your post about [topic] in the Indie Hackers community. Glad my advice was helpful!

If you ever need hands-on help with [specific service], I'm available. I've helped [similar founders] achieve [specific result].

No pressure — just wanted to offer!

Best,
[Your Name]
```

**After they comment on your post:**
```
Thanks for the thoughtful comment on my post! If you ever want to discuss [topic] further, feel free to DM me.

I'm available for [service] if you or someone you know needs help.
```

## 2. Product Hunt

### What It Is

Product Hunt is where new products launch. Founders, investors, and early adopters browse daily. The audience is tech-savvy, ambitious, and actively building.

### The 3-Pronged Strategy

**Prong 1: Support Products as an Early Adopter**

Find products in your niche that are launching. Upvote them and leave a thoughtful comment:

```
Very cool product! I love the [specific feature].

I've been working with [related technology] and I can see how this would help with [specific use case].

One question: how does [specific aspect] work in production?

Congrats on the launch! 🚀
```

This gets you on the radar of the founder. They'll likely check your profile.

**Prong 2: Share Your Expertise in Comments**

When founders ask questions or need help, offer it:

```
Founder: "Building this was harder than expected, especially the [specific challenge]."
You: "I've built [similar thing] and I'd be happy to share how I solved [challenge]. DM me if interested!"
```

**Prong 3: Launch Your Own Product**

When you launch a product (template, tool, course, app):
- Build a mailing list before launch
- Get early supporters
- Prepare a compelling story
- Respond to every comment on launch day
- Follow up with interested users

After launch, you have a portfolio item AND a lead generation asset.

### Product Hunt Tips

- Don't just upvote — leave valuable comments
- Follow products in your niche
- Engage with makers (founders who launched)
- Add your portfolio to your Product Hunt profile
- Use notifications to track new launches

## 3. Hacker News

### What It Is

Hacker News (news.ycombinator.com) is Y Combinator's community. It's the most intellectually rigorous tech community. The audience includes engineers, founders, and investors at the highest level.

### The Culture

HN has strong norms:
- No self-promotion
- High-quality discussion
- Skeptical of marketing
- Values substance over style

Breaking these norms gets you downvoted fast.

### How to Build Credibility on HN

**Write detailed, insightful comments:**
```
Topic: "React is too complex"

Good comment:
"I've been building React apps for 7 years and I disagree with some points. The complexity is real, but it solves real problems:
- Component reusability at scale
- Declarative state management
- Huge ecosystem

The real issue isn't React's complexity — it's that many apps don't need a framework at all."

This shows expertise, nuance, and authenticity.
```

**Share your experiences:**
```
"I tried [approach] vs [approach] on a project handling 50K users. Here's what I found..."

HN values real experience over theoretical knowledge.
```

**Be humble and specific:**
```
"I built something similar. Here's what I learned: [list]. Would love to hear how others approached this."
```

### When to Offer Your Services on HN

Almost never in public. But there are two exceptions:

**1. "Who's hiring?" thread (monthly)**
These threads are explicitly for job postings. You can offer freelance services:

```
[Location] / Remote — Freelance React Developer — Next.js, TypeScript, Node.js

I help startups build and scale their React applications. Recent projects:
- Built MVP for fintech startup (shipped in 6 weeks)
- Scaled React app to handle 100K+ daily users
- Migrated jQuery app to React (40% performance improvement)

Portfolio: [link]
Contact: [email]
```

**2. "Freelancer" thread (monthly)**
Similar to hiring thread, specifically for freelancers:

```
I'm a full-stack developer specializing in:
- React/Next.js frontends
- Node.js/Python backends
- AWS/Vercel deployment
- Payment integration

Available for contract or project-based work.
Portfolio: [link]
```

### Collecting Email Leads from HN

In your HN profile, add:

```
I'm a freelance [service] developer. If you need help with [specific problem], email me: [email]
```

When people check your profile (and they will after a good comment), they see this.

## 4. Cross-Platform Strategy

### The Playbook

1. **Write a detailed post on Indie Hackers** about a project or approach
2. **Share it on Hacker News** for technical discussion (if applicable)
3. **Launch a related product/tool on Product Hunt**
4. **Mention all of it on LinkedIn and Twitter**
5. **Collect leads from all channels** into your CRM

### The "Build in Public" Framework

Document your work across platforms:

```
Platform: Indie Hackers
Content: "How I built [project]"
Audience: Founders, bootstrappers

Platform: Hacker News
Content: "Technical deep-dive on [approach]"
Audience: Engineers, CTOs

Platform: Product Hunt
Content: Launch the actual product/tool
Audience: Early adopters, tech enthusiasts

Platform: LinkedIn
Content: Summary + lessons learned
Audience: Professional network, potential clients
```

### The Funnel

```
Indie Hackers / HN / Product Hunt (authority building)
  ↓
Readers visit your profile
  ↓
They see your portfolio/website
  ↓
They subscribe to your newsletter / book a call
  ↓
Conversation → Proposal → Client
```

## 5. Time Investment

### Weekly Allocation

| Platform | Time | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Indie Hackers | 3 hours | 1 post, daily comments |
| Product Hunt | 1 hour | Check daily launches, comment on 2-3 |
| Hacker News | 2 hours | Check "Who's hiring", comment on 3-5 posts |
| Total | 6 hours | Builds authority and generates leads |

### The 90-Day Sprint

**Month 1: Learn and Engage**
- Read 50+ posts on Indie Hackers
- Understand the culture and norms
- Comment on 10+ posts per week
- Build profile and reputation

**Month 2: Create Content**
- Write 2 detailed posts on Indie Hackers
- Comment on 15+ Product Hunt launches
- Engage in HN discussions
- Start building email list

**Month 3: Launch**
- Launch something on Product Hunt
- Write your best post on Indie Hackers
- Get featured on HN (if you can)
- Convert attention into leads

## 7. Success Metrics

### Track These

| Metric | Indie Hackers | Product Hunt | Hacker News |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comments/week | 10+ | 5+ | 3+ |
| Posts written/month | 2-4 | 1 launch | 0-1 (harder to post) |
| Profile visits/month | 200+ | 100+ | 500+ |
| Inbound leads/month | 5+ | 2+ | 3+ |
| Clients/month | 1-2 | 0-1 | 0-1 |

### The Compound Effect

```
Month 1: 0 clients from these platforms
Month 3: 1 client
Month 6: 2-3 clients/month
Month 12: 5+ clients/month

Total first-year revenue from these platforms: $50K-$150K
```

Most freelancers give up in Month 1 because they see 0 results. The ones who persist see the compound effect kick in around Month 4-5.

## Final Truth

Indie Hackers, Product Hunt, and Hacker News don't exist for you to find clients. They exist for builders to share knowledge.

The freelancers who succeed on these platforms are the ones who genuinely help others without expecting anything in return. They share their hard-earned knowledge freely. They answer questions thoroughly. They celebrate others' successes.

And because of that, they become the first person founders think of when they need help.

It's not a tactic. It's a way of being. And it works better than any sales pitch ever could.
