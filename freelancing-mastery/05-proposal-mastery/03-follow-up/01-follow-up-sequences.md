# Follow-Up Sequences After Proposals

## Why Follow-Up Is the Highest-Leverage Activity

65-80% of proposals receive no response within the first 5 days. 80% of sales require 5+ follow-up attempts. Yet 90% of freelancers give up after 2 attempts.

This means: the freelancer who follows up systematically wins projects by default.

### The Follow-Up Math

| Attempt Number | % of Freelancers Who Follow Up | % of Deals Closed at This Touchpoint |
|---------------|-------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| 1 (initial send) | 100% | 2% |
| 2 (3 days later) | 25% | 5% |
| 3 (7 days later) | 10% | 12% |
| 4 (14 days later) | 5% | 18% |
| 5 (30 days later) | 2% | 25% |
| 6+ (ongoing) | <1% | 38% |

Every follow-up you send puts you ahead of 75-98% of your competition.

## Follow-Up Timing Matrix

### The Ideal Follow-Up Schedule

| Touchpoint | Timing | Channel | Goal |
|------------|--------|---------|------|
| 1 | Initial send | Email/platform | Deliver proposal |
| 2 | Day 3 | Email | Check-in, offer clarification |
| 3 | Day 7 | Email or phone | Share new insight/value-add |
| 4 | Day 14 | Email | Create urgency, offer meeting |
| 5 | Day 30 | Email | The "breakup" email |
| 6 | Day 60 | Email | Re-engagement with new value |
| 7 | Day 90 | Email | Final check, referral ask |

### Timing Nuances

**Best days for follow-up:**
- Tuesday: Highest open rates
- Wednesday: Second highest
- Thursday: Good for decision-oriented emails
- Monday: Poor — people are catching up
- Friday: Poor — people are winding down
- Weekend: Only if the client communicates on weekends

**Best times for follow-up:**
- 8-10am local client time (before the day gets busy)
- 1-2pm local client time (after lunch, before afternoon meetings)
- 4-5pm local client time (end-of-day check)

**Seasonal considerations:**
- Avoid end-of-quarter (finance teams are busy)
- Avoid Monday mornings (too busy)
- Avoid Friday afternoons (too checked out)
- December holidays: Pause unless urgent
- August: European clients may be on holiday

## Follow-Up Email Templates

### Template 1: Day 3 — Gentle Check-In

Subject: Quick check on the proposal

```
Hi [Name],

Just checking in on the proposal I sent on [date]. Wanted to make sure it landed OK and see if you had any initial questions.

Happy to hop on a 15-minute call if it would be helpful to walk through anything.

Best,
[Your Name]
```

**Why it works**: Short, low-pressure, offers value without demanding a decision. The assumption is they received it and might have questions.

### Template 2: Day 7 — New Insight / Value-Add

Subject: A thought on your [project name]

```
Hi [Name],

I've been thinking more about your [project/goal] and wanted to share something I came across.

[Insight, industry data, relevant case study, or idea]

I thought this might be useful for your planning, regardless of whether we end up working together.

The offer in the proposal still stands, but I wanted to share this regardless.

Best,
[Your Name]
```

**Why it works**: Provides value without asking for anything. Positions you as helpful and expert. Keeps the conversation alive without being pushy.

### Template 3: Day 14 — The Specific Question

Subject: Quick question about the proposal

```
Hi [Name],

Following up on the proposal I sent [date].

I had a specific thought: [question about a specific element of their project]

[Question that shows you've been thinking about their project]

I think this could make a meaningful difference in the [outcome]. Let me know your thoughts.

Best,
[Your Name]
```

**Why it works**: Shows you're still thinking about their project. Asks for a small, easy response. Opens a specific conversation thread rather than a vague "any thoughts?"

### Template 4: Day 21 — The Assume-Continue

Subject: Update on the proposal

```
Hi [Name],

I'm assuming things have been busy on your end. No problem at all.

In case it's helpful, I've updated the proposal with some additional options:

Option 1: [Same as before] — $[X]
Option 2: [Reduced scope] — $[X]
Option 3: [Phased approach] — $[X]/month

These options might make the decision easier depending on where your priorities are right now.

Would any of these work better for your current situation?

Best,
[Your Name]
```

**Why it works**: Gives them an easy out ("things have been busy") while offering a new reason to engage. The new options can make the decision easier.

### Template 5: Day 30 — The Breakup Email

Subject: Closing out the proposal

```
Hi [Name],

I haven't heard back on the proposal I sent a month ago, so I'm assuming either:
1. The timing isn't right
2. You found someone else
3. It's just not a priority right now

No hard feelings at all — these things happen.

Two requests:
1. If you do decide to revisit this project in the future, I'd love to be on your shortlist. Just reply to this email and I'll resend the proposal.
2. If you chose someone else, I'd genuinely love to know what tipped the scales — it helps me improve.

Either way, I wish you the best with [project].

Best,
[Your Name]
```

**Why it works**: The breakup email gets the highest response rate of any follow-up. It breaks the silence by acknowledging the elephant in the room. It's respectful, confident, and often prompts a response — even if it's just "sorry, we went with someone else."

### Template 6: Day 60 — Re-engagement with New Social Proof

Subject: Update + a relevant case study

```
Hi [Name],

Hope you've been well since we last spoke.

I wanted to share a case study I just completed that's very relevant to your project:

[Clients] had [similar problem]. We implemented [solution] and achieved [result].

It made me think of your situation and I wanted to send it over in case it's useful.

If you're ready to revisit the discussion, I'd be happy to update the proposal based on anything that's changed in the last few months.

Best,
[Your Name]
```

**Why it works**: New social proof gives a reason to re-engage. The case study provides value regardless. No pressure, just relevance.

### Template 7: Day 90 — The Referral Ask

Subject: Quick ask

```
Hi [Name],

I'm following up one last time on the proposal for [project].

Since the timing clearly hasn't worked out, I'll close the loop on my end.

If you know anyone else who might benefit from my services, I'd greatly appreciate an introduction or referral.

No worries if not — just thought I'd ask.

Wishing you and [Company] all the best.

[Your Name]
```

**Why it works**: Transitions from prospect to referral source. Even if they never buy, they might know someone who will. Professional and gracious close.

## Phone/Voice Note Follow-Ups

In addition to email, leave a 60-second voice note (Loom or WhatsApp voice):

### Voice Note Script: Day 5

```
"Hey [Name], [Your Name] here. Just following up on the proposal I sent. No pressure at all — I know you're busy. I just wanted to make sure everything was clear and see if you had any questions. Happy to jump on a quick call if that would help. Talk soon!"
```

### Voice Note Script: Day 15

```
"Hey [Name], [Your Name] again. I was thinking more about your project and had a couple of ideas I wanted to run by you. Nothing urgent — just some thoughts that might make the solution even stronger. Give me a shout when you have a minute. Thanks!"
```

### Voice Note Script: Day 30 — Breakup

```
"Hey [Name], [Your Name]. Look, I've been sending emails and I'm not sure if you're getting them or if the timing's just off. Either way, I wanted to say no hard feelings. If the project comes back around or you need help down the line, I'd love to work with you. Good luck with everything."
```

## Channel Strategy

### Multi-Channel Follow-Up Sequence

| Touchpoint | Primary Channel | Secondary Channel | Tertiary Channel |
|------------|----------------|-------------------|------------------|
| Initial | Platform (Upwork, etc.) | Email | — |
| Day 3 | Email | LinkedIn message | — |
| Day 7 | Email | Loom video | — |
| Day 14 | LinkedIn message | Email | Phone call |
| Day 21 | Email (with options) | LinkedIn | — |
| Day 30 | Email (breakup) | — | — |
| Day 60 | Email (case study) | LinkedIn | — |

**Platform-specific notes:**
- **Upwork**: Follow up within the platform. Never ask for external contact until they've agreed.
- **Toptal**: Use their messaging system. They have strong anti-spam.
- **Direct email**: Your best channel. Build a system.
- **LinkedIn**: Good for second touch if they're active there.
- **Phone**: Only if you have a prior relationship. Cold calling after a proposal is effective but aggressive.

### LinkedIn Follow-Up Templates

**Connection request:**
"Hi [Name], I sent over a proposal for [project] recently. Would love to connect and stay on your radar."

**Message after connecting:**
"Thanks for connecting! Just wanted to make sure you saw my proposal on [project]. Happy to chat if you have questions."

## Follow-Up Psychology

### Why Clients Don't Respond

| Reason | Frequency | What to Do |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| Too busy to decide | 40% | Make it easier — offer a smaller commitment |
| Decision paralysis | 20% | Narrow options, recommend one |
| Waiting for someone else | 15% | Offer to help them present it internally |
| Found someone else | 10% | Ask why, improve, move on |
| Budget not approved | 10% | Ask what the approval timeline is |
| Ghosting (no reason) | 5% | Breakup email, move on |

### The "Ghosting" Psychology

Most clients don't respond because:
1. They feel guilty saying no
2. They don't want to negotiate
3. They're afraid you'll pressure them
4. They're genuinely swamped
5. They're waiting for more information but forgot to ask

Your follow-up should address all five by:
1. Making it easy to say "not right now" (breakup email)
2. Pre-addressing price concerns (new budget-friendly options)
3. Being low-pressure (value-add emails)
4. Being short and respectful (brevity)
5. Offering specific information proactively (new insight email)

### The Reciprocity Principle

When you give value before receiving commitment, people feel obligated to respond:

- Share an article relevant to their industry
- Offer a free 15-minute consultation
- Send a template or checklist they'd find useful
- Introduce them to someone in your network
- Give them a compliment or public shoutout

## Why Each Follow-Up Works

### Follow-Up 2 (Day 3)
**Why**: The client read your proposal, got busy, and forgot to respond. This is a gentle nudge.
**Key**: Make it feel like a ping, not a demand.

### Follow-Up 3 (Day 7)
**Why**: You need a reason to contact them beyond "checking in." New value gives you that reason.
**Key**: The insight must be genuinely useful. Don't send fluff.

### Follow-Up 4 (Day 14)
**Why**: The window for a quick decision has passed. They need a specific reason to re-engage.
**Key**: Ask about one specific aspect of their project. Shows you're thinking deeply.

### Follow-Up 5 (Day 21)
**Why**: The original proposal is stale. New options refresh interest.
**Key**: The new options must genuinely be different, not just discounted.

### Follow-Up 6 (Day 30)
**Why**: The relationship needs closure or rejuvenation. The breakup email provides both.
**Key**: Be genuine. Sincerity converts better than sales tactics.

### Follow-Up 7 (Day 60)
**Why**: Enough time has passed that the situation may have changed.
**Key**: Use new social proof — a recent success story makes you relevant again.

### Follow-Up 8 (Day 90)
**Why**: This is the final goodbye. Extract whatever value remains.
**Key**: The referral ask is respectful and low-expectation.

## Follow-Up Cadence by Deal Size

| Deal Size | Follow-Up Cadence | Total Attempts | Duration |
|-----------|------------------|----------------|----------|
| < $2,000 | Day 3, Day 7, Day 14 | 3 | 2 weeks |
| $2,000 - $10,000 | Day 3, 7, 14, 21, 30 | 5 | 4 weeks |
| $10,000 - $50,000 | Day 3, 7, 14, 21, 30, 60 | 6 | 8 weeks |
| $50,000+ | Day 3, 7, 14, 21, 30, 60, 90 (+ phone) | 7+ | 12 weeks |

**Rule**: Your follow-up effort should be proportional to the potential deal value. Don't chase $1,000 projects for 3 months.

## When to Stop Following Up

### Stop immediately if:
- Client explicitly says no
- Client says "stop contacting me"
- Client says they went with someone else
- You get a bounce/unsubscribe

### Stop after breakup email if:
- No response within 14 days of breakup email
- You've reached your pre-determined attempt limit
- The project is clearly dead (checked LinkedIn, they hired someone)

### Nurture (slow, quarterly) vs. Stop:
- Nurture if: They showed genuine interest, have a real project, but timing was off
- Stop if: They were tire-kicking, never serious, or you sense disinterest

## Advanced Follow-Up: The Accountability Close

For clients who are "almost ready" but dragging their feet:

```
Hi [Name],

I noticed we've been going back and forth for [X] weeks now. Let me make this simple:

If this project is a priority, I'm ready to start next week. Here's exactly what I need from you to get started: [specific action].

If it's not the right time, no pressure — let's put it on hold and revisit in [month].

I just want to make sure I'm not chasing a project that isn't going to happen, so I can focus on clients who are ready to move.

Does that make sense?

Best,
[Your Name]
```

This is a respectful "put up or shut up" that works surprisingly well. It forces a decision.

## Follow-Up for Different Proposal Types

### For Hourly/Consulting Proposals:
Focus on: Availability, quick wins, low-risk trial
"Let's just start with 5 hours to test the waters. If you see value, we continue."

### For Fixed-Price Proposals:
Focus on: Scope clarity, milestones, payment terms
"I can split this into [X] milestones. You only pay as each one is delivered."

### For Retainer Proposals:
Focus on: Predictability, savings, relationship
"Start with a 1-month trial. If you don't see value after 30 days, cancel with no penalty."

### For Enterprise Proposals:
Focus on: Internal buy-in, compliance, procurement timeline
"I can provide a one-pager for your procurement team and join the call with your legal department."

## The Invisible Follow-Up: Social Proof Marketing

While you're following up directly, the client is likely researching you. Ensure your online presence reinforces your proposal:

- **LinkedIn**: Post something relevant to their industry this week
- **GitHub**: Push a commit (shows you're active)
- **Blog/Newsletter**: Send them a relevant article
- **Testimonials**: Ask happy clients to leave reviews publicly

These "invisible follow-ups" build credibility without you sending another email.

## The Ultimate Follow-Up System

### Weekly Routine (30 minutes every Tuesday + Thursday)

1. Open your CRM/spreadsheet
2. Filter for follow-ups due today or overdue
3. For each client:
   - Read the last message sent
   - Check that the context is still relevant
   - Send the appropriate follow-up template
   - Update the next follow-up date
4. Update proposal statuses
5. Remove dead leads
6. Review your pipeline value

### Monthly Routine (1 hour on the 1st of each month)

1. Review win/loss data
2. Analyze which follow-up sequence works best
3. Update templates based on what's working
4. Purge leads older than 90 days
5. Set quarterly follow-up goals

### Quarterly Routine (2 hours)

1. Full pipeline audit
2. Re-engage promising leads with new case studies
3. Update pricing and proposals based on market
4. Plan content calendar for invisible follow-up
