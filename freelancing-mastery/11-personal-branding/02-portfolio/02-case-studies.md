# Writing Compelling Case Studies: Problem-Solution-Results Framework

## Why Case Studies Matter More Than Anything

Case studies are your most powerful sales tool. A testimonial says "I am good." A case study proves it. A case study is a story that shows exactly how you solved a specific problem for a specific client with specific results.

**Case studies outperform every other content type**:
- 89% of buyers say case studies influence their purchasing decisions
- Case studies are the #1 content format for B2B buyers
- A good case study can generate leads for years
- Case studies close deals faster than any other marketing content

Every case study you write is a sales asset that works for you 24/7.

## The Problem-Solution-Results Framework

The most effective case study structure is simple: Problem, Solution, Results.

**Problem**: What was the client struggling with?
**Solution**: How did you solve it?
**Results**: What measurable impact did you deliver?

This framework works because it mirrors how buyers think: "I have a problem. Can you solve it? What results can I expect?"

## Case Study Structure (Detailed)

### Title

**Formula**: [Client name] + [Outcome/Result] + [Timeframe]

**Examples**:
- "How Allbirds Increased Checkout Conversion by 34% in 60 Days"
- "How a B2B SaaS Startup Achieved SOC 2 Compliance in 30 Days"
- "How We Helped a DTC Brand Scale from $2M to $5M in 12 Months"

**Title best practices**:
- Include a number (percentage, dollar amount, timeframe)
- Name the client (if permitted)
- Promise a specific result
- Keep it under 80 characters

### Executive Summary (3-5 sentences)

The executive summary should tell the whole story in a nutshell. Many readers will only read this.

**Structure**:
1. Who the client is (industry, size, relevant context)
2. What problem they faced
3. What you did (high-level)
4. What result they got (with number)
5. What this meant for their business

**Example**:
"Allbirds, a DTC footwear brand doing $20M+/year in revenue, was struggling with a 3.2% checkout conversion rate - below the industry average of 4.1%. We implemented a comprehensive checkout optimization program including UX redesign, performance optimization, and A/B testing. Within 60 days, their checkout conversion rate increased to 4.3%, representing an additional $2.1M in annual revenue."

### The Problem Section

**Purpose**: Make the reader think "I have that exact problem."

**What to include**:
- Client's situation before you (describe the pain)
- Specific metrics (what was the baseline conversion rate, load time, cost?)
- Impact on their business (lost revenue, wasted time, customer frustration)
- What they tried before (and why it did not work)
- The stakes (what would happen if the problem persisted)

**Writing tips**:
- Be specific with numbers ("loading time was 4.2 seconds" not "loading time was slow")
- Use client quotes if possible ("We were losing $50k/month and did not know why")
- Create empathy - make the reader feel the pain

**Example**:
"Allbirds was seeing strong traffic to their website - over 500,000 monthly visitors. But their checkout conversion rate had been stuck at 3.2% for 18 months. Industry benchmarks suggested they should be at 4.1%+.

The impact was significant. Each percentage point of conversion rate represented approximately $6M in annual revenue. Their 3.2% rate meant they were leaving over $5M on the table every year.

They had tried various fixes: new CTA buttons, cart page redesign, abandoned cart emails. Nothing moved the needle more than 0.1%. They needed a fresh approach."

### The Solution Section

**Purpose**: Demonstrate your expertise and process.

**What to include**:
- Your approach (methodology, framework, process)
- Specific actions you took (what did you actually do?)
- Challenges you encountered and how you overcame them
- Tools and technologies used
- Timeline (how long did each phase take?)
- Collaboration details (how did you work with the client?)

**Writing tips**:
- Focus on the thinking behind the actions, not just the actions
- Show your expertise through decisions you made
- Make it educational (reader should learn something)
- Be honest about challenges (adds credibility)

**Example**:
"We approached the problem using our 5-phase CRO methodology: Discovery, Analysis, Hypothesis, Testing, Implementation.

**Phase 1 - Discovery**: We analyzed 30 days of checkout data. Heatmaps showed users were dropping off at the shipping options page. Session recordings revealed confusion about shipping speed and cost.

**Phase 2 - Analysis**: We identified 3 key issues: shipping costs were not shown until after users entered their address, the progress indicator was misleading, and the payment form had 7 fields (industry best practice is 3).

**Phase 3 - Hypothesis**: We hypothesized that showing shipping costs earlier and simplifying the payment form would increase conversion by 20%.

**Phase 4 - Testing**: We built 3 variants and ran an A/B test with 50/50 traffic split for 14 days.

**Phase 5 - Implementation**: The winning variant showed a 34% improvement. We implemented it across all traffic within 48 hours."

### The Results Section

**Purpose**: Prove you deliver value. Make the reader want to hire you.

**What to include**:
- Primary metric (the main KPI that improved)
- Secondary metrics (other improvements)
- Percentage change and absolute change
- Dollar impact (revenue gained, costs saved)
- Timeline (how long to achieve results)
- Comparison to baseline and industry benchmarks

**Writing tips**:
- Lead with the biggest number
- Use before/after comparisons
- Include a visual (chart, graph, screenshot)
- Quote the client about the results

**Example**:
"The results exceeded our projections.

**Primary metric**: Checkout conversion rate increased from 3.2% to 4.3% - a 34% improvement.

**Revenue impact**: Based on Allbirds' average order value of $95 and 500,000 monthly visitors, the conversion improvement represents approximately $5.2M in additional annual revenue.

**Secondary improvements**:
- Page load time decreased by 47% (from 4.2s to 2.2s)
- Cart abandonment rate dropped from 72% to 61%
- Mobile conversion rate improved 42% (even more than desktop)

**Client reaction**:
"This was the best investment we have made this year. The ROI was 20x in the first quarter alone." - Jane Smith, Head of E-commerce, Allbirds"

### The Client Testimonial

**Placement**: Between the results and the CTA.

**Structure**:
- Quote from client (specific, enthusiastic)
- Client name, title, company
- Client photo (if possible)
- Permission to use (get it in writing)

**Good testimonial**:
"Working with [You] was transformative for our business. They identified issues we had overlooked for years and implemented solutions that drove real, measurable results. I would recommend them to anyone serious about improving their conversion rate."

**Better testimonial**:
"We were skeptical that anyone could improve our conversion rate after so many failed attempts. But [You] delivered a 34% improvement in 60 days. That is $5M in additional revenue. The ROI speaks for itself."

### Call to Action

**Purpose**: Convert the reader into a lead.

**Options**:
- "Want similar results for your business? Book a free strategy session."
- "Ready to [achieve similar outcome]? Let us talk."
- "See if we can help you achieve [result]."

**CTA best practices**:
- Make it specific to the case study topic
- Use action-oriented language
- Remove friction (link to Calendly directly)
- Offer something valuable (free audit, strategy session, proposal)

## Case Study Variations

### Short-Form Case Study (1 page)

For social media, email newsletters, or as lead magnets:

**Structure**:
- Title
- 2-sentence problem
- 2-sentence solution
- 3 key results (bullet points)
- Client quote
- CTA

### Long-Form Case Study (2000+ words)

For your website, SEO, and in-depth prospecting:

**Structure**:
- Full sections as described above
- Include images, screenshots, charts
- Step-by-step walkthrough
- Technical details
- Lessons learned

### Video Case Study

For YouTube, social media, and embedding on your site:

**Structure**:
- Client introduction (30 seconds)
- Problem description (60 seconds)
- Solution walkthrough (90 seconds)
- Results display (60 seconds)
- Client testimonial (30 seconds)
- CTA (15 seconds)

**Total**: 4-5 minutes

### Infographic Case Study

For social sharing and quick consumption:

**Structure**:
- Before state (with metric)
- Key actions (3-5 icons with labels)
- After state (with improved metric)
- Dollar impact
- Timeline

## Finding Good Case Study Candidates

### Selection Criteria

Not every project makes a good case study. Choose projects where:

1. **You solved a real problem** (not just executed tasks)
2. **You have measurable results** (numbers are essential)
3. **The client is willing to participate** (get permission)
4. **The results are impressive** (something to brag about)
5. **The project aligns with your ideal client** (prospective clients will relate)

### How to Ask

**Ask after project completion** (when goodwill is highest):

"Hi [Client],

Thank you again for the opportunity to work on [Project]. I am really proud of what we achieved together.

I am building my portfolio and would love to feature this project as a case study. Would you be open to a 20-minute call to discuss the project?

I will draft the case study and send it to you for approval before publishing anything. I am happy to link back to your website and include any messaging you want.

If you are open to it, please let me know a time that works for you.

Best,
[Your name]"

## Case Study Interview Questions

When interviewing the client for the case study, ask:

### Problem Questions
- "What was the situation before we started working together?"
- "What specific challenges were you facing?"
- "What had you tried before and why did it not work?"
- "What was the impact of this problem on your business?"
- "Why did you decide to reach out to us specifically?"

### Solution Questions
- "What was your experience working with us?"
- "What did you think of our process?"
- "Was there anything that surprised you about how we worked?"
- "How was the communication and collaboration?"

### Results Questions
- "What specific results did you see?"
- "How did these results impact your business?"
- "What would have happened if you had not made this change?"
- "What has been the long-term impact?"

### Testimonial Questions
- "What would you say to someone considering working with us?"
- "Would you recommend us to others? Why?"
- "Is there anything else you want to add?"

## Getting Permission

Always get written permission before publishing a case study.

**Permission form should include**:
- What will be published (text, images, quotes)
- Where it will be published (website, social media, etc.)
- Approval process (client reviews draft before publishing)
- Right to remove (client can request removal at any time)
- Signature and date

**Confidential concerns**:
- If client cannot share their name, use "Anonymous Client" or "Client A"
- If they cannot share specific numbers, use percentages or ranges
- If they cannot share details, get a general testimonial

## Case Study Metrics

Track performance:

- Views (how many people read it)
- Time on page (are they reading the whole thing?)
- CTA clicks (are readers taking action?)
- Conversion rate (what % of readers contact you?)
- Lead quality (are case study leads better?)
- Attribution (which case studies drive the most revenue?)

## Common Case Study Mistakes

**Mistake 1: No specific numbers**
"We improved their business significantly" - meaningless.

**Fix**: Always include specific metrics. Before and after.

**Mistake 2: Too much about you, not enough about the client**
"I used React and Node.js and followed a 5-step process" - no one cares.

**Fix**: Focus on the client's problem and results. You are the guide, not the hero.

**Mistake 3: No visuals**
Wall of text = no one reads it.

**Fix**: Include screenshots, charts, before/after images, client photos.

**Mistake 4: Generic results**
"The client was happy with the results."

**Fix**: Specific, quantified results. "Revenue increased 34% from $2M to $2.7M."

**Mistake 5: No CTA**
Reader finishes the case study but does not know what to do next.

**Fix**: Always end with a clear call to action.

**Mistake 6: Not getting approval**
You publish the case study, client gets upset, you take it down, relationship suffers.

**Fix**: Get written approval before publishing. Let them review the draft.

**Mistake 7: Writing for yourself, not your audience**
Using jargon only you understand.

**Fix**: Write for your ideal client. Use their language. Solve their problems.
