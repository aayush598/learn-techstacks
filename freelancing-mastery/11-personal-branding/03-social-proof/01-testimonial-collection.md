# Systematic Testimonial Collection: Video, Written, and LinkedIn

## Why You Need a Testimonial System

Most freelancers collect testimonials haphazardly: they ask once, maybe get one, and then forget. This is leaving money on the table.

A systematic testimonial process ensures:
- You get a testimonial from every client
- Testimonials include specific results (not just vague praise)
- You have a steady stream of social proof
- You capture testimonials at the peak of client happiness

## The Testimonial Collection Timeline

### The Peak Happiness Moments

Ask for testimonials when the client is happiest:

**Moment 1: Project launch/delivery**
The client just saw the finished product. They are excited. Strike now.

**Moment 2: Result achieved**
The client achieved a measurable result from your work. Revenue went up. Traffic increased. They are thrilled.

**Moment 3: Positive feedback**
The client sends you an unsolicited positive email. Reply: "Thank you! Would you mind if I used that as a testimonial?"

**Moment 4: Renewal or repeat business**
The client chooses to work with you again. Say: "We are excited to work together again. Would you be willing to share why you chose to continue working with us?"

**Moment 5: Referral**
The client refers someone to you. Say: "Thank you for the referral! I really appreciate it. Would you be willing to share a testimonial about your experience?"

### The 7-Day Post-Project Sequence

**Day 1 (project complete)**: Send the final deliverable. Celebrate the completion.

**Day 3 (follow-up)**: Check if everything is working well. Address any issues.

**Day 5 (feedback request)**: Ask for a testimonial. Make it easy.

**Day 7 (thank you)**: Thank them for their business. Share the testimonial if they provided one.

## Testimonial Request Templates

### Email Request Template

```

Hi [Client Name],

Thank you again for the opportunity to work on [Project Name]. I was thrilled with how it turned out and I hope you are too.

Thank you in advance!

Best,
[Your Name]
```

## How to Get Specific, Compelling Testimonials

### The Problem: Vague Testimonials

"You were great to work with" is not compelling.

"I increased my revenue by 40% in 3 months" is compelling.

### The Solution: Guided Questions

Instead of asking for a testimonial, ask specific questions:

**Question 1: The problem**
"What was the biggest challenge you were facing before working with me?"

**Question 2: The decision**
"What made you decide to work with me instead of someone else?"

**Question 3: The experience**
"What was it like working with me throughout the project?"

**Question 4: The results**
"What specific, measurable results did you see from our work?"

**Question 5: The recommendation**
"What would you say to someone who is considering working with me?"

### The Draft-and-Approve Method

Most clients do not know how to write a good testimonial. Write it for them.

**Step 1**: Ask the guided questions (above) to get raw material.

**Step 2**: Turn their answers into a polished testimonial.

**Step 3**: Send them the draft for approval.

**Step 4**: Publish after they approve.

**Step 5**: Thank them and share the published version.

### The Structure of a Perfect Testimonial

**Headline**: The single most compelling result (e.g., "34% increase in conversion rate")

**Body**: Problem - Solution - Results format in 3-5 sentences

**Attribution**: Full name, title, company, photo

**Example**:
"We were stuck with a 3.2% conversion rate for 18 months. [Name] came in, analyzed our checkout flow, and implemented changes that increased conversion to 4.3% in just 60 days. That represents $2M in additional annual revenue. I would recommend [Name] to anyone looking to improve their e-commerce performance."

- Jane Smith, Head of E-commerce, Allbirds

## Video Testimonials

### Why Video

- 5x more impactful than text
- Shows real emotion and enthusiasm
- Harder to fake (more credible)
- Can be repurposed (social media, website, proposals)
- Builds a personal connection with prospects

### How to Get Great Video Testimonials

**Preparation**:
1. Send questions in advance
2. Provide a quiet, well-lit space
3. Use a professional mic (lav mic is best)
4. Frame the shot (head and shoulders, good background)
5. Test audio and video before recording

**The interview format**:

"You": "Tell me about the challenge you were facing before we started working together."

[Client responds]

"You": "What made you decide to work with us?"

[Client responds]

"You": "What specific results did you see?"

[Client responds]

"You": "What was it like working with our team?"

[Client responds]

"You": "What would you say to someone considering working with us?"

[Client responds]

**Tips for natural responses**:
- Do not give them a script (sounds robotic)
- Ask open-ended questions (cannot answer yes/no)
- Let them talk (do not interrupt)
- Encourage, do not direct
- Keep it conversational

**After recording**:
- Thank the client
- Send them the raw file
- Edit for brevity (2-3 minutes max)
- Add intro/outro with your branding
- Post on your website and YouTube

### Video Testimonial Tech Setup

**Minimum**: Smartphone (good lighting, good audio)
**Better**: DSLR + lav mic + ring light
**Best**: Professional videographer + studio lighting + multiple angles

**Remote recording options**:
- Zoom (record locally for best quality)
- Riverside.fm (records separate tracks)
- StreamYard (live recording with branding)

## LinkedIn Recommendations

### Why LinkedIn Recommendations Matter

- Visible in search results
- Appear on your profile
- Build credibility with your network
- Show up in LinkedIn search algorithms

### How to Get LinkedIn Recommendations

**Step 1**: Build a relationship. Do great work. Stay in touch.

**Step 2**: Ask at the right time (peak happiness).

**Step 3**: Make it easy. Provide a template or suggested text.

**Step 4**: Offer to reciprocate (write one for them first).

**Step 5**: Follow up once if they do not respond.

### How to Write a LinkedIn Recommendation for Someone Else

The best way to get a recommendation is to give one.

**Recommendation structure**:
1. Your relationship (how you know them)
2. What they did (specific project or work)
3. The impact (what results they achieved)
4. Their strengths (what makes them great)
5. Endorsement (who you would recommend them to)

**Example**:
"I worked with [Name] on a complete redesign of our e-commerce platform. They brought deep technical expertise and a collaborative approach that made the project run smoothly. The result was a 34% increase in conversion rate and a site that loads 3x faster. I would recommend [Name] to any company looking to improve their online presence."

## Repurposing Testimonials

### One Testimonial, Many Uses

A single testimonial can be:

1. **Full quote** on your website
2. **Short quote** (pull a key sentence) for social media
3. **Graphic** (text overlay on photo) for Instagram
4. **Video clip** for LinkedIn
5. **Excerpt** in proposals
6. **Full case study** if you have detailed results
7. **Infographic** (before/after with quote)
8. **Email signature** snippet

### Testimonial Graphics

Create branded graphic cards for each testimonial:
- Client photo or logo
- Key quote (2-3 sentences)
- Name, title, company
- Key result (in large text)
- Your branding (logo, colors)

**Tools**: Canva, Figma, Adobe Express

## Common Testimonial Collection Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Asking too late**
"Asking 6 months after project completion when the client has forgotten the details."

**Fix**: Ask within 1 week of project completion or result achievement.

**Mistake 2: Not being specific**
"Can you write a testimonial?" leads to vague responses.

**Fix**: Ask specific questions. Guide them to mention results.

**Mistake 3: No follow-up**
"70% of people need a reminder. Do not give up after one ask."

**Fix**: Follow up 2-3 times. Be polite. Make it easy.

**Mistake 4: Not getting approval**
"Publishing without approval is risky and damages relationships."

**Fix**: Always send the draft for approval before publishing.

**Mistake 5: No system**
"Asking randomly with no system means inconsistent results."

**Fix**: Create a systematic process. Automate what you can.

**Mistake 6: Not reciprocating**
"If someone writes you a recommendation, write one back."

**Fix**: Always offer to reciprocate. Build goodwill.
