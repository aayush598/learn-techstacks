# Getting on Podcasts: Pitching Yourself and Delivering Value as a Guest

## Why Podcasts Are a High-ROI Channel

Podcast appearances are one of the most underutilized marketing channels for freelancers. Each appearance gives you:

- **40-60 minutes of undivided attention** from a targeted audience
- **Authority transfer** (the host's credibility rubs off on you)
- **Evergreen content** (episodes stay online forever)
- **Relationship with the host** (potential referral partner)
- **Content for repurposing** (clips, quotes, social posts)
- **Backlinks** (for SEO, if shownotes include your link)

**Typical ROI of one podcast appearance**:
- 100-500 new website visitors
- 10-50 new email subscribers
- 2-10 inbound leads
- 1-3 booked calls
- $1k-$20k+ in new client revenue

## Step 1: Getting Ready

### Define Your Message

Before pitching, get crystal clear on:

**Your core message**: One sentence about what you do and who you help.

**Your unique angle**: What makes your perspective different.

**Your story**: Why you do what you do and how you got here.

**Your value**: What listeners will learn from you.

### Create a Guest Kit

A one-page PDF that podcast hosts can review:

**Header**: Your name + tagline + professional headshot

**Episode topics**: 5-10 topics you can discuss (with descriptions)

**Sample questions**: 5-10 questions the host can ask you

**Bio**: 50-word and 100-word versions

**Links**: Website, LinkedIn, past podcast appearances

**Testimonials**: Quotes from past hosts (get these after each appearance)

### Identify Your Target Podcasts

**Criteria**:
- Audience matches your ideal client
- Episode format includes guest interviews
- Active (published in the last 30 days)
- Reasonable listenership (100+ downloads/episode for B2B)
- Host is engaged and promoting episodes

**Where to find podcasts**:
- **Apple Podcasts**: Search for topics related to your niche
- **Spotify**: Same search strategy
- **Podcast directories**: ListenNotes, Podchaser
- **Guest matchmaking services**: MatchMaker.fm, PodcastGuests.com
- **Twitter/LinkedIn**: Search for "looking for podcast guests" + your niche
- **Competitor research**: See where your competitors have been guests

**Podcast discovery tools**:
- ListenNotes (searchable podcast database)
- Podchaser (podcast database with guest opportunities)
- Chartable (podcast charts and trends)

**Target list**: Start with 20-30 podcasts. Aim to appear on 3-5 per month.

### The Podcast Tier System

| Tier | Downloads/Episode | Effort to Get On | Value |
|------|-------------------|-------------------|-------|
| Tier 1 | 100-1000 | Low | Good for practice, building portfolio |
| Tier 2 | 1000-10,000 | Medium | Decent leads, good authority |
| Tier 3 | 10,000-100,000 | High | Excellent leads, strong authority |
| Tier 4 | 100,000+ | Very High | Breakout opportunities |

**Strategy**: Start with Tier 1-2 to build your guest portfolio and recording skills. Then move to Tier 3-4.

## Step 2: Pitching Yourself

### The Pitch Sequence

**Step 1: Warm up**
Before pitching, engage with the podcast:
- Listen to 2-3 episodes
- Leave a review on Apple Podcasts
- Share an episode on social media with a thoughtful comment
- Tag the host

**Step 2: Personalized pitch**
Use the template below. Personalize it based on what you heard.

**Step 3: Follow up**
Wait 7 days. Send a brief follow-up. Wait 7 more days. Send one more. Then move on.

### Podcast Pitch Templates

**Cold email pitch**:

```
Subject: Podcast guest idea: [Topic] for [Podcast Name]

Hi [Host Name],

I have been enjoying your podcast, especially the episode with [Guest Name] about [Topic]. The discussion on [specific point] really resonated.

I am a [your title] who helps [target audience] achieve [outcome]. I thought your listeners might enjoy an episode about:

1. [Topic 1 - specific, benefit-focused]
2. [Topic 2 - specific, benefit-focused]
3. [Topic 3 - specific, benefit-focused]

For example, I recently helped [client] achieve [result]. I would love to share the framework we used.

I have appeared on [other podcasts] and can provide references if helpful.

Would you be open to a conversation about being a guest?

Best,
[Your Name]
[Link to your website/social]
```

**Referral pitch (warm)** :

```
Subject: [Referrer Name] suggested I reach out

Hi [Host Name],

[Referrer Name] suggested I reach out to you about being a guest on [Podcast Name]. They thought your listeners would enjoy my perspective on [topic].

I help [target audience] achieve [outcome]. Some topics I cover:

- [Topic 1]
- [Topic 2]
- [Topic 3]

I have been a guest on [other podcasts] and am happy to share references.

Would you like to set up a time to chat?

Best,
[Your Name]
```

**Follow-up (day 7)** :

```
Subject: Quick follow-up

Hi [Host Name],

Just following up on my previous email about being a guest on [Podcast Name]. I know you are busy.

I wanted to share a specific episode idea: [Specific topic that solves a specific problem for their audience].

Let me know if this is of interest!

Best,
[Your Name]
```

**Follow-up (day 14)** :

```
Subject: Final follow-up

Hi [Host Name],

I know you are busy, so I will keep this brief.

If you ever need a guest to discuss [your topic], I would love to be considered. No rush at all.

In the meantime, keep up the great work with the podcast.

Best,
[Your Name]
```

### Pitch Best Practices

**Do**:
- Personalize every pitch (mention a specific episode you liked)
- Lead with value (what you can teach their audience)
- Be specific about topics
- Keep it brief (5-7 sentences)
- Include social proof (other appearances, client results)

**Don't**:
- Send a mass email
- Pitch without listening to the show first
- Lead with a sales pitch for your services
- Make the email about you (make it about their audience)
- Attach files (use links instead)

### Pitch Subject Lines That Work

- "Podcast guest idea for [Podcast Name]"
- "Topic idea: [Specific benefit for their audience]"
- "[Mutual connection] suggested I reach out"
- "Listener + potential guest: [Topic]"
- "Love your episode on [Topic], have an idea for another"

## Step 3: Preparing for the Episode

### Pre-Interview Preparation

**Before recording**:

- **Know the show**: Listen to 2-3 episodes. Understand the format, the host's style, the audience.
- **Know the host**: What do they care about? What questions have they asked other guests?
- **Prepare your key messages**: 3-5 points you want to make regardless of questions.
- **Prepare stories**: 2-3 case studies or examples ready to share.
- **Prepare a "one thing"**: What is the single most valuable thing you want listeners to take away?

**Send to the host in advance**:
- Your bio (50 words)
- 3-5 suggested topics
- 5-10 sample questions they can ask
- Links to relevant resources

### Talking Points vs Script

Do NOT read from a script. It sounds robotic. Instead, prepare talking points:

**Talking points format**:
```
Topic: [Topic]
Key point: [Single sentence]
Supporting story: [30-second anecdote]
Call to action: [What do you want listeners to do?]
```

### Anticipate Questions

Think about what questions the host might ask and prepare answers:

**Common questions**:
- "Tell us about your background. How did you get started?"
- "What is the biggest mistake you see [target audience] making?"
- "What is your framework or approach to [topic]?"
- "Can you share a success story?"
- "What is one thing our listeners can do today to [get result]?"
- "What is coming next in [your field]?"
- "Where can people find you?"

## Step 4: Delivering a Great Interview

### During the Interview

**Audio quality**:
- Use a good microphone (USB mic like Blue Yeti or Shure MV7)
- Record in a quiet room with soft surfaces (no echo)
- Close windows and doors
- Mute notifications
- Use a stable internet connection (Ethernet > WiFi)

**Video quality** (if recording video):
- Good lighting (face lit from front)
- Clean, professional background
- Camera at eye level
- Solid color clothing (no busy patterns)

**During the conversation**:
- Be energetic and enthusiastic (energy translates through audio)
- Listen actively to the host (do not just wait for your turn to speak)
- Keep answers concise (45-90 seconds per response)
- Use conversational language (not jargon)
- Tell stories (concrete > abstract)
- Pause before speaking (avoids talking over the host)
- Say the host's name and podcast name (helps with editing)

**Structuring your answers**:

1. **Acknowledge the question**: "That is a great question because..."
2. **State your answer**: The core point in 1-2 sentences
3. **Give an example**: Specific story or case study
4. **Summarize**: "So the key takeaway is..."

### What NOT to Do

- Sell your services directly
- Badmouth competitors
- Be negative or complain
- Ramble (keep answers focused)
- Interrupt the host
- Use too much jargon
- Sound rehearsed or robotic

### Handling Difficult Questions

**If you do not know the answer**:
"That is a great question. I do not have a perfect answer, but here is my perspective based on my experience..."

**If the host challenges you**:
"That is a fair point. Here is why I see it differently..."

**If you make a mistake**:
"Let me clarify that..."

## Step 5: After the Episode

### Post-Interview Follow-Up

**Immediately after**:
- Thank the host
- Share the recording (if you recorded your own backup)
- Ask when the episode will be published

**Day of publication**:
- Share on all your social channels (tag the podcast)
- Thank the host publicly
- Share a quote or clip

**Within 48 hours**:
- Send the host a thank-you note
- Share the episode with your email list
- Engage with people who comment/share

### Repurposing Your Appearance

One podcast appearance can generate:

- **Social media posts**: 5-10 quotes and clips
- **Blog post**: Transcribe and edit the episode
- **Email newsletter**: Feature in your next email
- **YouTube video**: If video was recorded
- **LinkedIn content**: Key insights as posts
- **Website**: Add to "Press/Media" or "Podcast" page

### Building the Relationship

- Stay in touch with the host (congratulate them on milestones)
- Share their content regularly
- Offer to be a guest again (with a different topic)
- Refer guests to them
- Collaborate on other projects

## Common Podcast Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Not preparing**
Winging it leads to rambling, unfocused answers.

**Fix**: Prepare talking points. Rehearse your key messages.

**Mistake 2: Poor audio**
If your audio sounds bad, people will not listen regardless of content.

**Fix**: Invest in a decent microphone and quiet recording space.

**Mistake 3: Being too salesy**
Listeners tune out when they feel sold to.

**Fix**: Teach, do not sell. Provide value. The sales come naturally.

**Mistake 4: Talking too long**
Long, rambling answers lose the audience.

**Fix**: Keep answers to 60-90 seconds. If you need more time, ask "Do you want me to go deeper on that?"

**Mistake 5: No call to action**
Listeners enjoyed the episode but do not know what to do next.

**Fix**: Have a specific, valuable next step (free resource, consultation, etc.)

**Mistake 6: Not promoting the episode**
You did all the work of being a guest but did not share it.

**Fix**: Promote the episode across all channels. It is not bragging if it provides value.

**Mistake 7: Not following up**
Missed opportunity to build a relationship with the host.

**Fix**: Thank the host, share the episode, stay in touch.

## Summary

Podcast appearances are one of the highest-ROI marketing activities. Create a target list of 20-30 podcasts. Pitch personalized with value-focused topics. Prepare thoroughly with talking points (not scripts). Deliver a great interview: concise, energetic, story-driven. Repurpose every appearance across channels. Track results and build relationships with hosts. Consistent podcast appearances build authority, generate leads, and create evergreen content that works for you long after the episode airs.
