# Getting Speaking Gigs: Conference Proposals, Stages, and Authority Building

## Why Speaking Matters for Your Income

Speaking at conferences is the highest-leverage authority-building activity available to freelancers.

**What speaking does for your business**:
- Positions you as an expert (you were chosen to speak = you must know your stuff)
- Generates inbound leads (audience members reach out after your talk)
- Creates premium content (recording of your talk = content asset)
- Builds your network (other speakers, organizers, sponsors)
- Justifies premium rates ("I saw you speak at [Conference]" = instant trust)
- Differentiates you (most freelancers never speak. Speakers stand out.)

**ROI of one conference talk**:
- Preparation time: 20-40 hours
- Speaking time: 30-60 minutes
- Leads generated: 5-20 qualified leads
- Value of those leads: $10k-$200k
- Perpetual asset: Recording can be used for years

## How to Get Your First Speaking Gig

### The Chicken-and-Egg Problem

You cannot get speaking gigs without speaking experience. You cannot get speaking experience without speaking gigs.

**Solution**: Create your own opportunities.

**Path 1: Start small**
- Local meetups (5-50 people)
- Online webinars (attendees from your network)
- Internal company events
- Podcast guest appearances (talk about your talk topic)
- Virtual conferences (lower bar for entry)

**Path 2: Create your own event**
- Host a webinar on your topic
- Organize a virtual workshop
- Partner with a co-working space for a lunch-and-learn
- Start a local meetup and be the first speaker

**Path 3: Apply aggressively**
- Submit to every relevant CFP (call for proposals)
- Most conferences accept 20-30% of submissions
- Apply to 20+ conferences to get 3-5 accepted

### Building Your Speaker Profile

**Speaker website/one-pager**:
- Professional headshot
- 2-3 talk topics with descriptions
- Past speaking engagements (even small ones)
- Video clips (30-60 seconds each)
- Biography (50 words, 100 words, 200 words)
- Testimonials from past speaking
- Contact information

**Speaker reel** (2-3 minute video):
- Highlights from past talks
- Audience engagement
- Stage presence
- Diverse settings

### The Speaker One-Sheet

A one-page PDF that conference organizers can quickly scan:

**Header**: Your name + tagline + professional headshot

**Talk topics**: 3 topics with titles and descriptions

**Bio**: 100-word professional bio

**Past speaking**: Logos of past events (even small ones)

**Video link**: QR code or link to speaking clips

**Contact**: Email, website, phone

## Crafting a Winning Talk Proposal

### The CFP (Call for Proposals)

Most conferences have a CFP process. Your proposal is evaluated by a committee.

**Evaluation criteria**:
- Relevance to the audience (40%)
- Novelty and uniqueness (25%)
- Speaker expertise (20%)
- Entertainment value (15%)

### Proposal Structure

**Title**: Catchy but descriptive. Include a number or promise.

**Examples**:
- "10 Things Nobody Tells You About Shopify Checkout Optimization"
- "From Zero to $10k/month: The First 90 Days of Freelancing"
- "How We Increased Conversion Rate by 340% (And How You Can Too)"

**Abstract** (100-200 words):
- Hook (grab attention in first sentence)
- Problem (what the audience struggles with)
- Solution (what you will teach them)
- Key takeaways (3-5 specific learnings)
- Why you (brief credentials)

**Example abstract**:
"Most Shopify stores have a checkout conversion rate below 3%. That means for every 100 visitors who add to cart, 97 leave without buying. In this talk, I will share the exact 5-step framework we used to increase checkout conversion from 3.2% to 4.3% for a major brand. You will learn: (1) The #1 reason people abandon checkout, (2) How heatmap analysis reveals hidden friction points, (3) A simple A/B testing framework that anyone can implement, (4) The 3 things on your checkout page that are costing you sales, and (5) How to measure and prove your results."

**Key takeaways** (3-5 bullet points):
- "How to identify the top 3 friction points in your checkout flow"
- "A 5-step framework for conversion rate optimization"
- "How to run A/B tests that produce statistically significant results"
- "How to calculate the revenue impact of your optimization efforts"

**Speaker bio** (50-100 words):
"[Name] helps [target] achieve [outcome]. They have delivered [results] for [notable clients]. They have [years] of experience in [field]. [Personal detail that shows human side]."

**Format notes**:
- Preferred format (keynote, breakout, workshop, panel)
- Length preference (30, 45, 60 minutes)
- Technical requirements (projector, wifi, etc.)
- Willingness to travel

### Talk Topics That Get Accepted

**Trending topics** (higher chance of acceptance):
- AI and automation for your field
- Remote work best practices
- Data-driven decision making
- Diversity and inclusion
- Mental health and burnout

**Evergreen topics** (always needed):
- Practical how-to talks
- Case studies with specific numbers
- Frameworks and methodologies
- Lessons learned from failures
- Future predictions

**Topics to avoid**:
- Vendor pitches (sell your product)
- "What is [technology]?" (too basic)
- Overdone topics with no new angle
- Pure theory with no practical application

### How to Find CFPs

**Platforms**:
- Papercall.io (aggregates CFPs)
- Sessionize.com (speaker management)
- Lanyrd (conference database)
- Conference websites directly

**Strategies**:
- Set up Google Alerts for "[your niche] conference CFP"
- Follow conference organizers on LinkedIn
- Join speaker communities (Facebook groups, LinkedIn groups)
- Ask other speakers how they found their gigs

## Preparing Your Talk

### Talk Structure

**Opening** (2-3 minutes):
- Hook: Start with a surprising fact, question, or story
- Credibility: Why you are qualified to speak on this topic (brief, humble)
- Roadmap: What you will cover and what they will learn

**Body** (20-40 minutes):
- 3-5 main points
- Each point: concept, example, application
- Transitions between points
- Stories and anecdotes
- Data and evidence

**Closing** (3-5 minutes):
- Summary of key takeaways
- Call to action (what to do next)
- Memorable closing statement
- Questions

### Slide Design

**Principles**:
- One idea per slide
- More images, less text
- Large fonts (30pt+ for body, 50pt+ for headlines)
- High contrast
- Consistent design

**Slide types**:
- Title slide
- Problem/solution slide
- Data slide (charts, graphs, numbers)
- Framework slide (visual diagram)
- Example/case study slide
- Quote slide
- Transition slide
- Summary slide

**Don't**:
- Read your slides
- Use slide transitions (simple fades only)
- Overcrowd slides
- Use clip art or low-quality images
- Use your slides as speaker notes

### Storytelling in Talks

**The Case Study Story**:
- Setup: The client's situation and pain
- Conflict: What was not working
- Resolution: What you did
- Result: The outcome
- Lesson: What the audience should learn

**The Personal Story**:
- Setup: Where you were
- Conflict: The challenge
- Resolution: What you learned
- Application: How the audience can benefit

**Stories to include**:
- A failure and what you learned
- A surprising insight
- A client success story
- Your personal journey

### Rehearsal

**Rehearsal process**:
1. Write the talk word-for-word (to clarify thinking)
2. Practice with notes (3-5 times)
3. Practice with slides, no notes (3-5 times)
4. Record yourself and watch (1-2 times)
5. Give a practice talk to a small audience (1 time)
6. Final rehearsal (1-2 times)

**Total practice**: 10-20 hours for a 30-45 minute talk

**What to practice**:
- Timing (within 10% of allocated time)
- Transitions between slides
- Gestures and movement
- Eye contact
- Voice modulation
- Handling questions

## At the Conference

### Before the Conference

- Confirm travel and accommodation
- Pack backup (clicker, adapters, printed slides, water)
- Prepare your introduction (for the MC)
- Share on social media: "Excited to speak at [Conference]!"
- Connect with other speakers
- Review the schedule

### During the Talk

**Pre-talk**:
- Arrive early to test AV
- Meet the AV team
- Have water available
- Breathe

**During**:
- Start on time
- Make eye contact with audience
- Move around the stage (do not hide behind podium)
- Speak clearly and at a moderate pace
- Pause for emphasis
- Watch the time

**Dealing with nerves**:
- It is normal. Even experienced speakers get nervous.
- Focus on serving the audience, not on yourself.
- Remember: You know more about your topic than anyone in the room.
- Deep breaths before walking on stage.

### After the Talk

- Stay for questions
- Thank the organizers
- Connect with audience members who approach you
- Collect business cards
- Share on social media: "Just spoke at [Conference] about [topic]. Here is what I covered..."
- Follow up with new connections within 48 hours

## Getting the Most ROI from Speaking

### Lead Generation

**At the conference**:
- Include a lead magnet in your talk (free resource)
- Offer a "speaker discount" for your services
- Have a signup sheet or QR code for your email list
- Attend networking events and after-parties

**After the conference**:
- Email your list about the talk recording
- Connect with attendees on LinkedIn
- Follow up with qualified leads
- Offer a free consultation to conference attendees

### Content Repurposing

One talk can become:
- Blog post (full transcription)
- Twitter thread (5-10 tweets of key points)
- LinkedIn post (summary with key takeaways)
- YouTube video (the recording)
- Infographic (key framework or data)
- Short video clips (30-60 second highlights)
- Email sequence (break talk into 5 emails)
- Podcast episode (if you have a podcast or guest on one)

### Building Relationships

- Connect with other speakers (they are potential podcast hosts, referral partners, collaborators)
- Connect with organizers (they may ask you back or recommend you)
- Connect with sponsors (potential clients or partners)
- Follow up within 48 hours

## Scaling: From One Talk to a Speaking Career

### Building a Speaking Portfolio

**Year 1**: 3-5 local meetups and webinars
**Year 2**: 3-5 regional conferences
**Year 3**: 3-5 national conferences
**Year 4+**: Keynote invitations and paid speaking

### Getting Paid to Speak

Most conferences do not pay speakers. They cover travel and accommodation. Keynote speakers at major conferences get paid $2k-$20k+.

**Path to paid speaking**:
1. Build a reputation (free talks first)
2. Get a booking agent or agency
3. Develop a signature talk
4. Build demand (organizers seek you out)
5. Charge for your expertise

### Speaker Representation

When you are getting 10+ speaking requests/year, consider:
- Speaker agency (they find gigs, negotiate fees)
- Speaker bureau (conference organizers hire through them)

**Major speaker agencies**:
- The Washington Speakers Bureau
- Leading Authorities
- Keppler Speakers

## Common Speaking Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Trying to cover too much**
50 slides for a 30-minute talk = rushed, unclear, forgettable.

**Fix**: Cover 3-5 points deeply. Less is more.

**Mistake 2: Reading from slides or notes**
Audience can read slides faster than you can speak. They do not need you to read to them.

**Fix**: Use speaker notes (not visible to audience) and speak naturally.

**Mistake 3: No call to action**
Audience leaves inspired but has no idea what to do next.

**Fix**: End with a specific, actionable call to action.

**Mistake 4: Not rehearsing enough**
First time delivering the talk = on stage. This is a disaster.

**Fix**: Rehearse 10+ times before the actual event.

**Mistake 5: Ignoring the audience**
Staring at slides, reading notes, no eye contact.

**Fix**: Talk TO the audience. Make eye contact. Read the room.

**Mistake 6: Trying to sell from the stage**
"Sounds like a sales pitch" is the fastest way to lose the audience.

**Fix**: Teach, do not sell. If you provide value, they will seek you out.

**Mistake 7: Bad AV preparation**
Your slides do not work. The mic is wrong. The projector is too dim.

**Fix**: Arrive early. Test everything. Have backups.
