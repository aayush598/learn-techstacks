# Qualifying Leads

## Stop Wasting Time on Tire-Kickers and Unicorns

The biggest mistake freelancers make is treating every lead equally. Great leads pay quickly, respect your process, and deliver referrals. Bad leads waste your time, haggle on price, and demand the impossible.

This guide covers BANT, MEDDIC, and CHAMP frameworks adapted for freelancers, plus red flags and scoring systems.

---

## Why Qualification Matters

### The Cost of Bad Leads

| Factor | Time Wasted | Opportunity Cost |
|--------|-------------|------------------|
| Unqualified prospect call | 45 min | Could have closed a $5K deal |
| Custom proposal for tire-kicker | 4 hours | Could have delivered billable work |
| Follow-up emails over 2 weeks | 2 hours | Could have closed a qualified lead |
| Negotiation with budget-mismatch | 3 hours | Could have found better client |

**Rule:** One hour of qualification saves ten hours of chasing dead leads.

### The 80/20 Rule of Clients

80% of your revenue comes from 20% of your clients. The top 20% share these traits:
- They have budget (and they know it)
- They have authority (and they use it)
- They have a real problem (and they feel it)
- They respect your expertise (and they pay for it)
- They give referrals (and they're connected)

Your qualification system should filter FOR these clients and filter OUT everyone else.

## Framework #1: BANT for Freelancers

BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) is the classic qualification framework. Here's how to adapt it for freelancing.

### Budget

**What to qualify:**
- Do they have a specific budget allocated?
- Is the budget realistic for the work needed?
- Is there flexibility, or is it hard-capped?

**Questions:**
- "What budget range have you set aside for this project?"
- "How did you arrive at that number?"
- "Is that a hard cap, or is there flexibility for the right solution?"

**Qualified** = Has budget within your range OR has flexibility
**Unqualified** = No budget, "we'll figure it out later," or unrealistic expectations

**Freelancer Adaptation:**
Unlike enterprise sales, freelancers often work with smaller budgets. Adjust your minimum threshold:
- If your minimum project is $5K, don't waste time on $500 budgets
- If they say "we have no budget but this is urgent," they're lying about urgency
- If they won't share the budget, they're not serious

**The Budget Red Flags:**
- "We'll figure out budget after we see the proposal" — NOPE
- "This should be cheap, it's just a simple website" — It never is
- "Can you do it for exposure?" — Expose your bank account to zero deposits
- "What's your hourly rate? Our budget is $20/hour" — Find overseas talent then

### Authority

**What to qualify:**
- Can this person say yes?
- Do they control the budget?
- Who else must approve?

**Questions:**
- "Besides yourself, who else will be involved in this decision?"
- "What's your decision-making process for projects like this?"
- "If we came up with a solution you loved, would you be able to approve it?"

**Qualified** = Decision maker OR has direct access to decision maker
**Unqualified** = "I need to check with my manager/partner/board" (and can't get you access)

**The Authority Ladder:**
| Level | Title | Power |
|-------|-------|-------|
| 5 | Founder/CEO | Full authority |
| 4 | VP/Director | Can approve within budget |
| 3 | Manager | Can recommend, needs approval |
| 2 | Team Lead | Can influence, no approval |
| 1 | Individual Contributor | No authority, scouting |

**Warning:** If you're talking to Level 1-2, you need access to Level 4-5 before you send a proposal. Otherwise, you're writing a proposal for someone who can't say yes.

### Need

**What to qualify:**
- What problem are they solving?
- How painful is the problem?
- What happens if they don't solve it?

**Questions:**
- "What's the biggest challenge you're facing right now?"
- "What's the cost of NOT solving this?"
- "What have you tried before, and why didn't it work?"

**Qualified** = Clear problem with significant pain
**Unqualified** = Vague "we should maybe do something" with no urgency

**Pain Levels:**
| Level | Description | Close Probability |
|-------|-------------|------------------|
| Critical | Revenue loss, legal risk, customer churn | 80%+ |
| High | Efficiency loss, competitive disadvantage | 60%+ |
| Medium | Nice to have, improvement opportunity | 30%+ |
| Low | Curiosity, "just looking" | <10% |

**Pain Amplification:** If the pain isn't strong enough, don't try to create it artificially. Either they have a real problem or they don't. Move on.

### Timeline

**What to qualify:**
- When do they need this done?
- What's driving the timeline?
- Is the timeline realistic?

**Questions:**
- "When would you like this to be live?"
- "What's driving that date?"
- "What happens if it's delayed?"

**Qualified** = Clear timeline within next 90 days with consequences for delay
**Unqualified** = "Sometime this year" or "no rush" or "yesterday" (unrealistic)

**Timeline Scoring:**
| Timeline | Score | Action |
|----------|-------|--------|
| ASAP / Yesterday | 5 | Check if realistic, charge premium |
| Within 30 days | 10 | Hot lead, prioritize |
| 30-60 days | 8 | Warm lead, nurture |
| 60-90 days | 5 | Warm lead, stay in touch |
| 90+ days | 2 | Cold lead, add to drip campaign |
| "No timeline" | 0 | Not a lead yet |

## Framework #2: MEDDIC for Freelancers

MEDDIC (Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, Champion) is an enterprise framework that works amazingly for high-ticket freelance projects ($10K+).

### Metrics

**What to qualify:**
- What numbers matter to them?
- What's the current baseline?
- What's the target improvement?

**Questions:**
- "How do you measure success for this initiative?"
- "What's your current performance on those metrics?"
- "What would be a meaningful improvement?"

**Freelancer Adaptation:**
If they can't articulate metrics, they're not serious about ROI. No metrics = no budget justification for you.

**Example Metrics by Project Type:**

| Project Type | Metrics |
|-------------|---------|
| Web Dev | Conversion rate, traffic, load time, bounce rate |
| E-commerce | Revenue, AOV, cart abandonment, customer acquisition cost |
| SaaS | Churn rate, activation rate, DAU/MAU, NPS |
| DevOps | Deploy frequency, MTTR, uptime, cost reduction |
| SEO | Organic traffic, keyword rankings, domain authority |
| Security | Vulnerability count, audit findings, breach risk score |

### Economic Buyer

**What to qualify:**
- Who controls the budget?
- Who signs the check?
- Do you have access to them?

**Questions:**
- "Who has final approval on this budget?"
- "Can we include them in our next conversation?"
- "What would make them feel confident about this investment?"

**The Rule:** If you haven't spoken to the person who signs the check, you haven't qualified the deal.

### Decision Criteria

**What to qualify:**
- How will they choose between options?
- What factors matter most?
- What would disqualify a vendor?

**Questions:**
- "What criteria will you use to evaluate proposals?"
- "What's most important to you — price, timeline, quality, or experience?"
- "What would make you immediately say no to a proposal?"

**The Frame:** Once you know their criteria, you can position yourself perfectly. If timeline is key, emphasize your speed. If quality matters, show your portfolio. If price is sensitive, demonstrate ROI.

### Decision Process

**What to qualify:**
- How do decisions actually get made?
- Who needs to be involved at each stage?
- What's the timeline for a decision?

**Questions:**
- "Walk me through your decision process from start to finish"
- "After we talk, what happens next on your end?"
- "What approvals are needed before you can move forward?"

**Understand the Process:**

```
Example Decision Process:
1. Initial discovery call (you are here)
2. Internal team review of proposal
3. Technical evaluation (CTO reviews)
4. Budget approval (CFO signs off)
5. Final decision (CEO approves)
6. Contract and payment processing

Each step takes 3-7 days = 3-6 weeks total
```

If they can't outline this process, they don't have one — meaning they're not ready to buy.

### Identify Pain

**What to qualify:**
- What's the specific problem?
- What's the impact of the problem?
- Who else is affected?

**Questions:**
- (Use all pain discovery questions from the question bank)
- Focus on: emotional, financial, and operational pain
- Quantify everything: "How much does this cost you per month?"

**Pain Depth Check:**
| Pain Level | They Say | Reality |
|------------|----------|---------|
| Surface | "Our site is slow" | Vague complaint |
| Moderate | "We're losing customers because of slow load times" | Real problem |
| Deep | "We lost $50K last quarter from cart abandonment due to slow pages" | Critical pain |

Only pursue Deep pain. Surface and Moderate will stall.

### Champion

**What to qualify:**
- Who internally wants this to happen?
- Why do they want it?
- Will they sell for you internally?

**Questions:**
- "Who else in your organization would benefit from this solution?"
- "What's their stake in seeing this succeed?"
- "Would you be willing to introduce me to them?"

**Champion Characteristics:**
| Trait | Strong Champion | Weak Champion |
|-------|----------------|---------------|
| Authority | Has influence | Just likes the idea |
| Motivation | Solves their problem | "Would be cool" |
| Access | Connected to decision makers | Isolated |
| Willingness | Will sell internally | "I'll mention it" |
| Timeline | Wants it now | "Whenever" |

**The Test:** Ask your champion to set up a meeting with the economic buyer. If they can't or won't, they're not a real champion.

## Framework #3: CHAMP for Freelancers

CHAMP (Challenges, Authority, Money, Prioritization) is designed for consultative selling — perfect for freelancers who sell expertise.

### Challenges

Focus on the problem before the solution. Don't talk about your service until you deeply understand their challenge.

**The Challenge Audit:**
1. What's the specific challenge?
2. How does it manifest day-to-day?
3. Who feels it most acutely?
4. What's the domino effect on other areas?
5. What's the emotional impact on stakeholders?

**Challenge Types:**
| Type | Example | Freelancer Opportunity |
|------|---------|----------------------|
| Technical | "Our system crashes weekly" | High, very tangible |
| Operational | "We can't scale our team" | Medium, process-focused |
| Strategic | "We don't know our tech direction" | High, consulting opportunity |
| Competitive | "Our competitor just launched X" | Urgent, time-sensitive |

### Authority

Same as BANT/MEDDIC. Must qualify decision-making power.

### Money

**The CHAMP Money Approach:**
Don't ask "what's your budget?" Instead, uncover the financial impact.

**Money Questions:**
- "What's the financial impact of this challenge?"
- "If we solve this, what revenue would it unlock?"
- "What's the cost of maintaining the status quo?"

**The ROI Calculation:**
Help them calculate ROI before you talk price.

"Based on what you shared, it sounds like this problem is costing you about $X per month. Solving it could save $Y per year. Does that math feel right?"

Then: "Our investment for this would be around $Z. That's a 3-month payback period. How does that compare to your ROI threshold?"

### Prioritization

**What to qualify:**
- Where does this project rank among their priorities?
- What competing initiatives exist?
- What would need to happen for this to become priority #1?

**Questions:**
- "What's competing for your team's attention right now?"
- "On a scale of 1-10, how urgent is solving this?"
- "If we don't address this, what happens?"

**The Reality Check:**
If this project is priority #5, you will not be paid on time, get feedback on time, or get decisions made. Only pursue projects that are priority #1 or #2.

## Lead Scoring System

### The 0-100 Point System

Score every lead after the discovery call. Only pursue leads scoring 70+.

**Category 1: Budget (30 points max)**

| Criteria | Points |
|----------|--------|
| Has allocated budget within your range | 30 |
| Has budget but needs to adjust | 20 |
| Has budget slightly below minimum | 10 |
| No budget / "we'll figure it out" | 0 |
| Expects you to work for exposure | -50 |

**Category 2: Authority (20 points max)**

| Criteria | Points |
|----------|--------|
| Direct decision maker | 20 |
| Influencer with access to decision maker | 15 |
| Recommender, will champion internally | 10 |
| No authority, no champion potential | 0 |

**Category 3: Need (25 points max)**

| Criteria | Points |
|----------|--------|
| Critical pain with quantified impact | 25 |
| Clear problem with moderate impact | 18 |
| Vague need, exploring options | 10 |
| No real problem identified | 0 |

**Category 4: Timeline (15 points max)**

| Criteria | Points |
|----------|--------|
| Need it now (within 30 days) | 15 |
| Need it within 30-60 days | 12 |
| Need it within 60-90 days | 8 |
| No timeline / flexible | 3 |

**Category 5: Fit (10 points max)**

| Criteria | Points |
|----------|--------|
| Perfect fit — done this exact project before | 10 |
| Good fit — relevant experience | 7 |
| Stretch — could figure it out | 4 |
| Bad fit — not your area of expertise | 0 |

### Scoring Thresholds

| Score | Category | Action |
|-------|----------|--------|
| 85-100 | Hot Lead | Send proposal within 24 hours, high priority |
| 70-84 | Warm Lead | Send proposal, follow up weekly |
| 50-69 | Warm-ish Lead | Send proposal with caveats, manage expectations |
| 30-49 | Cold Lead | Don't send proposal; nurture with content |
| 0-29 | Dead Lead | Move to "not now" list, check back in 6 months |
| Below 0 | Bad Lead | Graciously decline or fire immediately |

## Red Flags

### Immediate Disqualifiers (Fire Instantly)

| Red Flag | Why It's Fatal |
|----------|----------------|
| "Can you do it for cheaper?" before scope is defined | They're price shoppers |
| "This is super simple" | Scope will explode |
| "We need it done yesterday" | Unrealistic expectations |
| "We've had bad experiences with freelancers" | They're the problem |
| "We'll give you exposure/equity" | They don't value your work |
| "Just copy what [competitor] did" | They want cheap plagiarism |
| "We don't have a clear brief yet" | They're not ready |
| "Our last developer disappeared" | They drove them away |
| "We might have more work after this" | Classic negotiation tactic |
| Asked 5 other freelancers for proposals | Commodity mindset |

### Yellow Flags (Proceed With Caution)

| Yellow Flag | Risk | Mitigation |
|-------------|------|------------|
| Multiple stakeholders | Decision paralysis | Map all stakeholders, build consensus |
| Vague scope | Scope creep | Fixed price with change order process |
| Previous failed attempts | Trust issues | Deliver quick wins, build trust |
| No budget transparency | Budget mismatch | Add budget guardrails |
| Non-technical founder | Unrealistic expectations | Educate early, set boundaries |
| "We need a proposal to present to our board" | Multiple layers | Get introduced to board |
| They want a detailed spec before paying | Free consulting | Charge for discovery phase |
| They're comparing you to agencies | Commodity mindset | Differentiate on expertise |
| They use "we" but it's just them | Inflated authority | Verify decision structure |
| They're raising funding | Cash flow risk | Require deposit, milestone payments |

### Pattern Recognition: The Tire-Kicker Profile

**Tire-kickers consistently exhibit:**
1. They schedule calls and reschedule multiple times
2. They ask extensive questions without ever committing
3. They want "just a rough estimate" repeatedly
4. They've been "researching options" for months
5. They ask for discounts or matching competitor prices
6. They avoid discussing budget directly
7. They want proposals from multiple people concurrently
8. They CC multiple people to create competitive pressure
9. They're vague about their timeline
10. They're overly impressed by your credentials (shopping, not buying)

**The Tire-Kicker Test:**
Ask for a small commitment — a paid discovery session ($500-1000). Real clients will invest. Tire-kickers will disappear.

## Lead Qualification Process

### Step 1: Initial Contact Qualification (5 minutes)
- Did they come through a referral? (+20 points automatically)
- Do they have a clear project description? (Yes = qualified, No = needs more info)
- Do they mention budget range? (Yes = +20, No = investigate)
- Is their company real? (Check website, LinkedIn, existence)

### Step 2: Discovery Call Qualification (30-45 minutes)
- Run through CHAMP or BANT questions
- Score them using the 0-100 system
- Identify red flags
- Determine next step

### Step 3: Proposal Decision (based on score)
- 70+: Invest time in custom proposal
- 50-69: Send a templated proposal with discovery phase as first step
- Below 50: Don't send a proposal; offer a paid consultation instead

### Step 4: Post-Proposal Qualification
- Did they open the proposal? (Track with document software)
- Did they respond within 48 hours? (Serious clients respond quickly)
- Did they ask clarifying questions? (Engagement is good)
- Did they try to negotiate? (Interested, but may have budget concerns)

### Step 5: The Final Qualification Filter
Before accepting the project, ask yourself:
1. Can I solve their problem? (Competence)
2. Can we work together? (Chemistry)
3. Will they pay me fairly? (Compensation)
4. Will this project help my career? (Growth)
5. Will I enjoy this work? (Energy)

If you answer "no" to any of these, seriously consider walking away.

## The Power of Saying No

### When to Walk Away

| Situation | Walk Away Reason |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Budget is 50%+ below your minimum | They'll never pay full price |
| They're rude/disrespectful in the first call | It only gets worse |
| They want to own your IP for a flat fee | You're giving away future value |
| They have no clear decision process | It will take months |
| The project doesn't interest you | Your work quality will suffer |
| They've had 3+ failed attempts | The problem is them |
| They want a proposal with no commitment | Free consulting |

### How to Fire a Lead

**The Soft Fire:**
"I appreciate you considering me, but I don't think I'm the right fit for this project. I want to make sure you get the best results, and I believe another freelancer with more specific experience in [area] would serve you better. If you'd like, I can recommend a few options."

**The Hard Fire:**
"I've reviewed the requirements and I don't think we're aligned on scope and budget. I don't want to waste your time with a proposal that won't meet your expectations. I'm going to pass on this opportunity."

**The Price Fire:**
"Based on what you're looking for, my rate is $X/hour and I estimate this will take Y hours. If that doesn't fit your budget, I completely understand. Let me know if you'd like to adjust the scope to match."

## The Qualification Mindset

**You are not desperate for clients. You are selective about projects.**

Every hour spent on a bad lead is an hour stolen from a great lead. Every bad client you take will cost you 3x what they pay you in stress, scope creep, and opportunity cost.

The goal is not to close every deal. The goal is to close the RIGHT deals.

| Lead Type | % of Leads | % of Revenue | Time Investment |
|-----------|------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Ideal Client | 20% | 80% | 20% |
| Good Client | 30% | 15% | 30% |
| Bad Client | 50% | 5% | 50% |

Stop spending 50% of your time on 5% of your revenue.

Qualify hard. Fire fast. Win big.
