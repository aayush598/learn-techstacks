# Discovery Question Bank

## 100+ Questions to Uncover Pain, Budget, and Close Deals

The quality of your questions determines the size of your deals. These questions are organized by category and project type. Memorize them. Adapt them. Use them ruthlessly.

---

## Universal Questions (Use on EVERY Call)

### Opening Questions

1. "What prompted you to reach out now?"
2. "What's changed in your business that's making this a priority?"
3. "What's the story behind this project?"
4. "What have you tried so far, and what happened?"
5. "If this project succeeds, what will that mean for your business?"
6. "What's the one thing you absolutely need this to accomplish?"
7. "Who else have you talked to about this?"
8. "What made you decide to look for outside help versus doing it in-house?"
9. "What does your ideal outcome look like?"
10. "What keeps you up at night about this project?"

## Advanced Probing Techniques

### The 5 Whys
For every answer, ask "why" up to 5 times to reach root cause.

**Example:**
- "Our site is slow." → "Why is that important?"
- "Because users are leaving." → "Why are they leaving?"
- "Because pages take too long to load." → "Why do they load slowly?"
- "Because we have unoptimized images." → "Why are images unoptimized?"
- "Because we don't have a process for image optimization."

Now you have a real problem to solve, not a symptom.

### The Contrast Question
"You mentioned budget is around $10K. If I could show you a solution that delivers 3x the results for $20K, would that be worth exploring?"

### The Assumption Challenge
"What makes you think that's the right approach?"
"What if we tried the opposite — what would happen?"
"What evidence do you have that supports that assumption?"

### The Scale Question
"On a scale of 1-10, how urgent is this?"
"On a scale of 1-10, how confident are you in the current approach?"
"On a scale of 1-10, how likely are you to move forward this quarter?"

### The Frame Question
"If money were no object, what would the ideal solution look like?"
"If you had unlimited resources, how would you approach this?"

## Question Sequencing Strategy

### The Pain-First Sequence
1. "What's happening now?" (Situation)
2. "What's not working?" (Problem)
3. "How does that affect you?" (Impact)
4. "What would ideal look like?" (Vision)
5. "What's that worth to you?" (Value)

### The Budget-First Sequence
1. "Do you have a budget set aside?" (Direct)
2. "How'd you arrive at that number?" (Logic)
3. "What's the cost of not solving it?" (ROI)
4. "If we deliver exceptional results, would you invest more?" (Expansion)
5. "What budget would make this a no-brainer?" (Ideal)

### The Authority Sequence
1. "Who else needs to be involved?" (Scope)
2. "What does each person care about?" (Motivations)
3. "How will you gather their input?" (Process)
4. "Who has final sign-off?" (Authority)
5. "What would make them say yes?" (Influence)

### The Timeline Sequence
1. "When does this need to be done?" (Deadline)
2. "What happens if it's late?" (Consequences)
3. "Is the timeline flexible?" (Pressure)
4. "What could we cut to hit the date?" (Priorities)
5. "What's the cost of waiting?" (Urgency)

## Red Flag Questions

### Questions That Reveal Bad Clients

Ask these early to avoid wasting time:

- "How many freelancers have you worked with on similar projects?" (Multiple failures = red flag)
- "What happened with your last freelancer?" (If they trash them, they'll trash you)
- "How long did it take you to decide to invest in this?" (Months of deliberation = slow decision maker)
- "What's your experience working with external contractors?" (No experience = high maintenance)
- "How do you handle scope changes?" (If vague, expect scope creep)

### Questions That Test Seriousness

- "Have you already set aside budget, or are you exploring?" (Casual = low priority)
- "What other projects are competing for this budget?" (Multiple = not committed)
- "When would you like to start?" (Next month = not urgent)
- "What's the approval process look like?" (Complex = long sales cycle)

## The Million-Dollar Question Pattern

The most powerful questions follow this structure:

**Time** + **Problem** + **Impact** + **Desire**

"If you don't solve this in the next [timeframe], what's the [impact] on your business, and how does that affect your ability to [desire]?"

**Example:**
"If you don't improve your conversion rate in the next 3 months, what's the revenue impact on your business, and how does that affect your ability to hit your annual growth target?"

## After the Question: Listen

Questions are only powerful if you listen to the answers.

**Listening Rules:**
- After asking, shut up. Count to 5 in your head.
- Take notes. It shows you care.
- Ask follow-ups based on what they said, not your script.
- Paraphrase what they said to confirm understanding.
- Never interrupt, even if you've heard it before.
- The best question is often "Tell me more about that."

**The 80/20 Listening Rule:**
They should talk 80% of the time. You talk 20%. If you're talking more than them, you're losing the sale.
