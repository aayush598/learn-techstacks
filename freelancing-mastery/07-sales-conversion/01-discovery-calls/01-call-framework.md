# Discovery Call Framework

## The $10,000 Discovery Call System

Most freelancers treat discovery calls as casual "get to know you" chats. That's why they lose deals. Every discovery call is a structured sales process designed to qualify, educate, and close. This framework ensures you control the conversation, extract maximum information, and position yourself as the only logical choice.

---

## Pre-Call Preparation (30 Minutes)

### 15-Minute Research Block

Research the prospect before the call. Never walk in blind.

**Company Research:**
- Check LinkedIn company page: size, funding, recent hires, leadership
- Review their website: products/services, pricing, messaging, blog
- Check Crunchbase/AngelList: funding rounds, investors, growth stage
- Look at competitors: who they compete with, market positioning
- Read recent news: product launches, acquisitions, lawsuits, PR

**Personal Research:**
- Check prospect's LinkedIn: their role, background, interests, mutual connections
- Look at their recent posts or articles: what they care about
- Check mutual connections: warm intros increase close rate by 500%
- Note their professional background: do they have technical expertise?

**Technical Research:**
- Check their tech stack (BuiltWith, Wappalyzer)
- Review their GitHub if applicable
- Test their website performance, UX issues
- Identify obvious problems: slow load times, broken features, outdated design
- Review their API docs if relevant

### 10-Minute Preparation Block

**Define Your Goal:**
- Primary goal for this call (e.g., schedule a proposal meeting)
- Minimum goal (e.g., qualify and send pricing)
- Dream goal (e.g., close on the call)

**Prepare Your Questions:**
- Select 5-7 high-impact questions from your question bank
- Order them strategically to build toward your goal
- Prepare follow-up probes for each answer

**Set Your Environment:**
- Test audio and video
- Close all distracting apps
- Have your CRM/note-taking tool ready
- Open your portfolio and case studies for screen sharing
- Water and notebook at hand

**Mental Preparation:**
- Review your value proposition
- Remind yourself of your rates and boundaries
- Set an intention for the call
- Take 3 deep breaths before dialing

## Call Framework Structure (45-60 Minutes)

### Phase 1: Opening (3-5 Minutes)

**Purpose:** Build rapport, set agenda, establish authority

**Script Framework:**

"Hi [Name], great to meet you. Thanks for taking the time today.

Before we dive in, here's how I'd like to structure our call:
1. First, I'll learn about your project and goals
2. Then I'll share some relevant experience
3. And by the end, we'll define clear next steps

How does that sound?"

**Rapport Building (Authentic Only):**
- Reference something from their LinkedIn or company news
- Find a genuine connection point
- "I saw your recent post about [topic] — I've been following that trend too"

**Rule:** Never rush rapport. Never fake it. Authentic connection beats scripted small talk.

**The Agenda Setter Pattern:**
```
"Quick agenda from my side:
- I want to understand your project and what success looks like
- Share some relevant case studies
- Talk about how we might work together
- Leave with clear next steps

I'll keep an eye on time. Does 45 minutes work for you?"
```

This sets you as the leader. You control the call. You respect their time.

---

### Phase 2: Qualification & Needs Analysis (15-25 Minutes)

**Purpose:** Uncover pain, budget, timeline, decision process

**The Qualification Stack (Mandatory):**

1. **Project Context**
   - "What prompted this project now?"
   - "What happened that made you realize you needed this?"
   - "What have you tried before?"

2. **Pain Amplification**
   - "What's the cost of NOT doing this?"
   - "How does this problem affect your team/customers/revenue?"
   - "What's the biggest frustration with the current situation?"

3. **Success Definition**
   - "What does success look like 3 months after launch?"
   - "How will you measure ROI?"
   - "What would make this project a 10/10 for you?"

4. **Decision Process**
   - "Who else is involved in the decision?"
   - "What's your process for making decisions like this?"
   - "What would make you feel confident moving forward?"

5. **Budget & Timeline**
   - "Do you have a budget range for this project?"
   - "When do you need this live by?"
   - "What's driving that timeline?"

**Active Listening Techniques:**
- Take notes visibly ("Let me write that down")
- Paraphrase back to confirm understanding
- "So if I understand correctly, the main priority is..."
- Use silence: after they finish, wait 3 seconds before responding
- Ask one question at a time
- Don't interrupt, even if you know the answer

**The Pain Funnel Questions:**

| Level | Question Type | Example |
|-------|--------------|---------|
| Surface | Situation | "What's your current setup?" |
| Problem | Difficulty | "What's not working well?" |
| Impact | Consequence | "How does that affect revenue?" |
| Vision | Solution | "What would ideal look like?" |
| Value | Priority | "How much is solving this worth?" |

**Budget Qualification Scripts:**

"If you don't mind me asking, what budget did you have in mind for this project?"

"To make sure I'm recommending the right approach, do you have a range you're working with?"

"I've seen projects like this range from $5K to $50K depending on scope. What feels right for your situation?"

**The Budget Ladder:**
- If they say $2K, you propose a $5K solution
- If they say $5K, you propose a $10K solution
- If they say $10K, you propose a $20K+ solution
- Always offer a scaled-down option too

**Decision Authority Questions:**

"Besides yourself, who else will have input on this decision?"

"To help me understand your process — how did you make the last decision like this?"

"If we came up with a solution you loved, what would happen next?"

**Timeline Questions:**

"When does this need to be live?"

"What's driving that date?"

"If we could deliver faster, would that be valuable?"

### Phase 3: Presenting Your Solution (10-15 Minutes)

**Purpose:** Show you understand their problem and have a solution

**The Solution Presentation Structure:**

1. **Bridge Statement**
   "Based on what you've shared, here's what I'm hearing..."
   - Summarize their pain in 2-3 sentences
   - Show you were listening
   - Get confirmation: "Does that sound right?"

2. **Solution Overview**
   "Here's how I'd approach solving this..."
   - High-level approach, NOT technical details
   - Focus on outcomes, not features
   - Use analogies and stories

3. **Relevant Case Study**
   "I worked with a similar company that had this same challenge..."
   - Use the CARS framework (Challenge, Action, Result, Similarity)
   - Show concrete numbers and outcomes
   - Make it relatable to their situation

4. **Process Overview**
   "Here's how we'd work together..."
   - Phases: Discovery, Build, Launch, Support
   - Timeline overview
   - Communication cadence

5. **Risk Reversal**
   "And here's how I ensure you're protected..."
   - Milestone-based delivery
   - Regular check-ins
   - Money-back guarantees if applicable

**The CARS Case Study Framework:**

| Element | Description | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| Challenge | What was the problem? | "Their site was loading in 8 seconds" |
| Action | What did you do? | "We rebuilt the frontend stack" |
| Result | What happened? | "3 second load time, 40% more conversions" |
| Similarity | Why it's relevant | "They were also in e-commerce, same traffic" |

**Avoid These Presentation Mistakes:**
- Diving into technical architecture too early
- Using jargon they don't understand
- Overpromising or making guarantees you can't keep
- Showing every feature — only show what solves their problem
- Reading from slides or notes
- Going over time

**The 80/20 Rule of Presentations:**
- 80% about their problem and your understanding
- 20% about your solution
- Let them ask for details; don't volunteer everything

---

### Phase 4: Objection Handling (5-10 Minutes)

**Purpose:** Address concerns before they become deal-killers

**Common Objections & Responses:**

**"We need to think about it"**
"I understand. What specific concerns would you like to discuss? Often it helps to address those now so you have all the information."

**"Your price seems high"**
"Compared to what? Let me show you the ROI calculation..."

**"We're talking to other freelancers"**
"Great, you should compare. What criteria are you using to decide? I want to make sure you have everything you need to evaluate my proposal fairly."

**"We need a more detailed proposal first"**
"I'd be happy to put that together. Before I do, can I ask a few quick questions to make sure I'm pricing it right?"

**Timing:** Handle objections as they arise. Don't save them for the end.

**Objection Response Framework (LAIR):**
1. **Listen** — Let them finish. Don't interrupt.
2. **Acknowledge** — "That's a valid concern."
3. **Investigate** — "Can you tell me more about what worries you?"
4. **Respond** — Address the specific concern

---

### Phase 5: Closing & Next Steps (5-10 Minutes)

**Purpose:** Secure commitment to move forward

**The Close Sequence:**

1. **Summarize Value**
   "So just to recap what we discussed..."
   - Restate their problem
   - Restate your solution
   - Restate the value/ROI

2. **Ask for the Next Step**
   "Based on our conversation, I'd recommend we..."
   Option A: Send a proposal (standard)
   Option B: Schedule a deep-dive session (complex projects)
   Option C: Start with a paid discovery phase (uncertain projects)

3. **Set Timeline Expectations**
   "I can have the proposal to you by [day]."
   "When would you like to make a decision?"
   "I have availability starting [date]."

4. **Create Urgency (If Appropriate)**
   "I'm currently wrapping up a project, so I have bandwidth for the next two weeks."
   "After that, my next availability would be [date]."

5. **Confirm Next Steps**
   "So you'll expect my proposal by Wednesday, and we can review it together on Friday?"
   Get verbal commitment.

**The Assumptive Close:**
"Great, I'll start putting together the proposal. What's the best email to send it to?"

**The Choice Close:**
"Would you prefer Option A with the faster timeline, or Option B with the lower upfront cost?"

**The Calendar Close:**
"Let's schedule our follow-up call now. Does Tuesday or Thursday work better?"

**The Summary Close:**
"Let me make sure I have everything right..." (recap entire conversation)

## Post-Call Actions

### Immediate (Within 1 Hour)

1. **Send Follow-Up Email**
   - Thank them for their time
   - Summarize key discussion points
   - Restate next steps
   - Attach any promised materials

2. **Update CRM**
   - Log all notes from the call
   - Update lead status
   - Set follow-up reminder
   - Tag with relevant categories

3. **Debrief Yourself**
   - What went well?
   - What could improve?
   - Did you miss any key questions?
   - Rate the lead (Hot/Warm/Cold)

4. **Send Calendar Invite**
   - If next steps were agreed, send the invite immediately
   - Include agenda for next meeting

### Follow-Up (Within 24 Hours)

**Proposal Delivery:**
- If you promised a proposal, deliver it
- Track when they open it
- Follow up 48 hours after sending

**Warm Lead Nurturing:**
- Send relevant case study or article
- Connect on LinkedIn
- Add to email sequence

## Advanced Call Strategies

### The Reversal Technique
Instead of selling to them, make them sell to you. Start with:
"I'm not sure if this is the right fit for my current bandwidth."
"We're being selective about projects this quarter."
"Let me understand more before I commit to anything."
This positions you as high-demand and scarce.

### The Consultant Frame
Never sound like a salesperson. Sound like a consultant:
- "Let me help you think through this."
- "Here's what I've seen work for similar situations."
- "One thing to consider..."
- "Have you thought about...?"

### The Silence Power Move
After asking about budget, STAY SILENT. First person to speak loses. Wait them out. It feels uncomfortable. Do it anyway.

### The Pre-Frame
"We cover a range of budgets from $5K to $150K. I want to make sure I recommend the right approach for you regardless of where you fall."

### The Trial Close
Use throughout the call:
- "If we could solve X, would that be valuable?"
- "How does that sound so far?"
- "If we could deliver in that timeframe, would that work?"

### The Case Study Close
When they object, ask:
"Let me tell you about a client who had the same concern. They ended up seeing a 300% ROI within 3 months. Want to learn from their experience?"

## Call Script Templates

### The Validation Script
"I've reviewed your site and here's what I noticed...
1. Your load time is affecting conversions
2. Your mobile experience needs improvement
3. Your competitor is outperforming you on SEO

What's been your biggest challenge with these issues?"

### The Discovery Script
Walk through each phase methodically. Don't skip steps. Each phase builds on the last.

### The No-BS Script
"Before I invest time in a proposal, I need to understand a few things. Is there a budget allocated for this project? I don't want to waste your time or mine if we're not in the same ballpark."

### The ROI Script
"Let me make sure this makes financial sense. Based on what you shared, if this project generates [X] in additional revenue or saves [Y] in costs, the ROI would be [Z]. Does that math work for you?"

## Metrics to Track

| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| Call-to-Proposal Rate | 70%+ | Are you qualifying properly? |
| Proposal-to-Close Rate | 50%+ | Are you pricing and presenting well? |
| Average Call Length | 35-45 min | Too short = didn't qualify; too long = lost control |
| Follow-Up Response Rate | 80%+ | Are you providing value in follow-up? |
| Time to Close | < 14 days | Longer = lower close probability |

## Red Flags During Calls

| Red Flag | Action |
|----------|--------|
| They won't discuss budget | Don't send a proposal |
| They're not the decision maker | Ask to include decision maker |
| They've tried and failed before | Understand why, adjust approach |
| They want a proposal "just for comparison" | Qualify harder or walk away |
| They're rude or dismissive | Fire the client now |
| They don't know what they want | Propose paid discovery phase |
| Timeline is "yesterday" | Charge urgent premium |
| Scope is "just simple" | It's never simple |

## Energy Management

### Before the Call
- Stand up and move
- Smile before dialing (changes your voice)
- Review your last win
- Set an intention

### During the Call
- Sit up straight (improves confidence)
- Smile while speaking (warms tone)
- Match their energy level
- Use hand gestures even on video (adds emphasis)

### After the Call
- Take a 2-minute reset break
- Don't chain more than 3 calls
- Celebrate wins, learn from losses
- Review recording if you recorded it

## The Million-Dollar Mindset

Discovery calls are not about selling. They're about diagnosing. A doctor doesn't prescribe before diagnosing. Neither should you.

Your goal is not to convince them to hire you. Your goal is to determine if you can help them and if they are a client worth taking.

This shift — from seller to diagnostician — is what separates $50K freelancers from $500K freelancers.

Charge more. Qualify harder. Fire faster. Win bigger.
