# Pricing & Negotiation Call Script

**Duration:** 30-45 minutes
**Prerequisite:** Client has seen the proposal
**Goal:** Address concerns, handle objections, close the deal

---

## Pre-Call Preparation (15 minutes)

- [ ] Review proposal details (scope, pricing, timeline)
- [ ] Review discovery call notes (pain points, budget range, decision process)
- [ ] Identify 2-3 potential compromises you're willing to make
- [ ] Set your walk-away number (absolute minimum)
- [ ] Prepare 1-2 case studies for social proof
- [ ] Have alternative options ready (phased, reduced scope, payment terms)

**Your BATNA (Best Alternative To Negotiated Agreement):**
- Walk-away price: $[X]
- Minimum acceptable: $[X]
- Ideal close: $[X]

---

## Script Structure

---

### 0:00 — Opening (2 minutes)

> "Hey [Name], thanks for jumping on. I know you've had a chance to look over the proposal — I'm excited to walk through it together.
>
> **Plan for today:**
> 1. Quick recap of where we left off (5 min)
> 2. Walk through the proposal and answer questions (20 min)
> 3. Discuss next steps and how to move forward (5 min)
>
> Sound good?"

---

### 0:02 — Recap Alignment (3 minutes)

> "Just to make sure we're aligned — when we last spoke, you mentioned [key problem / goal]. Did I capture that correctly in the proposal?"

> "Is there anything that's changed since we last talked?"

**Confirm:**
- Problem is still the same
- Priorities haven't shifted
- Timeline is still valid

---

### 0:05 — Proposal Walkthrough (10 minutes)

> "Let me walk through the structure of the proposal quickly:"

**For each section, highlight the VALUE, not the features:**
- "We start with [Phase 1] which addresses [pain point] specifically"
- "You'll get [deliverable] by [date], which means [business impact]"
- "The total investment is $[X], which breaks down to [daily/weekly cost]"

> "What questions do you have about the approach?"

**Pause and let them respond.** Don't rush to the price.

---

### 0:15 — Price Discussion (10 minutes)

**When they ask about price (let them bring it up):**

> "As you saw, the total investment is $[X]. How does that compare to what you were expecting?"

**Common responses:**

**If they say "That's more than I expected":**
> "I understand. Can you tell me more about what you were expecting? That way I can see if there's a way to bridge the gap."

**If they say "That's within range" or no objection:**
> "Great. Does the pricing work with your budget? If so, I can send over the signature link right after this call."

**If they say "Can you do better on price?":**
> "I'm open to finding a solution that works for both of us. Let me ask — is the concern the total investment, or is it more about cash flow / budget approval?"

---

### 0:25 — Handling Negotiation (10 minutes)

**Acknowledge, Explore, Respond:**

> "I hear you. Let me make sure I understand the constraint — is it [budget / perceived value / approval / comparison]?"

**Then choose your response based on the real objection:**

#### If the concern is budget:
> "Here are a few options I can offer:"
>
> **Option A — Phased approach:** "We split the project into two phases. Phase 1 is $[X] and covers [core scope]. Phase 2 ($[X]) kicks off after you've seen results from Phase 1."
>
> **Option B — Adjusted scope:** "We can reduce the scope to [X core features], which brings the total to $[X]. We add the remaining features as a separate engagement later."
>
> **Option C — Payment flexibility:** "I can offer a payment plan — [X]% now, [X]% over [X] months."

#### If the concern is value:
> "I understand the skepticism. Let me share what a similar investment delivered for [Client Name]:"
> - "They invested $[X]"
> - "Within [timeframe], they saw [metric improvement]"
> - "That translated to $[X] in [revenue savings / new revenue]"
>
> "Based on your situation, I'd expect a similar ROI. By [timeframe], this project should have paid for itself."

#### If they say a competitor is cheaper:
> "I appreciate you being transparent. Can I ask what's included in their proposal?"
>
> "What I've found is that cheaper options often have hidden costs — longer timelines, less experienced teams, or missing scope. My proposal includes [differentiator 1], [differentiator 2], and [differentiator 3]."
>
> "At the end of the day, you get what you pay for. My clients consistently tell me the investment was worth it because [reason]."

#### If they stall / "I need to think about it":
> "Totally fair. What specific questions are still on your mind?"
>
> "Is there anything in the proposal that doesn't feel right?"
>
> "What would need to be true for you to feel confident moving forward?"

---

### 0:35 — Trial Close & Next Steps (5 minutes)

> "Based on everything we've discussed, does the [project/proposal] make sense for [Company]?"

**If yes:**
> "Great! I'll send the agreement via DocuSign right after this call. Once you sign and the deposit is processed, we can kick off on [date]. Sound good?"

**If not yet:**
> "What's the one thing that needs to happen for you to feel ready to move forward?"
>
> "Can I help clarify anything or provide additional information?"

**If no (firm pass):**
> "Totally understand. I appreciate you considering it. If anything changes, feel free to reach out."
> "One last thing — is there anyone else in your network who might benefit from this kind of solution?"

---

### 0:40 — Close

> "Thanks for the honest conversation, [Name]. I'll [action item: send revised proposal / send agreement / check back in X days]."

---

## Post-Call Actions (15 minutes)

- [ ] Send follow-up email within 2 hours
- [ ] If won: Send agreement for signature
- [ ] If revised: Send updated proposal with changes clearly marked
- [ ] If lost: Tag for future nurture and move on
- [ ] Update CRM with outcome and notes

## Negotiation Cheat Sheet

### Price Reduction Concessions (in order of preference)

| Concession | Impact on You | Impact on Client | Use When |
|-----------|---------------|------------------|----------|
| Adjust payment terms | Low (get paid same, just slower) | High (cash flow relief) | Budget constraint |
| Reduce scope | Medium (less work) | Medium (less value) | They can't afford full |
| Offer discount for upfront payment | Low (you get money sooner) | Medium (save X%) | Cash flow objection |
| Offer multi-project discount | Low (more total revenue) | High (savings + commitment) | Long-term potential |
| Reduce rate | High (devalues you) | High (savings) | Last resort — rarely worth it |

### What NOT to do

| Mistake | Why |
|---------|-----|
| Discount without something in return | Sets precedent that your price is flexible |
| Justify price with your costs | Client doesn't care about your costs — they care about value |
| Negotiate against yourself | Don't lower price before they push back |
| Say "our price is firm" with no flexibility | Can seem rigid — offer options instead |
| Give a range | They'll hear the low end |
| Accept first "no" | Often a test — push gently |

### Price Anchoring Scripts

> "Based on what you shared about the impact of this problem, I estimate it's costing you about $[X] per [month/year]. Our investment of $[X] pays for itself in [timeframe]."

> "Companies our size typically invest between $[X] and $[X] for this kind of solution. Our proposal comes in at $[X]."

> "I know $[X] sounds like a lot upfront. But consider what happens if you don't solve this — [cost of inaction]. This investment prevents that."

---

## Objection Quick Reference

| Objection | Redirect |
|-----------|----------|
| "It's too expensive" | "Compared to what? What's the cost of not solving this?" |
| "I need to think about it" | "What specific question is still unanswered?" |
| "I need to talk to my partner/team" | "Understood. What do you think they'll be most concerned about?" |
| "Can you do better?" | "I can adjust scope or terms. What would make this work for you?" |
| "We have a smaller budget" | "Let's see what we can do within that budget. What's most important to include?" |
| "We're not ready yet" | "What's the trigger that would make you ready?" |
| "Send me more info" | "I want to make sure I send the right info. What specifically would help?" |
