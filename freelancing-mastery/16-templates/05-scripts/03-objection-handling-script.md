# Objection Handling Scripts

**Core Framework:** LAER (Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond)

1. **L**isten — Let them finish. Don't interrupt.
2. **A**cknowledge — Validate their concern. "That's a fair concern."
3. **E**xplore — Ask questions to understand the real objection.
4. **R**espond — Address the specific concern with evidence.

---

## Objection 1: "It's too expensive / We don't have the budget"

**LAER Framework:**

> "I hear you. That's a significant investment." (Acknowledge)
>
> "Can I ask — when you say it's too expensive, is it the total number, or is it more about the timing / cash flow?" (Explore)

**If it's about total cost (value objection):**
> "I understand. Let me reframe this. Based on what you shared about [problem impact], this problem is costing you about $[X] per [month/year]. Our investment of $[X] is less than [X] months of that cost. So this is essentially an investment that pays for itself in [timeframe]."

**If it's about cash flow:**
> "I can work with you on payment terms. We could do [proposal options]:"
> - "3 monthly payments of $[X]"
> - "50% now, 50% in [X] months"
> - "A phased approach starting with $[X] for Phase 1"

**If they have a specific number in mind:**
> "If we were to work within a $[Y] budget, here's what we could do: [reduced scope]. We'd add the remaining features as a Phase 2 when budget allows. How does that sound?"

---

## Objection 2: "I need to think about it"

**LAER Framework:**

> "Of course — it's a big decision. I want to make sure you have everything you need to make the right call." (Acknowledge)
>
> "Can I ask what specifically is top of mind? Is there anything in the proposal that doesn't sit right?" (Explore)

**If they're genuinely uncertain:**
> "A few things that might help your thinking:"
> - "I can share a case study from a similar client"
> - "I can connect you with [reference client] for a quick chat"
> - "We could start with a small paid pilot — $[X] for [limited scope] — so you can see results before committing fully"

**If they're stalling:**
> "What would need to be true for you to feel confident moving forward?"
>
> "Is there a specific date you'd like to revisit this by?"

**If they're hoping you'll come down on price:**
> "If we can find a way to make the numbers work, would you be ready to move forward today?"

---

## Objection 3: "I need to talk to [partner/team/boss]"

**LAER Framework:**

> "Makes sense — I always want everyone involved to be aligned." (Acknowledge)
>
> "What do you think their main concerns will be?" (Explore)
>
> "Is there anything I can help prepare to make that conversation easier?" (Respond)

**Preparing them for the internal conversation:**
> "Here's a one-page summary you can share with them. It covers the key points: problem, solution, investment, and expected ROI."
>
> "Would it help if I joined the conversation? I'm happy to do a quick 15-minute call with your team."

**Common concerns from decision-makers:**
> "If they're worried about [cost/risk/timeline], here's what I'd suggest saying: [specific talking point]."

---

## Objection 4: "We're talking to other freelancers/agencies"

**LAER Framework:**

> "I'm glad you're being thorough. That's smart." (Acknowledge)
>
> "Can I ask what criteria you're using to compare?" (Explore)
>
> "What's most important to you in making this decision?" (Respond)

**Differentiate yourself:**
> "I understand. A few things that set me apart:"
> 1. **[Differentiator 1]:** [Evidence — case study, years, specialization]
> 2. **[Differentiator 2]:** [Evidence]
> 3. **[Differentiator 3]:** [Evidence]
>
> "Has anyone else on your list done [specific relevant achievement]?"

**Win by adding value:**
> "Regardless of who you choose, I'd like to share [insight / resource] that I think will be valuable for your project. [Specific insight]."

---

## Objection 5: "We can do it in-house / We'll build it ourselves"

**LAER Framework:**

> "I can see why you'd consider that. Having it on your team gives you more control." (Acknowledge)
>
> "What's your timeline for getting this done in-house? And who's available to work on it?" (Explore)

**Common responses:**
> "Often when I work with companies that initially plan to build in-house, they find that:"
> 1. "The opportunity cost of pulling engineers off core product work is higher than expected"
> 2. "The timeline stretches because of competing priorities"
> 3. "They lack specific expertise in [area], which leads to rework"
>
> "What if we partner on the initial build, and then your team takes over maintenance? That way you get the expertise now and ownership later."

---

## Objection 6: "Your timeline is too long"

**LAER Framework:**

> "I understand — speed matters. Let me explain why I suggested that timeline." (Acknowledge)
>
> "What's driving your deadline?" (Explore)

**If they need it faster:**
> "To hit a faster timeline, I could:"
> - "Add a second resource (additional cost: $[X])"
> - "Reduce scope to [core features] and deliver in [X] weeks"
> - "Work overtime/premium hours (additional cost: $[X])"
>
> "Which approach would work best for you?"

**If the timeline is realistic but uncomfortable:**
> "I've found that rushing leads to technical debt that costs more to fix later. I'd rather deliver quality on a realistic timeline than deliver mediocre work fast. Let me show you what goes into each phase..."

---

## Objection 7: "We're not ready yet / Timing isn't right"

**LAER Framework:**

> "I appreciate you being upfront about that." (Acknowledge)
>
> "What needs to happen between now and when you'd be ready?" (Explore)
>
> "What's the trigger that would make this a priority?" (Respond)

**If it's a timing issue:**
> "Totally understand. What if we scope out a plan now and revisit execution in [timeframe]? I can keep my calendar open."
>
> "In the meantime, here's a checklist of things you can prepare: [list of pre-work]."
>
> "I'll follow up in [timeframe]. If anything changes before then, feel free to reach out."

---

## Objection 8: "Your rate is higher than [Upwork/freelancer platform]"

**LAER Framework:**

> "I know there's a wide range of rates out there. Let me explain what my rate includes." (Acknowledge)
>
> "What's most important to you for this project — the lowest price, or the best outcome?" (Explore)

> "What you're paying for with my rate:"
> 1. "[X] years of specialized experience in [niche]"
> 2. "Proven results — [specific case study]"
> 3. "Reliability — I've never missed a deadline"
> 4. "Communication — daily updates, weekly calls"
> 5. "Quality — thorough testing, documentation, knowledge transfer"
>
> "When clients go with the cheapest option, they often end up spending more in the long run due to rework, delays, and communication issues. I've had clients come to me after a failed cheaper attempt."

---

## Objection 9: "I'm not sure I trust freelancers / I've had bad experiences"

**LAER Framework:**

> "I'm sorry you had that experience — unfortunately there are people who give freelancing a bad name." (Acknowledge)
>
> "Can I ask what specifically went wrong?" (Explore)

> "What I do differently:"
> 1. "Written scope of work — everything is documented upfront"
> 2. "Milestone-based payments — you only pay when milestones are met"
> 3. "Daily updates — full transparency on what I'm working on"
> 4. "References — you can talk to 3 past clients today"
> 5. "Money-back guarantee on the first milestone if you're not satisfied"
>
> "Would it help to start with a small paid trial — maybe [X] days / $[X] — so you can see how I work before committing to the full project?"

---

## Objection 10: "Send me a proposal and I'll get back to you"

**LAER Framework:**

> "Happy to put that together. To make sure it's the best proposal for you, can I ask a few quick questions?" (Acknowledge)
>
> "What's the timeline for your decision?" (Explore)
>
> "Is there a budget range I should keep in mind?" (Respond)

**Don't just send a proposal — get a commitment first:**
> "I'll have the proposal ready by [day]. How about we schedule a 20-minute walkthrough on [day] to review it together? That way I can answer any questions in real-time."

## Advanced Objection: The Silent Objection

Sometimes they don't voice the objection at all. Watch for:

| Sign | Hidden Objection |
|------|-----------------|
| "Send me more info" | Not convinced of value / price objection |
| Crickets after proposal | Indecision or price objection |
| "Can you add [X] for free?" | They think your price is too high |
| Delaying decisions | Risk aversion or not a priority |
| Asking for references | Trust objection |
| "Let me run it by legal" | Usually price or they found someone cheaper |

**How to surface silent objections:**
> "Based on our conversation, I sense there might be something holding you back. Can I ask what it is?"

> "Sometimes when clients go quiet, it means there's a concern they haven't voiced. Is there anything about the proposal that doesn't feel right?"

> "If you could wave a magic wand and change one thing about this proposal, what would it be?"

---

## Objection Response Cheat Sheet

| Objection | Best Response Strategy |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Too expensive | Reframe as investment vs. cost of inaction |
| Need to think | Get specific: "What's holding you back?" |
| Need to consult others | Arm them with a one-pager for their boss |
| Comparing competitors | Differentiate on value, not price |
| Building in-house | Opportunity cost argument |
| Timeline too long | Offer accelerated option (more $ or less scope) |
| Not ready yet | Set follow-up date + provide prep checklist |
| Rate too high | Itemize what's included + ROI proof |
| Bad past experience | Guarantees, references, milestone payments |
| "Send proposal" | Lock in a follow-up call before sending |

## Post-Objection Closing Scripts

**After handling the objection, trial close:**

> "I'm glad we talked through that. Does that address your concern?"

> "Is there anything else holding you back?"

> "If we can [resolve the concern], are you ready to move forward?"

> "So what would it take to get started today / this week?"

**If you've genuinely addressed all objections and they're still hesitant, it's probably a value issue or they're not a good fit. Walk away gracefully:**

> "I've shared what I believe is the best solution for your situation. If it's not the right time or fit, I completely understand. My door is always open."

> "I want this to be a good decision for you. If you're not 100% confident, it's better to wait than to regret. Let me know when you're ready."
