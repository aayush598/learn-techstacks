# Payment Terms: Getting Paid What You Deserve, On Time

## The Payment Structure Framework

Your payment terms are as important as your price. A $15,000 project paid over 6 months is worth less than a $15,000 project paid in 30 days. The structure affects your cash flow, risk, and leverage.

### The Payment Term Spectrum

| Payment Structure | Risk to You | Risk to Client | Cash Flow Impact |
|-----------------|------------|----------------|------------------|
| 100% upfront | None | High | Excellent |
| 50/50 (start + completion) | Low | Medium | Good |
| Milestone-based | Low-Medium | Low-Medium | Good |
| Monthly (retainer) | Low | Low | Predictable |
| Net 30 (after delivery) | Medium | None | Delayed |
| Net 60-90 | High | None | Poor |
| Post-launch payment | Very High | None | Dangerous |
| Equity/revenue share | Extreme | None | Speculative |

## Payment Structure 1: Upfront Deposits

### Why You Need a Deposit

| Deposit % | Client Commitment | Your Protection |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 0% | Low | None — you're financing the project |
| 25% | Moderate | Covers discovery phase |
| 33% | Good | Standard for most freelancers |
| 50% | Strong | Common for new clients |
| 100% | Full | Only for small projects |

### Recommended Deposit by Project Size

| Project Size | Recommended Deposit | Why |
|-------------|-------------------|-----|
| < $1,000 | 100% | Too small to invoice twice |
| $1,000 - $5,000 | 50% | Standard protection |
| $5,000 - $15,000 | 30-50% | Balance of protection and client comfort |
| $15,000 - $50,000 | 25-33% | Client may resist higher; milestone structure better |
| $50,000+ | 20-25% | Large projects need phased payments |

### Deposit Communication Scripts

**New client:**
"To get started, I require a [X]% deposit. This reserves your spot in my schedule and covers the initial discovery and planning phase."

**Client who resists:**
Client: "We don't pay deposits."
You: "I understand. For new clients, a deposit is standard practice. It ensures mutual commitment to the project. I offer two options:

1. [X]% deposit to begin, with the balance on delivery
2. If deposits are a policy issue, your accounting can pay Net 15 on all milestone invoices

Which works better?"

### When to Waive the Deposit

| Situation | Waive Deposit? | Alternative |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| Existing client (paid reliably) | Yes | Can waive or reduce |
| Referral from trusted source | Maybe | 25% instead of 50% |
| Enterprise client with PO system | Maybe | Net 30 instead of deposit |
| Government agency | Usually | They often can't prepay |
| Friend/family | No | Actually, ESPECIALLY charge friends |

**Rule**: The less established the relationship, the higher the deposit.

## Payment Structure 2: Milestone Payments

### Why Milestones Work

Milestones align payment with progress. Both parties see value delivered before the next payment is due.

### Ideal Milestone Structure

**For a $15,000, 8-week project:**

| Milestone | % | Amount | Trigger | Timeline |
|-----------|---|--------|---------|----------|
| 1: Kickoff | 30% | $4,500 | Contract signed | Week 1 |
| 2: Design approval | 25% | $3,750 | Design sign-off | Week 3 |
| 3: Development complete | 25% | $3,750 | Feature complete | Week 6 |
| 4: Launch | 20% | $3,000 | Final acceptance | Week 8+ |

### Milestone Best Practices

1. **Keep milestones 2-4 weeks apart** — don't go more than 4 weeks without payment
2. **Make milestones deliverable-based** — "when X is approved," not "on X date"
3. **Include a final holdback (10-20%)** — ensures final acceptance
4. **Never let the balance exceed the remaining work** — at any point, you should be ahead
5. **Keep milestone amounts meaningful** — at least 20% each

### Without a deposit, the payment schedule should be shorter:

| Weeks | Milestone | Payment |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| 1-2 | Discovery complete | $3,000 |
| 3-4 | Design complete | $3,000 |
| 5-6 | Development sprint 1 | $3,000 |
| 7-8 | Development sprint 2 | $3,000 |
| 9-10 | Testing & launch | $3,000 |

## Payment Structure 3: Retainers

### Monthly Retainer Structure

| Retainer Type | Hours/Month | Price | Best For |
|--------------|-------------|-------|----------|
| Basic | 5-10 | $X | Bug fixes, minor updates |
| Standard | 15-20 | $X | Ongoing development |
| Premium | 30-40 | $X | Dedicated support + features |

### Retainer Payment Terms

- **Billed in advance**: Payment due on the 1st of each month
- **Minimum commitment**: 3 months recommended
- **Unused hours**: Roll over up to [X] months, or use-it-or-lose-it
- **Overage**: Additional hours billed at [1.5x] retainer rate

### Retainer Contract Clause

```
MONTHLY RETAINER AGREEMENT

Term: Minimum [3] months, then month-to-month
Billing: $[X]/month, invoiced on the 1st, due Net 15
Included hours: [X] hours/month
Overage rate: $[X]/hour (billable in 30-min increments)
Unused hours: Roll over up to [X] months
Scope of retainer: [List of included services]
Exclusions: [List of excluded services]
Notice period: [30] days to cancel
```

## Payment Structure 4: Net Terms

### Net Terms Explained

| Term | Meaning | Best For |
|------|---------|----------|
| Net 7 | Due in 7 days | Small clients, individuals |
| Net 15 | Due in 15 days | Most clients |
| Net 30 | Due in 30 days | Enterprise clients |
| Net 45 | Due in 45 days | Large enterprises |
| Net 60+ | Due in 60+ days | Avoid unless necessary |

### Net Terms Strategy

| Client Type | Default Terms | Stretch Goal |
|-------------|--------------|--------------|
| Individual / small biz | Net 7-15 | Net 7 |
| Mid-market | Net 15-30 | Net 15 |
| Enterprise | Net 30-45 | Net 30, fight for Net 15 |
| Government | Net 30-60 | Net 30 (may not be negotiable) |

### Net Terms Script

"My standard payment terms are Net 15. For enterprise clients, I can accommodate Net 30."

## Late Payment Protection

### Late Fee Policy

| Policy | Amount | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Grace period | 5-7 days after due date | Standard courtesy |
| Late fee | 1.5% per month (18% APR) | Common in freelance contracts |
| Work pause | After 15-30 days overdue | Most effective leverage |
| Collections | After 60-90 days | Last resort |

### Late Payment Communication Sequence

**Day 1 (due date):**
"Just a friendly reminder that invoice #123 for $X is due today. Let me know if you need any clarification."

**Day 7 (grace period ends):**
"Following up on invoice #123 for $X, which is now [X] days overdue. Please remit payment at your earliest convenience."

**Day 15 (late fee + work pause):**
"Per our contract, a [X]% late fee has been applied to invoice #123. Total now due: $[X]. Please note that work on this project will pause until payment is received."

**Day 30 (escalation):**
"This is a formal notice that invoice #123 for $X is [X] days overdue. If payment is not received within 7 days, I will escalate to [collections / legal action]."

**Day 60 (final — collections):**
"This is my final attempt to resolve this payment. Your invoice of $X is now 60 days overdue. I am turning this over to [collections agency / small claims court]."

### Work Pause Script

"Per our contract, I am pausing work on this project until all overdue invoices are paid. I will resume work within 24 hours of receiving payment. I look forward to getting back to it."

## Escrow and Platform Payments

### When to Use Escrow

| Platform | Fee | Protection | Best For |
|----------|-----|------------|----------|
| Upwork | 5-20% | Payment protection | Small projects on Upwork |
| Escrow.com | 3-5% | Full escrow | Large projects ($10k+) |
| Toptal | None (on Toptal) | Payment guarantee | Toptal projects |
| Direct (invoice) | Varies | Your contract | Repeat clients |

### Escrow Communication

"For projects over $X, I recommend using [Escrow.com / platform]. This protects both of us — you know your payment is safe until you're satisfied, and I know the funds are secured before I start."

## Currency and Exchange Rates

### For International Clients

| Issue | Solution |
|-------|----------|
| Currency volatility | Price in your currency, not theirs |
| Transfer fees | Use Wise (TransferWise) for low fees |
| Exchange rate fluctuations | Set a rate lock period (e.g., 14 days) |
| Different timezones | Bill in USD/EUR if you're in those regions |

### International Payment Script

"I invoice in [USD/EUR]. Payment can be made via [Wise / PayPal / wire transfer]. If exchange rates fluctuate significantly before payment, we may need to adjust the amount to reflect the original value."

## Payment Term Negotiation

### What Clients Ask For

| Client Request | Your Response |
|----------------|--------------|
| "We pay Net 60" | "I can do Net 30 for the first invoice, then Net 60 ongoing. Or Net 60 with a [5]% surcharge to offset the delay." |
| "We only pay after launch" | "I need to be paid for work as it's delivered. Let's use milestone payments: 25% at each of 4 milestones." |
| "Can we do 10% upfront instead of 50%?" | "I can adjust the upfront to 25%, but I'll need to add more milestones to keep the payment intervals short." |
| "We need a discount for early payment" | Good request! "I can offer 5% off if you pay 100% upfront." |
| "We pay on receipt of PO" | "I accept POs. Please issue the PO for the full project amount before I begin." |

### Discount for Early/Upfront Payment

Offer a discount for better terms:

- "If you pay 100% upfront, I'll give you a 5% discount."
- "If you set up automatic monthly payments, I'll reduce the retainer by 10%."
- "If you pay within 7 days, I'll waive the [setup fee / onboarding fee]."

|-------------|--------------------------|
| Small project (<$2k) | 100% upfront |

## Payment Term Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It's Dangerous | Response |
|----------|-------------------|----------|
| "We'll pay you when the project is done" | You've financed their project for weeks/months | "I need milestone payments to cover costs during the project." |
| "Our standard is Net 90" | You'll wait 3+ months for payment | "I can do Net 30 for the first two invoices and then revisit." |
| "Can you start without a contract?" | No legal protection if they don't pay | "I need a signed agreement before I can begin any work." |
| "We're between funding rounds" | They may run out of money | "I'll start when funding is secured, with a 50% deposit." |
| "Payments are handled by a different department" | You'll have no direct payment contact | "Please loop in the right contact so I can send invoices to the right place." |
| "Our accounting system is complex" | Expect slow, bureaucratic payment | "I understand. I can work within your system, but Net 30 terms are important." |

## Getting Paid: The Ultimate Checklist

### Before Starting Work

- [ ] Signed contract with clear payment terms
- [ ] Deposit received (if required)
- [ ] Client's billing contact confirmed
- [ ] Invoice template ready
- [ ] Payment methods confirmed
- [ ] Late fee policy communicated

### During Project

- [ ] Invoice sent immediately upon milestone completion
- [ ] Payment tracked in system
- [ ] Follow-up sent on due date
- [ ] Late fee applied after grace period
- [ ] Work paused if payment exceeds [X] days overdue

### After Project

- [ ] Final invoice sent
- [ ] Final payment received
- [ ] All receipts archived
- [ ] Payment history logged for future reference
- [ ] Client classified as "paid on time" or "slow payer"
