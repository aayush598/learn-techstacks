# Handling Objections: "Too Expensive," "Budget Constraints," "Need to Think"

## The Objection Resolution Framework

Every objection follows a pattern. Master this pattern, and no objection can stop you.

### The Objection Resolution Formula

```
Objection → Validate → Probe → Address → Confirm → Close
```

### The 5-Step Framework

| Step | What You Say | Goal |
|------|-------------|------|
| 1. Validate | "I understand why you feel that way." | Build rapport, show empathy |
| 2. Probe | "Can you tell me more about what specifically concerns you?" | Find the root cause |
| 3. Address | "Here's how I typically handle that concern..." | Solve the objection |
| 4. Confirm | "Does that address your concern?" | Verify resolution |
| 5. Close | "Great, then let's move forward with..." | Proceed to close |

## Objection 1: "It's Too Expensive"

### Why They Say It

| Root Cause | Frequency | What They Really Mean |
|------------|-----------|----------------------|
| Don't see value | 50% | "I don't understand why this is worth that much" |
| Can't afford it | 25% | "I legitimately don't have that much budget" |
| Testing you | 15% | "I want to see if you'll negotiate" |
| Comparative shopping | 10% | "Someone else quoted less" |

### How to Respond

**Step 1: Validate**
"I completely understand — this is a significant investment. Most of my clients feel that way when they first see the price."

**Step 2: Probe**
"Can I ask — is this more than you were expecting, or does it just feel like a lot for what you had in mind?"

**Step 3: Address (choose one)**

**If they don't see value:**
"Let me help you see the value more clearly. Your current situation costs you:
- $[X] in [lost revenue / wasted time / overhead]
- $[X] in [opportunity cost]
Total: $[X] per [month/year]

My solution delivers [specific outcome], which is worth $[Y] to your business. The investment of $[Z] pays for itself in [timeframe]."

**If they can't afford it:**
"What budget were you working with? Let me see what we can accomplish within that range."

**If they're testing:**
"I don't negotiate on price, but I can adjust scope. Here are two options at different investment levels..."

**If they have a cheaper quote:**
"You can definitely find someone cheaper. Here's what you typically get at that price point vs. working with me..."

**Step 4: Confirm**
"Does that help you see the value differently?"

**Step 5: Close**
"Great. Let's proceed with [option]."

### Script Bank

**The ROI Script:**
"I understand the price seems high. Let me ask — what's it costing you not to solve this problem? If we look at it as an investment that pays for itself in [X] months, does the price make more sense?"

**The Scope Reduction Script:**
"Let me see if I can help. What's the most important outcome you need? If I focus on just that, I can reduce the scope and the price. We can always add more later."

**The Phased Script:**
"We could split this into phases. Phase 1 covers the essentials at $X, and you see results before committing to Phase 2 at $Y."

**The "No Discount" Script:**
"I don't discount my work, but I care about making this work for you. Instead of lowering the price, let me show you how we can adjust the scope or timeline to fit your budget."

### What NOT to Say

❌ "I can lower it to $X." (You've revealed your bottom)
❌ "How much were you thinking?" (You let them anchor)
❌ "That's my best price." (Weak, defensive)
❌ "I have overhead to cover." (Not their problem)
❌ "Everyone else charges $X." (Appeals to authority — weak)

## Objection 2: "We Don't Have the Budget"

### Why They Say It

| Root Cause | What They Mean | Your Response |
|------------|---------------|--------------|
| True budget constraint | "We genuinely have $X allocated" | Offer scope reduction |
| Low priority | "This isn't important enough" | Show cost of inaction |
| Needs approval | "I need to convince my boss" | Help them build the case |
| Testing seriousness | "I want to see if you're flexible" | Hold firm, offer options |
| Fear of commitment | "I'm not sure about this" | Reduce risk, trial period |

### How to Respond

**Step 1: Validate**
"I understand budget constraints — every business has them."

**Step 2: Probe**
"What budget range have you allocated for this project? Maybe I can tailor a solution that fits."

**Step 3: Address**

**If they have a specific number:**
"Okay, for $X I can deliver [core features]. Here's what that includes..."

**If they don't have a number:**
"What would need to be true for this to be a priority for your budget?"

**If they need internal approval:**
"Can I help you build the case for your manager? I can provide a one-page ROI summary."

**If they're testing:**
"I understand. Let me suggest we start with a small pilot project to demonstrate value, and then revisit the full budget."

**Step 4: Confirm**
"Does that approach work within your budget constraints?"

**Step 5: Close**
"Great. Let me send the revised SOW."

### The "No Budget" Script

Client: "We'd love to work with you, but we have no budget for this right now."

You: "I understand completely. A few options:

1. Can we start with a paid pilot at $[X] to show value?
2. Can I put together a proposal for next quarter's budget?
3. Can we do a phased approach starting with the most critical piece?

If none of those work, I'll check back in [month]. In the meantime, I'll send you a case study relevant to your situation."

### The "Budget Freeze" Script

Client: "We have a hiring freeze / budget freeze."

You: "I understand those constraints. Here's what other clients in your situation have done:

1. Hire me as a contractor (not employee) so it's not a headcount issue
2. Use operational budget instead of capital budget
3. Start with a small, self-funded pilot from the department budget

Would any of those approaches work?"

## Objection 3: "I Need to Think About It"

### Why They Say It

| Root Cause | Verbal Cue | Your Response |
|------------|-----------|--------------|
| Not convinced | "Let me think about it" | Probe for real concern |
| Needs approval | "I need to check with my partner" | Offer to join the conversation |
| Information overload | "Let me digest this" | Send summary, schedule follow-up |
| Fear of wrong decision | "I want to be sure" | Offer guarantee, smaller commitment |
| Avoidance | "I'll get back to you" | Set specific follow-up date |

### How to Respond

**Step 1: Validate**
"Absolutely, it's a big decision. I'd never want you to rush into it."

**Step 2: Probe**
"What specifically do you want to think about? Maybe I can help clarify."

**Step 3: Address**

**If they're not convinced:**
"What's the main thing giving you pause? If I could address that, would you be ready to move forward?"

**If they need approval:**
"Would it help if I joined that conversation? I can answer technical questions directly."

**If information overload:**
"Let me send you a one-page summary covering the key points. We can talk again on [day]."

**If fear of wrong decision:**
"I understand. Let me make this risk-free for you: [risk reversal offer]. Does that help you feel more comfortable?"

**Step 4: Confirm**
"If I [address concern], would you be comfortable moving forward?"

**Step 5: Close**
"Great. Let's schedule that conversation for [date]."

### The "Think About It" Script

```
Client: "I need to think about it."

You: "I understand. Can I ask — what specifically do you want to think about?"

Client: "I just want to make sure it's the right decision."

You: "What would need to be true for you to feel confident it's the right decision?"

Client: [Reveals real concern]

You: "If I could address that concern, would you be ready to proceed?"
```

### The Time-Bound "Think About It"

If they're genuinely going to think, set a specific follow-up:

"When should I check back with you? How about [day and time]? That way I'm not bothering you — I'll just follow up then."

This gives you permission to follow up and keeps the conversation alive.

## Objection 4: "We Have a Cheaper Quote"

### Why They Say It

| Root Cause | What They Mean |
|------------|---------------|
| Legitimate lower quote | "Someone is cheaper" |
| Leverage for negotiation | "I want you to match or beat it" |
| Value comparison | "Help me understand why you're more" |
| Misunderstanding scope | "They quoted less for less work" |

### How to Respond

**Step 1: Validate**
"I understand there are cheaper options. You should explore all your options — it's a smart business decision."

**Step 2: Probe**
"Can I ask what their proposal includes? I want to make sure we're comparing the same scope."

**Step 3: Address**

**If they're comparing different scope:**
"I see — their quote covers [less scope]. My quote includes [more scope], which is why the price is different. Here's a side-by-side comparison..."

**If they're comparing different quality:**
"You can definitely get a cheaper solution. Here's what you typically get at that price point: [limitations]. And here's what my solution provides: [benefits]. It depends on which is more important to you."

**If they're leveraging for negotiation:**
"I don't match competitor pricing — I compete on value, not price. If price is the deciding factor, their option might be better for you. If quality and reliability matter more, let's talk about what makes my approach different."

**Step 4: Confirm**
"Does that comparison help you understand the difference?"

**Step 5: Close**
"If value and quality are your priorities, I'd love to work with you. Let's move forward."

### The "Cheaper Competitor" Script

```
Client: "We have a quote for $5,000 less than yours."

You: "That's a significant difference. Can I ask — what does their $5,000 less get you?"

Client: "They said they can build the same thing."

You: "I'd be careful with that. In my experience, 'the same thing' can mean very different things. Let me share a story about a client who chose a cheaper option and what happened..."

[Share a relevant case study or cautionary tale]

"If you want, I can provide references from clients who've chosen me over cheaper options. They can tell you why it was worth it."
```

## Objection 5: "We Need to Discuss Internally"

### Why They Say It

| Root Cause | What They Mean |
|------------|---------------|
| Multiple stakeholders | "Others need to approve" |
| CYA — covering liability | "I don't want to decide alone" |
| Partner/boss gatekeeper | "I'm not the decision-maker" |
| Delay tactic | "I'm not ready to decide" |

### How to Respond

**Step 1: Validate**
"Of course — important decisions should involve the right people."

**Step 2: Probe**
"Who else needs to be involved in the decision? What do they typically care about — budget, timeline, technical details?"

**Step 3: Address**

**Provide a decision-maker packet:**
"Let me prepare a one-page summary you can share with your team. It covers the key points: investment, ROI, timeline, and risks. I can also join a call with them if that would help."

**Understand their criteria:**
"What would your [partner/boss] need to see to feel good about this investment?"

**Offer a trial:**
"To make it easy for your team to say yes, let's start with a paid pilot project. Low risk, quick results, and they can see the value firsthand."

**Step 4: Confirm**
"Would a one-pager and a call with your team help move this forward?"

**Step 5: Close**
"Great. I'll send the one-pager by [day]. Let's schedule a call with your team for [date]."

### The Internal Champion Script

"Since you're the internal champion for this project, let me give you everything you need to sell this internally:

1. One-page ROI summary
2. Case study from a similar company
3. Risk reversal guarantee
4. Reference contact

Would it help if I joined the internal call to answer technical questions?"

## Objection 6: "We're Not Ready Yet"

### Why They Say It

| Root Cause | What They Mean |
|------------|---------------|
| Too early | "We're still exploring" |
| Not urgent | "This isn't a priority right now" |
| Fear | "We're afraid to commit" |
| Process | "We have a Q4 planning cycle" |

### How to Respond

**Step 1: Validate**
"I understand — timing is everything."

**Step 2: Probe**
"What needs to happen for you to be ready? Is it a specific date, a budget cycle, or just wanting to be more certain?"

**Step 3: Address**

**If it's too early:**
"When do you expect to make a decision? I'll check back then."

**If no urgency:**
"I understand this isn't urgent now. Let me stay in touch and check back in [month]. In the meantime, I'll send you relevant case studies."

**If fear:**
"Let me make this easy. I'll give you a free 30-minute consultation to clarify what you need. No obligation — just value."

**Step 4: Confirm**
"So if I check back in [date], that works?"

**Step 5: Close**
"Perfect. I'll follow up then. In the meantime, here's something relevant to read..."

## Objection 7: "We Need to See a Proposal First"

### Why They Say It

| Root Cause | What They Mean |
|------------|---------------|
| Process | "Our procurement requires written quotes" |
| Indecision | "I'm not sure yet" |
| Comparison shopping | "I want to compare options" |

### How to Respond

If you haven't sent a proposal yet:

"I'd be happy to send a proposal. Before I do, can I ask a few quick questions to make sure it's accurate? [Ask about budget, timeline, decision process]."

This prevents you from doing free work and getting ghosted.

## Objection 8: "You Don't Have Experience in Our Industry"

### How to Respond

"You're right that most of my work has been in [adjacent industry], not specifically in [their industry]. Here's why I'm confident I can deliver:

1. The technical challenges are identical — [specific commonality]
2. I've solved [similar problem] for [similar company] with great results
3. I'll invest [X] hours in your industry upfront to come up to speed
4. I always work closely with your team who has the domain expertise

Let me show you a case study from a similar project that demonstrates the relevant skills."

---

## Objection 9: "We Want to Start Small"

### How to Respond

"That's a smart approach. Let's define a small initial project that:

1. Solves a real problem (so you see value quickly)
2. Takes no more than [X] weeks
3. Costs no more than $[X]

If it goes well, we expand. If not, you've only invested a small amount."

---

## Objection 10: "We'll Let You Know" (The Ghost)

### How to Respond

Set a specific follow-up before you end the conversation:

"I understand. When should I check back with you? I'll reach out on [date] if I don't hear from you before then."

If they ghost, use the breakup email sequence from the follow-up section.

## The Ultimate Objection Handling Script

### Universal Template

```
Client: "[Objection]"

You: "I understand [validate]. Can I ask you a question about that? [probe]"

[Client responds]

You: "That makes sense. Here's something to consider: [address]."

[Client responds]

You: "Does that address your concern? [confirm]"

[Client: "Yes"]

You: "Great. Then shall we move forward with [close]?"
```
