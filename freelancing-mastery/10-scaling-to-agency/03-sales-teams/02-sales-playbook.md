# The Agency Sales Playbook: Scripts, Objections, and Closing

## The Sales Philosophy

Selling services is not about convincing people to buy something they do not need. It is about helping qualified buyers make the right decision. You are a trusted advisor, not a pushy salesperson.

Your goal is to disqualify bad fits quickly and make it easy for good fits to say yes.

## The Complete Sales Process

1. **Prospecting** → Finding potential clients
2. **Qualification** → Determining if they are a good fit
3. **Discovery** → Understanding their needs deeply
4. **Proposal** → Presenting your solution and price
5. **Objection Handling** → Addressing concerns
6. **Closing** → Getting the yes
7. **Onboarding** → Setting up for success

## Stage 1: Prospecting

### Ideal Client Profile (ICP)

Before you prospect, define exactly who you are looking for:

**Company attributes**:
- Industry: [specific industries]
- Size: [number of employees or revenue]
- Location: [geographic focus if any]
- Tech stack: [relevant technologies]

**Pain points**:
- [Pain point 1: e.g., outdated website]
- [Pain point 2: e.g., slow load times]
- [Pain point 3: e.g., low conversion rates]

**Decision maker**:
- Title: [CEO, CTO, Marketing Director, etc.]
- Authority: [Can they approve $5k–$50k?]
- Urgency: [How soon do they need to solve this?]

**Firmographic triggers**:
- Just raised funding
- Recently hired for related role
- New product launch
- Competitor just launched something
- Growing fast (hiring, expanding)

### Prospecting Channels

**1. LinkedIn Outreach**

Profile optimization:
- Professional headshot
- Headline that states what you do for clients (not what you do)
- Featured section with case studies
- Recommendations from clients

Connection request template:
```
Hi [Name], I've been following [Company] and was impressed by [specific thing]. I help [ICP] achieve [result]. Would love to connect.
```

First message after connection:
```
Thanks for connecting, [Name]. Quick question — are you guys currently [handling X in-house / struggling with Y]?

If so, I'd be happy to share how we helped [similar company] achieve [specific result]. No pitch, just wanted to see if it's relevant.
```

**2. Email Outreach**

Subject line templates:
- `[Company] + [Result]`
- `Quick thought re: [Specific thing on their site]`
- `[Mutual connection] suggested I reach out`

Email template:
```
Hi [Name],

[Personalized observation about their business — not generic]

We help [ICP] achieve [specific result]. For example, we recently worked with [Client Name] and [specific outcome with numbers].

Would you be open to a 15-minute call to see if we can do something similar for [Company]?

Best,
[Your name]
```

**3. Referral Requests**

The highest-converting prospecting channel.

Referral request template:
```
Hi [Past Client],

Thanks again for the opportunity to work on [Project]. I'm glad it went well.

I'm looking to work with more clients like you. Do you know 2-3 people in your network who might benefit from similar work?

I'd be happy to offer you [discount/referral fee] for any referral that turns into a project.

Thanks,
[Your name]
```

**4. Content Marketing**

Write content that attracts your ICP:
- Case studies with specific numbers
- How-to guides for their problems
- Industry analysis
- Comparison posts

Content distribution:
- LinkedIn articles
- Medium
- Your blog
- Industry publications

## Stage 2: Qualification

### The BANT Framework

Use BANT to qualify leads quickly:

**B**udget: Do they have budget allocated?
**A**uthority: Are you talking to the decision maker?
**N**eed: Is there a genuine need?
**T**imeline: When do they need it done?

### Qualification Questions

Ask these in the first conversation:

- "What prompted you to reach out / look into this?"
- "What have you tried so far to solve this?"
- "What is your budget range for this project?"
- "Who else is involved in the decision?"
- "What is your ideal timeline?"
- "What happens if you do not solve this?"

### Red Flags to Disqualify

Disqualify immediately if:
- **No budget**: They want it for free or way below your minimum
- **No authority**: They need to "run it by" someone you cannot talk to
- **No urgency**: "Maybe next year" means never
- **Unrealistic expectations**: Want everything for nothing
- **Multiple vendors**: They are price shopping 10 agencies
- **Bad fit**: Your services do not actually solve their problem

### The 80/20 Rule for Proposals

Only send proposals to 20% of leads. The other 80% should be disqualified or nurtured. If you send proposals to everyone, you are wasting time on people who will never buy.

## Stage 3: Discovery

### The Discovery Call Structure

**Before the call**:
- Research the company (website, social, competitors)
- Review any information they provided
- Prepare 3-5 specific questions

**Call structure (45 minutes)**:

**0-5 min: Rapport and agenda**
- "Thanks for your time. Here is what I want to cover today..."
- "My goal is to understand your situation and see if we can help."
- "I'll leave 10 minutes at the end for questions about us."

**5-25 min: Problem exploration**
- "Tell me about your current situation with [topic]."
- "What is working well? What is not?"
- "How long has this been a challenge?"
- "What have you tried so far?"
- "What is the impact of this problem on your business?"

**25-35 min: Vision creation**
- "If you could wave a magic wand, what would the ideal outcome look like?"
- "What would that mean for your team/business?"
- "How would you measure success?"
- "What would that be worth to you?"

**35-45 min: Next steps**
- "Based on what you have shared, here is how we might help..."
- "I would like to put together a proposal. Is that fair?"
- "What questions do you have about us?"
- "When would you like to move forward?"

### Discovery Questions by Category

**Business questions**:
- "What are your revenue goals for the next 12 months?"
- "How does this project fit into your broader strategy?"
- "Who are your main competitors and how do you compare?"

**Technical questions**:
- "What is your current tech stack?"
- "What tools are you using for [specific function]?"
- "What are the technical constraints we need to work within?"

**Personal questions**:
- "What keeps you up at night about this?"
- "What would make this a home run for you personally?"
- "What is your biggest fear about this project?"

### Discovery Call Checklist

After every discovery call, you should know:
- [ ] What is their current situation
- [ ] What is their desired outcome
- [ ] What is the gap between current and desired
- [ ] What is the financial impact of solving the problem
- [ ] What is the cost of not solving it
- [ ] Who is involved in the decision
- [ ] What is their budget range
- [ ] What is their timeline
- [ ] What are their objections or concerns

## Stage 4: Proposal

### Proposal Structure

**1. Executive Summary** (1 paragraph)
- Their situation and desired outcome
- Your proposed solution at a high level
- The investment and timeline

**2. Their Situation** (2-3 paragraphs)
- Demonstrate you understand their problem
- Reflect their language and priorities
- Show empathy for their challenges

**3. Our Solution** (detailed)
- What you will deliver
- How you will deliver it
- Timeline and milestones
- Who will be on their team

**4. Results and ROI** (specific numbers)
- Quantified outcomes they can expect
- Industry benchmarks
- ROI calculation

**5. Investment** (pricing)
- Total investment
- Payment terms
- What is included (and what is not)
- Payment schedule

**6. Why Us** (differentiation)
- Relevant case studies (2-3)
- Client testimonials
- Your unique approach

**7. Next Steps** (clear call to action)
- How to accept the proposal
- Timeline for decision
- What happens after acceptance

### Proposal Pricing Strategies

**Fixed price**:
- Best for: Well-defined scope
- Pros: Client likes certainty, easy to compare
- Cons: You bear scope risk

**Time and materials**:
- Best for: Open-ended or evolving scope
- Pros: You get paid for all work
- Cons: Client bears uncertainty

**Value-based pricing**:
- Best for: High-ROI projects
- Pros: Higher margins, aligned incentives
- Cons: Harder to sell, requires trust

**Tiered pricing**:
- Best for: Clients who need options
- Structure: Good, Better, Best
- Pros: Most clients choose the middle option
- Cons: More complex proposal

**Tiered pricing example**:
```
Basic: $10k — Core features, 4-week delivery
Professional: $20k — Core features + analytics + design, 6-week delivery
Enterprise: $35k — Everything + ongoing support + dedicated team
```

### Proposal Tips

- Send the proposal within 48 hours of the discovery call
- Make it visual (charts, mockups, screenshots)
- Keep it concise (5-10 pages max)
- Lead with value, not features
- Include specific, quantified outcomes
- Make it easy to say yes (clear next step)

## Stage 5: Objection Handling

### Common Objections and Responses

**Objection: "It is too expensive."**

Response: "I understand budget is a concern. Let me ask — what is the cost of NOT solving this problem? [Based on our conversation, you are losing $X/month due to this issue. Our solution pays for itself in Y months.] Can we look at this as an investment rather than an expense?"

**Objection: "We need to think about it."**

Response: "Of course. What specifically are you thinking about? [Is it the scope, the price, the timeline?] Perhaps I can help clarify. What would make you feel confident moving forward?"

**Objection: "We are talking to other agencies."**

Response: "That makes sense. What are you looking for that you have not found yet? I want to be transparent — if we are not the best fit, I will tell you. But if we are, I want you to feel confident choosing us."

**Objection: "We need to check with [boss/partner/board]."**

Response: "I understand. Can we set up a call where I present to them as well? I find it helps to have me answer their questions directly."

**Objection: "Your timeline is too long."**

Response: "I hear you. We could accelerate the timeline, but it would require [additional resources / reduced scope / higher cost]. Would you like me to put together an expedited option?"

**Objection: "We can do this in-house."**

Response: "You certainly can. Our value is [speed / expertise / specific outcome]. If you do it in-house, what is the opportunity cost of your team not working on [their core function]? We can typically deliver in [X weeks] versus [Y months] internally."

**Objection: "The economy is uncertain."**

Response: "I hear that from many clients. The companies that invest in growth during uncertainty come out ahead. Our solution is designed to [improve efficiency / reduce costs / drive revenue]. Can we structure this in a way that minimizes your risk?"

### The LAER Objection Handling Framework

**L**isten: Let them fully express the objection. Do not interrupt.
**A**cknowledge: Validate their concern. "I understand why you would feel that way."
**E**xplore: Ask questions to understand the root concern.
**R**espond: Address the specific concern with evidence and logic.

## Stage 6: Closing

### Closing Techniques

**The Assumptive Close**
Act as if they have already decided. "Great, let me send you the contract. Would you prefer monthly or quarterly billing?"

**The Alternative Close**
Give them a choice between two options, both of which involve moving forward. "Would you prefer the Basic or Professional package?"

**The Urgency Close**
Create legitimate urgency. "We have capacity to start in 2 weeks. If we wait until next month, our team is booked."

**The Summary Close**
Summarize the value you have discussed. "We have talked about how we can [outcome A], [outcome B], and [outcome C]. Does that sound like the right solution for you?"

**The Trial Close**
Test their readiness. "If we could [handle their main concern], would you be ready to move forward?"

### Closing Sequence

After sending a proposal, follow this sequence:

**Day 1**: Send the proposal with a clear call to action.
**Day 3**: Follow up. "Just checking in on the proposal. Any questions?"
**Day 7**: Follow up with additional value. "I found this case study you might find relevant."
**Day 14**: Final follow-up. "I want to respect your time. Should I close out this proposal, or would you like to discuss further?"
**Day 30**: Move to nurture. Add to newsletter, connect on LinkedIn, check in quarterly.

### Handling Silence

Silence after a proposal is normal. Do not panic. Do not discount. Follow the sequence.

Most sales happen between follow-ups 3 and 7. Do not give up after 2 follow-ups.

## Sales Scripts

### Discovery Call Script

```
"Hi [Name], thanks for your time today.

Here is what I was thinking for our call: I'd love to learn about your current situation, what is working, what is not, and what you are hoping to achieve. Then I'll share some thoughts on how we might help. And I'll leave plenty of time for your questions.

Does that sound good?

Great. So tell me — what prompted you to reach out?"

[Listen and ask follow-up questions]

"That is really helpful. Can I ask — what have you tried so far to address this?"

[Listen]

"And if you could wave a magic wand, what would the ideal outcome look like?"

[Listen]

"Fantastic. Based on what you have shared, here is how I think we can help..."

[Share high-level approach]

"I would love to put together a proposal for you. Is that something you would be interested in?"

[If yes: "Great, what else do you need from me to make a decision?"]
```

### Objection Script: Price

```
Client: "That is more than we budgeted."

You: "I understand. Can I ask what you were expecting?"

Client: [Gives number]

You: "I see. Let me share why our pricing is what it is. [Explain value and ROI]. Based on what you shared about [their pain], our solution delivers [specific value]. 

Here is a thought — would it help if we [adjusted scope / changed timeline / offered payment plan]?

Or let me ask this differently — if we could deliver [their desired outcome], what would that be worth to your business?"
```

### Closing Script

```
"So [Name], to summarize — we will [solution] to help you achieve [outcome]. The investment is [price], and we can start [date].

Are you ready to move forward?"

[If yes: "Great! I will send the contract right away."]

[If hesitation: "What is holding you back? I want to make sure we address any concerns."]
```

## Sales Metrics and Goals

### Individual Metrics

| Metric | Target |
|--------|--------|
| Outbound activities/day | 30+ |
| Conversations/day | 5+ |
| Meetings booked/week | 5-10 |
| Proposals sent/week | 3-5 |
| Win rate | 25-40% |
| Average deal size | $5k-$50k |
| Sales cycle | 30-60 days |
| Pipeline value | 3x monthly quota |

### Pipeline Health

A healthy pipeline has deals at every stage:

| Stage | Number of Deals | Value |
|-------|----------------|-------|
| Qualification | 20+ | $X |
| Discovery Done | 10+ | $X |
| Proposal Sent | 5+ | $X |
| Negotiation | 3+ | $X |
| Closing | 2+ | $X |

### Forecasting

Monthly forecast formula:
```
Closed deals this month + Deals at Closing stage × 80% + Deals at Negotiation × 50% + Deals at Proposal × 20% = Forecast
```

Sales is a process, not an event. Define your ICP, prospect consistently, qualify ruthlessly, discover deeply, propose clearly, handle objections directly, and close confidently. Track your metrics. Run this playbook consistently and your agency will never lack clients.
