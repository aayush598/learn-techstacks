# Hiring Salespeople: When, How, and Compensation Structures

## The Salesperson Decision

Hiring a salesperson is a bigger decision than hiring a freelancer. A bad developer costs you money. A bad salesperson costs you money AND damages your brand with potential clients.

But a good salesperson is the highest-ROI hire you can make. One good salesperson can double your revenue in 3-6 months.

## When to Hire a Salesperson

### The Revenue Threshold

Do not hire a salesperson before you hit $20k/mo consistently. Below that, you should be doing your own sales. You understand your value proposition better than anyone. You can close deals at a higher rate. You learn what works and what does not.

At $20k/mo+, your time is better spent on delivery, strategy, and scaling than on sales calls.

### Signs You Are Ready

- You have more delivery capacity than sales (you can do the work but cannot find clients)
- Your pipeline is consistently empty
- You spend more than 10 hours/week on sales activities
- Your close rate is below 30% (indicating you need a specialist)
- You have standardized your service offerings (salesperson can sell a defined product)
- You have documented sales processes (scripts, objection handling, pricing)
- You can afford to pay a base salary for 3+ months before they close their first deal

### The Pre-requisites

Before hiring a salesperson, you MUST have:
1. **Defined ICP**: Ideal Client Profile (exactly who you sell to)
2. **Proven offer**: Something you have sold successfully multiple times
3. **Sales process**: Documented steps from lead to close
4. **Pricing**: Clear, structured pricing (not custom every time)
5. **Delivery capacity**: You CAN deliver more work if they sell it
6. **Tracking**: CRM set up with pipeline stages

If you lack any of these, fix it before hiring. A salesperson cannot fix a broken offer or unclear targeting.

## Finding Salespeople

### Where to Look

**Best sources for salespeople**:
- **Referrals**: Ask your network for salespeople they have worked with
- **LinkedIn**: Target people with "Sales" titles in adjacent industries (web dev agencies, marketing agencies)
- **Upwork**: Surprisingly good for commission-only sales reps
- **Sales communities**: RevGenius, Sales Hacker, r/sales
- **Your clients**: Some clients may want to join your team

**What to look for**:
- Experience selling services (not products—services are harder)
- Experience in your industry or adjacent industries
- Proven track record with numbers you can verify
- Coachable attitude (they will need to learn your process)
- Hunter mentality (not just account management)

### Red Flags in Sales Candidates

- Cannot provide specific numbers (quota attainment, deals closed, revenue generated)
- Only experience selling products (services are fundamentally different)
- Overemphasis on commission structure in the first conversation
- Negative about past employers or clients
- Unwilling to start with a trial period
- No experience with your target buyer

## Compensation Structures

### The Base + Commission Model

This is the standard for agency sales roles.

**Structure**:
- **Base salary**: $30k–$60k/year (covers living expenses)
- **Commission**: 5-15% of revenue they generate
- **OTE (On-Target Earnings)**: $60k–$150k/year

**Commission calculation**:
- Commission is paid on revenue collected, not signed
- Commission is paid monthly or quarterly
- Commission rate can be tiered (higher rate for exceeding quota)
- Commission is typically 10% for the first year, 5% for renewals

**Tiered commission example**:
```
Monthly Revenue Generated | Commission Rate
$0–$20k                  | 5%
$20k–$50k                | 10%
$50k+                    | 15%
```

### Commission-Only Model

**Best for**: Startups with limited cash, testing a salesperson

**Structure**:
- No base salary
- Higher commission rate: 15-25%
- Potential for monthly minimum guarantee (recoverable draw)

**Pros**:
- No financial risk to you
- Salesperson is highly motivated
- Easy to part ways if it does not work

**Cons**:
- Harder to attract top talent
- Salesperson may be less loyal
- They may prioritize easy deals over strategic ones
- Higher churn (salespeople leave when they do not close quickly)

### Recoverable Draw

**A compromise between base + commission and commission-only**

- Salesperson gets a monthly draw (e.g., $4k/month)
- The draw is recouped from future commissions
- Once commissions exceed the draw, they keep 100% of commissions
- If they leave, outstanding draw is typically forgiven

**Example**:
```
Month 1: Draw $4k, Commissions $0 → Company pays $4k (draw balance: $4k)
Month 2: Draw $4k, Commissions $3k → Company pays $4k (draw balance: $5k)
Month 3: Draw $4k, Commissions $8k → Company pays $8k, $4k to salesperson ($4k repaid to draw, balance: $1k)
Month 4: Draw $4k, Commissions $10k → Company pays $10k, $6k to salesperson (draw repaid, balance: $0)
```

### Full-Time Employee vs. Contractor

| Factor | Employee | Contractor |
|--------|----------|------------|
| Cost | Higher (taxes, benefits) | Lower |
| Control | More control | Less control |
| Commitment | Higher expected | Lower commitment |
| Risk | More risk for you | Less risk |
| Best for | Full-time, dedicated role | Testing, part-time |

### Commission-Only Contractor

**When to use**: You are just starting sales hiring, limited budget, want to test the waters

**Structure**:
- 20% commission on first-year revenue
- 10% commission on renewals in year 2+
- Paid monthly on collected revenue
- No base, no benefits

**Example deal**:
- Salesperson closes a $30k project
- You collect $30k from client
- Salesperson gets $6k commission
- You keep $24k (minus delivery costs)

### Full-Time Salaried Employee

**When to use**: You have consistent revenue, need a dedicated salesperson, want long-term commitment

**Structure**:
- Base salary: $40k–$60k
- Commission: 10% on first-year revenue
- Benefits: health insurance, PTO
- OTE: $80k–$120k

**Example deal**:
- Salesperson closes $15k/month in new business consistently
- Monthly commission: $1,500
- Monthly salary: $4,000
- Monthly total: $5,500
- Revenue generated: $15,000
- Your margin (at 50%): $7,500 - $5,500 = $2,000 profit + capacity freed up

## The Salesperson Ramp

Expect a 3-6 month ramp period before a new salesperson is fully productive.

**Month 1**: Learning your product, market, and process. Should close 0-1 deals.
**Month 2**: Starting to prospect. Should close 1-3 small deals.
**Month 3**: Becoming productive. Should close 3-5 deals at increasing value.
**Month 4-6**: At full productivity. Should be hitting quota consistently.

**Ramp support**:
- Provide call scripts and objection handling docs
- Join them on calls for the first month
- Weekly pipeline reviews
- Daily activity tracking (calls, emails, meetings)
- Shadow you on sales calls

## Training Your Salesperson

### Week 1: Product and Market Training

- Your service offerings in detail
- Case studies of past projects
- Client testimonials (2x the deals)
- Your ICP and target industries
- Competitor landscape
- Your pricing structure

### Week 2: Sales Process Training

- Your CRM and how to use it
- Lead qualification criteria
- Discovery call structure
- Proposal process
- Objection handling
- Closing techniques

### Week 3: Shadowing and Role Play

- Shadow you on 5+ sales calls
- Role-play discovery calls
- Role-play objections
- Practice proposals and follow-ups

### Week 4: Live Calls with Support

- Take lead on calls with you observing
- You provide feedback
- They refine their approach
- Start managing their own pipeline

### Ongoing: Weekly 1:1 Coaching

- Review pipeline
- Review calls (record and discuss)
- Work on specific skills
- Set weekly activity goals

## Metrics to Track

### Activity Metrics (Leading Indicators)
- Outreach activities per day (target: 30+)
- Conversations had per day (target: 5+)
- Meetings booked per week (target: 5-10)
- Proposals sent per week (target: 3-5)

### Pipeline Metrics (Lagging Indicators)
- Pipeline value (target: 3x monthly quota)
- Deals at each stage
- Average deal size
- Win rate (target: 25-40%)

### Outcome Metrics
- Revenue closed per month
- Revenue collected per month
- Commission earned
- Cost of acquisition (their comp / revenue they close)

### Quality Metrics
- Client satisfaction score on deals they close
- Deal quality (do closed deals stick?)
- Average payment terms

## Managing Your Salesperson

### Daily

- Check CRM activity (are they prospecting?)
- Quick check-in (5 min): How many conversations? Any deals advancing?

### Weekly

- 1-hour pipeline review
- Review every open deal
- Identify next steps for each
- Address stalled deals
- Review activity metrics

### Monthly

- Review results against quota
- Celebrate wins
- Address shortfalls
- Adjust strategy if needed
- Forecasting for next month

### Quarterly

- Performance review
- Quota setting for next quarter
- Comp plan adjustment if needed
- Training needs assessment

## When a Salesperson Is Not Working

### Warning Signs

- Low activity (below 20 outreaches/day)
- Pipeline consistently empty
- No deals closing after 3 months
- Client complaints about pushy tactics
- Promises clients things you cannot deliver
- Blaming external factors for lack of results

### Remediation Steps

1. **Clear conversation**: Review metrics, set expectations
2. **Increased coaching**: Join calls, provide scripts, role-play
3. **30-day improvement plan**: Specific targets, daily check-ins
4. **Decision point**: If not improved in 30 days, part ways

### Termination Considerations

- Check if they have non-solicit/non-compete
- Transition active deals to you
- Pay all commissions earned
- Learn from the experience (what went wrong in hiring/onboarding?)

## Scaling from 1 to Multiple Salespeople

**1 salesperson**: You manage them directly
**2-3 salespeople**: You need a sales manager or lead
**4+ salespeople**: You need a sales manager, possibly SDRs (lead gen)

**Sales management structure**:
- **Sales Development Reps (SDRs)**: Outbound prospecting, set meetings
- **Account Executives (AEs)**: Close deals
- **Sales Manager**: Manages team, runs pipeline reviews
- **VP Sales**: Strategy, hiring, compensation design

For most agencies under $1M/year, you need 1 salesperson + you as sales support.

## The Cost-Benefit Analysis

**Investment**:
- Salesperson comp: $60k–$120k/year
- Training time: 20 hours of your time in month 1
- Tools: CRM ($60/mo), LinkedIn Sales Nav ($100/mo)

**Return**:
- Each salesperson should generate $200k–$500k/year in revenue
- At 50% margin, that is $100k–$250k gross profit
- Minus their comp: $40k–$130k net profit contribution

**ROI**: 2-5x on their fully loaded cost

## Summary

Hire a salesperson when you hit $20k+/mo, have a documented process, and cannot keep up with sales yourself. Start with a commission-only or recoverable draw structure to minimize risk. Expect a 3-month ramp. Train them thoroughly on your product, process, and ICP. Track activity metrics daily and pipeline metrics weekly. A good salesperson doubles your revenue. A bad one wastes time. Hire slowly, fire quickly.
