# Standard Operating Procedures: The Backbone of Your Agency

## Why SOPs Are the Key to Scaling

Without SOPs, your agency is a collection of tribal knowledge. Only you know how things work. New hires take months to get productive. Quality is inconsistent. You are the bottleneck.

With SOPs, anyone can do anything. New hires ramp in days. Quality is consistent. You can take vacations. Your business runs without you.

Every SOP you create is an investment in your freedom. The goal is to make yourself replaceable in every operational role.

## The Cost of Not Having SOPs

- **Ramp time**: 3-6 months for new hires to become fully productive
- **Quality variance**: Each person does things differently, results vary wildly
- **Single point of failure**: Only you know critical processes
- **Scaling friction**: Each new hire requires your direct training time
- **Inconsistency**: Clients get different experiences from different team members
- **Burnout**: You are constantly answering questions and putting out fires

With SOPs, a new hire can be productive in 1-2 weeks.

## What Makes a Good SOP

A good SOP is:
- **Specific**: Step-by-step instructions, not general guidelines
- **Complete**: Covers edge cases, exceptions, and common problems
- **Accessible**: Lives where people can find it (Notion, wiki, shared drive)
- **Living**: Updated regularly based on feedback
- **Tested**: Someone who has never done the task can follow it

## SOP Template Structure

Every SOP in your agency should follow this exact template:

```
# [SOP Name]

## Purpose
Why this process exists. What problem it solves. Why it matters.

## Scope
What this SOP covers and what it does not cover.

## Prerequisites
What you need before starting (tools, access, information).

## Steps
Numbered, sequential steps. Each step is a specific action.

## Quality Standards
What "good" looks like. How to know if the output is acceptable.

## Common Issues
What typically goes wrong and how to fix it.

## Tools Required
What tools are used in this process.

## Owner
Who is responsible for maintaining and updating this SOP.

## Version History
Date, version, author, changes made.
```

## How to Create SOPs: Step by Step

### Step 1: Identify Priority Processes

List every process in your business. Rank by:
- **Frequency**: How often is this process used?
- **Criticality**: How important is it to business success?
- **Complexity**: How hard is it to get right without documentation?

Prioritize SOPs that are high frequency, high criticality, and high complexity.

### Step 2: Document the Current Process

Do not design the perfect process yet. First, document what you actually do.

**Method**:
1. Record yourself doing the process (Loom is great for this)
2. Write down every step as you do it
3. Note decisions and why you make them
4. Capture edge cases you have encountered
5. Identify pain points and inefficiencies

### Step 3: Refine the Process

Review the documented process. Ask:
- Are there unnecessary steps?
- Can any steps be automated?
- Are there steps that cause confusion?
- Can the process be simplified?
- What improvements have you learned from past mistakes?

Optimize before you standardize. Documenting a bad process just makes bad processes repeatable.

### Step 4: Write the SOP

Using your template, write the SOP. Include:
- Clear, specific instructions
- Screenshots or video links where helpful
- Examples of good and bad outcomes
- Common mistakes and how to avoid them
- Decision trees for common questions

**Writing tips**:
- Use active voice ("Do X" not "X should be done")
- Be specific ("Click the green 'Save' button in the top right" not "Save the file")
- Include time estimates where relevant
- Use bullet points and numbered lists
- Keep steps short and single-action

### Step 5: Test the SOP

Give the SOP to someone who has never done the task. Watch them try to follow it.

**What to look for**:
- Where do they get confused?
- What questions do they ask?
- What steps are unclear?
- What is missing?

Revise based on their feedback. Then test with another person. Repeat until someone can complete the task without asking questions.

### Step 6: Implement and Train

- Publish the SOP in your documentation system
- Announce the new SOP to the team
- Walk through it in a team meeting
- Assign ownership for maintenance

### Step 7: Maintain and Update

SOPs are living documents. Set a recurring review cadence:
- **Monthly**: Quick review for obvious updates
- **Quarterly**: Full review with team feedback
- **After any incident**: Update SOPs to prevent recurrence

## SOP Writing Best Practices

### Use the "Explain Like I'm 5" Test

If a new hire could follow your SOP on their first day without asking you a single question, it is good enough. If they ask questions, update the SOP.

### Include Visuals

A screenshot is worth 100 words of description. For every step that involves a UI:
- Take a screenshot
- Annotate it (arrow, circle, highlight)
- Embed it in the SOP

Better yet, record a 2-minute Loom video and embed it.

### Write for the Worst Case

Your SOP should handle the 80% case, but also document:
- What happens when things go wrong
- Who to contact for specific issues
- What to do if a step does not work
- How to escalate problems

### Use Decision Trees

For complex processes, include decision trees:

```
Is the client new?
├── Yes → Go to Client Onboarding SOP #101
├── No → Is this a new project type?
│   ├── Yes → Schedule discovery call
│   └── No → Follow standard project workflow
└── Unsure → Ask your manager
```

### Create Checklists

For multi-step processes, create a checklist version:

**Client Onboarding Checklist**
- [ ] Kickoff call scheduled
- [ ] Client added to Slack channel
- [ ] Project board created in Linear
- [ ] Google Drive folder created
- [ ] Billing information collected
- [ ] Contract signed
- [ ] Timeline shared with client
- [ ] First meeting scheduled

## SOP Implementation Strategy

### Phase 1: Document Your Top 10

Identify your 10 most critical processes. Document them this week. Do not worry about perfection—good enough is better than not done.

**Your first 10 SOPs should be:**
1. Client onboarding
2. Daily standup
3. Task assignment and tracking
4. Quality review
5. Client communication
6. Invoicing
7. New hire onboarding
8. Development workflow
9. Deployment
10. Incident response

### Phase 2: Test and Refine

For each SOP, have someone on your team (or a friend) follow it. Revise based on their questions. Do this for 2 weeks.

### Phase 3: Build the Habit

Make SOP creation part of your culture:
- Every new process gets documented
- Every process change updates the SOP
- SOP review is part of monthly retrospectives
- Creating SOPs is recognized and rewarded

### Phase 4: Continuous Improvement

Set a quarterly goal to improve your SOPs:
- Reduce steps in key processes by 20%
- Add automation to reduce manual steps
- Collect feedback from team on clarity
- Update based on lessons learned

## SOP Metrics

Track these metrics to measure your SOP effectiveness:

- **Ramp time**: How long until new hires are fully productive (target: 2 weeks)
- **Error rate**: How often do process failures occur (target: <5%)
- **Question rate**: How many questions do team members ask per week (target: trending down)
- **SOP coverage**: What % of processes have documented SOPs (target: 100% of critical processes)
- **SOP freshness**: When was each SOP last updated (target: <90 days for active processes)
