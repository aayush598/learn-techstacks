# The Complete Agency Tool Stack

## The Tool Stack Philosophy

Your tools are your agency's infrastructure. Bad tools create friction, waste time, and cost money. Great tools automate the boring, make collaboration seamless, and give you data to make decisions.

The right tool stack should:
- **Automate repetitive tasks** (invoicing, reporting, follow-ups)
- **Centralize information** (no more searching across 5 tools for the truth)
- **Enable collaboration** (async progress, shared context)
- **Provide visibility** (who is doing what, when it is due, how profitable it is)
- **Scale with you** (do not rebuild your stack every 6 months)

## The Complete Agency Stack by Category

### 1. CRM (Client Relationship Management)

**Primary recommendation**: Pipedrive
**Cost**: $15–$60/seat/month
**Why**: Pipeline-focused CRM designed for service businesses. Unlike Salesforce (too complex) or HubSpot (too expensive at scale), Pipedrive gives you visibility into deal stages without over-engineering.

**Setup**:
- Pipeline stages: Lead → Discovery → Proposal → Negotiation → Closed Won → Closed Lost
- Custom fields: deal value, service type, source, close date
- Activities: automated follow-up reminders
- Email sync: connect your email to log all communications
- Reports: win rate, average deal size, pipeline value

**Alternative**: HubSpot CRM (free tier is generous, paid tiers get expensive)

**Features you must use**:
- Deal pipeline with stage probabilities
- Activity tracking and reminders
- Email integration and logging
- Custom fields for your service types
- Pipeline value reporting

### 2. Project Management

**Primary recommendation**: Linear (for dev-focused teams) or ClickUp (for mixed teams)
**Cost**: Linear $8–$16/seat/month, ClickUp $5–$12/seat/month
**Why Linear**: Fast, keyboard-friendly, developer-focused. Best for teams doing technical delivery.
**Why ClickUp**: Most feature-rich, customizable, good for mixed teams with different workflows.

**Setup in Linear**:
- Teams: separate for each service line or client type
- Workflow: Backlog → To Do → In Progress → In Review → Done
- Labels: priority (P0-P3), service type, client
- Cycles: 2-week sprints for predictable workloads
- Roadmap: monthly and quarterly views

**Setup in ClickUp**:
- Spaces: Client Work, Internal Ops, Sales, Finance
- Folders: per client or project
- Lists: To Do, In Progress, Review, Complete
- Custom statuses per workflow
- Automations: status changes, assignments, due dates

**Essential project management features**:
- Task assignment with deadlines
- Dependencies between tasks
- Time tracking integration
- Client view (limited access for clients)
- Reporting (burndown, velocity, workload)

**Alternative**: Asana (good balance), Basecamp (simple, client-friendly), Notion (flexible but less structured)

### 3. Communication

**Primary**: Slack
**Cost**: $8–$15/seat/month
**Why**: Industry standard. Deep integrations. Channels keep conversations organized.

**Slack structure for agencies**:
```
#general — company announcements
#random — water cooler, non-work
#standups — daily async updates
#wins — celebrate successes
#help — ask for help
#client-[name] — per-client channels
#proj-[name] — per-project channels
#sales — deal discussions
#ops — internal operations
```

**Additional communication tools**:
- **Loom** ($12.50/seat/month): Async video updates. Share your screen, record a message, send the link. Saves hours of meetings.
- **Google Meet / Zoom** ($0–$20/month): Video calls. Keep meetings recorded for absent team members.
- **Twist** ($8/seat/month): Async-first Slack alternative. Threads are first-class citizens. Good for teams that struggle with Slack's real-time nature.

### 4. Documentation and Knowledge Base

**Primary**: Notion
**Cost**: $10–$18/seat/month
**Why**: Combines docs, wikis, databases, and project management. Your entire agency's knowledge lives in one place.

**Notion structure**:
```
Home
├── Dashboard — quick links, metrics, priorities
├── Projects — database of all projects
├── Clients — database of all clients
├── SOPs — all processes documented
├── Sales — playbook, scripts, proposals
├── Finance — budgets, invoices, profitability
├── People — team info, onboarding, reviews
└── Reference — templates, guides, examples
```

**Essential Notion features**:
- Linked databases (project linked to client, tasks linked to project)
- Templates for recurring documents
- Published pages (share with clients without giving them access)
- Comments and mentions

**Alternatives**: GitBook (developer-friendly), Confluence (enterprise), Slab (simple)

### 5. Finance and Accounting

**Primary**: QuickBooks Online + Freshbooks
**Cost**: QuickBooks $30–$200/month, Freshbooks $15–$50/month
**Why QuickBooks**: Industry standard for accounting. Your accountant will thank you.
**Why Freshbooks**: Better invoicing for freelancers. Use Freshbooks for invoicing clients, QuickBooks for full accounting.

**Finance tool setup**:
- **Invoicing**: Freshbooks or QuickBooks (professional templates, automatic reminders, payment links)
- **Expense tracking**: QuickBooks bank feed (auto-categorize expenses)
- **Time tracking**: Harvest or Toggl (integrate with project management)
- **Payroll**: Gusto ($40/month + $6/person) if you have employees
- **Reporting**: QuickBooks (P&L, balance sheet, cash flow)

**Alternative**: Xero (good for international), Wave (free but limited), Bench (outsourced bookkeeping)

### 6. Time Tracking

**Primary**: Toggl Track
**Cost**: $9–$18/seat/month
**Why**: Simple, fast, integrates with everything. One-click timer. Good reporting.

**Setup**:
- Projects: mirror your project management
- Clients: each client
- Tags: billable vs non-billable, service type
- Reports: weekly time by project, utilization rate, profitability

**Alternatives**: Harvest (time + invoicing combined), Clockify (free tier is generous), Timely (automatic time tracking)

### 7. File Storage and Collaboration

**Primary**: Google Workspace
**Cost**: $6–$18/seat/month
**Why**: Google Docs (collaboration), Google Sheets (reporting), Google Drive (storage), Gmail (email).

**Structure**:
```
Google Drive
├── Clients
│   ├── [Client Name]
│   │   ├── 01 Proposals
│   │   ├── 02 Contracts
│   │   ├── 03 Deliverables
│   │   ├── 04 Invoices
│   │   └── 05 Reference
│   └── ...
├── Internal
│   ├── Templates
│   ├── Finance
│   └── Marketing
└── Team
    ├── Onboarding
    └── HR
```

**Alternatives**: Microsoft 365 (better for enterprise), Dropbox (simpler file sync), Figma (design files)

### 8. Design Tools

**Primary**: Figma
**Cost**: $12–$45/seat/month
**Why**: Industry standard for UI/UX design. Real-time collaboration. Developer handoff built in.

**Additional design tools**:
- **Canva** ($12.99/month): Quick graphics, social media, presentations
- **Adobe Creative Cloud** ($55/month): Full creative suite if needed
- **CleanShot X** ($29 one-time): Screenshots and screen recording with annotations

### 9. Development Tools

**Primary**: GitHub
**Cost**: $4–$21/seat/month
**Why**: Code hosting, version control, CI/CD, project management for code.

**Additional dev tools**:
- **Vercel / Netlify** ($0–$20/month): Hosting and deployment
- **Sentry** ($0–$26/month): Error tracking
- **Datadog / New Relic** ($0–$15/month): Monitoring and observability
- **1Password** ($8/seat/month): Password management and secrets sharing

### 10. HR and People Management

**Primary**: Gusto
**Cost**: $40/month + $6/person
**Why**: Payroll, benefits, hiring, onboarding. Simplifies US employment.

**Additional people tools**:
- **Deel** ($0–$19/contractor/month): International contractor payments
- **Lattice** ($11/seat/month): Performance reviews, OKRs, engagement
- **Calendly** ($10/seat/month): Scheduling meetings without back-and-forth

### 11. Marketing and Sales

**Primary**: LinkedIn Sales Navigator
**Cost**: $80–$100/month
**Why**: Find decision-makers, warm leads, track companies.

**Additional sales tools**:
- **Hunter.io** ($34/month): Find email addresses
- **Mailchimp** ($13/month): Email marketing and newsletters
- **Typeform** ($25/month): Client intake forms, surveys
- **DocuSign** ($10–$40/month): E-signatures for contracts

### 12. Automation and Integration

**Primary**: Zapier or Make
**Cost**: $20–$30/month
**Why**: Connect your tools without code. Automate repetitive tasks.

**Essential automations**:
- New deal in Pipedrive → Create project in Linear
- New invoice in Freshbooks → Create task in project management
- Time entry in Toggl → Create invoice line item
- New client in project management → Add to Slack channel
- Form submission → Create lead in CRM

### 13. Client Portal and Reporting

**Primary**: Notion (shared pages) or Basecamp
**Cost**: Included in existing subscriptions
**Why**: Give clients visibility into progress without giving them access to your internal tools.

**Client portal should include**:
- Project timeline and milestones
- Current status
- Deliverables (with approval workflow)
- Communication history
- Invoice history

## Tool Stack Evaluation Framework

When evaluating any tool, ask:

1. **Does it solve a real problem?** Do not adopt tools for features you will not use.
2. **Does it integrate with my existing stack?** If it does not connect to your CRM and project management, it will create data silos.
3. **What is the switching cost?** Moving data and retraining the team is expensive. Choose tools you can grow with.
4. **Is it adopted by the team?** The best tool is the one people will actually use. Involve the team in decisions.
5. **What is the total cost?** Not just subscription, but implementation, training, and maintenance time.

## Tool Integration Map

Your tools should form a connected ecosystem:

```
Sales (Pipedrive)
  │
  ├── New deal → Create project (Linear)
  ├── New deal → Add to Slack channel (#sales)
  └── Deal closed → Create client (QuickBooks)
  
Project Management (Linear)
  │
  ├── Task completed → Log time (Toggl)
  ├── Status update → Slack notification
  └── Project completed → Create invoice (Freshbooks)
  
Time Tracking (Toggl)
  │
  ├── Time entry → QuickBooks
  └── Weekly report → Slack (#ops)
  
Finance (QuickBooks)
  │
  ├── Invoice paid → Slack notification
  └── Monthly P&L → Google Sheets dashboard
```

## Tools You Should NOT Use

- **Trello**: Too simple for agency work. Outgrown within weeks.
- **Basecamp as PM**: Good for client collaboration, terrible for internal task management.
- **Salesforce**: Overkill for agencies under $1M revenue. Will cost you more in setup time than it saves.
- **Monday.com**: Expensive for what it offers. ClickUp is better at half the price.
- **Jira**: Designed for software teams. Too complex for most agency workflows.

## Cost-Benefit Analysis

If your tool stack costs $500/month and saves each team member 2 hours/week:

- 2 hours/week × 4 team members = 8 hours/week saved
- 8 hours × $100/hour billable rate = $800/week
- $800 × 4 weeks = $3,200/month value
- ROI: $3,200 / $500 = 6.4x/month

Invest in good tools. They pay for themselves.

## Action Items This Week

1. Audit your current tool stack. List every tool and what you pay.
2. Identify gaps and overlaps. Where are you missing a tool? Where are you paying for two tools that do the same thing?
3. Choose your stack from the recommendations above.
4. Set up your foundation tools (Google Workspace, Slack, Notion) this week.
5. Create your integration plan (Zapier or native integrations).
6. Cancel tools you are not using (most people have 3-5 unused subscriptions).
7. Schedule training sessions for the team on any new tools.

## Summary

Your tools are your infrastructure. Invest in them. Choose tools that integrate well, scale with you, and your team will actually use. Start with the foundation (email, chat, docs), then add sales, project management, and finance. Automate connections between tools. Review your stack quarterly and cancel what you are not using. A well-chosen tool stack saves each team member 5-10 hours/week and gives you visibility into every aspect of your business.
