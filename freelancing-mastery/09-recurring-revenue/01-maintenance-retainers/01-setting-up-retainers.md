# Setting Up Retainer Agreements

## Why Retainers Are the Fastest Path to $10k+/Month

Retainers are the single highest-leverage move you can make as a freelancer. A $3k/month retainer is worth $36k/year in guaranteed revenue. Replace one-off project churn with predictable monthly income and you unlock the ability to scale, hire, and actually take time off.

The goal: 80% of your income from recurring sources (retainers + products). This guide shows you exactly how to structure, propose, and close retainer agreements.

---

## The Three Retainer Types

### 1. Hourly Block Retainers

You sell a fixed number of hours per month at a discounted rate. Client gets priority access. You get predictable minimums.

**Pricing structure:**
- Standard hourly rate: $150/hr
- Block retainer rate: $125/hr (17% discount for commitment)
- 10-hour block: $1,250/month
- 20-hour block: $2,500/month
- 40-hour block: $5,000/month

**Best for:** Ongoing maintenance, bug fixes, small feature work, content updates

**Pros:** Simple to understand, easy to scope, low friction to sell
**Cons:** Still trading time for money, hours cap your income, clients may try to maximize every minute

**Contract language:**
```
Retainer Hours. Client commits to [N] hours per month at $[X]/hour.
Unused hours expire at end of each month. Hours exceeding the block
are billed at $[Y]/hour (standard rate). Block rate is locked for
12 months.
```

**Key rule:** Unused hours do NOT roll over. This forces clients to either use them or lose them. If you allow rollover, you're deferring revenue and creating a liability on your books.

### 2. Scope-Based Retainers

You define a specific scope of work delivered each month for a flat fee. This is the MOST profitable retainer type because you decouple your time from the deliverable.

**Example scopes:**
- WordPress maintenance: $2,000/month (updates, backups, security scans, 1 hour of content changes)
- Server management: $3,000/month (monitoring, patching, uptime, SSL renewal)
- Code review + QA: $2,500/month (2 PR reviews per week, monthly audit)
- Technical advisory: $5,000/month (architecture reviews, code audits, strategy calls)

**Best for:** Maintenance-heavy work, specialized expertise, ongoing operations

**Pros:** Uncapped profit potential (get more efficient over time), clients love fixed pricing, easier to scale with virtual assistants or junior devs
**Cons:** Requires clear scope boundaries to prevent scope creep, harder to sell initially

**The efficiency flywheel:**
- Month 1: You spend 15 hours delivering $3,000 = $200/hr
- Month 6: You automate/optimize to 8 hours = $375/hr
- Month 12: You delegate to junior = $1,000+/hr (you keep the spread)

**Contract language:**
```
Scope of Work. Consultant will deliver the following services monthly:
[list 5-10 specific deliverables]. Any work outside this scope
requires a separate Statement of Work. Client receives [2] revision
rounds per deliverable.
```

### 3. On-Call / Availability Retainers

You charge for availability and rapid response, not for specific work. You're a firefighter, not a gardener.

**Structure:**
- Monthly fee: $1,000-$10,000/month (varies by response time)
- Response SLA: 1 hour during business hours, 4 hours after hours
- Included hours: 5-10 hours of work included
- Overage: 1.5x-2x standard rate beyond included hours

**Tiered on-call packages:**

| Tier | Monthly Fee | Response Time | Included Hours | Overage Rate |
|------|-------------|---------------|----------------|--------------|
| Standard | $1,000/mo | 4 business hours | 5 hours | $200/hr |
| Premium | $2,500/mo | 1 business hour | 10 hours | $250/hr |
| Critical | $5,000/mo | 30 min any time | 15 hours | $300/hr |
| Enterprise | $10,000/mo | 15 min any time | 30 hours | $350/hr |

**Best for:** Production-critical systems, e-commerce sites, high-traffic applications, startups without DevOps

**Pros:** Charges for readiness (not just work), massive margins on unused time, sticky once they depend on you
**Cons:** Can be disruptive to personal life (set boundaries), burnout risk if understaffed

**Key pricing insight:** Your on-call fee is an insurance premium. The client is paying for peace of mind. Price it like insurance, not like labor. A startup losing $10k/hour when their site is down will happily pay $5k/month for 15-minute response.

## The Retainer Proposal Framework

### Step 1: Diagnose Pain Before Pitching

Never lead with "I offer retainers." Lead with their problem.

**Discovery questions:**
- "How much time do you spend on [maintenance/ops/updates] each month?"
- "What happens when something breaks at 2 AM?"
- "How much revenue did you lose in the last incident?"
- "If you could snap your fingers and never worry about [X] again, what would that be worth to you?"
- "Are you currently paying someone else less but spending more time managing them?"

**What you're listening for:** Frequency of pain, cost of failure, existing spend, frustration with current setup.

### Step 2: Quantify the Cost of NOT Having a Retainer

Make the alternative more expensive.

**Client pain calculation worksheet:**
```
Current hourly rate for emergency fixes: $200/hr
Hours spent on emergencies per month: 8
Monthly cost: $1,600

Plus: Lost sleep, context switching, delayed projects
Plus: Opportunity cost of not building revenue-generating features
Plus: Risk of extended downtime during vacation

Total cost of current approach: $3,000-$5,000/month (conservative)
Retainer cost: $2,500/month

Savings: $500-$2,500/month + peace of mind
```

### Step 3: Structure the Proposal

**Retainer proposal template:**

```
CLIENT: [Name]
DATE: [Date]
PREPARED BY: [Your Name]
VALID FOR: 14 days

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
[3 sentences: their problem, your solution, the investment]

THE PROBLEM
[2-3 bullets quantifying their current pain/cost]

THE SOLUTION
[Describe the retainer type and why it makes sense]

SCOPE OF SERVICES (Monthly)
1. [Deliverable 1]
2. [Deliverable 2]
3. [Deliverable 3]
4. [Deliverable 4]
5. [Deliverable 5]

[For hourly: "Up to X hours of development work"]
[For scope-based: "The above deliverables delivered monthly"]
[For on-call: "On-call availability with Y-hour response SLA"]

EXCLUSIONS (Not Included)
- [Service 1]
- [Service 2]
- [Service 3]
[This section is CRITICAL for preventing scope creep]

INVESTMENT
Option A (Monthly retainer): $X/month, 12-month commitment
Option B (Quarterly retainer): $X/month, 3-month commitment (10% premium)
Option C (Project basis): $X (50% premium to incentivize retainer)

TERMS
- Payment: Net 7 or auto-charge
- Invoicing: 1st of each month, auto-pay discount available
- Reporting: Monthly summary of work completed
- Termination: 30 days written notice

NEXT STEPS
1. Schedule 30-min call to answer questions
2. Sign agreement (DocuSign)
3. First payment, work begins
```

### Step 4: Handle Objections

**"It's too expensive."**
"We're talking about $X per month to eliminate the risk of [specific pain]. What's the cost of one major incident? How much time are you currently spending that you could reinvest in growth?"

**"We might not need you every month."**
"That's the point of the retainer. You're paying for availability and peace of mind. When you need me, I'm here immediately. Without a retainer, you're competing with my other clients for availability."

**"Can we start month-to-month?"**
"I offer a 3-month minimum to establish the relationship and processes. After that, it's month-to-month with 30-day termination. If I'm not delivering value in 3 months, you should cancel."

**"Can we lower the hours?"**
"Yes, let me show you what you'd lose. [Drop to minimum viable retainer - the smallest engagement that still makes sense for both parties.]"

### Step 5: Close with a Trial

If they're still hesitant, offer a "retainer trial":

**Retainer trial structure:**
- 2 months at 80% of full price
- Full scope included
- If they cancel after 2 months, they keep all work delivered
- After trial, convert to full price with 12-month commitment

**Conversion rates:** 80%+ of trials convert. The client gets hooked on the peace of mind.

## Retainer Contract Essentials

### Must-Have Clauses

1. **Term:** "This agreement shall commence on [Date] and continue for [12] months, automatically renewing for successive [1]-month periods unless terminated with [30] days written notice."

2. **Scope of Work:** Detailed scope of included services with measurable deliverables. This is your scope creep defense.

3. **Exclusions:** Explicit list of what's NOT included. "This retainer does not include: database migrations, third-party API integrations, redesign work, content creation."

4. **Overage Billing:** "Work exceeding the included scope or hours will be billed at $[X]/hour, invoiced monthly, due Net 15."

5. **Response Time (for on-call):** "Consultant will respond to critical issues within [30] minutes during business hours and within [2] hours outside business hours. Non-critical issues will be addressed within [24] business hours."

6. **Payment Terms:** "Fees are due on the 1st of each month. Late payments incur a [5%] late fee after [15] days. Services may be paused after [30] days of non-payment."

7. **Termination:** "Either party may terminate with [30] days written notice. Client will be billed for the full notice period. Upon termination, consultant will provide [30] days of transition support."

8. **Confidentiality:** Standard NDA language covering client data, code access, and business information.

9. **Intellectual Property:** "All work product created under this retainer is the property of Client upon full payment. Consultant retains the right to use general methodologies and techniques."

10. **Limitation of Liability:** "Total liability shall not exceed fees paid in the preceding [6] months. Consultant is not liable for consequential damages including lost revenue or data loss."

### Pricing Escalation Clause

Include annual price increases automatically:

```
Annual Fee Adjustment. On each anniversary of this agreement,
monthly fees will increase by [5-10%] to account for inflation,
increased expertise, and expanded service capabilities. Client
will be notified [60] days in advance.
```

**Why this matters:** Your costs go up every year. Your skills get more valuable. Don't let clients lock in 2024 prices forever. 5% annual increase is standard and most clients won't blink.

## Retainer Onboarding Process

### Week 1: Setup and Discovery

- Kickoff call with stakeholders (45 min)
- Review existing infrastructure/codebase (2-4 hours)
- Set up monitoring, alerts, access credentials (2 hours)
- Document current state, known issues, backlog (1 hour)
- Create shared dashboard (Trello/Linear/Notion) (1 hour)
- Establish communication channels (Slack/Discord) (30 min)

### Week 2: Stabilization

- Fix critical issues found during audit (2-4 hours)
- Set up automated backups, security scans (1 hour)
- Create runbooks for common procedures (2 hours)
- Establish reporting templates (1 hour)
- First weekly status report (30 min)

### Week 3-4: Optimization

- Resolve medium-priority backlog items (3-5 hours)
- Implement monitoring and alerting improvements (2 hours)
- Create monthly report template (1 hour)
- First monthly review call (45 min)
- Document all processes, credentials, procedures (2 hours)

### Ongoing Monthly Cadence

- **Weekly:** 15-min status check-in, review open items, prioritize
- **Bi-weekly:** Bug fixes, minor updates, performance review
- **Monthly:** Full report to client, invoice, planning session for next month
- **Quarterly:** Strategic review, audit, recommendation for upcoming quarter

## Retainer Conversion Funnel

Build retainer opportunities into every project:

### Phase 1: Project Delivery (Months 1-3)
- Overdeliver on project scope
- Document ongoing maintenance needs
- Plant seeds: "This is the kind of thing I handle on retainer for clients"
- Fix things you notice without being asked (wow them)

### Phase 2: The Pitch (Month 3)
- Present a "Post-Launch Health Assessment"
- Show them what's needed for the next 12 months
- Offer the retainer as "the smart way to handle this"
- Include a case study of another client's retainer success

### Phase 3: The Close
- Offer a 2-month trial retainer at 80% price
- Lock in annual commitment with monthly billing
- Start with scope-based retainer (most predictable)
- Expand scope after 6 months (upsell more services)

**Conversion benchmarks:**
- Projects that don't mention retainers during delivery: 10% conversion
- Projects with retainer conversation at launch: 30% conversion
- Projects with structured retainer pitch at month 3: 50% conversion
- Projects where you fix something unexpected for free: 70% conversion

## Retainer Operations: Running It Efficiently

### Tools Stack

- **Project management:** Linear (dev work) + Notion (client portal)
- **Time tracking:** Toggl Track (automatic tracking per client)
- **Invoicing:** FreshBooks or Wave (recurring invoices)
- **Communication:** Slack channels per client (dedicated)
- **Monitoring:** Better Uptime or Checkly (for infrastructure retainers)
- **Reporting:** Google Data Studio or simple Notion dashboards
- **Credentials:** 1Password Teams (shared vaults per client)
- **Code repos:** GitHub (private repos per client)

### Time Management Framework

**Rule of thirds for retainer time:**
- 1/3: Active work for retainer (the deliverables)
- 1/3: Reactive work (emails, emergencies, questions)
- 1/3: Buffer + education (staying current, improving systems)

**Weekly time budgeting:**
```
Monday: Deep work on retainer deliverables (3-4 hours)
Tuesday: Client meetings + reactive (2-3 hours)
Wednesday: Deep work + improvements (3-4 hours)
Thursday: Buffer + reactive (2-3 hours)
Friday: Reports, planning, education (2-3 hours)
```

### Scaling Retainers

**Solo capacity:** 5-8 retainer clients maximum solo
- 5 retainers at $3k = $15k/month (25 hours/week)
- 5 retainers at $5k = $25k/month (30 hours/week)
- 8 retainers at $2k = $16k/month (20 hours/week)

**First hire trigger:** When you have 5+ retainers or $15k+/month retainer revenue, hire a junior to handle routine work.

**Scaling model:**
1. You sell retainers at $5k/month
2. Junior dev does the work at $1k/month (part-time)
3. You oversee, handle escalations, maintain client relationship
4. Profit: $4k/month per retainer (80% margin)
5. With 10 retainers: $40k/month passive-ish income

## Action Items: This Week

### Day 1-2: Identify Retainer Candidates
- Go through your last 10 clients
- Which ones need ongoing maintenance?
- Which ones had emergencies or urgent requests?
- List them and note their pain points

### Day 3: Package Creation
- Define your retainer type (scope-based is best)

- Set up your retainer proposal template

### Day 4: Outreach
- Email past clients with "How's everything running?"

- Send proposal within 24 hours of the call

### Day 5: Close
- Follow up on proposals
- Handle objections
- Close at least 1 retainer before end of week
- Start onboarding process

## The $100k Retainer Goal

To hit $100k/year from retainers:
- Option A: 3 clients at $2,800/month = $100,800/year
- Option B: 5 clients at $1,700/month = $102,000/year
- Option C: 2 clients at $4,200/month = $100,800/year

Most freelancers with basic technical skills can close 3 retainer clients in 90 days. That's $100k/year with 20-25 hours of work per week.

**The math on retainers vs projects:**

| Metric | One-off Projects | Retainers |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| Client acquisition cost | High (new client each time) | Low (once per client) |
| Revenue predictability | Zero | 100% predictable |
| Time per sale | 10-20 hours | 2-3 hours |
| Upsell potential | Limited | Built-in |
| Exit value of business | Low (no recurring) | 3-5x annual recurring |
| Stress level | High (feast/famine) | Low (consistent) |
