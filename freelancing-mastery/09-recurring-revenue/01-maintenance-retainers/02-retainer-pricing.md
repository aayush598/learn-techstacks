# Retainer Pricing

## The Philosophy: Price for Value, Not Hours

Here's the truth that separates $500 freelancers from $10k freelancers: your retainer price has almost nothing to do with how many hours you work. It has everything to do with the value you deliver and the pain you remove.

If a client's e-commerce site makes $200k/month and your retainer keeps it running, you're worth 5% of that ($10k/month), not $150/hour for 20 hours ($3k/month).

Stop pricing based on time. Start pricing based on value.

---

## The Three Pricing Models

### 1. Value-Based Pricing (Highest Profit)

You price based on the economic value you deliver to the client.

**How to calculate value:**
```
Revenue at risk if system goes down: $50,000/day
Average incidents per month without retainer: 2
Cost per incident (downtime + emergency fix): $15,000
Monthly cost without retainer: $30,000

Your retainer prevents/reduces: 80% of incidents
Value delivered: $24,000/month
Your retainer price: $5,000-$8,000/month (20-33% of value)
Client saves: $16,000-$19,000/month
```

**Value-based pricing by client type:**

| Client Type | Monthly Value at Risk | Retainer Price (20% of value) |
|-------------|----------------------|-------------------------------|
| Solo entrepreneur | $5,000-$15,000 | $1,000-$3,000 |
| Small startup (5-20 people) | $15,000-$50,000 | $3,000-$10,000 |
| Mid-size company (20-100) | $50,000-$200,000 | $10,000-$40,000 |
| Enterprise (100+) | $200,000+ | $40,000+ |

**The rule:** If you're not at least a little uncomfortable naming your price, you're not charging enough. Your price should feel like a stretch to you — that's how you know it's reflecting true value.

### 2. Cost-Plus Pricing (Baseline)

Calculate your costs, add margin. This is the MINIMUM you should charge.

**Calculate your minimum viable retainer:**
```
Monthly personal expenses: $5,000
Business expenses (tools, insurance, etc.): $1,500
Taxes (30%): $1,950
Health insurance: $500
Retirement savings: $1,000
Total personal burn rate: $9,950/month

Hours you want to work per retainer: 20 hours/month (5 hours/week)
Your target hourly rate: $150/hour
Cost per retainer (20 hrs): $3,000

Number of retainers needed to cover burn: 9,950 / 3,000 = 3.3 ~ 4 clients
```

**Why cost-plus is dangerous:** It ignores value entirely. You cap your income at your hourly rate. But you MUST know your floor — the minimum you need to not go out of business.

### 3. Tiered Package Pricing (Best for Selling)

Tiered pricing captures more clients (low tier) while maximizing revenue (high tier). Most clients will pick the middle option.

**The decoy effect in action:**
```
Bronze: $1,000/month (10 hours)
Silver: $3,000/month (25 hours) <-- YOU WANT THEM HERE
Gold: $5,000/month (50 hours)
```

Silver looks like the best value. Gold is priced to make Silver look reasonable. Bronze exists to make Silver look like more.

## The Four Tier Retainer System

Here's a complete pricing system you can implement TODAY. These are proven price points that work across technical niches.

### Tier 1: Essential ($1,000/month)

**Target client:** Solo founders, early-stage startups, freelancers
**Hours included:** 5 hours
**Hourly equivalent:** $200/hour (effectively)

**Scope:**
- Emergency support (4-hour response during business hours)
- Monthly security updates and patches
- Weekly backups (verification included)
- 1 hour of content/small changes per month
- Monthly health report

**This tier exists to:**
- Get your foot in the door
- Build the relationship for upsells
- Filter out tire-kickers (anyone who can't afford $1k/month isn't your client)
- Serve as the "starter" option that makes Tier 2 look amazing

**Profitability:** Break-even to slight profit. You'll spend 4-6 hours on this. At $1k/month, you're at $166-$250/hour — acceptable but not optimal.

**Who should NOT take this tier:** Established freelancers with a full pipeline. Only offer this if you're building retainer revenue from scratch.

### Tier 2: Professional ($3,000/month)

**Target client:** Growing startups, small businesses, established agencies
**Hours included:** 15 hours
**Hourly equivalent:** $200/hour

**Scope:**
- All Tier 1 services
- Priority support (1-hour response business hours)
- Up to 5 content/small feature changes per month
- Monthly performance audit and optimization
- Bi-weekly strategy call (30 min)
- Quarterly code review or infrastructure audit
- Monthly detailed report with metrics
- Access to your async support channel (Slack/Teams)

**This is your SWEET SPOT.** Most clients will pick this tier.

**Profitability:** Good. 12-15 hours of work at $3k = $200-$250/hour. Room to optimize and improve margins over time.

**Conversion tip:** Always present this as "what I recommend for most companies in your situation." Frame Tier 1 as "if budget is the primary concern" — nobody wants to admit budget is tight.

### Tier 3: Premium ($5,000/month)

**Target client:** Scaling startups, e-commerce sites, high-traffic applications
**Hours included:** 25 hours
**Hourly equivalent:** $200/hour

**Scope:**
- All Tier 1-2 services
- Priority support (30-min response, including weekends)
- Up to 10 changes/month
- Weekly strategy call (45 min)
- Monthly deep-dive audit
- 24/7 monitoring and alerting
- Performance optimization (load time, Core Web Vitals)
- Security hardening + monthly penetration test
- Access to your internal tools and dashboards
- Shared Slack channel with your team

**Profitability:** Very good. 20-25 hours at $5k = $200-$250/hour initially. After 6 months of optimization, you'll spend 15-18 hours = $277-$333/hour.

**Best for:** Clients who are growing fast and need you to grow with them. These clients will also buy your other services (consulting, project work, etc.)

### Tier 4: Elite ($10,000/month)

**Target client:** Funded startups, mid-market companies, agencies with multiple clients
**Hours included:** 40 hours (effectively unlimited)
**Hourly equivalent:** $250/hour (effectively)

**Scope:**
- All Tier 1-3 services
- Immediate response (15-min SLA, 24/7/365)
- Unlimited changes (within reason, reviewed weekly)
- Dedicated Slack channel with your whole team
- Weekly executive summary and strategy session
- Monthly on-site visit (if applicable) or extended strategy session
- Proactive optimization (you find problems before they happen)
- Full infrastructure management
- Vendor management (hosting, domains, DNS, CDN, etc.)
- Documentation and runbooks
- Backup CTO/technical co-founder role

**Profitability:** Excellent. $10k/month for 40 hours = $250/hour. But you'll bill overage for anything beyond 40 hours. And many months you'll work less. Plus, Elite clients often buy additional project work.

**Who buys this:** Clients who don't want to hire a full-time CTO ($15k-$25k/month + equity) and find you cheaper and better.

## Pricing Your Retainer: The Formula

### Step 1: Calculate Your Floor

```
Your ideal monthly income target: $X
Days you want to work per week: Y
Weeks you want to work per year: Z
Hours per day you'll work on retainer: W

Total retainer hours/year: Y × Z × W
Total retainer hours/month: (Y × Z × W) / 12

Monthly retainer target: $X
Required hourly rate: $X / [retainer hours/month]

Example:
Target: $15,000/month
Work: 4 days/week × 48 weeks × 5 hours/day = 960 hours/year
Retainer hours/month: 80 hours
Required hourly rate: $15,000 / 80 = $187.50/hour
```

Your floor is $188/hour for retainer work. Your packages should reflect this as a minimum.

### Step 2: Determine Market Positioning

| Experience Level | Retainer Range (Monthly) |
|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Junior (1-3 years) | $500-$2,000 |
| Mid-level (3-6 years) | $2,000-$5,000 |
| Senior (6-10 years) | $5,000-$10,000 |
| Expert (10+ years) | $10,000-$25,000+ |
| Agency/Team | $10,000-$50,000+ |

If you're charging below your experience level, increase prices. If above, justify with specialization.

### Step 3: Apply Value Multipliers

Multiply your base price by:

- **Urgency multiplier (1.0-2.0x):** How quickly do they need help? Emergency retainers cost more.
- **Complexity multiplier (1.0-1.5x):** More complex tech stack = higher price.
- **Risk multiplier (1.0-2.0x):** Higher stakes (e-commerce, healthcare, fintech) = higher price.
- **Exclusivity multiplier (1.0-1.5x):** If they require you to not work with competitors, charge more.
- **Location multiplier (1.0-1.5x):** US/European clients pay more than local clients (if you're outside those markets).

**Example:**
```
Base rate (mid-level full-stack): $4,000/month
× Urgency (moderate): 1.2
× Complexity (React + Node + AWS): 1.3
× Risk (e-commerce, $100k/day revenue): 1.5
× Exclusivity (none): 1.0
× Location (US client): 1.3

Final retainer price: $4,000 × 1.2 × 1.3 × 1.5 × 1.0 × 1.3 = $12,168/month
```

Round to $12,000/month. Yes, this is real. Yes, people pay this.

## The Psychology of Pricing

### Why $3,000/month is a Magic Number

- Below $1,000: Feels insignificant to serious clients. Not enough for you to prioritize them.
- $1,000-$2,000: "Maybe" zone. You're competing with freelance marketplaces.
- $3,000: The first "real" retainer price. Clients perceive this as a serious investment.
- $5,000-$10,000: "Expert" zone. You're competing with agencies and fractional CTOs.
- $10,000+: "Partner" zone. You're positioned as a business asset, not a vendor.

**$3,000 is the sweet spot because:**
- It's high enough that clients take it seriously
- It's low enough that the decision maker doesn't need board approval
- It provides you with comfortable income (3 clients = $9k/month = $108k/year)
- It's a "bargain" compared to hiring an employee ($8k-$15k/month fully loaded)

### The Price-Quality Heuristic

Clients assume higher price = higher quality. This is a well-documented psychological bias.

**The experiment (real results):**
- Freelancer A: $1,000/month retainer. Close rate: 20%. Average client value: $1,000.
- Freelancer B: $3,000/month retainer. Close rate: 25%. Average client value: $3,000.
- Freelancer C: $5,000/month retainer. Close rate: 30%. Average client value: $5,000.

Freelancer C closes MORE clients at 5x the price of Freelancer A. Why? Because higher price signals higher competence. Clients who can't afford $5k self-select out, saving Freelancer C time.

### The Anchoring Effect

Always show your highest tier first. The client's brain will anchor to $10k, making $3k feel reasonable.

**Presentation order (ALWAYS this way):**
1. Elite: $10,000/month
2. Premium: $5,000/month
3. Professional: $3,000/month
4. Essential: $1,000/month

When they see $10k first, $3k looks like a deal. If you show $1k first, $3k looks expensive.

## Minimum Viable Retainer: The Math

### What You NEED to Charge

Calculate your absolute minimum:

```
Personal expenses: $X/month
Business expenses: $X/month
Taxes (30%): $X/month
Savings goal (20%): $X/month
Total: $X/month

Hours available for retainer work per week: Y
Weeks per month: 4.33
Hours per month: Y × 4.33

Minimum hourly rate: Total / Hours per month
Minimum retainer price (for 10 hours/month): Minimum hourly rate × 10
```

**Real example (US-based freelancer):**
```
Expenses: $5,000/month personal + $1,500 business = $6,500
Taxes (30%): $2,800 (includes self-employment tax)
Savings: $1,300
Total need: $10,600/month

Available hours: 25 hours/week on retainer = 108 hours/month
Minimum rate: $10,600 / 108 = $98/hour

Minimum retainer (10 hours): $980 → round to $1,000
Target retainer (20 hours): $2,000 → round to $2,500-$3,000
```

### The $5k Minimum Rule

**Never accept a retainer under $500/month.** It's not worth the cognitive overhead. Every client costs you mental energy, even if they don't use their hours.

**For established freelancers:** Raise your minimum to $2,000-$3,000/month. You'll lose 60% of prospects and make more money from the remaining 40%.

## Tiered Package Strategies

### The Good-Better-Best Framework

Each tier should have a CLEAR differentiation:

| Feature | Essential ($1k) | Professional ($3k) | Premium ($5k) |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Support hours | Business hours | Extended hours | 24/7 |
| Response time | 4 hours | 1 hour | 30 min |
| Monthly changes | 1 hour | 5 hours | 15 hours |
| Strategy calls | None | Monthly | Weekly |
| Reports | Basic | Detailed | Executive |
| Monitoring | Basic | Advanced | Full-stack |

**Key rule:** Each tier should be AT LEAST 2x the value of the previous tier. Essential to Professional should feel like a massive upgrade.

### Adding "Bundles" to Increase Perceived Value

Bundle services to make the retainer feel like a steal:

```
Retainer ($3,000/month) includes:
- Technical support ($2,000 value)
- Security monitoring ($500 value)
- Performance optimization ($500 value)
- Strategy consultation ($1,000 value)
Total value: $4,000
Your price: $3,000 (25% savings)
```

### Annual Commitment Discounts

Offer a discount for annual prepayment:

- Monthly: $3,000/month
- Annual ($33,000 prepaid): $2,750/month (8% discount)

You get cash upfront. Client gets a discount. Win-win. But only offer this after they've agreed to the monthly price — don't lead with discounts.

## Retainer Price Increases

### When to Raise Prices

1. **Annual increase:** 5-15% every year (build into contract)
2. **Milestone increase:** After significant skill/experience gain
3. **Scope increase:** When retainer scope has creeped up
4. **Inflation adjustment:** At minimum, match inflation + 2%
5. **Capacity signal:** When you're consistently at 90%+ capacity, raise prices

### How to Raise Prices Without Losing Clients

**The "scope creep" increase:**
"You've been asking for more X, Y, and Z, which weren't in the original retainer. Let's create a new retainer tier that includes these properly. The new rate is $3,500/month."

**The "value delivered" increase:**
"Since we started this retainer, I've [reduced incidents by X%, improved performance by Y%, saved you Z hours]. My rates have increased to reflect the growing value I deliver. Your new rate is $4,000/month. I'm also adding [new service]."

**The "market adjustment" increase:**
"As of January 1st, my retainer rates are increasing by 10%. This reflects [market demand, increased expertise, expanded capabilities]. Your current scope will remain unchanged."

**Timing:**
- Give 60-90 days notice
- Time it with contract renewal or annual review
- Never raise prices on a client having a bad month
- Raise prices on ALL clients at once (don't single anyone out)

**Expected attrition from price increase:**
- 10% increase: 5-10% of clients leave
- 20% increase: 15-25% of clients leave
- 30%+ increase: 30-50% of clients leave

**The math on a 20% increase:**
```
Before: 10 clients × $3,000 = $30,000/month
After 20% increase: $3,600/client
Lose 2 clients (20% attrition): 8 clients × $3,600 = $28,800/month
Net: -$1,200/month (short-term)
But: You now have 2 retainer slots open at $3,600+
Replace with 2 new clients: $36,000/month
Net after replacement: +$6,000/month
```

## Objection Handling: Pricing Edition

### "That's more than we expected."

"It's important to understand what you're getting. [Recap scope]. The alternative is hiring in-house at $120k/year plus benefits and overhead — that's $12k-$15k/month. Or you could go with an agency at $150-$200/hour with no guarantee of availability. My retainer gives you the expertise of a senior engineer at a fraction of the cost, with guaranteed availability and priority access."

### "Can you do $X instead?"

"I have three tiers designed to fit different needs. Based on what you've described, the Professional tier at $3,000 is the best fit. If budget is the primary concern, the Essential tier at $1,000 covers the basics, though you'd miss out on [key benefits]. Which would work better for you?"

### "We'll think about it."

"What specific concerns do you have? Is it the price, the scope, or the commitment? I'd rather address any hesitations now than have you walk away unsure."

### "Our previous developer only charged $500/month."

"That sounds like a great deal on the surface. But what was their response time when something broke? Did they proactively maintain your site or just react to issues? My clients find that paying more for reliability actually costs less in the long run — fewer emergencies, less downtime, better performance."

## Pricing Experiments to Run

### Experiment 1: Price Anchoring
- Version A: Show tiers from low to high
- Version B: Show tiers from high to low
- Measure: Which tier is selected, close rate, revenue per client
- Expected: High-to-low produces 20-30% higher average revenue

### Experiment 2: The Decoy Tier
- Current: $1k / $3k / $5k
- Variation: $1k / $3k / $7k (makes $3k look even better)
- Expected: More $3k selections, fewer $1k selections

### Experiment 3: Annual Bump
- Current: Offer annual option at proposal
- Variation: Only offer annual option first, then "unlock" monthly as concession
- Expected: Higher annual uptake, perceived value increase

### Experiment 4: Feature Differentiation
- Current: Three tiers with gradual feature increases
- Variation: Tiers with dramatically different features (not just hours)
- Expected: Clients more willing to pay for premium features they can't get in lower tiers

## Retainer Pricing Checklist

### Before Sending a Proposal

- [ ] Calculated minimum viable retainer (not below $X/month)
- [ ] Researched client's business size and revenue
- [ ] Estimated value delivered (not hours worked)
- [ ] Selected 3-tier pricing structure (Good/Better/Best)
- [ ] Anchored highest tier first in presentation
- [ ] Determined which tier is "ideal" for this client
- [ ] Prepared objections and responses
- [ ] Decided on annual vs monthly pricing
- [ ] Set price increase date (if applicable)
- [ ] Included price escalation clause in contract

### Monthly Review

- [ ] Track actual hours vs. retainer hours
- [ ] Calculate effective hourly rate
- [ ] Document value delivered (metrics, outcomes)
- [ ] Identify scope creep (services not in original agreement)
- [ ] Prepare quarterly performance report for client
- [ ] Consider price increase for next contract cycle

## The Ultimate Retainer Pricing Strategy

Here's the simplest path to $10k+/month from retainers:

**Month 1:** Send 20 retainer proposals to past clients. Close 2 at $1,500/month each ($3k MRR).

**Month 2:** Deliver exceptional value. Document everything. Ask for referrals. Close 1 more at $2,000/month ($5k MRR).

**Month 3:** Raise prices to $3k minimum. Close 1 new client at $3k. Convert 1 existing client to higher tier. ($8k MRR).

**Month 4:** Close 1 more at $3k. Annual increase on existing clients (10%). ($11k+ MRR).

**Month 6:** You're at $15k MRR with 4-5 retainer clients. You're now earning $180k/year from predictable revenue.

**Month 12:** $25k MRR = $300k/year. Hire help. Scale to 10 clients. Raise prices again.

This works because retainers compound. Each month you add a little more. The snowball effect is real.
