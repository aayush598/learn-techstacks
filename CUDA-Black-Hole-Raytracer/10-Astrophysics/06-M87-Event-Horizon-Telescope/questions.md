# Astrophysics — M87 Event Horizon Telescope Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What did the EHT image of M87* show?
**A:** The first black-hole image: a ring of ~42 +- 3 microarcsec diameter enclosing a dark shadow - the photon-ring/horizon-scale signature of a spinning hole.

## Q2: What is M87*?
**A:** The supermassive black hole (~6.5e9 solar masses) in the galaxy M87, at ~55 Mly - the largest-angular-size horizon in the sky, hence the EHT's prime target.

## Q3: What does the 2019 image reveal about spin?
**A:** The asymmetry (bright crescent to the south) is consistent with a bright approaching-side Doppler-boosted disk - consistent with modest spin and a viewing angle ~17 deg.

## Q4: What is the 1.3mm (230 GHz) observing band?
**A:** Where the disk's synchrotron is optically thin and the ring's photon-ring dominates - the physical window the EHT observes.

## Q5: How does the raytracer reproduce M87*?
**A:** Render a GRMHD (MAD-family) disk at a~0.9, inclination ~17 deg, with electron-heating closure, then blur to the EHT's ~20-25 microarcsec beam.

## Q6: What is the ring's physical size?
**A:** ~5.2 M for a Schwarzschild photon-sphere edge (projected); for M87*, 42 microarcsec maps to ~9.5 M in diameter at the distance - the shadow-observable link.

## Q7: What parts of the image are robust?
**A:** The ring DIAMETER is robust to emission details (geometry-set); the asymmetric brightness and inner-faintness are model-dependent - the fit's lever arm.

## Q8: What is the EHT's resolution/how achieved?
**A:** Very-long-baseline interferometry (VLBI) with telescopes across the planet forming a ~12,000 km baseline - resolution ~20 microarcsec, enough for the ring.

## Q9: What did follow-up (2018+, 2024) add?
**A:** The ring persists with polarization maps (magnetic fields at the ring) and a hinted jet rotation - the same ray-traced geometry extended to Q/U channels.

## Q10: How does a renderer 'fit' the EHT image?
**A:** Run a grid of (a, inclination, model) renders, convolve with the beam, compare residuals to the observed image - the forward-model fitting loop.

## Q11: What is the summary?
**A:** M87* defined the observable: a ~5M diameter ring with a Doppler-asymmetric crescent; your raytracer renders exactly that from Kerr GRMHD disks and matches it against 1.3mm data.

## Q12: Why is m87 event horizon telescope central to interpreting black-hole images?
**A:** Observables like the ring, shadow, and Doppler asymmetry trace directly to m87 event horizon telescope, so understanding it lets you read images as physics.

## Q13: What does m87 event horizon telescope predict about the photon ring?
**A:** The bright ring radius is set by m87 event horizon telescope quantities like the photon sphere and spin, making it a probe of the spacetime itself.

## Q14: How does m87 event horizon telescope explain the shadow?
**A:** Captured photons leave a dark central region whose size and asymmetry depend on m87 event horizon telescope - mass, spin, and viewing angle.

## Q15: What observations constrain m87 event horizon telescope?
**A:** EHT images of M87* and Sgr A* measure ring diameter and asymmetry; m87 event horizon telescope inversions use those to constrain the spacetime.

## Q16: Why do m87 event horizon telescope models show the disk brighter on one side?
**A:** Relativistic beaming amplifies emission from the approaching side; m87 event horizon telescope visibility depends on the disk velocity and line of sight.

## Q17: How does m87 event horizon telescope guide the choice of simulation data?
**A:** Disk state (SANE/MAD), electron distribution, and spin control morphology; m87 event horizon telescope selection is part of the science, not just plumbing.

## Q18: What does m87 event horizon telescope say about temperature?
**A:** The inner flow reaches billions of Kelvin; m87 event horizon telescope emission intensity maps to temperature and magnetic field through emissivity relations.

## Q19: How is m87 event horizon telescope measured with EHT?
**A:** Very long baseline interferometry synthesizes a horizon-scale aperture; m87 event horizon telescope plausibility is judged against its reconstructed images.

## Q20: What distinguishes m87 event horizon telescope for supermassive vs stellar black holes?
**A:** Scales shift by mass, but dimensionless quantities like the photon-ring radius are universal; m87 event horizon telescope transfers across masses by scaling.

## Q21: What role do jets play in m87 event horizon telescope?
**A:** Poynting-flux outflows can shine in their own right and shape the outermost emission; m87 event horizon telescope includes them for completeness.

## Q22: How does m87 event horizon telescope treat the electron thermodynamics?
**A:** Since GRMHD often does not track electron vs ion temperature, m87 event horizon telescope uses proxies like R-beta prescriptions to convert fluid heating into emissivity.

## Q23: What is the next frontier in m87 event horizon telescope?
**A:** Time-dependent images and movie predictions from turbulent simulations observed by upgraded EHT arrays; m87 event horizon telescope is moving from stills to dynamics.
