# Astrophysics — Temperature Profiles Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What is the temperature profile of the disk?
**A:** The radial run of temperature: thin disks follow T(r) ~ r^-3/4 (viscous); hot GRMHD flows carry an electron temperature set by heating/cooling balance.

## Q2: Why does temperature matter so much?
**A:** Synchrotron emissivity is extremely temperature-sensitive (via the electron gamma distribution) - the ring's brightness and color follow T_e primarily.

## Q3: What is the ion vs electron temperature split?
**A:** Magnetic dissipation heats ions preferentially; electrons cool faster; the ratio T_e/T_i ~ 0.1-1 depends on the heating prescription (Rbeta, or set models).

## Q4: How is the electron temperature obtained?
**A:** From a closure: fraction of the dissipation to electrons (Rbeta), a scaling on the magnetic energy density, or a plasma-beta relation - the model's choice.

## Q5: What is the radial profile's inner hotness?
**A:** In the innermost radii gas reaches the virial temperature ~ GM m_p/(r R) - the steep inward heating that lights the ring.

## Q6: What does the vertical temperature profile look like?
**A:** Colder near the midplane of a thin disk, hotter in the corona - the corona-vs-disk contrast shows in the image's limb structure.

## Q7: What is the effect of temperature on the spectrum?
**A:** Higher T_e shifts the synchrotron turnover to higher frequency and steepens the emitted bands - temperature IS the color knob of the renderer.

## Q8: How does the temperature drive SSA?
**A:** The optically-thin/thick transition scales with T (via spectral slope); a hotter inner region can become self-absorbed at lower bands - a coupled effect.

## Q9: What are typical electron temperatures rendered?
**A:** For M87-class flows ~ 1e10-1e11 K in the ring; the renderer must sample the local T_e robustly near the ISCO where the profile is steep.

## Q10: How do you validate a temperature model?
**A:** The emitted SED's slope/peak must match the observed (for fits) or the analytic expectation for a given T(r) - the temperature-to-observable chain tested end-to-end.

## Q11: What is the summary?
**A:** Temperature profiles are the emissivity's thermostat - ion/electron closures set radial and vertical T_e; the renderer inherits ring brightness and color from them.

## Q12: Why is temperature profiles central to interpreting black-hole images?
**A:** Observables like the ring, shadow, and Doppler asymmetry trace directly to temperature profiles, so understanding it lets you read images as physics.

## Q13: What does temperature profiles predict about the photon ring?
**A:** The bright ring radius is set by temperature profiles quantities like the photon sphere and spin, making it a probe of the spacetime itself.

## Q14: How does temperature profiles explain the shadow?
**A:** Captured photons leave a dark central region whose size and asymmetry depend on temperature profiles - mass, spin, and viewing angle.

## Q15: What observations constrain temperature profiles?
**A:** EHT images of M87* and Sgr A* measure ring diameter and asymmetry; temperature profiles inversions use those to constrain the spacetime.

## Q16: Why do temperature profiles models show the disk brighter on one side?
**A:** Relativistic beaming amplifies emission from the approaching side; temperature profiles visibility depends on the disk velocity and line of sight.

## Q17: How does temperature profiles guide the choice of simulation data?
**A:** Disk state (SANE/MAD), electron distribution, and spin control morphology; temperature profiles selection is part of the science, not just plumbing.

## Q18: What does temperature profiles say about temperature?
**A:** The inner flow reaches billions of Kelvin; temperature profiles emission intensity maps to temperature and magnetic field through emissivity relations.

## Q19: How is temperature profiles measured with EHT?
**A:** Very long baseline interferometry synthesizes a horizon-scale aperture; temperature profiles plausibility is judged against its reconstructed images.

## Q20: What distinguishes temperature profiles for supermassive vs stellar black holes?
**A:** Scales shift by mass, but dimensionless quantities like the photon-ring radius are universal; temperature profiles transfers across masses by scaling.

## Q21: What role do jets play in temperature profiles?
**A:** Poynting-flux outflows can shine in their own right and shape the outermost emission; temperature profiles includes them for completeness.

## Q22: How does temperature profiles treat the electron thermodynamics?
**A:** Since GRMHD often does not track electron vs ion temperature, temperature profiles uses proxies like R-beta prescriptions to convert fluid heating into emissivity.
