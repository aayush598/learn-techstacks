# Astrophysics — Mri Turbulence Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What is the MRI?
**A:** The magnetorotational instability: differential rotation stretches magnetic fields, driving turbulent angular-momentum transport - the engine of accretion.

## Q2: Why is the disk turbulent (not laminar)?
**A:** Keplerian profiles are Rayleigh-stable hydrodynamically but unstable with even a weak seed field - turbulence is the equilibrium state of magnetized accretion.

## Q3: How does the MRI transport angular momentum?
**A:** The Reynolds and Maxwell stresses (B_r B_phi terms) torque the gas outward - the source of the effective viscosity feeding inward.

## Q4: What is the alpha parameter?
**A:** alpha = (stress)/(pressure) ~ 0.01-0.1 from MRI simulations - the modern replacement for the Shakura-Sunyaev 'viscosity' with a physical origin.

## Q5: How is alpha measured in a GRMHD run?
**A:** Time-and-volume-average the stress tensor components against pressure over the saturated-turbulence window - the single most useful diagnostic number.

## Q6: What does turbulence do to density?
**A:** It spawns the intermittent clumps/spiral patterns in the disk - the emissivity's peppered texture that differentiates a GRMHD image from a smooth model.

## Q7: How does the MRI set the disk's emission?
**A:** The turbulence's magnetic amplification concentrates B in intermittent patches - synchrotron lights up the variable, non-azimuthally-symmetric emission.

## Q8: What is MRI's saturation mechanism?
**A:** The turbulence saturates when the dynamo's growth matches numerical/physical dissipation - maintaining the stress against reconnection and diffusion.

## Q9: Why do thin disks need resolving the MRI?
**A:** The fastest-growing wavelength scales with sqrt(v_A^2/Omega^2); under-resolved grids kill the instability and the disk becomes artificially laminar.

## Q10: How does the MRI appear in images?
**A:** Migrating bright clumps and azimuthal brightness variation - the 'flickering files' with orbital-period structure, a distinguishing dynamical signature.

## Q11: What is the summary?
**A:** MRI turbulence transports angular momentum physically, textures the density field, and sets the disk's variability - the reason GRMHD images wiggle and flare.

## Q12: Why is mri turbulence central to interpreting black-hole images?
**A:** Observables like the ring, shadow, and Doppler asymmetry trace directly to mri turbulence, so understanding it lets you read images as physics.

## Q13: What does mri turbulence predict about the photon ring?
**A:** The bright ring radius is set by mri turbulence quantities like the photon sphere and spin, making it a probe of the spacetime itself.

## Q14: How does mri turbulence explain the shadow?
**A:** Captured photons leave a dark central region whose size and asymmetry depend on mri turbulence - mass, spin, and viewing angle.

## Q15: What observations constrain mri turbulence?
**A:** EHT images of M87* and Sgr A* measure ring diameter and asymmetry; mri turbulence inversions use those to constrain the spacetime.

## Q16: Why do mri turbulence models show the disk brighter on one side?
**A:** Relativistic beaming amplifies emission from the approaching side; mri turbulence visibility depends on the disk velocity and line of sight.

## Q17: How does mri turbulence guide the choice of simulation data?
**A:** Disk state (SANE/MAD), electron distribution, and spin control morphology; mri turbulence selection is part of the science, not just plumbing.

## Q18: What does mri turbulence say about temperature?
**A:** The inner flow reaches billions of Kelvin; mri turbulence emission intensity maps to temperature and magnetic field through emissivity relations.

## Q19: How is mri turbulence measured with EHT?
**A:** Very long baseline interferometry synthesizes a horizon-scale aperture; mri turbulence plausibility is judged against its reconstructed images.

## Q20: What distinguishes mri turbulence for supermassive vs stellar black holes?
**A:** Scales shift by mass, but dimensionless quantities like the photon-ring radius are universal; mri turbulence transfers across masses by scaling.

## Q21: What role do jets play in mri turbulence?
**A:** Poynting-flux outflows can shine in their own right and shape the outermost emission; mri turbulence includes them for completeness.

## Q22: How does mri turbulence treat the electron thermodynamics?
**A:** Since GRMHD often does not track electron vs ion temperature, mri turbulence uses proxies like R-beta prescriptions to convert fluid heating into emissivity.

## Q23: What is the next frontier in mri turbulence?
**A:** Time-dependent images and movie predictions from turbulent simulations observed by upgraded EHT arrays; mri turbulence is moving from stills to dynamics.
