# Astrophysics — Sgra Event Horizon Telescope Interview Questions and Answers

## Q1: What did the EHT image of Sgr A* show?
**A:** A ring of ~51.8 +- 2.3 microarcsec diameter around a shadow - the Milky Way's central black hole, with the same geometric physics at ~4 million solar masses.

## Q2: What is Sgr A*?
**A:** Sagittarius A* - the ~4.15e6 solar-mass black hole at the Galactic Center, 8.2 kpc away, seen through a turbulent scattering screen.

## Q3: How does Sgr A* differ from M87*?
**A:** ~1500x less massive, faster forcing and variability (minutes-hours vs days), and viewed through interstellar scattering - a harder image with a richer time-domain.

## Q4: What is the scattering-screen issue?
**A:** The line of sight diffracts in the interstellar medium - smears/blurs the image below ~0.2 arcsec-ish and adds a red noise; the renderer must account for it in comparisons.

## Q5: What is the fit result?
**A:** The ring diameter ~51 microarcsec is spin-compatible; the image fluctuates between frames consistent with a hot, compact disk seen at moderate inclination.

## Q6: How does the image support the black-hole hypothesis?
**A:** The shadow size matches a Kerr horizon at the parallax-measured mass/distance - a clean geometric confirmation of the Schwarzschild/Kerr expectations.

## Q7: What do the time-variable images show?
**A:** Fast brightness clumps orbiting within minutes - the MRI turbulence/dirty-blobs of GRMHD models become a time-domain observable at the Galaxy's center.

## Q8: How do you compare a renderer to Sgr A*?
**A:** Render at 230GHz with interstellar-scattering convolution and a short movie cadence; compare the 'signature image' ensembles, not one snapshot.

## Q9: What is the role of the crescent parameterization?
**A:** The m-ring (asymmetric ring) decomposition parametrizes radius, width, and asymmetry - the compact summary both renders and data report.

## Q10: What is the summary?
**A:** Sgr A* is the dynamic M87-class target - a ~51-microarcsec ring, blurred by scattering, fluctuating on minute scales; your raytracer replicates that with hot GRMHD disks and movie-mode light curves.
