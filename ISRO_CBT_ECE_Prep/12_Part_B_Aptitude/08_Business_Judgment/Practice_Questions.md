## Question 1: Conflict Resolution

You discover that a junior colleague has been reporting incorrect test data in project reports. What is the best course of action?

(A) Ignore it, as they are new and will learn
(B) Confront them aggressively in front of the team
(C) Speak with them privately, understand the reason, and if it persists, report to the supervisor
(D) Report directly to the management without talking to the colleague

**Solution:**
- Option A: Unethical — incorrect data compromises project integrity
- Option B: Unprofessional — public confrontation damages relationships
- Option C: Balanced approach — address the issue professionally, allow correction, escalate if needed
- Option D: Skipping the direct conversation is unnecessarily escalatory

In ISRO context: data integrity is critical, but so is professional conduct.

**Answer: (C)** — Address directly but professionally, escalate if needed.

## Question 2: Deadline Pressure

Your team is working on a critical deliverable due in 3 days. You discover a significant error in the calculations that will take 2 days to fix. The client has been promised the deliverable. What should you do?

(A) Submit the deliverable as-is and fix the error later
(B) Inform the client immediately about the delay and revised timeline
(C) Ask the team to work overtime without informing anyone
(D) Hide the error and hope no one notices

**Solution:**
- Option A: Submitting erroneous data is unacceptable for mission-critical work
- Option B: Transparent communication with the client — professional and ethical
- Option C: Overtime without approval and without addressing the root issue
- Option D: Hiding errors is unethical and dangerous

ISRO culture: transparency about problems is valued. Clients/stakeholders respect honest communication.

**Answer: (B)** — Inform the client immediately with a revised timeline.

## Question 3: Ethical Dilemma

A vendor offers you a personal gift worth ₹5,000 after winning a contract with your organization. What is the appropriate response?

(A) Accept the gift as a gesture of goodwill
(B) Politely decline, and if the vendor insists, report to your ethics officer
(C) Accept but don't tell anyone
(D) Ignore the situation

**Solution:**
- Option A: Accepting gifts may create conflicts of interest
- Option B: Professional decline + escalation if needed — follows most organizational policies
- Option C: Concealing is unethical
- Option D: Ignoring doesn't resolve the issue

Most organizations (including ISRO) have strict policies on accepting gifts from vendors.

**Answer: (B)** — Decline politely, report if the vendor insists.

## Question 4: Resource Constraint

Your project has a tight budget. You need to choose between two approaches: a cheaper method with 80% reliability or an expensive method with 99.9% reliability. The project is mission-critical. What do you choose?

(A) Choose the cheaper method to stay within budget
(B) Choose the expensive method and find budget from other areas
(C) Choose the cheaper method and add extra testing to compensate
(D) Defer the decision until more budget is available

**Solution:**
- Option A: 80% reliability is unacceptable for mission-critical
- Option B: Quality and reliability take priority over budget for mission-critical work
- Option C: Extra testing doesn't guarantee the method becomes 99.9% reliable
- Option D: Deferring may miss the deadline

ISRO context: mission success is non-negotiable. Budget can be adjusted, but reliability cannot be compromised.

**Answer: (B)** — Choose the reliable method, find budget from other areas.

## Question 5: Quality vs Speed

During a code review, you find that a teammate has bypassed a mandatory testing step to meet the deadline. The code appears to work. What should you do?

(A) Approve the code since it works
(B) Reject the code, require the testing step to be completed
(C) Approve it but document the bypass
(D) Report the teammate to management

**Solution:**
- Option A: Bypassing mandatory testing is a quality risk
- Option B: Maintain quality standards — the testing step exists for a reason
- Option C: Documentation without correction doesn't fix the quality gap
- Option D: Reporting should come after the immediate issue is resolved

The correct professional response is to enforce quality standards first.

**Answer: (B)** — Reject the code, require the mandatory testing to be completed.

## Question 6: Team Management

A highly experienced team member is consistently missing deadlines due to personal issues. The project is suffering. What is the best approach?

(A) Immediately terminate their employment
(B) Ignore the situation as they are experienced
(C) Have a private conversation, understand the situation, and explore accommodations
(D) Assign all their work to other team members without discussion

**Solution:**
- Option A: Too harsh without understanding the situation
- Option B: Ignoring doesn't solve the project impact
- Option C: Empathetic approach — understand, accommodate if possible, maintain professional standards
- Option D: Without discussion, this creates resentment and overloads others

Professional approach: address the issue with empathy while maintaining project requirements.

**Answer: (C)** — Private conversation, understand the situation, explore accommodations.

## Question 7: Communication

You notice a potential safety issue in a system during testing. It hasn't caused any incident yet but could in certain conditions. What do you do?

(A) Wait until it actually causes a problem
(B) Document it in your personal notes
(C) Report it immediately through the proper safety reporting channels
(D) Discuss it informally with colleagues

**Solution:**
- Option A: Waiting for an incident is dangerous and irresponsible
- Option B: Personal notes don't alert the organization
- Option C: Proper safety reporting ensures the issue is tracked and addressed
- Option D: Informal discussion doesn't trigger formal safety processes

ISRO has zero tolerance for safety concerns. Report immediately through proper channels.

**Answer: (C)** — Report immediately through proper safety reporting channels.

## Question 8: Project Scope Creep

The client has requested 5 additional features after the project scope was finalized. Your team is already at capacity. What is the best response?

(A) Accept all features to keep the client happy
(B) Reject all additional features
(C) Evaluate each feature, prioritize based on impact, and negotiate timeline/budget adjustments
(D) Ask the team to work overtime to accommodate everything

**Solution:**
- Option A: Accepting without capacity leads to quality/delay issues
- Option B: Blanket rejection damages the client relationship
- Option C: Professional approach — evaluate, prioritize, negotiate
- Option D: Overtime leads to burnout and reduced quality

Best practice: structured change management process.

**Answer: (C)** — Evaluate, prioritize, and negotiate adjustments.

## Question 9: Confidentiality

A friend working at a competitor company asks you about the technical specifications of a project you're working on. What should you do?

(A) Share the information as they are a friend
(B) Share only general information, nothing specific
(C) Decline to share any project-related information
(D) Ask them to share their project information first

**Solution:**
- Option A: Sharing confidential information is a serious violation
- Option B: Even general information may be sensitive
- Option C: Correct — maintain confidentiality at all times
- Option D: This doesn't make sharing any more acceptable

Confidentiality is a core professional obligation, especially in defense/space organizations.

**Answer: (C)** — Decline to share any project-related information.

## Question 10: Decision Under Uncertainty

You need to choose a technology for a 5-year project. Technology A is new and promising but unproven. Technology B is established but has known limitations. What should you do?

(A) Choose A since it's promising
(B) Choose B since it's safe
(C) Conduct a thorough evaluation including pilot testing, risk assessment, and expert consultation before deciding
(D) Choose whichever is cheaper

**Solution:**
- Option A: New and unproven carries high risk for a 5-year commitment
- Option B: Established but limited may not meet future needs
- Option C: Evidence-based decision with proper evaluation — professional approach
- Option D: Cost alone shouldn't drive technology selection

For mission-critical long-term projects, thorough evaluation is essential.

**Answer: (C)** — Conduct thorough evaluation before deciding.

## Question 11: Accountability

You made an error in a report that was already submitted to management. What is the best course of action?

(A) Hope no one notices
(B) Blame it on a team member
(C) Acknowledge the error, correct it, and submit a revised report
(D) Wait until someone questions it

**Solution:**
- Option A: Passive — errors compound if not corrected
- Option B: Unethical — taking someone else's blame
- Option C: Professional accountability — correct the error proactively
- Option D: Reactive instead of proactive

Professional integrity requires owning and correcting mistakes.

**Answer: (C)** — Acknowledge, correct, and submit a revised report.

## Question 12: Stakeholder Management

Two senior managers have different requirements for the same system. Both insist their requirement is priority. How do you handle this?

(A) Follow the requirements of the more senior manager
(B) Ignore both and implement what you think is best
(C) Document both requirements, present a comparative analysis to both managers, and escalate if they can't agree
(D) Implement both requirements even if they conflict

**Solution:**
- Option A: Based on hierarchy, not merit
- Option B: Exceeds your authority
- Option C: Professional approach — document, analyze, escalate
- Option D: Conflicting requirements lead to system failure

Escalation through proper channels is the correct approach.

**Answer: (C)** — Document, analyze, and escalate for resolution.

## Question 13: Time Management

You have three tasks due today: A (important, not urgent), B (urgent, not important), C (urgent and important). How do you prioritize?

(A) Start with A since it's important
(B) Start with B since it's urgent
(C) Start with C (urgent and important), then handle the others
(D) Do all three simultaneously

**Solution:**
Eisenhower Matrix: Urgent + Important (C) → Do first
Important + Not Urgent (A) → Schedule
Urgent + Not Important (B) → Delegate if possible

**Answer: (C)** — Start with C (urgent and important), then handle others.

## Question 14: Process Compliance

You notice that a shortcut in the testing process saves 50% time but slightly reduces test coverage. The project manager suggests using it. What should you do?

(A) Follow the manager's suggestion since they are the authority
(B) Refuse and document your concerns formally
(C) Use the shortcut but increase other testing to compensate
(D) Use the shortcut and don't document the reduced coverage

**Solution:**
- Option A: Authority doesn't override quality standards
- Option B: Formal documentation of concerns is the correct professional approach
- Option C: Compensation may not fully address the coverage gap
- Option D: Undocumented quality reduction is a serious risk

For mission-critical projects, process compliance is mandatory. Document concerns formally.

**Answer: (B)** — Refuse and document your concerns formally.

## Question 15: Team Collaboration

A new team member is struggling to contribute effectively. They seem hesitant to ask questions. What should you do?

(A) Ignore them since they should figure it out themselves
(B) Assign them only trivial tasks
(C) Proactively offer help, create a supportive environment, and pair them with a mentor
(D) Report their performance issues to management immediately

**Solution:**
- Option A: Unsupportive — new members need guidance
- Option B: Doesn't help them grow
- Option C: Constructive — supportive environment helps new members succeed
- Option D: Premature — should try to support first

Professional team culture includes helping new members integrate.

**Answer: (C)** — Proactively help, create support, provide mentoring.
