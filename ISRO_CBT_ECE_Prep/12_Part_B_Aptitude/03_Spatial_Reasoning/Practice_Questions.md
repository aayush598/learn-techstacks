## Question 1: Paper Folding — Single Fold

A square paper is folded once from left to right (vertical fold on the left side). A single circular hole is punched in the center of the folded paper. When unfolded, how many holes appear and where?

(A) 2 holes — one on the left half, one on the right half, equidistant from center
(B) 1 hole at the center
(C) 2 holes — both on the right half
(D) 4 holes symmetrically placed

**Solution:**
- Step 1: Paper folded left to right → the left half overlaps the right half
- Step 2: Hole punched at center of folded paper → this is at the center of the original paper (where the fold line is)
- Step 3: When unfolded, the hole at the fold line stays as ONE hole (since it's on the fold)

Wait — the center of the folded paper is at the fold line, so the hole appears ON the fold line.

Actually, if the paper is folded from left to right:
- The fold is on the LEFT side of the folded piece
- The center of the folded piece is in the middle of the RIGHT half of original
- The hole punched at center of folded piece → on the right half
- When unfolded: the hole appears on the right half, and its mirror appears on the left half

**Answer: (A)** — 2 holes, one on each half, equidistant from center.

## Question 2: Paper Folding — Double Fold

A square paper is folded in half horizontally (top to bottom), then folded in half vertically (left to right). A single hole is punched at the top-left corner of the folded paper. When unfolded, where are the holes?

(A) 2 holes along the top edge
(B) 4 holes — one in each quadrant
(C) 1 hole at the center
(D) 4 holes along the edges only

**Solution:**
- Fold 1: Top to bottom → fold line is horizontal through center
- Fold 2: Left to right → fold line is vertical through center
- Hole punched at top-left corner of folded paper

The top-left corner of the doubly-folded paper corresponds to:
- "Top" after fold 1 = original top (which is now at center after folding down)
- "Left" after fold 2 = original left (which is now at center after folding right)

The top-left corner of the folded piece = the center of the original paper, but at the intersection of the two fold lines.

When unfolded:
- Across vertical fold: hole mirrors from left to right → 2 holes
- Across horizontal fold: each hole mirrors from top to bottom → 4 holes

Position: The corner of the folded piece is at the center, but specifically it's the corner where both folds meet. This corresponds to the center of the original paper.

4 holes appear at positions that are symmetric about both fold lines.

**Answer: (B)** — 4 holes, one in each quadrant, symmetric about both fold lines.

## Question 3: Paper Folding — Three Folds

A paper is folded three times: left-to-right, top-to-bottom, then left-to-right again. One hole is punched. When unfolded, how many holes appear?

(A) 4
(B) 6
(C) 8
(D) 16

**Solution:**
Each fold doubles the number of layers:
- After fold 1: 2 layers
- After fold 2: 4 layers
- After fold 3: 8 layers

One hole through 8 layers = 8 holes when unfolded.

**Answer: (C)** — 8 holes.

## Question 4: Cube from Net

The following net is given (in a cross/plus shape):
```
    [A]
[B][C][D][E]
    [F]
```

When folded into a cube, which face is opposite to face C?

(A) E
(B) D
(C) A
(D) F

**Solution:**
In the cross-shaped net:
- C is at the center of the horizontal strip
- Opposite faces are separated by exactly one square
- C and E are separated by D → C is opposite E
- B and D are separated by C → B is opposite D
- A and F are separated by C → A is opposite F

**Answer: (A)** — Face E is opposite to face C.

## Question 5: Dice Problem

Two views of a die are shown:
- View 1: Shows faces 1, 2, 3
- View 2: Shows faces 1, 4, 5

What number is on the face opposite to face 1?

(A) 2
(B) 3
(C) 6
(D) 4

**Solution:**
From View 1: Face 1 is adjacent to 2 and 3
From View 2: Face 1 is adjacent to 4 and 5

Face 1 is adjacent to: 2, 3, 4, 5
A face can have at most 4 adjacent faces (out of 5 other faces)
The only face NOT adjacent to 1 is face 6.

Therefore, face 6 is opposite to face 1.

**Answer: (C)** — 6 is opposite to 1.

## Question 6: Standard Die Property

On a standard die (opposite faces sum to 7), if face 2 is on top and face 3 is on the front, which face is on the right?

(A) 1
(B) 4
(C) 5
(D) 6

**Solution:**
Standard die: opposite pairs (1,6), (2,5), (3,4)
- Top: 2, Bottom: 5 (since 2+5=7)
- Front: 3, Back: 4 (since 3+4=7)
- Remaining pair: 1 and 6

On a standard right-hand die with 2 on top and 3 on front:
The right face is 1.

This follows the right-hand rule convention for standard dice where the faces 1, 2, 3 appear counter-clockwise around their common vertex.

**Answer: (A)** — 1 is on the right.

## Question 7: Block Counting

A 3D structure is built with unit cubes:
- Bottom layer: 3×3 grid = 9 cubes
- Middle layer: 2×2 grid centered on top of bottom layer = 4 cubes
- Top layer: 1 cube centered on top

How many cubes total?

(A) 12
(B) 13
(C) 14
(D) 15

**Solution:**
- Bottom layer: 3×3 = 9 cubes
- Middle layer: 2×2 = 4 cubes
- Top layer: 1×1 = 1 cube
- Total: 9 + 4 + 1 = 14 cubes

**Answer: (C)** — 14 cubes.

## Question 8: Paper Folding — Diagonal Fold

A square paper is folded along the diagonal from bottom-left to top-right. A hole is punched at the midpoint of the folded edge (which is the diagonal). When unfolded, how many holes appear?

(A) 1 hole at the center
(B) 2 holes symmetric about the diagonal
(C) 4 holes
(D) 2 holes on the same diagonal

**Solution:**
- Fold along diagonal: creates a triangle
- Punch at midpoint of folded edge = midpoint of the diagonal
- The folded edge IS the diagonal
- A hole on the fold line appears as ONE hole when unfolded
- The hole is at the midpoint of the diagonal = center of the square

**Answer: (A)** — 1 hole at the center.

## Question 9: Cube — Adjacent Faces

On a cube, faces A, B, C meet at one vertex. Which of the following CANNOT be true?

(A) A is opposite to D
(B) B is opposite to E
(C) C is opposite to F
(D) A is opposite to B

**Solution:**
If A, B, C meet at one vertex, they are ALL mutually adjacent.
- Adjacent faces CANNOT be opposite each other
- Therefore A cannot be opposite B, A cannot be opposite C, B cannot be opposite C

Option (D) says A is opposite B — but A and B meet at a vertex, so they're adjacent. This is impossible.

**Answer: (D)** — A cannot be opposite B because they share a vertex.

## Question 10: Cube Rotation

A cube has a dot on one face. If the cube is rotated 90° clockwise about its vertical axis, the dot that was on the front face moves to:

(A) Back
(B) Left
(C) Right
(D) Top

**Solution:**
90° clockwise rotation about vertical axis:
- Front → Right
- Right → Back
- Back → Left
- Left → Front
- Top → Top (unchanged)
- Bottom → Bottom (unchanged)

Dot on front face → moves to right face.

**Answer: (C)** — Right face.

## Question 12: Cube from Net — Complex

Net arrangement:
```
[1][2][3][4]
      [5]
      [6]
```

When folded, which number is opposite to 3?

(A) 1
(B) 4
(C) 5
(D) 6

**Solution:**
In this L-shaped net:
- Row: 1-2-3-4 (4 in a row)
- Below 3: 5, then 6 below 5

Fold the row into a ring: 1 is adjacent to 2 and 4, 3 is adjacent to 2 and 4
- 1 is opposite to 3 (separated by 2 in the row)
- 2 is opposite to 4 (separated by 3 in the row)
- 5 is opposite to... let me check: 5 is below 3, so 5 is adjacent to 3. 6 is below 5.

Actually, when the 4-square row folds into a ring (top face):
- 1, 2, 3, 4 form the four side faces
- 1 opposite 3, 2 opposite 4
- 5 folds down to become the bottom face
- 6 folds to become the back bottom? No...

Let me reconsider. In this net:
- 1-2-3-4 fold to form four sides of the cube
- 5 folds to be adjacent to 3 (bottom of 3)
- 6 folds to be opposite to 5

So: 1↔3, 2↔4, 5↔6

Wait, that gives 1 opposite 3, but let me verify: In the row 1-2-3-4, if we fold:
- 2 is the front face
- 1 folds to the left, 3 folds to the right
- 4 folds to the back

So 1 (left) opposite 3 (right), 2 (front) opposite 4 (back).
5 is below 3 → 5 is bottom. 6 is below 5 → 6 folds to be opposite 5 → 6 is top.

Actually: 5 is attached to 3. When 3 becomes the right face, 5 folds to the bottom. 6 is attached to 5. When 5 is bottom, 6 folds up to become... the left face? But 1 is already the left face.

Let me reconsider the net: 1-2-3-4 in a row, 5 below 3, 6 below 5.

This doesn't form a standard valid cube net because 6 is two steps below 3 with no connection to the horizontal strip.

Actually, looking at it again: this IS a valid net. When folded:
- 3 is the base face
- 2 folds up (front), 4 folds up (back)
- 1 folds from 2 to become left
- 5 folds down from 3 (below)
- 6 folds from 5

In this case: 3 is opposite 6 (separated by 5 in vertical direction)

**Answer: (D)** — 6 is opposite to 3.

## Question 13: Spatial Visualization — Assembly

Three views of an object are given:
- Front view: a 2×2 square with the top-right quarter missing
- Top view: a 2×2 square (complete)
- Side view: a 2×1 rectangle (vertical, 2 high, 1 wide)

What is the object?

(A) An L-shaped block (3 cubes)
(B) A 2×2×1 slab (4 cubes)
(C) A 2×2×2 cube with one corner removed (7 cubes)
(D) A T-shaped block (4 cubes)

**Solution:**
- Top view: 2×2 square → the base is a full 2×2 arrangement
- Front view: 2×2 with top-right missing → in the front face, top-right position has no cube
- Side view: 2×1 vertical → the depth is only 1 unit

If depth is 1, top view being 2×2 means the object is 2 wide, 2 tall, 1 deep.
Front view has top-right missing → 3 cubes in an L-shape.

**Answer: (A)** — An L-shaped block (3 cubes).

## Question 15: Paper Folding — Unfolding Pattern

A paper is folded left-to-right, then top-to-bottom. An "L" shape is cut from the folded piece (the L is at the bottom-left corner of the folded piece). What does the unfolded paper look like?

(A) Four L-shapes, each in a different quadrant
(B) Two L-shapes mirrored horizontally
(C) One large diamond shape
(D) A symmetrical cross pattern

**Solution:**
- Two folds create 4 layers
- Cutting through all 4 layers creates the same cut in each quadrant
- The L-shape at the bottom-left of the folded piece corresponds to:
  - Bottom-left of the quadrant that was folded
  - When unfolded horizontally: the L mirrors to the right → 2 L-shapes
  - When unfolded vertically: each L mirrors upward → 4 L-shapes

The L-shapes would be in the bottom-left of each quadrant (since the L was at the corner closest to the fold lines).

**Answer: (A)** — Four L-shapes, each in a different quadrant, symmetric about both fold lines.

## Question 16: Block Counting — Hidden Cubes

A structure has:
- Front column: 3 cubes high
- Middle column: 2 cubes high
- Back column: 1 cube high
- All columns are in a single row (1×3 grid from top view)

How many cubes are visible from the front?

(A) 3
(B) 5
(C) 6
(D) 9

**Solution:**
From the front view:
- The front column (3 high) is fully visible: 3 faces
- The middle column (2 high): only the top cube is visible above the front column if front is taller. Front is 3, middle is 2 → middle is completely hidden behind front.
- Back column (1 high): completely hidden behind front.

Wait — if they're in a single row and we look from the front, the front column blocks the others.

From the front: only the front column is visible → 3 faces.

But the question asks "how many cubes are visible from the front" — meaning how many cube faces can you see from the front direction.

If the columns are in a line (front to back):
- Front column: 3 cubes, all visible from front
- Middle column: 2 cubes, hidden behind front column (front is taller)
- Back column: 1 cube, hidden behind front column

Total visible from front: 3 cubes (the front column).

**Answer: (A)** — 3 cubes visible from the front.

## Question 17: Cube from Net — Orientation

Given the net:
```
  [W]
[R][B][L]
  [G]
  [Y]
```
(W=White, R=Red, B=Blue, L=Left, G=Green, Y=Yellow)

If Blue is on top, which color is in front?

(A) White
(B) Green
(C) Red
(D) Yellow

**Solution:**
In this cross net:
- B is at center → B is on top
- R is left of B → R folds to the left side
- L is right of B → L folds to the right side
- W is above B → W folds to the back
- G is below B → G folds to the front
- Y is below G → Y folds to the bottom

With B on top, G is in front.

**Answer: (B)** — Green is in front.

## Question 18: Spatial Visualization — Cross Section

A cylinder is cut by a plane parallel to its base. The cross-section is:

(A) A circle
(B) An ellipse
(C) A rectangle
(D) A parabola

**Solution:**
- Cutting a cylinder parallel to its base → the cross-section is a circle
- Cutting perpendicular to the base → rectangle
- Cutting at an angle → ellipse

**Answer: (A)** — A circle.
