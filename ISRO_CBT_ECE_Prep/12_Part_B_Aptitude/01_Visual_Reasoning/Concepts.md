# Visual Reasoning - Concepts & Strategies

## ISRO Exam Context
- Part B Aptitude: 15 questions, 20 marks, NO negative marking
- Visual reasoning typically accounts for 3-4 questions
- Time allocation: ~2 minutes per question
- Since no negative marking, attempt ALL questions

---

## 1. Shape Completion

### Concept
A figure is given with a missing portion. Identify which option completes the figure.

### Approach
- Look at the overall symmetry of the figure
- Check edge continuity — lines entering the missing region must exit coherently
- Count elements: if the original has N shapes, the answer preserves N
- Verify shading patterns match across the boundary

### Speed Tip
- Trace the boundary of the missing region with your finger on screen
- Eliminate options where any line doesn't connect properly

## 2. Odd One Out

### Concept
Four or five figures are given. One does not belong to the group.

### Common Classification Criteria
| Criterion | What to Check |
|-----------|--------------|
| Rotation | Is one figure a rotated version of others? |
| Symmetry | Does one lack the symmetry others share? |
| Element count | Does one have a different number of elements? |
| Shading | Is shading pattern different? |
| Topology | Does one have a different number of enclosed regions? |
| Open vs Closed | Are some figures open and one closed? |

### Speed Tip
- Check element count first (fastest to verify)
- Then check symmetry axis
- Then check rotation/reflection relationships

---

## 3. Figure Series Completion

### Concept
A sequence of figures follows a rule. Find the next figure in the series.

### Common Patterns
1. **Rotation**: Elements rotate by fixed angle (45°, 90°, 180°)
2. **Addition/Subtraction**: Elements added or removed one at a time
3. **Movement**: An element moves along a path (clockwise/anticlockwise)
4. **Alternation**: Properties alternate between two states
5. **Progressive change**: Size, shading, or position changes gradually

### Approach
- Compare figure 1 and figure 2 to find the transformation
- Verify the same transformation applies from figure 2 to figure 3
- Apply the rule to get the answer

### Speed Tip
- Focus on ONE element at a time across the series
- Ignore elements that stay constant

---

## 4. Visual Analogy

### Concept
A is to B as C is to ?. Find the relationship between first pair, apply to second.

### Approach
1. Identify ALL changes between figure A and figure B
2. List: rotation, reflection, element changes, shading changes, position shifts
3. Apply the EXACT same set of changes to figure C
4. Verify each change in your answer

### Common Transformations
- Mirror + color inversion
- 90° rotation + element addition
- Size reduction + position shift
- Shape morphing (circle becomes square, etc.)

### Speed Tip
- The relationship is usually a SINGLE transformation, not multiple
- If you find two changes, look for a simpler single-rule explanation

## 5. Mirror Images

### Concept
Find the mirror image of a given figure. Mirror line can be vertical, horizontal, or diagonal.

### Rules for Vertical Mirror (most common)
- Left and right are swapped
- Top and bottom remain the same
- Letters/numbers get flipped horizontally
- Symmetric figures remain unchanged

### Rules for Horizontal Mirror
- Top and bottom are swapped
- Left and right remain the same

### Key Mirror Images to Memorize
| Character | Vertical Mirror | Horizontal Mirror |
|-----------|----------------|-------------------|
| B | Not B (reversed) | B (if symmetric) |
| D | Not D (reversed) | D |
| E | Not E (reversed) | E |
| 1 | Reversed 1 | 1 |
| 2 | Reversed 2 | Reversed 2 |
| 3 | Reversed 3 | 3 |
| A | A | Not A |
| H | H | H |
| O | O | O |
| M | M | W |

### Speed Tip
- For vertical mirror: imagine folding the paper along the mirror line
- Check the answer against 2-3 distinctive features, not the whole image

## 6. Embedded Figures

### Concept
A simple figure is hidden within a complex figure. Identify which complex figure contains it.

### Approach
- Break the target figure into its component lines/shapes
- Look for each component in the complex figures
- Pay attention to orientation — the embedded figure may be rotated
- Check for the complete figure, not just parts

### Speed Tip
- Focus on the most distinctive feature of the target (e.g., a specific angle or curve)
- Scan complex figures for that feature first to narrow down candidates

## 7. Paper Cutting & Folding (Preview — detailed in 10_Mirror_Paper_Folding)

### Concept
A paper is folded, a cut is made, and you must identify the unfolded pattern.

### Basic Rules
- Each fold doubles the number of cut impressions
- Symmetry axis = fold line
- Cuts near the folded edge create symmetric patterns when unfolded

## General Speed-Solving Strategies for ISRO

### Time Management
- Total Part B: ~30 minutes for 15 questions
- Visual reasoning: allocate ~2 minutes per question
- If stuck after 90 seconds, mark and move on (no negative marking means come back)

### Elimination Strategy
1. Eliminate obviously wrong answers first
2. For odd-one-out: check 3 criteria (count, symmetry, rotation)
3. For series: verify your rule with ALL given figures before selecting

### Common ISRO Patterns
- 90° clockwise rotation is the most frequent transformation
- Element addition/subduction follows sequential order
- Mirror image questions often use vertical mirror
- Embedded figures are usually rotated but not resized

### Exam-Day Tips
- Keep scratch paper for drawing partial solutions
- For mirror images, use the concept of "folding"
- For paper folding, mentally unfold step by step
- Trust your first instinct if you're confident
