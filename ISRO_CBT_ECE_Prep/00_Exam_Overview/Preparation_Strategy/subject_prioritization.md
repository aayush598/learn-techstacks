# ISRO CBT ECE Exam — Subject Prioritization & Weightage Strategy

## Understanding Subject Weightage

Based on analysis of ISRO previous year papers (2015–2024), here is the approximate weightage distribution:

---

## Priority Tier System

### Tier 1: HIGH Priority (50–55% of paper)

These subjects appear most frequently and carry the highest marks. Master these first.

| Subject | Approx. Weightage | Questions (of 80) | Priority Score |
|---------|-------------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Network Theory** | 10–12% | 8–10 | ★★★★★ |
| **Analog Electronics** | 10–12% | 8–10 | ★★★★★ |
| **Signals & Systems** | 8–10% | 7–8 | ★★★★★ |
| **Digital Electronics** | 8–10% | 7–8 | ★★★★★ |
| **Control Systems** | 8–10% | 7–8 | ★★★★☆ |

**Why Tier 1?**
- Together these account for **~50%** of the total marks
- Questions are mostly **formula-based** and **predictable**
- High **return on investment** — less study time for more marks
- Well-covered in standard textbooks

---

### Tier 2: MEDIUM Priority (30–35% of paper)

Important subjects that require thorough but slightly less intensive preparation.

| Subject | Approx. Weightage | Questions (of 80) | Priority Score |
|---------|-------------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Communication Systems** | 8–10% | 7–8 | ★★★★☆ |
| **Electromagnetic Theory** | 7–9% | 6–8 | ★★★★☆ |
| **Microprocessors & Microcontrollers** | 6–8% | 5–7 | ★★★☆☆ |
| **Digital Signal Processing** | 5–7% | 4–6 | ★★★☆☆ |

**Why Tier 2?**
- Accounts for **~30%** of total marks
- Some topics are **memory-intensive** (e.g., 8085 instruction set)
- Questions can be **tricky** but follow patterns
- Essential for crossing the cutoff comfortably

---

### Tier 3: LOW Priority (10–15% of paper)

Subjects with lower weightage but still important for a high score.

| Subject | Approx. Weightage | Questions (of 80) | Priority Score |
|---------|-------------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **VLSI Design** | 3–5% | 3–4 | ★★☆☆☆ |
| **Electronic Measurements** | 3–5% | 3–4 | ★★☆☆☆ |
| **Power Electronics** | 3–5% | 3–4 | ★★☆☆☆ |

**Why Tier 3?**
- Only **~10%** of the paper
- Topics are **scattered** and unpredictable
- Can be covered with targeted study of key concepts
- Don't ignore — these can be the difference between selection and rejection

- **Hard + Low Weightage** → Study key topics only (VLSI, Power Electronics deep topics)

## Detailed Subject Breakdown

### 1. Network Theory (PRIORITY: ★★★★★)

| Subtopic | Importance | Expected Questions |
|----------|------------|---------------------|
| KVL/KCL analysis | Very High | 2–3 |
| Thevenin/Norton theorems | Very High | 2–3 |
| Two-port network parameters | High | 1–2 |
| Transient analysis (RL/RC/RLC) | High | 1–2 |
| Network theorems (Superposition, etc.) | Medium | 1 |
| AC steady-state analysis | Medium | 1 |

**Key Formulas to Memorize:**
- Z, Y, h, ABCD parameter relations
- Time constants (τ = L/R, τ = RC)
- Maximum power transfer theorem
- Reciprocity and symmetry conditions

## Final Advice

1. **Don't try to be perfect** — focus on being consistently good across all subjects
2. **Tier 1 subjects are your backbone** — 50% marks from just 5 subjects
3. **Don't skip any subject entirely** — even Tier 3 subjects have 2–4 questions
4. **Weightage changes year to year** — be prepared for surprises
5. **Strong subjects = speed** — these save time for hard questions
6. **Weak subjects = accuracy** — attempt only what you're sure about
7. **Practice previous year papers** — they reveal actual weightage patterns
8. **Track your accuracy per subject** — focus revision on low-accuracy subjects

---

*The goal is not to master every subject equally, but to maximize your total score by strategically allocating your limited preparation time.*
