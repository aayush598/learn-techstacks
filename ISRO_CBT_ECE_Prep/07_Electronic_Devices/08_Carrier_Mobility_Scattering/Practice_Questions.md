# Carrier Mobility & Scattering - Practice Questions (ISRO Style)

## Q1. Mobility Definition
**The mobility of carriers in a semiconductor is defined as:**

(a) Drift velocity per unit current
(b) Drift velocity per unit electric field
(c) Current per unit voltage
(d) Conductivity per unit doping

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** μ = vd/E. Mobility is drift velocity per unit electric field (cm²/V·s).

---

## Q2. Einstein Relation
**At 300K, if electron mobility is 1350 cm²/V·s, the diffusion coefficient is:**

(a) 35 cm²/s
(b) 52 cm²/s
(c) 135 cm²/s
(d) 350 cm²/s

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** Dn = μnVT = 1350 × 0.026 = 35.1 cm²/s

---

## Q3. Lattice Scattering
**Lattice scattering dominated mobility varies with temperature as:**

(a) T³/²
(b) T⁻³/²
(c) T¹/²
(d) T⁻¹

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Lattice scattering: μL ∝ T⁻³/². Mobility decreases with temperature.

---

## Q4. Impurity Scattering
**Ionized impurity scattering dominated mobility varies with temperature as:**

(a) T³/²
(b) T⁻³/²
(c) T²
(d) T⁻²

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** Impurity scattering: μI ∝ T³/²/NI. Mobility increases with temperature.

---

## Q5. Saturation Velocity
**The saturation velocity of electrons in silicon at 300K is approximately:**

(a) 10⁵ cm/s
(b) 10⁶ cm/s
(c) 10⁷ cm/s
(d) 10⁸ cm/s

**Answer: (c)**
**Explanation:** vsat ≈ 10⁷ cm/s for Si at 300K. This is the maximum drift velocity.

---

## Q6. Doping Effect on Mobility
**Increasing the doping concentration in a semiconductor:**

(a) Increases mobility
(b) Decreases mobility
(c) No effect on mobility
(d) First increases then decreases

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Higher doping → more impurity scattering → lower mobility.

---

## Q7. Electron vs Hole Mobility
**Electron mobility is always higher than hole mobility because:**

(a) Electrons have lower effective mass
(b) Electrons have higher charge
(c) Electrons are smaller
(d) Electrons move faster

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** μ = qτ/m*. Electrons have lower effective mass → higher mobility.

---

## Q8. Matthiessen's Rule
**Matthiessen's rule is used to:**

(a) Add mobilities directly
(b) Combine scattering mechanisms
(c) Calculate diffusion coefficient
(d) Find saturation velocity

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** 1/μ = 1/μL + 1/μI + 1/μN. Combines different scattering mechanisms.

---

## Q9. Temperature at Room T
**At room temperature (300K), the dominant scattering mechanism in pure silicon is:**

(a) Ionized impurity scattering
(b) Lattice scattering
(c) Neutral impurity scattering
(d) Surface scattering

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** In pure Si at 300K, lattice scattering dominates.

---

## Q10. Effective Mobility
**The effective mobility in a MOSFET is lower than bulk mobility due to:**

(a) Higher temperature
(b) Surface roughness scattering
(c) Lower doping
(d) Higher electric field

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Surface roughness, Coulomb scattering, and phonon scattering at interface reduce effective mobility.

---

## Q11. Critical Field
**The critical electric field for velocity saturation in Si is approximately:**

(a) 10³ V/cm
(b) 10⁴ V/cm
(c) 10⁵ V/cm
(d) 10⁶ V/cm

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Esat = 2vsat/μn ≈ 2×10⁷/1350 ≈ 1.5×10⁴ V/cm

---

## Q12. Mobility and Conductivity
**If doping increases by 10 times but mobility decreases by 3 times, conductivity:**

(a) Increases by 10 times
(b) Decreases by 3 times
(c) Increases by 3.3 times
(d) Remains same

**Answer: (c)**
**Explanation:** σ = qnμ. If n↑10 and μ↓3, σ↑(10/3) = 3.3 times.

---

## Q13. Ge vs Si Mobility
**Germanium has higher mobility than silicon because:**

(a) Ge has smaller bandgap
(b) Ge has lower effective mass
(c) Ge has higher doping
(d) Ge has larger lattice constant

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Ge has lower effective mass → higher mobility (μn = 3900 vs 1350 for Si).

---

## Q14. Temperature Coefficient
**At room temperature, the temperature coefficient of mobility in Si is:**

(a) Positive
(b) Negative
(c) Zero
(d) Depends on doping

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Lattice scattering dominates at room T → μ ∝ T⁻³/² → negative coefficient.

---

## Q15. Drift Velocity Formula
**At low electric fields, drift velocity is:**

(a) Proportional to E²
(b) Proportional to E
(c) Independent of E
(d) Inversely proportional to E

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** vd = μE (Ohmic region). Velocity proportional to field.

---

## Q16. High Field Behavior
**At high electric fields, drift velocity:**

(a) Increases linearly with E
(b) Increases as √E
(c) Saturates to vsat
(d) Decreases

**Answer: (c)**
**Explanation:** At high fields, optical phonon emission limits velocity to vsat.

---

## Q17. Mean Free Path
**The mean free path of carriers:**

(a) Increases with temperature
(b) Decreases with temperature
(c) Is independent of temperature
(d) Depends only on doping

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** λ = vthτ. As T increases, vth increases faster than τ decreases → λ increases.

---

## Q18. Alloy Scattering
**Alloy scattering is important in:**

(a) Pure silicon
(b) Compound semiconductors
(c) Doped germanium
(d) Oxide layers

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Alloy scattering occurs in compound semiconductors (GaAs, InGaN) due to random atom distribution.

---

## Q19. Mobility and Frequency
**Higher mobility results in:**

(a) Lower cutoff frequency
(b) Higher cutoff frequency
(c) No effect on frequency
(d) Lower power dissipation

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** fT = gm/(2πC). Higher μ → higher gm → higher fT.

---

## Q20. Strained Silicon
**Strained silicon improves mobility by:**

(a) Increasing doping
(b) Reducing effective mass
(c) Increasing temperature
(d) Decreasing bandgap

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Strain modifies band structure → reduces effective mass → increases mobility.

---

## Q21. Mobility Units
**The units of mobility are:**

(a) V/cm
(b) cm/s
(c) cm²/V·s
(d) A/cm²

**Answer: (c)**
**Explanation:** μ = vd/E → (cm/s)/(V/cm) = cm²/V·s

---

## Q22. Surface Scattering
**Surface scattering is dominant in:**

(a) Bulk semiconductors
(b) MOSFET channels
(c) PN junctions
(d) Metallurgical junctions

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Carriers in MOSFET channel are confined near Si-SiO₂ interface → surface scattering.

---

## Q23. Temperature Dependence
**If mobility at 300K is μ0, at 600K (lattice scattering), mobility is approximately:**

(a) μ0/2
(b) μ0/√2
(c) μ0 × √2
(d) μ0/2√2

**Answer: (d)**
**Explanation:** μ ∝ T⁻³/². At 600K: μ = μ0(300/600)³/² = μ0(1/2)³/² = μ0/(2√2)

---

## Q24. Einstein Relation Validity
**The Einstein relation D/μ = kT/q is valid for:**

(a) Non-degenerate semiconductors
(b) Degenerate semiconductors
(c) All semiconductors
(d) Metals only

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** Einstein relation assumes Boltzmann statistics → valid for non-degenerate semiconductors.

---

## Q25. Velocity Overshoot
**Velocity overshoot occurs in:**

(a) Long channel devices
(b) Short channel devices
(c) All devices at room temperature
(d) Only at cryogenic temperatures

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** In short channels, carriers traverse without reaching steady-state → velocity overshoot.
