## Q1. Intrinsic Carrier Concentration
**The intrinsic carrier concentration in silicon at 300K is 1.5 × 10¹⁰ cm⁻³. If the temperature is increased to 400K, ni approximately becomes:**

(a) 4.5 × 10¹² cm⁻³
(b) 1.5 × 10¹² cm⁻³
(c) 4.5 × 10¹⁰ cm⁻³
(d) 1.5 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻³

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** ni roughly doubles for every 11°C rise. From 300K to 400K (100°C rise), ni increases by ~100× → 1.5 × 10¹² cm⁻³. Using formula: ni(400) ≈ 4.5 × 10¹² cm⁻³.

## Q2. Mass Action Law
**In an N-type semiconductor with ND = 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ at 300K, the hole concentration is:**

(a) 1.5 × 10¹⁰ cm⁻³
(b) 2.25 × 10⁴ cm⁻³
(c) 10¹⁶ cm⁻³
(d) 10²⁶ cm⁻³

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** p = ni²/ND = (1.5 × 10¹⁰)² / 10¹⁶ = 2.25 × 10²⁰ / 10¹⁶ = 2.25 × 10⁴ cm⁻³

---

## Q3. Fermi Level Position
**The Fermi level in N-type silicon with ND = 10¹⁷ cm⁻³ at 300K is approximately (NC = 2.8 × 10¹⁹ cm⁻³):**

(a) 0.12 eV below EC
(b) 0.12 eV above EC
(c) 0.06 eV below EC
(d) 0.24 eV below EC

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** EF = EC - kT·ln(NC/ND) = EC - 0.026·ln(2.8×10¹⁹/10¹⁷) = EC - 0.026·ln(280) = EC - 0.026 × 5.63 = EC - 0.146 eV ≈ 0.12 eV below EC

---

## Q4. Conductivity Calculation
**The conductivity of a silicon sample doped with ND = 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ is (μn = 1350 cm²/V-s, q = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C):**

(a) 0.216 S/cm
(b) 2.16 S/cm
(c) 21.6 S/cm
(d) 2.16 × 10⁻² S/cm

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** σ ≈ qNDμn = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 10¹⁶ × 1350 = 1.6 × 10⁻³ × 1350 = 2.16 × 10⁻¹ = 0.216 S/cm

## Q5. Einstein Relation
**If the electron mobility in silicon is 1350 cm²/V-s at 300K, the electron diffusion coefficient is approximately:**

(a) 35 cm²/s
(b) 52 cm²/s
(c) 15 cm²/s

**Answer: (a)**

## Q6. Effective Density of States

## Q7. Band Gap and Temperature
**The band gap of silicon at T=0K is 1.17 eV. At 300K, it decreases to approximately:**

(a) 1.12 eV
(b) 1.08 eV
(c) 1.21 eV
(d) 0.67 eV

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** Eg(300K) = 1.17 - (4.73×10⁻⁴ × 300²)/(300+636) = 1.17 - 0.045 = 1.125 eV ≈ 1.12 eV

## Q8. Doping Compensation
**A silicon sample is doped with ND = 10¹⁷ cm⁻³ and NA = 3 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³. The majority carrier concentration is:**

(a) 10¹⁷ cm⁻³ (electrons)
(b) 7 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ (electrons)
(c) 3 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ (holes)
(d) 1.3 × 10¹⁷ cm⁻³ (electrons)

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** n ≈ ND - NA = 10¹⁷ - 3 × 10¹⁶ = 7 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ (N-type since ND > NA)

## Q9. Mobility and Temperature
**The mobility of carriers in semiconductors at low temperatures (impurity scattering dominant) varies as:**

(a) T⁻³/²
(b) T³/²
(c) T¹/²
(d) T⁻¹

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** At low temperatures, impurity scattering dominates: μ ∝ T³/². At high temperatures, lattice scattering dominates: μ ∝ T⁻³/².

## Q10. Drift Current Density
**In a silicon sample with n = 10¹⁶ cm⁻³, μn = 1350 cm²/V-s, and electric field E = 100 V/cm, the drift current density is:**

(a) 216 A/cm²
(b) 2.16 A/cm²
(c) 21.6 A/cm²
(d) 0.216 A/cm²

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** J = qnμnE = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 10¹⁶ × 1350 × 100 = 1.6 × 10⁻³ × 135000 = 216 A/cm²

## Q11. Resistivity Calculation
**The resistivity of intrinsic silicon at 300K is approximately:**

(a) 2.3 × 10⁵ Ω·cm
(b) 2300 Ω·cm
(c) 2.3 × 10⁻⁵ Ω·cm
(d) 2.3 × 10³ Ω·cm

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** ρ = 1/(qni(μn + μp)) = 1/(1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 1.5×10¹⁰ × (1350+480)) = 1/(1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 1.5×10¹⁰ × 1830) = 1/(4.4 × 10⁻⁶) ≈ 2.3 × 10⁵ Ω·cm

---

## Q12. Extrinsic vs Intrinsic
**A silicon sample at 300K has ND = 10¹⁶ cm⁻³. The temperature at which it becomes intrinsic is approximately:**

(a) 200°C
(b) 300°C
(c) 400°C
(d) 600°C

**Answer: (d)**
**Explanation:** When ni(T) = ND, semiconductor becomes intrinsic. For ND = 10¹⁶ cm⁻³, T ≈ 600K (327°C) ≈ 600°C.

## Q13. Mass Action Law Application
**In a silicon sample at 300K, n = 5 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ and p = 4.5 × 10³ cm⁻³. The product np is:**

(a) 2.25 × 10²⁰ cm⁻⁶
(b) 1.5 × 10¹⁰ cm⁻⁶
(c) 2.25 × 10¹³ cm⁻⁶
(d) 5 × 10²⁰ cm⁻⁶

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** np = 5 × 10¹⁶ × 4.5 × 10³ = 22.5 × 10¹⁹ = 2.25 × 10²⁰ cm⁻⁶ = ni²

## Q14. Conductivity Type
**A germanium sample has NA = 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ and ND = 10¹⁴ cm⁻³. The sample is:**

(a) N-type with σ ≈ 0.52 S/cm
(b) P-type with σ ≈ 0.3 S/cm
(c) Intrinsic
(d) Compensated

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** NA > ND → P-type. p ≈ NA - ND ≈ 10¹⁶ cm⁻³. σ ≈ qNAμp = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 10¹⁶ × 1900 = 0.304 S/cm

## Q15. Fermi Level in P-type
**In P-type silicon with NA = 10¹⁷ cm⁻³ at 300K, the Fermi level is approximately (NV = 1.04 × 10¹⁹ cm⁻³):**

(a) 0.12 eV above EV
(b) 0.12 eV below EV
(c) 0.06 eV above EV
(d) 0.24 eV above EV

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** EF = EV + kT·ln(NV/NA) = EV + 0.026·ln(1.04×10¹⁹/10¹⁷) = EV + 0.026·ln(104) = EV + 0.026 × 4.64 = EV + 0.121 eV ≈ 0.12 eV above EV

## Q16. Effective Mass
**The effective mass of electrons in GaAs is 0.067m₀. This results in:**

(a) Higher mobility than Si
(b) Lower mobility than Si
(c) Same mobility as Si
(d) No effect on mobility

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** μ = qτ/m*. Lower effective mass → higher mobility. GaAs has μn = 8500 cm²/V-s vs Si μn = 1350 cm²/V-s.

---

## Q17. Temperature Coefficient
**The temperature coefficient of conductivity in intrinsic silicon is:**

(a) Positive
(b) Negative
(c) Zero
(d) Depends on doping

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** For intrinsic Si, ni increases exponentially with T → σ increases → positive temperature coefficient. For metals (doped semiconductors), conductivity decreases with T → negative coefficient.

## Q18. Carrier Concentration
**At 300K, if EC - EF = 0.25 eV in silicon, the electron concentration is approximately (NC = 2.8 × 10¹⁹ cm⁻³):**

(a) 1.3 × 10¹⁵ cm⁻³
(b) 2.8 × 10¹⁹ cm⁻³
(c) 1.5 × 10¹⁰ cm⁻³
(d) 5.6 × 10¹⁵ cm⁻³

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** n = NC·exp(-(EC-EF)/kT) = 2.8×10¹⁹ × exp(-0.25/0.026) = 2.8×10¹⁹ × exp(-9.62) = 2.8×10¹⁹ × 6.7×10⁻⁵ = 1.88×10¹⁵ cm⁻³

---

## Q19. Combined Doping
**A silicon sample has ND = 5 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ and NA = 2 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³. The minority carrier concentration is:**

(a) 3 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³
(b) 4.5 × 10³ cm⁻³
(c) 7.5 × 10³ cm⁻³
(d) 1.5 × 10¹⁰ cm⁻³

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** N-type (ND > NA). n ≈ 3 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³. p = ni²/n = (1.5×10¹⁰)² / (3×10¹⁶) = 2.25×10²⁰/3×10¹⁶ = 7.5×10³ cm⁻³

## Q20. Hall Effect
**In an N-type semiconductor, the Hall coefficient is negative because:**

(a) Electrons have negative charge
(b) Holes are majority carriers
(c) The magnetic field is negative
(d) The current is negative

**Answer: (a)**
**Explanation:** RH = 1/(qn) for N-type. Since q is negative for electrons, RH is negative. Hall effect measures carrier type and concentration.

## Q21. Energy Band Diagram
**The Fermi level in intrinsic silicon at 300K lies:**

(a) Exactly at midgap
(b) Slightly above midgap
(c) Slightly below midgap
(d) At the conduction band edge

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Ei is slightly above midgap because Nv < Nc (mp* ≠ mn*). For Si, Ei ≈ EC - Eg/2 - 0.013 eV.

---

## Q22. Heavy Doping Effect
**When silicon is heavily doped (> 10¹⁸ cm⁻³), the bandgap narrows. This results in:**

(a) Decrease in ni
(b) Increase in ni
(c) No change in ni
(d) Increase in resistivity

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Bandgap narrowing reduces Eg → nie increases exponentially. nie = ni·exp(ΔEg/2kT).

---

## Q23. Diffusion Current
**The diffusion current density for electrons is given by:**

(a) Jn = qnμnE
(b) Jn = qDn(dn/dx)
(c) Jn = -qDn(dn/dx)
(d) Jn = qnDnE

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Diffusion current flows from high to low concentration. Jn,diff = qDn(dn/dx). For holes: Jp,diff = -qDp(dp/dx).

---

**Explanation:** Typical minority carrier lifetime in Si is 1-100 μs. This affects switching speed and recombination rate.

## Q25. Thermal Equilibrium
**Under thermal equilibrium, the relationship between electron and hole concentrations is:**

(a) n = p
(b) np = ni²
(c) n/p = μp/μn
(d) n + p = ni

**Answer: (b)**
**Explanation:** Law of mass action states np = ni² under thermal equilibrium, regardless of doping level.
